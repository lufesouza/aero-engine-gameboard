## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** We said we'd do a new airplane when the market, the technology and Boeing were all ready. We've done the work to get here: the development programmes we had in front of us are done, and the balance sheet is back where it belongs. So today we are launching a new Boeing single-aisle airplane.

We're planning for entry into service in 2040, and that plan has headroom built into it. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast. We won't build airplanes ahead of a mature design. We'll work the certification plan with the FAA from day one. We'll develop this airplane with our suppliers on cost-plus and risk-share terms, not fixed price.

On engines, we've asked CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce to bring us their best. CFM is our baseline, and we want more than one source of supply on this airplane.

Our 737 MAX and 787 customers come first while we do this. Stable production and the KPIs decide our rates. Debt stays first in our capital allocation. I'm not going to give you any more dates today. The financials will follow our production performance. We still have a lot of work to do.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. On that basis, Airbus is launching its next-generation single-aisle aircraft. We prepared it through a decade of technology roadmaps, and we are launching it now because those technologies are mature, not to meet anyone else's calendar. We target entry into service in the second half of the 2030s. Customers have been well served by a choice of engines on the A320 family, and the same will apply to its successor. We have selected CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce, and we invite all three to join the programme as risk-sharing partners. Until the new aircraft enters service, the A320neo and A350 families remain our priority, with a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up and continued work to secure our supply chain, engines and critical materials first. We will fund the programme with financial discipline, as disciplined investments in preparing our future portfolio, within our financial flexibility and our existing shareholder return policy. We will continue to work with our industry partners for a tariff-free, zero-for-zero regime for aerospace trade. A stronger and more sovereign European aerospace industry is built on one well-prepared programme at a time.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That doesn't change this year.

Let me tell you what we know and what we don't. Nothing new has launched yet in the narrowbody. When it does, our customers decide the aircraft and the timing, not us, and we don't have a birthright on that next order. So CFM is launching a new ducted engine programme, so that every airframer has a CFM engine to choose on whatever comes next. CFM is also partnering with Embraer, putting a CFM engine in front of a third airframer. And at GE we're launching a GEnx upgrade for the 787 and the airlines that fly it.

RISE remains our next generation. We're all in on the open fan technology, and it stays funded. But it's really not for us to say when that architecture enters service. If an airframer launches a new narrowbody now, the open fan won't be ready for it, and we won't ask anyone to wait.

Here's what we don't know: which aircraft launch, when, and with which engines. We won't give you dates our testing hasn't earned. You'll get them when it has. We'll fund this within our capital allocation framework and keep the balance sheet where it is. We want to be on all the critical platforms that matter in the decades ahead. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Bad news first. For too long the GTF fleet was not where it needed to be, and we own that. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF, and the fleet comes first. We keep inserting the Hot Section durability kit across the fleet, and no new programme takes parts or engineers away from that work. We control what we can control.

Pratt & Whitney will commit its next-generation geared turbofan, GTF2, in any round an airframer selects it, sole source or shared, on standard commercial terms. GTF2 will be ready six years from launch: 2036 for a 2030 commitment, and no earlier. Every life-limited part will be at full life at entry into service, and durability will be validated in severe-environment testing before then. If anything has to give, the date moves, not the testing.

We are making no Joint Venture commitment this round. We will not speak for any airframer's plans, dates or rates; those are theirs to announce. These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So, plainly. Rolls-Royce will launch UltraFan for any airframer that selects it, in the round it selects it. Not before. Entry into service no earlier than seven years from launch, with support in place before entry into service. We don't need partnership for capability, but our preference is that: the Joint Venture is open to Pratt & Whitney, and we commit once its commitment is on the record. Widebody is our core, and we will not put it at risk. We won't do anything not profitable. We don't commit that easily; when we commit, we deliver.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Engine competition with CFM as the baseline — We invite CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce to propose engines for the new airplane under code 7. CFM is the baseline launch engine and certifies first. A P&W or Rolls-Royce engine follows in sequence once it shows maturity and durability [BX-1298]. CFM must be able to support both our rates and our competitor's [BX-1264, BX-1325]. Goal: two or three engine sources on our narrowbody and three narrowbody engine makers in the market.
- **Boeing:** Certification plan with the FAA from day one, and no production-certification concurrency — We agree a transparent certification plan with the FAA before detailed design freezes. We build no production airplanes ahead of a mature, certifiable design [BX-1263, BX-1309]. We blame no one: we own the schedule [BX-1323].
- **Boeing:** No fixed-price development — Development contracts with suppliers and partners are cost-plus or risk-share, never fixed-price [BX-1262, BX-1304].
- **Boeing:** Balance sheet first in capital allocation — Debt repair stays first [BX-1305, BX-0556]. I recommend to the Board no shareholder returns and no new equity until we are solidly investment grade; the Board reserves both decisions. The $10B of undrawn revolvers stays undrawn.
- **Boeing:** 737 MAX and 787 customer commitment — Production stability on the 737 MAX and 787 lines comes first, gated by KPIs. The new airplane is built for commonality with the 737 fleet so that our customers' transition is planned, not forced [BX-1273, BX-1274].
- **Airbus:** Public invitation to engine makers to share risk on NGSA — Airbus has selected CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce (code 7) and invites all three to bring a new narrowbody engine to NGSA as risk-sharing partners, with entry into service in the second half of the 2030s. This supports the enabler (dual or triple sources, three narrowbody engine makers). It is unpriced, and it is a recommendation only, as the Board noted.
- **Airbus:** Secure our own supply for the A320 family ramp — Continue to bring critical work packages in-house and to de-risk titanium and rare earths for our own production system [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063]. This is aimed only at our own disciplined and controlled ramp-up. It is not aimed at any rival's supply chain.
- **Airbus:** Zero-for-zero trade advocacy through industry coalitions — Keep working with industry partners, Boeing included, for a tariff-free aerospace trade regime [AX-0088, AX-0046].
- **CFM/GE:** Offer a CFM engine to every airframer on every new narrowbody — This is H1: we refuse no airframer and lock none out [CX-0587, CX-0185]. The offer is a LEAP derivative or the new ducted engine, as each customer chooses. No price or terms are stated in public: CFM narrowbody pricing is a joint decision with Safran [CX-0184].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Public conditional GTF2 offer on standard terms — Pratt & Whitney will commit GTF2 in any round an airframer selects it, sole or shared source. It is ready six years from launch (2036 for a 2030 commitment, no earlier), with all life-limited parts at full life at entry into service and durability validated in severe-environment testing first. Standard commercial terms: no launch-style discount, concession or free upgrade [PX-0055][PX-0100]. The offer books no selection and names no airframer date.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF Hot Section durability kit insertion continues across the fleet — Continued insertion of the Hot Section kit across the GTF fleet, charged on standard commercial terms and not given away [PX-0088][PX-0106][PX-0100]. It has no effect on the game board. The fleet comes first [PX-0080].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Open door to Pratt & Whitney on the Joint Venture — Our preferred route is a partner. We commit to the Joint Venture once P&W's commitment is on the public record. This round there is no commitment and no terms.
