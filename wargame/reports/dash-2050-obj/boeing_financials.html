Boeing financials: the fps pays back in 2046, and the balance sheet stays repaired throughout

Boeing launched fps on the 10-year ramp in 2030, open to all three engine makers, and held everything else. Two views of its money follow. The dashboard values Boeing's new-airplane business at 2026 prices: +$0.66B of present value against the status quo, the same as in the previous game. The GM's balance-sheet tracker follows the whole company's cash and net debt in 2026 dollars, starting from the analysts' consensus and adding the game's changes. The two are never mixed in one chart.

Nothing moves until 2037; then Airbus's NGSA takes share for three years.
  • Through 2036 the board books the status quo every year: 800 narrowbodies at $48M and an 8% margin, 100 widebodies, $56.4B of revenue and $6.67B of operating profit.
  • NGSA enters service in 2037 and Boeing's share falls a point a year to 37% in 2039: 740 deliveries, $0.23B a year below the status quo.
From 2040 the fps lifts profit by $7.65B a year, rising to $10.18B.
  • The fps sells at $55M against the 737 MAX's $48M, at a 25.6% margin against 8%.
  • Share recovers a point a year from 38% in 2040 to 47% in 2049, so operating profit goes from $14.32B to $16.86B, against $6.67B in the status quo. The widebody is unchanged: no 787 Re-engine.
  • Over 2026-2060 the game adds $200.7B of operating profit at 2026 prices.
In present value, the bill and the debt penalty absorb almost all of it: ΔPV +$0.66B.
  • Narrowbody profit above the status quo: +$20.30B in PV at 2026 (10.5%).
  • The fps bill: the dashboard charges all $60.32B at entry into service in 2040, −$14.91B in PV.
  • The debt penalty: the board's α (0.3) on $45B of debt, rising by $19.12B undiscounted when the bill is added, −$4.73B in PV.
In cash, the fps costs the owners until 2045 and pays back from 2046.
  • The tracker spends the bill over 2030-2039 on the standard curve, from $3.02B in 2030 to $8.44B in 2036. Free cash flow never goes negative: from 2030 at least $7.12B in the base case and $2.52B in the stress case (2036).
  • Net debt is at its historical level from 2029 (2030 in the stress case), so the fps is paid from cash that would otherwise go to the owners.
  • Against Do Nothing (no fps while Airbus launches NGSA) the owners receive less until 2045 and more from 2046: $44.0B more by 2050 and $136.6B more by 2060, in 2026 dollars, undiscounted.
Every plan on the 10-year ramp keeps the stress line; the 7-year keeps it only with the 737 rate increase.
  • The 7-year ramp alone is worth $0.46B more and gives the owners $30.2B more by 2050, but its spend peaks at $12.54B in 2034 and the stress case puts net debt back to $0.47B at end-2035.
  • With the 737 rate increase the 7-year keeps the line (net debt $0.41B at its 2034 peak, zero by end-2035), but it is then worth +0.38, less than the 10-year's +0.66.
  • A 787 Re-engine beside the 10-year was worth $2.20B more and keeps every debt line; it was kept out by Boeing's one-development-at-a-time rule and its Board's downside test.
  • Do Nothing is −$3.64B: Boeing's narrowbody share falls to 20% by 2050.

Present value: how +$0.66B is built

ΔPV bridge, $B at 2026 (WACC 10.5%)

Narrowbody operating profit above the status quo, discounted; less the fps bill, charged in full at entry into service (2040) as the dashboard does; less the board's debt penalty, D × α × (D ÷ value)² with α 0.3, D = $45B and value $130B, which rises to D = $105.32B when the bill is added ($19.12B undiscounted). Widebody profit, the 737 rate increase, Delay Tactics and the two-front strain are zero for Boeing in this game.

Cash and net debt: the GM's tracker

Whole-company free cash flow and net debt in 2026 dollars. Base free cash flow is the analysts' consensus for 2027-2030 (deflated at 2.2%), flat after; the stress case is one-third lower. The game adds 79% of (its profit change − fps cash), and 3% real interest on any debt it adds. Debt comes first; once net debt is at its historical level (about zero), the surplus is cash to the owners. This is how Control's balance-sheet objective is scored; it does not change the dashboard's payoff.

Method and sources

  • Game. Run dash-2050-obj: four sealed rounds (2030, 2035, 2045, 2050) on combined_dash.py at the Game_v3.txt snapshot. Boeing's orders: fps on the 10-year ramp in 2030 with engine code 7, then hold. The final state equals the 2035 state.
  • P&L and ΔPV. The GM valuation of the final state (data/report_data.json): the dashboard's own airframer model at the game's dates (fps 2040, NGSA 2037). ΔPV is against the status quo in which nobody launches anything.
  • Tracker. wargame/dashgame/finance.py and finance_inputs.json. Start $22.53B net debt at end-2026; base free cash flow $5.55B, $9.44B, $12.20B, $13.79B (2027-2030, 2026 dollars), then $13.79B flat; tax 21%; real rate 3%; CPI 2.2%; development cash on a standard curve (5% in year 1 of 10, peaking at 14% in year 7).
  • Alternatives. data/boeing_fin.json, from make_boeing_fin_data.py, which replays each 2030 plan through the game rules.
