Speaker notes

WHAT TO SAY: Four moves. Move 1 is what we did, and we should keep it. The 10-year plan is the date we can trust; it keeps the balance sheet safe in a downturn without a 737 rate increase; and it is the ramp that lets the 787 Re-engine start in 2030 within our overlap cap. Opening the fps to all three engine makers met both supply goals at no cost to value, though both now rest on one new engine (see Risks). Move 2 is the change: launch the 787 Re-engine in 2030, alongside the fps. It is worth $2.2B over holding whether Airbus plays as it did (+$2.86B) or as the game's model predicts, holding the A350 but running Delay Tactics against the fps (+$1.42B). Holding is not safe either: against our hold, an A350 Re-engine was Airbus's best move in every round. This is a decision for the Board, because it means lifting two of its 2030 rules (one development at a time, the -$1B downside limit) and setting aside the company doctrine of no Re-engine before 2036.
