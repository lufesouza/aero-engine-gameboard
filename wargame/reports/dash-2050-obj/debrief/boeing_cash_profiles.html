How the numbers work. Free cash flow after the programmes = consensus free cash flow + 79% × (the programmes' profit change minus programme cash) minus interest on added debt (none here). After tax relief the $66.52B costs $52.55B: 38% of the decade's free cash flow, 57% in the stress case.

Programme cash follows the game master's development curve (5% of the bill in the first year, peaking at 14% in year 7 of 10), squeezed into five years for the 787 Re-engine. The overlap cost is the board's $3B cost of two developments at once, for the two years they overlap (2033-34). The value figures in the debrief book each bill at entry into service instead; this page shows cash as it is spent.

Not modelled: customer deposits, risk-sharing partners and engine makers' contributions, which would only help.

Source: war game 2030-2050, game master's balance-sheet tracker (whole company, 2026 dollars; consensus free cash flow from S&P Capital IQ to 2030, held flat after; stress case one-third lower; 21% tax; net debt first, then owners). Both plans replayed with every other company's orders as played.
