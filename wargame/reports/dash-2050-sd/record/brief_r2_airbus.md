## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** When I came in, I said our priority, far and away, was debt, and that we'd do a new airplane when the market, the technology and Boeing were ready. We've done the first part. The balance sheet is repaired, the 777X is delivering, the 737 MAX family is certified, and the 737 line is building steadily at 57 a month. A lot of people did hard work the right way to get here. I think we've turned the ship. Now the job is to keep it steady.

We are not launching a new airplane this year, and I'm not going to give you a date. The work on our next single-aisle goes on, in stages and with the data in front of us. We've asked all three engine makers to bring us their best proposals, with durability proven before entry into service. When we do launch, it will be on a conservative plan with headroom in it, and we will not take on fixed-price development risk.

On production, we'll keep the 737 where it is and keep it stable. A rate step only comes when the KPIs say so, with no subjectivity. We'll keep adding 787 capacity in Charleston as our customers need it. The 787 remains the most efficient airplane in its class. The financials will follow our production performance.

It is so much more important that we do this right than fast. I'd much rather be a month late than a month early. We'll keep earning our customers' trust in every transaction.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Today Airbus launches its next-generation single-aisle aircraft, for entry into service in the second half of the 2030s. This is the Airbus of the 2030s that we have been preparing for: a step change in efficiency, built on technologies we have matured and demonstrated, the wing first among them, and on a production system designed together with the aircraft. We will invite CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce to power it, so that our customers have a choice of engine and our ramp-up has secure supply. The A320neo family remains the backbone of our single-aisle offer. We will keep its lines running alongside the new aircraft's ramp-up, giving our customers a long and orderly transition. On widebodies our focus stays on the A350 family and the A350F. We will continue a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up, and we are securing key work packages, materials and skills in Europe for our own industrial system. The programme is funded from operating cash flow within our financial framework, preserving our financial flexibility and our shareholder return policy. With our industry partners on both sides of the Atlantic, we continue to support a zero-for-zero tariff regime for civil aerospace.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That's how we run 2030, and every year after.

Here's what we know. Our airframer customers are working on their next narrowbody plans. What they launch, and when, is their call, not ours. Whatever they launch, CFM will offer an engine for it, and we're working closely with both of them, privately, on what that aircraft needs. We don't have a birthright on that next order, and we won't act like we do.

RISE stays fully funded as our technology programme with our partners at Safran, and testing goes on. We won't put an entry-into-service date on it. It goes into service when an aircraft needs it, and on what we know today that is 2045 at the earliest. Meanwhile, what we learn there keeps flowing into the LEAP fleet.

On the widebody, we're investing in the GEnx for our 787 customers.

Here's what we don't know: which aircraft launch, when, and with what engines. When we know, you'll know. Until then we keep getting after LEAP durability and deliveries with our supply base. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Bad news first. The GTF has not always given our customers the time on wing they paid for, and we own that. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF. Our share of narrowbody engine deliveries has slipped from 26.7% in 2026 to 24.4% in 2029 as the market mix moved, though we still deliver our full share of the A320neo. We control what we can control. Pratt & Whitney will launch its next-generation geared turbofan, GTF2, in any round an airframer selects it, as sole or shared source, provided GTF2 can be ready by that airframe's entry into service. GTF2 is ready six years from launch, so a launch this year is ready in 2036. It will not enter service until its durability is proven and every life-limited part has full life. It builds on the durability work we are doing on the GTF fleet today. The offer is on standard terms, with no launch discounts; these are responsible bets with responsible paybacks. We are not committing to a Joint Venture today. We will consider one only once a partner's commitment, or an airframer's demand for one, is on the record, and any partnership will share risk and investment, never our core technology. The airframers will speak for their own programmes. The dividend comes first, and this plan needs no change to it or to our path on debt.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So, plainly. Rolls-Royce will launch UltraFan for a new single-aisle in the round an airframer selects it. Not before. UltraFan is more efficient than any other engine available, and we have done the work without waiting. Entry into service no earlier than seven years from launch, with support and MRO in place, through partner shops, before entry into service. Our preference is partnership. The Joint Venture is open to Pratt & Whitney, and we commit when our partner does. No widebody UltraFan until a Re-engine is on the table. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Engine-sourcing requirement for our next single-aisle — We invite all three narrowbody engine makers (CFM, P&W, Rolls-Royce) to bring proposals for a future Boeing single-aisle. Selection will be on value among engines actually launched, with proven durability before entry into service as a contract condition. No fps date and no launch commitment. This serves the dual- or triple-source engine enabler (unmet today). The engine code is decided at launch: code 3 (CFM) by default, or code 7 if P&W and Rolls-Royce can be ready by EIS.
- **Boeing:** Hold the 737 at 57 a month with KPI gates and stability first — No rate step requested. KPIs are monitored monthly. Confirm the LEAP-1B allocation with CFM at today's rate, and keep the FAA-agreed process in place for any future step.
- **Boeing:** 787 capacity in Charleston as customers need it — Continue the Charleston footprint for 787 rates in the teens [BX-1317, B-2336]. This is capacity growth, not a new programme.
- **Airbus:** Secure our own supply for the NGSA ramp — Keep the NGSA wing in-house: Wing of Tomorrow and the run-at-rate demonstrator, with aerostructures through Airbus Atlantic, Airbus Aerostructures and the ex-Spirit sites. Secure titanium and forgings through the Aubert & Duval co-ownership and additive (DED) parts. Extend the critical raw-materials and rare-earths watchlist to NGSA. This covers our own ramp only and makes no claim on any rival's capacity [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063].
- **Airbus:** Multi-source engine strategy and long-term capacity agreements — Open NGSA propulsion to CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce (code 7). Seek long-term capacity agreements with CFM covering the A320neo and NGSA from 2035. Certify CFM first and further makers as their engines mature, as on the A320neo. The aim is supply certainty, not leverage [AX-0054, A-0243, A-0360].
- **Airbus:** Orderly A320neo family transition (Alt supply plan) — Keep the A320neo lines running longer alongside the NGSA ramp. The steps are a ceiling, never above today's rate, and NGSA has first claim on resources. The plan is re-checked at the 2035 gate and reverts to base on a supply crunch, an engine slip or a quality event. The cost is budgeted from NGSA entry into service.
- **Airbus:** Zero-for-zero trade advocacy — Work with the European and US industry associations, Boeing included, to keep civil aerospace on a tariff-free, zero-for-zero footing [AX-0088, AX-0046].
- **Airbus:** Skills for the Airbus of the 2030s — Run apprenticeship, graduate and university programmes in Europe to grow NGSA engineering and production talent from our own pipeline. This is recruitment for the Airbus of the 2030s and targets no rival [AX-0073, A-0330].
- **CFM/GE:** Offer a CFM engine for any new narrowbody either airframer launches, at minimum a LEAP derivative — H1: we never refuse an airframer and never lock one out [CX-0587, CX-0185]. This is a product offer only, with no price, terms or concession [CX-0184]. It is what CFM does today on the 737 MAX and A320neo, so it fits joint practice and Safran's consent is assumed (inference). The board does not price it.
- **CFM/GE:** RISE stays funded as a technology programme with Safran: ground, durability and dust testing continue; no product launch and no entry-into-service date — H5: no cancel, and no programme dividend [CX-0174, CX-0398]. RISE becomes a product when an airframer wants it [CX-0469, CX-0205]. RISE is a joint programme with Safran, and continuing it as technology is joint practice. Ali's floor applies: no Open Fan before 2045, and that is a floor, not a promise. RISE technology keeps flowing into LEAP upgrades [CX-0162].
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP durability kits and supply-base work (FLIGHT DECK) continue ahead of any new programme — Safety, quality, delivery, cost [CX-0658]. 80% of shortages came from 9 suppliers at 15 sites [CX-0613]. We fix the supply base we have before we load it with a new programme (lean before capital) [CX-0540, CX-0653].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF fleet durability kit (Hot Section kit) offered through MRO at value — We keep offering the GTF durability upgrade to operators through our MRO network at value. It is sold, not given away, and it is not tied to any airframe campaign [PX-0088][PX-0100]. It has no effect on the game board. It keeps the fleet ahead of a new engine [PX-0080].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Standard commercial terms on GTF2; no launch-style discounts — Any GTF2 offer to Airbus or Boeing is on standard terms. There is no launch discount, concession or free upgrade to win a selection [PX-0055]. Mitchill's ground 3 (terms) passes. A rival's price does not move the answer.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Standard commercial terms only — No concessions to buy narrowbody or widebody share. Win-win terms, value remunerated, and every new contract goes through the Investment Committee [RX-0081, RX-0055, RX-0097].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Narrowbody support plan through partner shops — If an airframer selects UltraFan, the support plan goes live: a partner-shop network on the Singapore Joint Venture model, repair technology qualified in those shops, a spare-engine pool and an early-years intervention team, all in place before entry into service [RX-0283, RX-0287]. No dates are disclosed.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Joint Venture open to Pratt & Whitney — Rolls-Royce commits to the Joint Venture once Pratt & Whitney's commitment is on the public record, on terms that credit the technology Rolls-Royce brings [RX-0072, RX-0118]. No Joint Venture date is disclosed.
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan technology demonstration and Trent time-on-wing work continue — The self-funded UltraFan narrowbody technology work continues inside a flat R&D envelope [RX-0094, RX-0214]. The durability and time-on-wing programme on the installed Trent fleet continues [RX-0123]. No new programme is launched.
