## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** Five years ago I said I thought we'd turned the ship. We have, and the job now is to keep it steady. The balance sheet is repaired and it's staying repaired. The 777X and the 737 MAX family are delivering, and the 787 remains the most efficient airplane in its class.

On the 737, we're bringing the line back to full output, 57 a month, as demand comes back. We'll take that one step at a time. Each step comes when the KPIs say so, with no subjectivity, and when we and the FAA agree the line is ready. We'll make sure our suppliers and our engine supply are there before each step. I'd much rather be a month late than a month early.

On a new airplane: we're not launching one this year, and I'm not going to give you a date. I'll tell you plainly what's changed. The market case is better than it was. It isn't there yet. We will do a new airplane when the market, the technology and Boeing are all ready. Technology is ready, and Boeing is ready. The market is getting there, but it hasn't got there. The work goes on in stages. We're designing the production system together with the airplane, so that when we go, we go on a conservative plan with headroom in it, with no fixed-price development risk, and we build it at rate the right way.

On engines, we've updated our requirement. Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce are now one Joint Venture, so our plan is to give customers a choice of two engine sources at launch, CFM and the Joint Venture, with durability proven before entry into service.

We're not launching a 787 Re-engine this year. We'll keep adding capacity in Charleston as our customers need it.

Our dates are set by our own readiness. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast. The financials will follow our production performance, and we'll keep earning our customers' trust in every transaction.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Our next-generation single-aisle aircraft has been in service since the second half of the 2030s, and its production ramp-up is now reaching its planned rate. We will continue a disciplined and controlled ramp-up: every step is gated on safety, on quality and on the readiness of our supply chain, and safety and quality come before schedule. Our customers have a choice of engine, from CFM and from the Pratt & Whitney-Rolls-Royce Joint Venture, and our supply agreements are matched to our own volumes. The A320neo family is completing an orderly transition to the new aircraft. Demand for the new aircraft remains strong. With our suppliers we are preparing the next step in our industrial capacity, and we will take it when our safety, quality and supply indicators say so, not before. On widebodies, our focus stays on the A350 family and the A350F, which we continue to improve for our customers. We continue to secure key work packages, materials and skills in Europe for our own industrial system. The new aircraft was funded from operating cash flow within our financial framework, preserving our financial flexibility and our shareholder return policy. With our industry partners on both sides of the Atlantic, we continue to support a zero-for-zero tariff regime for civil aerospace.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order.

Here's what we know. Our new CFM engine, a step forward from today's LEAP, has been ready since 2041, and it's on Airbus's new single-aisle. We're working with Embraer, together with Safran, so that engine has a home on more than one airframe. We thank our customers for that.

We hold that engine to the standard our customers set and we set for ourselves: durability proven before entry into service, hot and harsh conditions included. Test and field data go to our customers and our Board first. They don't go into a press line.

We're not launching anything new this year. RISE stays our technology programme with Safran. Testing goes on, and what we learn keeps flowing into the LEAP fleet. We won't put a date on it. It goes into service when an aircraft needs it.

LEAP durability kits and deliveries still come first. On capacity, we work with both airframers, together with Safran.

Boeing has invited us to bring a proposal for its next single-aisle. Together with Safran, we've answered on product, and we'll be at the table. We don't have a birthright on that order. When and what Boeing launches is Boeing's call.

Here's what we don't know: when that airplane launches, and what it flies with. When we know, you'll know. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Bad news first. The new single-aisle market is more contested than when we committed. CFM's new engine has been ready since 2041, and CFM has a partnership with Embraer. We have no birthright on any aircraft, and we earn our place every day. The GTF has not always given our customers the time on wing they paid for, and we own that. The fleet work goes on. The Hot Section durability upgrade is offered to GTF operators through our MRO network, and fleet spares come ahead of new hardware.

We control what we can control. The Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce Joint Venture engine, built on GTF2's tested configuration, has been ready since 2036. It is fitted on Airbus's new single-aisle, which has been in service since 2037. Time on wing is the name of the game. We will report that engine's durability as the in-service data show it, and not ahead of them. We are sizing parts, spares and shop capacity to the fleet we support, the GTF and the new engine together.

The Joint Venture shares risk and investment equally. It never shares our core technology. Pratt & Whitney designs, makes and supports the gear and the core as its own modules, under its own type data. A partner's technology is credited through its own modules and an equal share, never through ours. Under the Joint Venture's terms, any Rolls-Royce content would come only on a later block. It would not enter service until its own durability is proven and every life-limited part has full life. Rolls-Royce speaks for its own decisions.

Our engine is on offer to any new single-aisle, Boeing's included, on standard terms, with no launch discounts. On a new airframe it will not enter service until its durability is proven. The airframers speak for their own programmes and dates.

The dividend comes first. This year's plan needs no new engine development spending and no change to the dividend or to our path on debt.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So, plainly. The Joint Venture engine with Pratt & Whitney is in service on Airbus's new single-aisle. Rolls-Royce content enters service when its own durability is proven, and not before. We give no date for it. Our support through partner shops is ours, and it is in place before any Rolls-Royce content enters service. Testing does not bend to anyone's date. Our answer to Boeing stands: Rolls-Royce's part of the engine, on standard terms, with durability proven and support in place before entry into service. On the widebody, we look after the Trent fleet through time on wing. No widebody UltraFan until a Re-engine is on the table. There's no change of strategy. We won't do anything not profitable. - **Boeing:** 787 capacity in Charleston as customers need it — Continue the Charleston footprint for 787 rates in the teens [BX-1317, B-2336]. This is capacity growth, not a new programme.

- The NGSA wing stays in-house.
- Aerostructures come through Airbus Atlantic, Airbus Aerostructures and the ex-Spirit sites.
- Titanium and forgings come through the Aubert & Duval co-ownership and additive (DED) parts.
- The critical raw-materials and rare-earths watchlist continues.
It serves our own production only and makes no claim on any rival's capacity [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063].
- **Airbus:** NGSA engine supply: CFM and the Pratt & Whitney-Rolls-Royce Joint Venture, at our own volumes — NGSA is fitted with CFM and the P&W-Rolls-Royce Joint Venture engine. That is two sources from three engine makers.
- The capacity agreements with both cover only our own NGSA volumes (and the A320neo tail), with no exclusivity and nothing above our own production.
- The aim is supply certainty, not leverage [AX-0054, A-0243, A-0360].
- **Airbus:** Prepare the next step in industrial capacity, gated on safety, quality and supply — Our own factory ceiling now limits deliveries, and the waiting backlog is growing. We prepare the step and do not commit to it.
- Capacity talks with both engine sources, for our own extra volumes only. No exclusivity, and nothing drawn from any rival's engine allocation.
- The in-house wing and the Aubert & Duval titanium and forgings are sized first.
- Staffing comes from our own skills pipeline.
The step is taken only once:
- NGSA holds its current rate on its KPIs with no open quality finding;
- both engine sources commit capacity;
- no supply crunch is reported.
In public we give no rate number and no date, only a gate. Internally, the capex comes to the Audit Committee as its own line against the $5.2B peak-cash line, and to the Board as an item if it exceeds EUR 300m (Toepfer's flag, Wagner's conditions).

- **Pratt & Whitney:** Joint Venture governed to its published terms; 'never' restated as the public floor — The formed Joint Venture runs to the terms we published in 2035, restated with one change: the Finance routing comes out of the public text.
(1) The partners share risk and investment equally. Each designs, makes and supports its own modules end to end, and a joint integration and test team works the interfaces.
(2) P&W designs, makes and supports the gear and the core as its own modules under its own type data, with no licence, no data access and no transfer [P-1233].

(4) The engine in service is GTF2's tested configuration. Under the Joint Venture's terms, any Rolls-Royce content would come only on a later block, and it would not enter service until its own durability is proven and every life-limited part has full life.
Rolls-Royce speaks for its own decisions. No date is given for any block.

We offer the Joint Venture engine, which has been ready since 2036, in the standard now in service. It is offered on standard commercial terms, with no launch discount, concession or free upgrade [PX-0055][PX-0100], and the same terms apply on NGSA.
- On a new airframe it will not enter service until its durability is proven, the contract condition Boeing has set.
- A newer block would be a new development with its own durability proof. None is promised.
- A rival's price, including CFM's new engine and its Embraer partnership, does not move the offer.
- Nothing is said on either airframer's dates or rates [PX-0062], and nothing is aimed at Boeing's timing either way.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Trent 1000 and Trent 7000 time-on-wing and durability work continues inside a flat R&D envelope — Longer-life HP turbine blades on the Trent 1000 and Trent 7000, and life-limited part extensions on the installed fleet. The money is redeployed inside a broadly flat envelope, not added. This is our answer on the widebody, not a new programme. No widebody programme is launched [RX-0123, RX-0214, RX-0101].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Narrowbody support plan through partner shops: live for the NGSA fleet and sized for a second airframe — Partner-shop network on the Singapore Joint Venture model, repair technology qualified in those shops, a spare-engine pool and an early-years intervention team. The plan is Rolls-Royce's own. It is in place before any Rolls-Royce content enters service, and it is sized for a second airframe before that airframe enters service. Our partner's shops add capacity after that and never replace this plan. No dates are disclosed [RX-0283, RX-0287].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Answer to Boeing's engine-sourcing invitation, renewed — Rolls-Royce offers its part of the Joint Venture engine for a future Boeing single-aisle, on standard terms, with no launch discount and no concession. Durability proven before entry into service, as a contract condition. Support and MRO through partner shops in place before entry into service. No dates and no new spend. Rolls-Royce speaks only for its own part [RX-0058, RX-0081, RX-0283].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Standard commercial terms only — No concessions to buy narrowbody or widebody share. Terms are win-win, value is remunerated, and every new contract goes through the Investment Committee [RX-0081, RX-0055, RX-0097].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Capital allocation frame — Cash from the Trent and Joint Venture fleets goes first to the balance sheet, then to a 30-40% dividend payout, then to investment and further distributions on fit, value and timing, through the Investment Committee at mid-to-high-teens hurdles. No equity raise, no leverage above about 1.5x, no borrowing for distributions [RX-0242, RX-0213, RX-0215, RX-0261].
