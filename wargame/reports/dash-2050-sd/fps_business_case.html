fps business case

The decision Boeing faced in 2030: launch a 10-year fps (in service 2040) or Do Nothing, with Airbus's NGSA already launched. The fps adds about $197B of operating profit over 2037-2059, but it needs $60.3B first, and its margin arrives late as production ramps up. As the game values it, the fps is worth −$2.19B at Renton against Do Nothing; with the bill spent as it would really go out, −$7.77B. Either way it is short of the +$1B Boeing's Board requires.

The 2037-2039 profit comes before the fps flies: with an fps announced, the dashboard's share rule lets NGSA gain only 1 point a year instead of 3, so the 737 MAX keeps more customers. In 2040 the game view also books the $60.32B bill, the $19.12B debt penalty and the $3.0B Renton saving.

From cash flow to value

The two views share the operating profit. They differ in when the bill is counted and in the debt penalty. The game books the bill at entry into service, which cuts its present value to $14.91B from $25.38B; the cash view spends it over 2030-2039 and drops the penalty, which is not a cash flow.

On the dashboard alone, with every forecast aircraft delivered, the same operating profit is worth $23.94B instead of $16.70B: in 2040-2044 the dashboard books $9.6B of incremental profit (PV) on fps margins from day one, while the ramp delivers $2.1B. That is the difference between the dashboard's +$4.30B and the game's −$2.74B at new sites.

Financing and valuation
Debt penalty (game view only)The dashboard adds a leverage haircut as if the whole bill were added to Boeing's $45B gross debt: α × ((D+bill)³ − D³) / EV², with α 0.3, EV $130B. That is $19.1B booked in 2040, −$4.73B at 2026. It is not a cash flow; the game's balance-sheet tracker shows Boeing debt-free from 2029 either way.
Discount rateBoeing's WACC, 10.5%, to 2026. It is applied to constant-2026-dollar flows, so it is conservative if read as a nominal rate.
HorizonThe dashboard counts 20 years from entry into service (to 2059). Nothing is counted after that, though the fps still earns about $12B a year in 2060.
BasisPre-tax operating profit. No tax, working capital, recurring capex or support revenue. Widebody unaffected (no 787 Re-engine in either case).

  • Two views, one decision. The game view is what Boeing's ExCo and Board saw and decided on. The cash view is how the same plan would look to a finance team: spend when it goes out, no leverage haircut. Neither includes tax.
