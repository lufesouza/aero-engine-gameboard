The MAX phase-out game: same orders, no fps, and a weaker fps case in every round

The game replayed in the world you set in the Do Nothing tuner. It differs from the conservative-case game (the earlier play of the same game on the conservative demand curve) in five ways. Demand follows the conservative curve to 2037 and your Single-Aisle Replacement Story page from 2038. That page counts replacement of today's fleet plus as much again for growth. Over 2026-60 the two curves total about the same (57,645 against 57,593 aircraft). Yours dips where they join in 2038 (1,402 against 1,719), and it falls away in the late 2050s and after 2060. The 737 MAX phases out from 2045, with none built from 2060. Airbus adds extra lines from 2045, up to 20 more aircraft a month. COMAC sells its capacity first, to its own customers: 150 a year in 2035, rising to 250 from 2050. A new entrant enters service in 2040 and sells to customers left waiting: those who cannot get the aircraft they want and do not switch. Every company played as before, through its Board and ExCo, in the same four rounds: 2030, 2035, 2045 and 2050.

How to read the figures. Values are $B, PV at 2026, against the status quo, in which nobody launches anything. Do Nothing is not the status quo: Boeing launches nothing, but Airbus and the engine makers still move. Each round Boeing's ExCo gets a brief: a grid that values every Boeing plan against each move Airbus could make that round, one column per move. Where the page quotes one figure, it uses the column for what Airbus then did. The 10-year fps at Renton is the fps on a 10-year development, built at Renton. The brief counts each programme's sales for 20 years from its entry into service, and Do Nothing's to 2060. Boeing's Board approves a launch only if it adds more than +$1B in the column its CEO expects, and vetoes any order that could lose more than $1B in any column it thinks plausible (its downside test).

All five companies' final orders match the conservative-case game, round by round, and Boeing held the fps in all four rounds. The one difference is how Boeing got there in 2030. The 787 Re-engine scored the same in both games: +$3.30B against Do Nothing, or −$2.48B if Airbus re-engines the A350 in the same round. The conservative-case CEO put even odds on that A350 move, which made the Re-engine worth +$0.41B, inside the $1B margin, and held it back. This game's CEO put the odds at about a third, which made it worth +$1.37B, and proposed it. The Board vetoed it on its downside test, because it loses $2.48B if Airbus does re-engine. In 2035 this game's CEO adopted the CFO's 30% odds of an A350 Re-engine (the conservative-case CEO used 40%). On that weighting the Re-engine's expected lead (+$0.91B) was inside the $1B margin, so the CEO held it back; it would have led by +$2.00B if Airbus held, as it did. In 2045 and 2050 its lead was inside $1B in every column. Airbus launched NGSA in 2030 on its Alt plan, which keeps the A320 longer. NGSA's engine choice set off Pratt & Whitney's GTF2 and Rolls-Royce's UltraFan NB launches in 2030, and CFM/GE upgraded the GEnx. In 2035 those two engines folded into the Pratt & Whitney-Rolls-Royce Joint Venture, and CFM/GE launched its ducted engine and an Embraer partnership.

2030: the biggest step is COMAC selling its capacity first (−$1.86B). Under Do Nothing, in the years the brief counts, COMAC takes 555 aircraft from Boeing: 316 in 2035-39, 239 in 2045-49, and none from 2050, when the shrinking MAX line is sold out anyway. From the fps plan it takes 2,189: the same 316 in 2035-39 and 1,873 in 2045-59, when an fps would have aircraft to sell. Measured against its own status quo, Do Nothing loses only $0.19B at this step and the fps plan $2.05B. The phase-out helps the fps case (+$0.63B). Over 2045-60 Boeing delivers 2,246 fewer 737s than in the conservative-case game, and Airbus 2,578 fewer. COMAC delivers 3,850 and the new entrant 490. Those gaps mix all five assumptions, which overlap. The table below takes the two that move the MAX's customers, inside this world. The phase-out costs Boeing 955 aircraft: 465 of those customers switch to Airbus and 490 buy from the new entrant. COMAC sells its 3,850 first, off the top of each year's demand, so it sells them with or without the phase-out. Without COMAC, Airbus would deliver 3,228 more, the new entrant 428 more and Boeing 299 more; that is 105 more than COMAC's 3,850, because without COMAC 105 customers are still waiting at the end of 2044 and are delivered later. The final state is not quite your tuner table, because Airbus played its Alt plan (the A320 kept longer). That gives it more capacity in 2037-44 than the base plan your table shows. As a result Boeing delivers fewer 737s than your table in 2037 (605 against 618), 2038 (470 against 543), 2044 (569 against 684) and 2045 (511 against 641). In every other year Boeing's deliveries match your table. In some years the bars rise above that year's demand: customers left unserved in earlier years wait, and a tenth of them give up each year.

Notes

  • The world is the settings behind your Do Nothing tuner table (1 Nov 2026), in sd_options_phaseout.json. With Airbus on its base A320 plan the game gives back your table to the aircraft (test_phaseout.py).
  • The phase-out applies to every Boeing plan, not only Do Nothing, because the MAX ages either way. COMAC and the new entrant compete in every plan too.
  • Everything else is the conservative-case game. Firm orders, Boards, ExCos and objectives are the same. So are the market rules: Boeing's share drifts down 1 point a year while NGSA flies alone, Boeing sells to the customers Airbus cannot build for, and Airbus adds 1% capacity a year when it is short.
  • The step tables (steps.py) switch your assumptions on in the order shown. Another order would split the total differently but give the same total. The first and last steps give back each game's own brief, every column, to the cent. Changes are shown as the differences of the rounded figures.
  • The demand curves after 2060 (tail.py) re-value every Boeing plan in every column of every round, from the state each round started in. The "as run" curve gives back the run's own briefs to the cent.
  • Source: extract.py, steps.py, tail.py, comac_units.py, who_picks_up.py and make_phaseout_html.py. The round records are in record/.
