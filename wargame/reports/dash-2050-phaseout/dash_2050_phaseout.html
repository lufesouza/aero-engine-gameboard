2030: the biggest step is COMAC selling its capacity first (−$1.86B). It takes 555 aircraft from Boeing under Do Nothing, all before 2050, but 2,424 from the fps plan, 1,873 of them in 2045-59. By then the MAX is phasing out, so under Do Nothing Boeing has no aircraft for those customers anyway; with an fps it would. Do Nothing's own value moves only −$0.19B at this step. The phase-out itself helps the fps (+$0.64B), because it makes Do Nothing worse. 2050: the biggest step is the demand curve (−$1.48B). An fps launched in 2050 enters service around 2060 and sells after 2060, where your page's curve falls away.

Your tuner table runs to 2060. After 2060 the run uses your page's curve, which counts replacement of today's fleet plus as much again for growth, so it fades out. This does not touch 2030: an fps launched then sells before 2060. It matters from 2035, and most in 2045 and 2050. With the conservative curve after 2060, the 2050 gap is −$0.93B, close to the conservative-case game's −$0.85B. Boeing's decision does not change under any of these tails. In no round does any fps plan reach +$1B in the CEO's expected column: the best, in 2050 with the conservative curve, is −$0.84B. The other companies were not replayed under these tails.

Notes

  • The world is sd_options_phaseout.json, the settings behind your Do Nothing tuner table (1 Nov 2026). The engine gives back that table to the aircraft (test_phaseout.py).
  • The phase-out applies to every Boeing plan, not only Do Nothing, because the MAX ages either way. COMAC and the entrant compete in every plan too.
  • Everything else is the conservative-case game: firm orders inside demand, Boeing losing 1 point a year while NGSA flies alone, Boeing taking Airbus's slack, Airbus adding 1% capacity a year when short, and the same Boards, ExCos and objectives.
  • The bridges (steps.py) switch your assumptions on in the order shown. A different order would split the total differently, but the total is the same. Both the first and the last step give back each game's own brief, every column, to the cent.
  • The demand tails (tail.py) re-value every Boeing plan in every column of every round, from the state each round started in. The "as run" tail gives back the run's own briefs to the cent.
  • Source: extract.py, steps.py, tail.py, comac_units.py and make_phaseout_html.py. The round records are in record/.
