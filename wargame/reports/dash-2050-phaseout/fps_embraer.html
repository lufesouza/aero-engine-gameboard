Embraer as a third maker, and a partner for the fps bill

Two questions. South Korea has proposed building a next-generation airliner with Embraer, and Brazil's government is at odds with Washington: what does a competent third maker, competing on price, do to Boeing's fps decision? And, separately, could Boeing close the bill with a partner? Both are priced here inside Boeing's own decision rule, before the game is re-run.

Basis. The franchise page's view (the brief plus continuing value, services, overhead, widebody erosion and closing costs), constant 2026 $B against Do Nothing, in the columns the CEO expected each round, at the game's 10.5% and at 8.1%. Where premiums are shown, the politics page's base government terms are on as well. Rivals' moves are held as the game played them; Embraer is priced through the game's own supply-and-demand engine (who buys whose airplane, year by year) and a price cut on every new single-aisle delivered. With Embraer off, every value, premium and government share here equals the politics page's. The game as played already has an unnamed third maker: an entrant in service in 2040 at up to 20 a month that sells only to customers Boeing and Airbus cannot serve. Here Embraer-Korea takes its place, with a smaller line that also competes. Readings: where a range is shown it spans the four readings of the CEO's rule on the main report (strictly; pairwise; worst column first; and with the CEO's $2B allowance, the leeway to pick a plan up to $2B below the best one). A fifth, a CEO who anticipates the Board and proposes only what it would approve, is named where it differs. Premium: the non-financial value the Board would need to put on staying in the business for its rule to launch an fps (the politics page's measure). NGSA: Airbus's next single-aisle, launched in 2030 and in service in 2037 in the game.

The answer

Embraer-Korea is a possible third maker, and Brazil is at odds with Washington Korea's president proposed building a next-generation airliner with Brazil in July 2026, and KAI and Embraer signed a first, non-binding memorandum on aerostructures. Embraer says it is studying either a commercial jet or a business jet, with no segment or timeline chosen; Korea calls the talks very early, and its main track is a core role on Boeing's and Airbus's next airplanes. Lula, re-elected on 25 October, has been in a tariff fight with Washington all year, and a US national-security finding on aircraft imports keeps tariffs on an Embraer narrowbody possible. This page takes a 20% chance of an Embraer-Korea airliner (36% if Boeing waits, 10% if Boeing launches in 2030), in service in 2040 at up to 8 a month.
If Embraer launches, it hurts the fps more than Do Nothing, mostly through the customers it wins and the prices it cuts In 2030 at 10.5% the 10-year fps at Renton falls from −$4.07B to −$4.77B against Do Nothing. As a competitor Embraer costs it −$1.09B: the customers it wins −$0.67B alone, a 2% cut in single-aisle prices (2.6% on the MAX while Boeing has no new airplane) −$0.46B alone. Its line is smaller than the game's own entrant, which gives +$0.39B back. In Do Nothing, Boeing's factories are already the limit and the MAX is running out, so Embraer sells mostly to customers Airbus would have had (in 2050 Airbus delivers 76 fewer, Boeing 15 fewer); with an fps it competes for the fps's customers and cuts the price of every fps sold. The premium Boeing would need rises from $5.48B to $6.26B ($5.63B and $6.07B in the low and high cases); at 8.1% from $0.50B to $2.22B, turning a near-launch into a no. In 2026 money the effect shrinks in later rounds, but in each round's own money it stays at about $1.2B-$1.5B. Weighted by the 20% chance, the 2030 premium is $5.70B at 10.5% and $0.96B at 8.1%.
A 2030 launch to keep Embraer out is worth little at the chances the evidence gives Embraer would bet the company on a gap: a 737 at the end of its life and no new Boeing airplane. Korea's support for Embraer is put at 12% if Boeing launches in 2030 and 45% if it waits (research, judgement), which makes Embraer's chance about 10% and 36%. At those chances the premium Boeing would need in 2030 is $5.33B at 10.5%, against $5.48B as played, and $0.30B at 8.1% (against $0.50B); if a launch kept Embraer out altogether, $5.10B at 10.5%. Only if Embraer were certain to launch otherwise would it fall to $4.41B at 10.5% (where the fps is worth −$3.00B against Do Nothing) and launch the 10-year fps at 8.1% with no premium at all. So Embraer is a modest reason to launch early, not a decisive one; it works only in 2030, as by 2035 Embraer has decided.
At 10.5% no partner package with any precedent closes the bill; at 8.1% a modest one does A partner that carries part of the bill and takes back a share of the fps's profit helps Boeing in two ways: the game's debt penalty falls (a partner carrying 30% of the bill saves Boeing about $2.2B in 2030 even at Boeing's own rate), and a partner whose money is cheaper than Boeing's values the fps's long, late profits more. In 2030 at 10.5% a partner whose money costs 3% must carry 27.1–35.9% of the bill, at 7% 37.7–50.7%; at Boeing's own rate no share up to 60% launches; in 2035 it takes 44.9% at 3%, and from 2045 none up to 60%. If Embraer launches anyway, 33.8–41.5% at 3%. Precedent sets the size: in dollars Japan's 787 package would be about 8% of an fps bill today, and Korea's firms could add about 3% (judgement). At that scale (11% at about 6%) a partner closes the 2030 bill at 8.1%, not at 10.5%. At 10.5% even a 35% state-loan package (about four times Japan's 787 package) launches the 7-year fps only with the CEO's $2B allowance or a CEO anticipating the Board. And these are break-even terms for the partner: it takes back 0.42-0.55 of a pro-rata share of the profit, where precedent is about 0.7-1.2. On pro-rata terms (a 20% or 35% package taking the same share of the profit) nothing launches at 10.5%; the repo's own Joint Venture terms (35% of the bill for 25% of the profit) launch the fps at 8.1%.
The two together: Korea as Boeing's partner, not Embraer's Korea wants a core, risk-sharing role on the next Boeing or Airbus airplane, and it is Embraer's most likely partner; Embraer said in 2016 that it would go bigger only if Boeing and Airbus gave up part of the market. A Boeing package that gives Korea (with Japan) that role carries part of the bill and lowers Embraer's chance of launching. At the evidence's chances the second effect is small: in 2030 at 10.5% the share needed falls only from 27.1–35.9% to 26.5–34.9% at 3%. Only if Embraer were certain to launch otherwise does it fall to 22.5–29.0%, and then a 35% state-loan package launches the fps. The risks are the ones Boeing knows: partners who lose money and are bought back, Airbus courting the same partners for its own 2030 launch, Lula's Brazil, and the memory of 2020, when Boeing walked away from Embraer.
For the re-run: the engine is ready, the game is not re-run Embraer-Korea's second-source share and the price cut are new options in the game's engine (off unless set; every existing test and the played game's replay unchanged); its line uses the game's existing entrant settings. The base settings are ready in data/sd_options_embraer_proposed.json. The game has no move for Boeing to sign partners: "fps via Embraer" carries a $100B bill on the board. A partner move would be a new order.

Embraer and Korea: what is public

Embraer and Korea

  • Korea asked. President Lee Jae Myung named joint development of a next-generation commercial aircraft with Brazil as one of three priorities, in Sao Paulo on 28 July 2026: "Let's build civilian aircraft together in the future, if possible." Lee's policy chief said Seoul wants in from the development stage, but the talks are "very early stage", with no form or terms agreed (Korea Herald, 28-29 July 2026; search extracts).
  • A first paper. KAI (Korea Aerospace Industries) and Embraer signed a non-binding memorandum on civil aviation and aerostructures on 28 July 2026, signed by both chief executives; it names no airliner. KAI and Korean Air each held talks with Embraer (Korean press). The 150-200-seat size comes from the Korean side; Embraer has not chosen a segment. Lula's state visit to Seoul in February 2026 had raised ties to a strategic partnership.
  • Embraer is careful. Its chief executive: "making studies for a new product, but we don't have any concrete plans yet" (October 2024), and in May 2026 "studies for a new cycle of products ... either a commercial jet or a business jet", with no segment or timeline chosen and a funding strategy that must not "put in risk the financial health of the company"; until 2030 it focuses on its current products. It cancelled its turboprop in November 2025 (immature engines and no launch partner were the reported reasons), and was reported in 2025 as saying the market has room for "three or four" makers.
  • But able. E-Jet and E2 each took about five years from launch to service, the E2 on a $1.7B budget; 78 commercial deliveries in 2025, about 120 a year targeted by 2030; $7.6B revenue in 2025, a $35.7B backlog (Q3 2026, reported), $11-13B market value. A narrowbody would cost about $10B (industry and Korean estimates), which Embraer cannot fund alone.
  • Korea's main track is Boeing and Airbus. Korea's space agency (KASA) set up a public-private task force on 16 July 2026 for Korean firms to become core risk-sharing partners on the Airbus and Boeing successors expected around 2030; Embraer is a parallel option. Korean Air finalised 103 Boeing aircraft on 16 September 2026, including 50 737-10s (reported).
  • Others warn. Airbus's chief executive said Embraer should "think twice" (June 2026); analysts split between a bet-the-company move and one Embraer's low debt could absorb (Leeham, September 2026).

Brazil and Washington

  • At loggerheads. Lula won the run-off on 25 October 2026 (about 51.8%) after running on sovereignty against US tariffs and sanctions; Xi congratulated Lula on 27 October; no US statement was found by 28 October (search extracts). A 25% US Section 301 tariff on most Brazilian goods has applied since 22 July 2026, after a May 2026 White House meeting brought only a working group.
  • Aircraft spared, Brazil's least. Civil aircraft were exempt from the 2025 40% tariff on Brazil and from the 2026 Section 301 and forced-labour tariffs. But from April 2025 to February 2026 Brazilian jets still paid the 10% baseline on their non-US content (US airlines paid it on commercial jets; Embraer paid $80M on business jets and parts), while deals with the EU, the UK and Japan had put their aircraft back to zero: Embraer "was the only manufacturer to pay tariffs on aircraft exports" (its chief executive, March 2026). The Supreme Court struck the emergency tariffs down on 20 February 2026. A July 2026 Section 232 finding says aircraft imports "threaten to impair" US national security and keeps tariffs possible (reported); Embraer had already kept a tariff assumption in its 2026 guidance pending the investigation (May 2026).
  • A narrowbody loses Embraer's defence. Embraer's US case rests on its US ties (more than 40% US content; $21B of US purchases against $13B of exports to the US over five years) and on jets the US does not build; a 737-class jet would compete head-on with Boeing. The Bombardier precedent: Commerce set duties of 292% on the CSeries in 2017; Airbus took control and planned US assembly in October 2017; the trade commission cancelled the duties 4-0 in January 2018 because Boeing had lost no sale.
  • Still allied, and tied in. Brazil remains a Major Non-NATO Ally (2019; no revocation found); North America is 58.7% of Embraer's revenue; the government holds a golden share that can veto a change of control; Brazil's development bank BNDES approved about R$9.6B a year for Embraer in 2023-25, mostly export credit.

How Embraer enters the game

Two channels, both through the game's own rules. Customers: Embraer-Korea wins a share of each year's new customers (after COMAC's home market), up to what its line can build: more while Boeing has no new narrowbody in service, when it is the only Western alternative to Airbus, less once an fps is. Like the game's own entrant, it also sells to customers nobody else can serve. The rest are split between Boeing and Airbus as before, and every plan, the status quo included, is re-delivered year by year. Prices: its campaigns cut the price of every new single-aisle delivered, Boeing's and Airbus's, by a market-average share at full effect, more on Boeing's MAX while Boeing has no new airplane; costs do not change, so profit falls by the whole cut. The status quo faces the same cuts, so only what an fps adds or loses against it counts.

Price: what a third maker has done before

  • The average moves a few percent. Irwin and Pavcnik estimate aircraft price-cost margins of about 14-17% and a 3.7% price rise after the 1992 US-EU subsidy rules; in their simulation of the A380's entry, rival prices moved only a few percent (wide-body data; abstracts only).
  • Contested deals go much deeper. Bombardier sold CS100s to Delta at close to two-thirds off list and booked a $492M loss on its 127 launch orders; Boeing sold United 737-700s at an estimated $22M, about 73% off list, to keep the CS100 out; JetBlue paid an estimated 66-72% off for A220s against the E2 (Moody's); Brazilian export finance gave Embraer a 13-15% edge over Bombardier's CRJ.
  • Before service, not after. The neo/MAX price war began at the 2010-11 launches, 5-6 years before either entered service.
  • The makers see it coming. Airbus's FY2025 Board Report: "the period of a duopoly in the mainline commercial aircraft market has ended". But slots are scarce: Boeing's CFO saw "a better pricing environment" in March 2026, partly because "there's minimal slots available", which argues for the low end while backlogs stay long. Embraer's commercial margin was 2.5-2.7% in 2024-25 and it says "we don't sell our aircraft below cost" (2024).
  • Airlines want a third. 71% of 492 respondents (45% of them airlines) to an Aviation Week/Bank of America survey would buy an Embraer narrowbody on its merits; United's chief executive: "we need more competition"; Ryanair's: playing two makers against each other is "doomed to failure". The trade commission found the CSeries unlikely to depress prices generally.

Who could partner Boeing

  • Japan. Boeing's partner for four decades: about 10% of the 767, 21% of the 777's airframe and risk capital, about 35% of the 787's structure and about 21% of the 777X's, through JADC, with state loans for 60-70% of their part. In dollars the 787 package was about $2.3-2.9B (2005), about 8% of an fps bill today (4-15%). Japan's own airliner, the SpaceJet, ended in February 2023 after about ¥1 trillion and no delivery; Japan committed to 100 Boeing aircraft in its 2025 US trade deal.
  • Korea. Korea's space agency launched a task force on 16 July 2026 to win core risk-sharing roles on the Airbus and Boeing successors (a target of up to 135 trillion won of orders over 20 years from 2035). KAI ($2.6B revenue) builds 737NG wing parts, 777X wing ribs and A320 and A350 parts; Korean Air builds 737-787 structures; Hanwha is a partner on Pratt & Whitney's GTF. Its share of an fps bill: about 3% (1-8%). The research puts the chance Korea backs Embraer rather than Boeing at 25%: 12% if Boeing launches in 2030, 45% if it has not by 2035 (judgement).
  • Embraer itself. Not as Boeing's supplier: "we do not contemplate any architecture where Embraer will be a sub-supplier ... or a major risk-sharing partner" (2011), though it agreed to Boeing's 80% joint venture in 2018. In 2016 it said it would go bigger only if Boeing and Airbus "give up a little bit of this market", the condition the MAX phase-out creates; since 2025 it studies larger airplanes without that condition. Brazil's golden share can veto a change of control, and Boeing walked away from the last deal.
  • Boeing's own stance. The next airplane "could require different kind of business partnerships, different type of ventures" (Ortberg, 28 July 2026). But Boeing has pulled core IP back since the 787 ("we paid people to develop IP that they now own", 2011) and built the 777X wing itself, which limits what it would hand a partner.
  • Airbus wants the same partners. Airbus plans to pick the NGSA's engine in 2027, launch around 2030 and enter service in 2037-38: it will be signing risk-sharing partners when Korea's task force is looking for a role, and KAI and Korean Air already build A320 and A350 parts.
  • Not researched. Saudi Arabia's PIF, Turkey, India, Italy's Leonardo and the UK were named in 2024 reports on Embraer's search for partners or are past Boeing and Airbus partners; the research ran out of search budget before reaching them.

A partner pays share p of the fps bill and takes back share s of the fps's operating profit, after any price cut, for as long as the view counts the fps (its 50 years in service here). s is set where the partner breaks even at its own cost of money in the CEO's expected columns, so the partner carries the risk of the other columns. The partner's money is booked at entry into service, as the game books Boeing's bill. Boeing keeps the game's own saving on the bill, debt penalty included, and gives up s of the profit. A partner sees the fps's own return; Boeing sees only what the fps adds over the MAX after its debt penalty. On pro-rata terms a partner would earn about 12% if its money is counted at entry into service, as the game counts Boeing's, and about 8% if it is spent over the development years: above or close to the 7% the research puts on a state-backed partner, below Boeing's 10.5%.

Notes

  • What is not modelled. Airbus's response (a cheaper NGSA, or its own Korean or Japanese partners): rivals' moves are held as played. Embraer-Korea is priced as one launch with a chance, not as a player choosing. US trade action against an Embraer narrowbody (some action about 50% likely, a block about 8%, per the research) is inside the share range, not modelled separately. A launch-period price war, deeper but temporary, is not modelled.
  • Price. The cut is a market average on every delivery, from 2039 at base, at full effect after 7 years. Campaigns where Embraer bids would see far more; the many it does not bid in, less. In the high case the larger cut on the MAX while Boeing has no new airplane partly favours the fps, which escapes it.
  • Partners. Partner money is valued at the partner's own rate; nothing here prices the strategic value a government puts on the work (jobs, a wing), which is what has let state-backed partners accept low returns. The game's debt penalty is what makes Boeing's balance sheet bind; a partner's balance sheet is outside it.
  • Checks. With Embraer off, every value equals the politics page's to 2e-15 and every premium and government share to the cent; the engine's price rule and this page's give the same value to 5e-15; a partner at Boeing's own rate leaves Boeing exactly the debt-penalty saving (plus a few million of two-front strain on the plans beside the Re-engine). An independent review of the model and the page was run and its findings fixed.
  • Sources: 188 findings from five research tracks, each followed by a verifier, in data/embraer_research.json. The verifiers could not open web pages (the session's search budget ran out and many hosts did not resolve), so they checked what they could against the Boeing and Embraer call transcripts, 10-Ks and Airbus's Board Report in the repository: 21 findings confirmed, 16 corrected (the corrections are used here), 151 could not be checked. Embraer's and Boeing's own statements quoted here are confirmed there; the Korean, Brazilian-election and US-tariff facts dated 2026 and the price and partner-package estimates rest on search extracts and judgement, and are reported, not verified.
