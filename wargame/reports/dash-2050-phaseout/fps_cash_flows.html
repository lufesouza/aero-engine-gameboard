The fps cash flows behind Boeing's 2030 decision

What Boeing's 2030 brief counts for the fps, year by year, in the column the CEO expected (Airbus launches NGSA). Every figure is in constant 2026 dollars, pre-tax, and measured against the status quo in which nobody launches anything. The brief discounts each year to 2026 at Boeing's 10.5% cost of capital. The discounted totals below are the brief's own values, to the cent.

What is counted. Narrowbody operating profit: 737 MAX delivered times its profit per aircraft, plus fps delivered times the fps's, less the status quo's MAX deliveries times the MAX profit. The fps's sales count for 20 years from its entry into service (2040-2059 for the 10-year, 2037-2056 for the 7-year), and Do Nothing's to 2060. The fps bill is booked as one lump in its entry-into-service year, with the dashboard's debt penalty for the extra debt it implies. Building at Renton avoids new-site capex, booked in the same year. No widebody change: no plan here re-engines the 787. Not counted: tax, working capital, and anything after the 20-year window.

For the net-debt test (not the value), the brief spreads the same bill over the development years on the game's phasing curve: 5% in the first year of ten, peaking at 14% in the seventh. The Renton saving is spread over the years the new site would have been built. Net debt is the GM's balance-sheet tracker in constant 2026 $B: base case, and the stress case with free cash flow one-third lower. Net debt is back to $0 by 2029 in the base case and 2030 in the stress case, before either fps spends, and both plans keep it there.

What would flip the decision, in these terms

Margin works on every fps delivered in the counted window. At the 39.9% margin that makes an fps Boeing's 2030 decision under the CEO's normal rule, each fps earns $21.9M instead of $14.1M. That adds +$8.47B to the 10-year (to +$2.10B) and +$12.97B to the 7-year (to +$4.26B) against Do Nothing. At that margin the 10-year beside the 787 Re-engine (+$4.30B) leads the Re-engine alone (+$3.30B), the CEO's other pick that year, by more than the CEO's $1B margin, and the 7-year at Renton (+$4.26B) is the smaller plan within $1B of it, so the CEO takes the 7-year (see the main report). Each point of margin is worth $0.60B for the 10-year and $0.91B for the 7-year.

  • Source: cashflows.py (data/cashflows.json) re-values each plan from the state the 2030 round started in, and checks that the discounted flows equal the brief's value of each plan and the fps-against-Do-Nothing items, to the cent. The year-by-year rows are in fps_cash_flows_2030.csv.
  • Status quo: nobody launches anything, so the values compare each plan with the same baseline; the fps against Do Nothing is the difference. In this world the MAX phases out from 2045 in every plan, the status quo included.
  • Discounting: each year at 10.5% to 2026; the bill and its debt penalty at the entry-into-service year, as the dashboard books them. A bill spent through the development years would cost more in today's money.
