## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** When I started, I said we'd do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready. We've spent these years earning that. The 777X and the 737-7 and -10 are delivering. Our production lines are stable on the KPIs. Our balance sheet is healthier. I think we've turned this ship. I don't think the work is done.

Today we are launching a new single-aisle airplane. It is a Boeing airplane, and we will own the design. We are taking the ten-year path, with entry into service in 2040. That is a planning date with headroom built in, not a stretch target. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast. We have tested the plan against a harder case, including disruption in the supply chain or our engineering workforce around entry into service, and it still stands. If the schedule moves, you'll hear it from us early, with the plan to fix it, and we'll reset once.

A few things will not change. We will certify before we build at rate. We won't take on fixed-price development. We'll share risk with our suppliers on terms that work for both of us, and we'll keep the supply chain funded and healthy. We'll fund this from our operations and our debt capacity. Paying down debt stays our priority, and we intend to stay solidly investment grade.

We are not changing production rates today. The 737 stays where its KPIs have it, and we are not starting a second development alongside this one. Doing less, and doing it better, is what got us here.

This decision rests on our own readiness, not on anyone else's timeline. The financials will follow our production performance. We have a lot of work in front of us, and we're going to do it one step at a time.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do.

Today Airbus launches its next-generation single-aisle aircraft to prepare the Airbus of the 2030s. We are launching it on its technology clock. Entry into service is planned for the second half of the 2030s, once the technologies have matured, and not before.

The A320neo family remains the backbone of our single-aisle business. Its production ramp-up continues in a disciplined and controlled way, and the people and suppliers who deliver it are protected.

We have invited all three engine makers to propose engines for the new aircraft. Our aim is CFM and at least one other maker on the aircraft, to give customers choice and to secure supply. We will also strengthen the European supply chain behind this programme. Securing work packages and critical materials such as titanium and rare earths remains a priority.

On the widebody side, the A350 family, including the A350F, remains our focus.

We will fund this investment within our financial framework: disciplined investment in our future portfolio, and shareholder returns while preserving financial flexibility.

On trade, we will keep working with industry partners on both sides of the Atlantic for a zero-for-zero tariff regime in civil aerospace.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That doesn't change this year. So let me tell you what we know and what we don't.

What we know: nobody has launched a new narrowbody yet. When one comes, we don't have a birthright on that next order. So CFM, together with our partners at Safran, is launching a new ducted engine now. That way a new CFM engine is there for whichever new airframe comes first. We don't expect it ready before 2036. We'll report against that gate every year, on test, not on slides. It is not our next-generation engine. That's still RISE. We're all in on that technology, and we're keeping it whole. When that architecture enters service is really not for us to say. The airframers and their airline customers will decide that.

CFM is also teaming with Embraer. Any airframer that wants a CFM engine can have one, and nobody gets locked out. On the widebody, GE is upgrading the GEnx for our 787 customers.

What we don't know: when the airframers move, and what they pick. So we plan on the low case, and we keep the balance sheet and our capital returns whole. You won't see us pointing fingers; you'll see us solving problems. We get paid in dollars, not in percentages. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Pratt & Whitney has no new engine programme to announce today, and our share of narrowbody engine deliveries has held at 24% for four years. That is not where we want to be. We know we have to earn the dependable-engine reputation every day, so the GTF fleet keeps first call on our parts, our shops and our engineers. Control what you can control. Our commitment is this. In any round in which an airframer names Pratt & Whitney on a new single-aisle, as sole source or alongside another engine, we will launch our next-generation geared turbofan, GTF2. It will be ready six years from launch, so no earlier than 2036, with time on wing and full-life parts proven before entry into service. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF. We will not buy a selection: standard terms, no launch-style discounts. We're not in the early entry phase, the launch phase. We have made no Joint Venture commitment. Any partnership we ever enter would share risk and investment, never our gear or core technology. We will not speak for any airframer's plans, dates or rates. When we've seen it and when we feel good about it, we invest.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So, let me be clear. Rolls-Royce will launch UltraFan for a new narrowbody in the round an airframer selects it, and only then. Entry into service no earlier than seven years from launch, with the full test programme and nothing compressed, and with support in place through partner shops before entry into service. We do not want to underestimate the risks. Partnership is our preference: we are open to Pratt & Whitney on aligned terms, and we are not committing today. Widebody is our franchise. No new widebody engine spend without a Re-engine, and durability work on the Trent fleet goes on. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Conservative EIS disclosure at launch — Publish entry into service in 2040, no earlier, as a planning baseline with buffer in it. State the risk case as a condition: a supply-chain or engineering-talent disruption around EIS. On the board that is S5: 5 pp of share lost for 5 years from EIS, -0.78 against Do Nothing's -3.64. Keep any earlier internal date as upside and never publish it. If the date moves, reset once and say so early [BX-0553, BX-1318, BX-1306].
- **Boeing:** No fixed-price development; risk-sharing supplier terms — Contract fps development on cost-plus or back-to-back terms with suppliers and the engine maker. No fixed-price development [BX-1262, BX-1304]. This is not priced on the board.
- **Boeing:** Certify before building at rate — The fps program plan carries no production-certification concurrency [BX-1263, B-2228]. The 10-year ramp leaves room to certify before the line goes to rate.
- **Boeing:** Fund from operations and debt capacity; keep deleveraging — No new equity. Stay solidly investment grade on the 10-year phasing, about $6.0B a year over 2030-39 against $6.7B a year of airplane operating profit (Malave's arithmetic). Debt stays the first call on free cash [BX-1305, BX-0556, B-2327].
- **Airbus:** NGSA engine competition open to all three makers — We announce that the next single-aisle will carry CFM plus at least one second source, and invite Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce and CFM to propose engines (code 7). The aim is supply certainty and customer choice; the code does not change our payoff. We revisit the code in 2035, before entry into service, against Wagner's criteria: published ready dates for GTF2, UltraFan NB or a Joint Venture engine against the NGSA entry year; CFM's choice between ducted and Open Fan; Boeing's fps code; and A320 engine delivery performance [AX-0054, A-0243, A-0360].
- **Airbus:** Secure NGSA supply first, by owning or locking it — Long-term agreements in Wagner's order of priority: engines, then the rate-capable wing work packages, titanium forgings and rare earths. This follows our Spirit, titanium and rare-earth de-risking [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063], and it is for our own programme and ramp only. Announced as strengthening a more sovereign European aerospace supply chain.
- **Airbus:** Open recruitment for the Airbus of the 2030s — A public European hiring programme in engineering for NGSA, sized so as not to draw on A320 ramp staff. It is ordinary hiring and not a Delay Tactics move.
- **Airbus:** Zero-for-zero trade advocacy — We continue to press for a tariff-free regime for civil aerospace through the industry coalitions on both sides of the Atlantic [AX-0088].
- **CFM/GE:** RISE continues whole as technology, 50-50 with Safran — Ground and component testing continues, funded as technology with no program dividend [CX-0174, CX-0214]. It becomes a product only when an airframer wants it [CX-0469]. No fly-demo, ground-test, flight-test or entry date is given (Ali's date ground).
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF durability kit (Hot Section) for the installed fleet — We keep offering the durability kit at shop visits. It is sold on standard terms, not given away [PX-0100][PX-0088][PX-0106]. We give no dated recovery of the AOG count; the brief has no 2030 count, and Eddy's dated recoveries and mine have slipped before [PX-0098][PX-0104]. It has no effect on the game board.
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan demonstrator path continues — UltraFan continues as a technology demonstrator: ground test, public R&D money where available, partner sought. Some of its technology goes into today's Trent fleet for time on wing. Qualitative only; it changes no payoff on the grid [RX-0057, RX-0094, RG-0065].
- **Rolls-Royce:** Trent 1000 and Trent fleet durability (time on wing) continues as funded — The durability programme (about £1bn over four years, separate from UltraFan money) continues [RX-0123, RX-0246]. This is not the t1000_upgrade order, which stays at hold. Qualitative only.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Open door to Pratt & Whitney on aligned terms — Stated as openness only, in the public statement. No jv_with_pw commitment this round. Revisit in 2035 if P&W's commitment is on the public record. Formed then, the Joint Venture is ready in 2041.
