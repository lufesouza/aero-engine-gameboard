Method

  • Fit. For each component, the annual path with the least squared second differences (the smoothest) that passes through your values in all eleven years and makes total demand match your four period totals exactly. The fleet (19.7k at end-2026 plus each year's growth) is held to your fleet column as a soft constraint; it ends within 15 aircraft of every value, inside the column's 0.1k rounding.
  • Old-generation replacement ends after 2045. Your table has 336 in 2045 and 0 in 2050. A straight run-down to 2050 would put about 650 more aircraft into 2046-2055 than your 18.7k total; ending it after 2045 fits.
  • Held at zero. No value may be negative; 5 tiny values sit at zero (MAX/neo/A220 2027, MAX/neo/A220 2028, post-2026 2046, post-2026 2047, post-2026 2048). Post-2026 replacement starts in 2046 (your table has 0 to 2045).
  • Rounding. Your 2045 total (2,130) is one above its parts (936 + 1,193); the curve keeps the parts.
  • Class share. Your table covers the whole single-aisle market, A220 included. The game models the 737/A320 market and applies its class share (94.83%, from the Cirium fleet and order scope) once, as it does now.
  • After 2070. The game holds the 2070 values (2,531 a year). That matters only for a programme entering service after 2051, whose 20-year window runs past 2070.
  • Not changed. Firm backlog, customer preference, the overflow and drop-out rates, and every maker's capacity stay as they are.
  • Source. dashgame/make_demand_conservative.py writes demand_conservative.json and asserts every check above. The game has not been run with it.
