## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** We said we would turn this big ship, and I think we've turned it. The 777X is in service, the MAX 7 and MAX 10 are certified, and our balance sheet is back where it belongs. That is the work of a lot of people doing it the right way, and the financials followed our production performance.

It doesn't change how we run the place. The 737 line stays on the plan we've laid out, and the KPIs tell us when we're ready, not the calendar. We'll keep building 787 capacity in Charleston, because customers want more of that airplane and we intend to keep it the most efficient in its segment.

On a new airplane, our thinking hasn't changed. We will do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready, and all three have to be there. When we do, it will be planned around a stable supply of parts, starting with engines, and more than one engine source. I'm not going to give a date before we're ready to launch. When we launch, we'll set the schedule with headroom in it, and we won't take on development at a fixed price. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Airbus is launching its next-generation single-aisle aircraft. It is the centre of our work to prepare the Airbus of the 2030s, and we plan for it to enter service in the second half of this decade, once its technologies, its production system and its engines are mature. We are inviting all three narrowbody engine makers, CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce, to power it. A stable, diversified engine supply is a condition of a disciplined and controlled ramp-up and of serving our customers well. The A320neo family, with its two engine options, stays in production through the transition: we will keep its lines running longer while the new aircraft ramps up, so customers are served without a break. We will take on one major development at a time and fund it from our own cash flow, preserving our financial flexibility and a strong balance sheet. With our partners across the industry, on both sides of the Atlantic, we will keep working for a tariff-free, zero-for-zero trade regime in civil aerospace. And we will recruit and train, in Europe and across our global footprint, the people who will design and build this aircraft.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That's how we run this business, and it's how we made this call.

Here's what we know. Our customers fly LEAP and GEnx today, and our job is to deliver those engines and keep them on wing. LEAP durability upgrades are going into new engines and, through the shops, into the fleet. On the 787 we're upgrading the GEnx, because our airline customers make their money on time on wing, and so do we.

Here's what we don't know: when the next narrowbody will be launched, and by whom. That's really not for us to say; it's the airframers' call. CFM, with our partners at Safran, will be ready to support every airframer that moves, with a LEAP derivative or with a new engine. CFM will commit a new engine with a committed airframer. We don't have a birthright on that next order. We intend to earn it.

RISE stays all in as technology, together with Safran. We'll tell you about dates when the testing tells us. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** I'll start with the bad news. For years the GTF fleet was not where our customers needed it to be. We own that, and we have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF. So we control what we can control. Pratt & Whitney will launch its next-generation geared turbofan, GTF2, in any round in which an airframer selects Pratt & Whitney, sole or shared. GTF2 will be ready six years from launch. It will not enter service until its durability is proven: endurance testing in severe environments, life-limited parts at full life, and time on wing, not just fuel burn. We will not compress testing to make a date. It comes on standard terms. We're not in the early entry phase. For the fleet flying today, the GTF Hot Section kit is available at shop visit on standard terms, and we will report its time on wing once the fleet has proven it. We have no Joint Venture to announce. We speak for our engines, not for any airframer's dates or rates. When we've seen the demand and we feel good about it, we invest. These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So. Widebody is our core, and we will protect it. We are not after market share. Our route into the narrowbody is a partnership, not a solo programme: Rolls-Royce stands ready to commit to a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney, on terms that credit the technology we bring. We take a narrowbody engine forward only for an airframe that has chosen it. Entry into service will be no earlier than six years from the Joint Venture's formation, with in-service support through partner shops in place before entry into service. We don't commit that easily. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.
- **Embraer:** Safety first and quality always: that is the foundation of our culture. We believe we are still in our harvest season, right? We focused on delivering the results in our plan to 2030 with the products we have: the E2 family, our executive jets, defence and services. We keep doing that, with a very healthy cash generation to support a potential next move. On a new product, I will give the same answer I always give. We are investing in new technologies because it is important that we are prepared to make that decision when the time comes. We need a strong product with a strong value proposition and a robust, I mean, funding strategy, because we don't want to put in risk the financial health of the company. Any large investment will be made with partners, and we talk to many partners and to all the engine makers. Today we have no decision to announce, and we will not discuss a segment or a date. This does not depend only on us. It depends on the technology available in the market and on the partners, and the investment has to prove its return. On the decisions of other manufacturers: those are their decisions, and I can only speak for Embraer. On trade, Embraer is about engineering, and we focus on data and things that are under our control. More than 40% of our aircraft is US content, so tariffs mean fewer US parts bought and fewer US jobs. We continue to work with both governments for zero tariffs in aerospace, and we are cautiously optimistic. We compete hard in every campaign, but we don't sell our aircraft below cost.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Continue the Charleston 787 capacity expansion — Production capacity for 787 rates in the teens, not a development program [BX-1317, B-2336]. Funded inside the debt lines. Not priced on the board.
- **Boeing:** Next-narrowbody engine requirement: a stable, multi-source engine supply — Tell CFM, P&W and Rolls-Royce that Boeing's next narrowbody will be planned around a stable supply of parts, engines first, with more than one engine source and durability first [BX-1298]. No launch, no date and no engine selection this round. Serves Control's engine enabler without paying a premium (Board recommendation 9; inference).
- **Airbus:** Open NGSA engine selection: code 7 (CFM, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce) — We announce that NGSA is open to all three narrowbody engine makers, with CFM as the floor. The invitation gives P&W and RR a live airframe that selects them this round, so their launch_if_selected engines can be ready by an EIS in the second half of the decade (GTF2 2036, UltraFan NB 2037). It is a request and costs nothing. No Airbus payment is offered to induce an engine launch: any such payment would be a separate Board item above EUR 300m, and none is proposed [A-0243, A-0360, AX-0054].
- **Airbus:** Long-term, rate-gated engine supply agreements — Agreements with CFM and P&W cover the LEAP-1A and GTF tail on the A320 Alt plan through about 2045. Framework agreements with all three makers cover NGSA engines. Both are backed by long-range rate signals given early, as for the A320neo ramp [A-0159]. Announced in general terms only, with no volumes or internal plan targets [AX-0005].
- **Airbus:** Recruiting and training for the Airbus of the 2030s — Our own hiring, apprenticeships and training for NGSA engineering and the 2040s ramp, through schools and our European and global footprint [A-0485]. It does not target any rival's staff.
- **Airbus:** Industry-coalition trade advocacy: zero-for-zero tariffs in civil aerospace — We work with industry associations on both sides of the Atlantic, Boeing included as an ally on trade, to keep a tariff-free regime for aircraft and parts [AX-0088, AX-0046].
- **CFM/GE:** RISE kept whole as technology with Safran — RISE stays fully funded as a joint CFM technology programme, with no product launch and no 'program dividend' [CX-0174, CX-0214]. Testing continues with our partners, and RISE technology keeps flowing into LEAP upgrades [CX-0162]. We give no entry date and no flight-test date [CX-0205, CX-0009]. This fits joint practice, and Safran is a full partner in RISE.
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP durability and delivery first — LEAP durability hardware keeps going into new engines and, at shop visits, into the fleet. Spares never crowd out OE engines [CX-0529]. We report facts only, with no new promise dates [CX-0436].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Public conditional GTF2 offer — P&W will launch GTF2 in any round in which an airframer selects a code that includes P&W, sole or shared (codes 2, 6, 7). It is ready six years from launch, on standard terms, and does not enter service until durability is proven. No EIS date is stated, nothing is said about an airframer's dates, and no selection is claimed. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF Hot Section durability kit for the in-service fleet — Offered at shop visit on standard terms; upgrades are sold, not given [PX-0100]. No time-on-wing number is announced until it is proven in service (Mitchill's ground-4 advice). No grid effect [PX-0088][PX-0106].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Standard terms restated — No launch-style pricing, concessions or free upgrades to win a selection. We are not in the early entry phase [PX-0055]. No grid effect.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Open the door to a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney — The public statement says Rolls-Royce prefers a partner for the narrowbody and stands ready to commit to a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney, on terms that credit the UltraFan technology we bring. No dates. It is not a `jv_with_pw: commit` order; we commit once P&W's commitment is on the public record (the Board's rule, and the team rule of partner first).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Continue UltraFan technology maturation inside the existing R&D envelope — Demonstrator and geared-architecture work goes on inside base R&D and feeds Trent time on wing. No programme launch, no new bill, no dates.
- **Embraer:** Deliver the plan to 2030 and build cash for a potential next move — Keep delivering the E2 family, the business jets, defence and services to plan. Productivity comes before new capacity, and commercial output rises toward about 120 jets a year only where the return justifies it. The cash this builds is there to support a potential next move [EX-0080, EX-0081, EX-0053, EX-0079]. The board does not price this move.
- **Embraer:** Keep technology readiness funded for a new product cycle — Studies continue for a new cycle of products, naming no segment and no date. We keep talking to all the engine makers and select none, since engine selection comes before any business case [EX-0112, EX-0141, EX-0133, EX-0044]. We stay prepared to make that decision when the time comes.
- **Embraer:** Trade: argue for zero tariffs on aerospace, on data — More than 40% of our aircraft is US content [EX-0124]. Tariffs mean fewer aircraft sold in the US, fewer US parts bought and fewer US jobs [EX-0101]. We ask both governments to talk and take no side, and we discuss no politics [EX-0100, EX-0098, EX-0106].
