## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** Five years ago I said I thought we'd turned the ship. I'm comfortable now saying it's turned. The 737 line has run steady at the rate we set. We keep adding 787 capacity in Charleston, and the balance sheet is where it belongs and has stayed there. That isn't luck. It's a lot of people doing it the right way, every day.

Over the next several years more customers will want 737s than we're going to build. I know that's frustrating for some of them, and I don't take it lightly. But I'd much rather have a customer waiting than a production system that goes unstable again. The KPIs tell us when we can do more, not the calendar.

On a new airplane, how we decide hasn't changed. We will do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready, and all three have to be there. We're in good shape financially and the technology is coming along, but the business case isn't there yet. I'm not going to launch an airplane to meet a date. We're keeping the work going on the airplane and on how we'd build it, so we're prepared when the case is there. When we do launch, the airplane will be planned around a stable supply of parts, engines first, with more than one engine source and durability ahead of everything else. I won't give you a date before we're ready to launch. When we launch, there'll be headroom in the schedule, and we won't take on development at a fixed price.

We'll keep working with the rest of the industry for a tariff-free environment in aerospace. That's good for everybody in this business.

It is so much more important that we do this right than fast.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Our next-generation single-aisle aircraft is in development, and it is the centre of our work to prepare the Airbus of the 2030s. We continue to plan for it to enter service in the second half of this decade, once its technologies, its production system and its engines are mature. Safety and quality come before the calendar. Its engine selection stays open to all three narrowbody engine makers, and we plan for a dual-source engine supply, with each engine entering service only once its durability is proven. How the engine makers organise themselves is their decision. A stable, diversified supply of parts, engines first, is a condition of a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up. The A320neo family, with its two engine options, stays in production through the transition: its lines keep running longer while the new aircraft ramps up, so customers are served without a break. We are giving our suppliers long-range signals for that ramp, and we continue to secure our own materials and work packages. We take on one major development at a time and fund it from our own cash flow, preserving our financial flexibility and a strong balance sheet. With our partners across the industry, on both sides of the Atlantic, we keep working for a tariff-free, zero-for-zero trade regime in civil aerospace. And we keep recruiting and training, in Europe and across our global footprint, the people who will design and build the Airbus of the 2030s.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That's how we run this business, and it's how we made this call.

Here's what we know. CFM, with our partners at Safran, is on Airbus's next single-aisle, and we appreciate the confidence. We'll support that airplane with the LEAP family, and we'll earn our place on it every day, on time on wing. LEAP-1A durability hardware is in every new -1A engine and is going into the fleet through the shops. On the -1B, we'll tell you the results when the fleet data is in. The GEnx upgrade is ready for our 787 customers.

Here's what we don't know: when the next new narrowbodies will be launched, by whom, and on which engines. That's really not for us to say; it's the airframers' call. We don't have a birthright on that next order. We intend to earn it. A LEAP derivative is on offer to every airframer, and CFM will commit a new engine with a committed airframer.

RISE stays all in as technology, together with Safran. When that architecture enters service is really not for us to say. We'll tell you about dates when the testing tells us.

We get paid in dollars, not in percentages. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** I'll start with what we still owe our customers. For years the GTF fleet was not where it needed to be, and we own that. The next engine will be judged on time on wing, and that is how we will judge it ourselves. So we control what we can control. GTF2, launched in 2030, is in its last year of development and is due to be ready in 2036, six years from launch. It is not proven yet, and it will be proven before it enters service. Airbus has named Pratt & Whitney alongside CFM and Rolls-Royce for its next-generation single-aisle, and we intend to earn that place. GTF2 will not enter service until its durability is proven: endurance testing in severe environments, life-limited parts at full life at entry into service, and time on wing before fuel burn. We will not compress testing to make a date. Any feature that is not proven in time waits for a later block. We will sign Airbus's rate-gated supply agreements on standard terms. We are planning our castings and forgings ahead of the ramp, and the fleet flying today keeps first call on material. For any airframer that selects Pratt & Whitney, GTF2 comes on standard terms. We're not in the early entry phase. The GTF Hot Section kit remains available at shop visit, and we will report its time on wing once the fleet has proven it. We have no Joint Venture to announce: we share risk and investment with partners, not our core technology. We speak for our engines, not for any airframer's dates or rates. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF. These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So. Rolls-Royce commits to a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney for the next narrowbody. Our preference has always been partnership, and there is no change of strategy. The commitment stands, for an airframe that has chosen both of us, on terms that credit the technology we bring. No solo narrowbody programme. The engine enters service only when it is ready and its durability is proven. Maturity sets the date, and we compress nothing. Support through partner shops will be in place before entry into service. Widebody is our core, and we will protect it. We are not after market share. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.
- **Embraer:** Safety first and quality always: that is the foundation of our culture. We believe we are still in our harvest season, right? We keep delivering our plan with the products we have: the E2 family, our executive jets, defence and services. And we keep a healthy balance sheet to support a potential next move.

On a new airplane I will give the same answer I always give. We are investing in new technologies so that we are prepared to make that decision when the time comes. A launch needs a strong product with a strong value proposition and a robust, I mean, funding strategy, because we don't want to put in risk the financial health of the company. We have done the work, and the numbers today do not prove the return. So today we have no decision to announce, and we will not discuss a segment or a date. We continue to talk to our partners, including in Korea, and to all the engine makers. Any large investment will be made with partners, with the funding defined at the time of approval. I will not say conservative, but I would say, realistic.

On the decisions of other manufacturers: those are their decisions. We have our own strategy, and I can only speak for Embraer.

On trade, Embraer is about engineering. We focus on data and things that are under our control. More than 40% of our aircraft is US content, so tariffs mean fewer US parts bought and fewer US jobs. We continue to work with both governments for zero tariffs in aerospace, and we are cautiously optimistic.

We compete hard in every campaign, but we don't sell our aircraft below cost. As we proudly say in Embraer, We Live for the Challenge.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Continue the Charleston 787 capacity expansion — Production capacity for 787 rates in the teens, not a development program [BX-1317, B-2336]. Funded inside the debt lines. Not priced on the board. Pope finds that the added 787 demand fits inside it.
- **Boeing:** Next-narrowbody engine requirement: a stable, multi-source engine supply (restated) — We tell CFM, P&W and Rolls-Royce again that Boeing's next narrowbody will be planned around a stable supply of parts, engines first, with more than one engine source and durability first [BX-1298]. No launch, no date and no engine selection this round. It serves Control's engine enabler at no grid cost (Board recommendation 5).
- **Boeing:** Zero-for-zero tariffs in civil aerospace, with the industry — We keep working with industry associations for a tariff-free regime for aircraft and parts. A tariff-free environment is good for every company in the industry [B-2290]. No grid effect.
- **Airbus:** NGSA engine selection stays open to all three makers (code 7, final) — We restate that NGSA's selection names CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce. CFM is the floor and P&W GTF2 is the planned second source. Each engine enters service only once its durability is proven. How the engine makers organise themselves is their decision, and we neither invite nor discourage any partnership. We offer no payment, advance or take-or-pay to induce any engine launch (anything above EUR 300m would be a separate Board item, and none is proposed). We exchange no confidential information [A-0243, A-0360, AX-0054].
- **Airbus:** Long-range, rate-gated supply signals for the 2037-45 ramp — We give CFM and P&W signals early, as for the A320neo ramp, covering the LEAP-1A and GTF tail on the A320 Alt plan and the NGSA engine sets. They are sized to our own needs only: on two sources our CFM call stays at or below today's 51 sets a month (Wagner). We announce them in general terms only, with no volumes and no internal plan targets [A-0159, AX-0005].
- **Airbus:** Secure our own materials and work packages — Titanium through Aubert & Duval, additive manufacturing, the raw-materials watchlist, and the Spirit work packages fully integrated. All of it serves our own ramp only [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063].
- **Airbus:** Recruiting and training for the 2039-44 ramp and the Airbus of the 2030s — Our own hiring, apprenticeships and training, in Europe and across our global footprint. It targets no rival's staff [A-0485, A-0366].
- **Airbus:** Zero-for-zero trade advocacy in an industry coalition — With industry associations on both sides of the Atlantic, Boeing included as an ally on trade. It stays strictly to public trade policy, with no exchange of pricing, capacity, programme or engine-selection information [AX-0088, AX-0046].
- **CFM/GE:** RISE kept whole as technology with Safran — RISE stays fully funded as a joint CFM technology programme, with no product launch and no 'program dividend' [CX-0174, CX-0214]. It is never cancelled for cost [CX-0398]. Testing continues with our partners, and RISE technology keeps flowing into LEAP upgrades [CX-0162]. No entry date and no ground-test or flight-test date [CX-0205, CX-0009]. This fits joint practice, and Safran is a full partner in RISE.
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP durability and delivery first — LEAP-1A durability hardware is in every new -1A engine and goes into the fleet at shop visits [CX-0209, CX-0225]. We report the -1B when the fleet data is in, and we make no claim before then [CX-0210]. Spares never crowd out OE engines [CX-0529]; this is joint practice with Safran. Facts only, no new promise dates [CX-0436].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Answer Airbus's rate-gated supply agreements on standard terms — P&W will sign the rate-gated agreements for the A320neo GTF tail on the Alt plan and the NGSA framework agreement on standard terms. Volumes stay inside our castings and forgings plan, with no discount, concession or free upgrade. We announce no volumes, no NGSA share and no Airbus date or rate [PX-0055, PX-0062, PX-0099]. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF2 available to any new narrowbody that selects P&W, on standard terms — This answers Boeing's call for a stable supply from more than one engine source, durability first, and Embraer's talks with all engine makers. GTF2 is ready six years from launch (2036), on standard terms, for any fps or new narrowbody that selects P&W, sole or shared. We claim no selection and say nothing on any airframer's dates [PX-0109, P-0746, PX-0062]. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Answer Rolls-Royce's invitation — We have no Joint Venture to announce, and we are not opening terms this round. We share risk and investment with partners, never our core technology [P-1233]. The answer is respectful and leaves the door neither shut nor open. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF Hot Section kit continues at shop visit, standard terms — Upgrades are sold, not given [PX-0100]. No time-on-wing figure until the fleet proves it, per Mitchill's ground-4 advice [PX-0088, PX-0106]. No grid effect.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Standing Joint Venture commitment with Pratt & Whitney, on standard terms — The `jv_with_pw: commit` is public at once and stands until withdrawn. It is for an airframe that has chosen both makers. The terms credit the technology Rolls-Royce brings, which asks for value; it is not a concession. There is no payment to P&W or to any airframer, no launch-style pricing and no free upgrades. No dates.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Continue UltraFan technology maturation inside the existing R&D envelope — Demonstrator and geared-architecture work carries on inside base R&D and feeds Trent time on wing. No new programme, no new bill, no dates.
- **Embraer:** Deliver the plan and keep building cash to support a potential next move — We keep delivering the E2 family, the business jets, defence and services to plan in the harvest season. Productivity comes before new capacity. Commercial output rises toward about 120 jets a year only where the return justifies it, keeping supply-chain headroom [EX-0053, EX-0080, EX-0077, EX-0078, EX-0087]. The cash and the low net debt ($0.7B) are kept to support a potential next move. The board does not price this.
- **Embraer:** Keep technology readiness funded for the next product cycle — Studies continue for a new cycle of products, naming no segment and no date. We keep talking to all the engine makers and select none, because engine selection comes before any business case [EX-0112, EX-0141, EX-0133, EX-0044]. Meijer's readiness advice is logged for any later case: supplier headroom for a second line beside the E2 ramp, P&W's durability-before-entry-into-service stance, and a four-year clean-sheet certification against the E2's roughly five years.
- **Embraer:** Trade: argue on data, not politics — We keep working with both governments for zero-for-zero in aerospace. Our case is more than 40% US content and US jobs: tariffs mean fewer US parts bought and a limited ability to invest in the future [EX-0098, EX-0100, EX-0101, EX-0124]. The 10% share cut is already in the brief. It is not a reason for this decision, and on this grid it is worth $0.00-0.05B.
