## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** The balance sheet is where it belongs, and it has stayed there for a long time now. The 737 and 787 lines are running stable. That's the base a lot of people built by doing it the right way every day, and we're not going to give it back.

The 737 MAX is coming toward the end of its production life, and its rate will come down over the next several years. We'll bring it down the way we brought it up: in steps, on the data, keeping the line and our suppliers stable. Some customers will wait longer than they'd like. I don't take that lightly, but I'd much rather have a customer waiting than a production system that goes unstable. We're keeping our people. As the 737 steps down, skilled people will move to the 787 in Charleston and across the company, and we'll keep our experienced narrowbody team together. We keep adding 787 capacity in Charleston.

On a new airplane, I'll be straight with you. We haven't launched one, and I'm not going to pretend that waiting is free. But how we decide hasn't changed. We will do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready, and all three have to be there. We're ready financially, and the technology is there. The business case isn't there yet. I'm not going to launch an airplane to meet a date, and I'm not going to launch one that doesn't pay. We're keeping the work going on the airplane and on how we'd build it, so we're prepared when the case is there. When we launch, the airplane will be planned around a stable supply of parts, engines first, with more than one engine source and durability ahead of everything else. There'll be headroom in the schedule, and we won't take on development at a fixed price. You'll get a date from me when we launch, not before.

We'll keep working with the rest of the industry for a tariff-free environment in aerospace. That's good for everybody in this business.

It is so much more important that we do this right than fast.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Our next-generation single-aisle aircraft has been in service since 2037. Its production ramp-up continues in a disciplined and controlled way, paced by our customers' demand and the maturity of our production system, and never at the expense of safety or quality. It flies with two engine sources, CFM and Pratt & Whitney. A stable, diversified supply of parts, engines first, remains a condition of that ramp, and we are extending our long-range, rate-gated signals to our suppliers for the years ahead, sized to our own needs. How the engine makers organise themselves is their decision. On widebodies, the A350 family continues to serve our customers and we keep improving it step by step. Any further step will be taken only once its engine is mature and its durability is proven, and we will speak of it in windows, not dates. We take on one major development at a time and fund it from our own cash flow, preserving our financial flexibility and a strong balance sheet. With our partners across the industry, on both sides of the Atlantic, we keep working for a tariff-free, zero-for-zero trade regime in civil aerospace. And we keep recruiting and training, in Europe and across our global footprint, the people who will design and build the next generation of Airbus aircraft.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That's how we run this business, and it's how we made this call.

Here's what we know. CFM, with our partners at Safran, is on Airbus's new single-aisle, and we'll earn our place on it every day, on time on wing. We'll support that ramp on the signals Airbus gives us, jointly with Safran, and spares won't crowd out new engines. LEAP-1A durability hardware is certified. It's in every new -1A engine and going into the fleet through the shops. We'll report LEAP time on wing in cycles once the fleet has proven it. The GEnx upgrade is ready for our 787 customers.

Here's what we don't know: when the next new narrowbodies will be launched, by whom, and on which engines. That's really not for us to say. It's the airframers' call. We don't have a birthright on that next order. We intend to earn it. A LEAP derivative is on offer to every airframer, and a new engine comes when an airframer and the returns are both there.

RISE stays all in as technology, together with Safran. When that architecture enters service is really not for us to say. We'll tell you about dates when the testing tells us.

We get paid in dollars, not in percentages. It's that simple, ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** I'll start where we always start, with the fleet. For years the GTF fleet was not where it needed to be, and our customers carried that. We own it, and we have not forgotten it. So we control what we can control.

GTF2 has been in service on Airbus's next-generation single-aisle since 2037, alongside CFM. We judge it the way our customers do, on time on wing. We will report that record as the fleet measures it, and not before. Any later-block feature enters the fleet only when it is proven and instrumented. We will not compress testing to meet anyone's rate.

Supply is where we have let customers down before. So we are adding castings and forgings capacity in steps, with our suppliers, and the fleet flying today keeps first call on material.

For any airframer that selects Pratt & Whitney, sole or shared, GTF2 is available today on standard terms. We're not in the early entry phase, the launch phase. The GTF Hot Section kit remains available at shop visit, on the same terms.

Rolls-Royce has made its commitment public, and we respect it. We have no Joint Venture to announce. We share risk and investment with partners, never our core technology.

We keep investing in geared technology for the next cycle. It is technology, and we have no product or date to announce.

We speak for our engines, not for any airframer's dates or rates. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF. These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So. Our commitment to a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney stands, as we said it would. When we commit something, we honor it. It is on standard terms, terms that credit the technology we bring. No payment, no concession. No solo narrowbody programme. Maturity sets the date, and we compress nothing. Support through our partners will be in place before we take any share of the fleet. Widebody is our core, and we will protect it. We are not after market share. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.
- **Embraer:** Safety first and quality always: that is the foundation of our culture. We believe we are still in our harvest season, right? We keep delivering our plan with the products we have: the E2 family, our executive jets, defence and services. And we keep a healthy balance sheet to support a potential next move.

On a new airplane I will give you the same answer I always give. We are investing in new technologies so that we are prepared to make that decision when the time comes. A launch needs a strong product with a strong value proposition and a robust, I mean, funding strategy, because we don't want to put in risk the financial health of the company. We have done the work again this year, and the numbers do not prove the return. The investment has to prove its return. So today we have no decision to announce, and we will not discuss a segment or a date. We continue to talk to our partners, including in Korea, and to all the engine makers. Any large investment will be made with partners, with the funding defined at the time of approval. I will not say conservative, but I would say, realistic.

On the decisions of other manufacturers: those are their decisions. We have our own strategy, and I can only speak for Embraer.

On trade, Embraer is about engineering. We focus on data and things that are under our control. More than 40% of our aircraft is US content, so tariffs mean fewer US parts bought and fewer US jobs. We continue to work with both governments for zero tariffs in aerospace, and we are cautiously optimistic.

We compete hard in every campaign, but we don't sell our aircraft below cost. As we proudly say in Embraer, We Live for the Challenge.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** Continue the Charleston 787 capacity expansion — This is production capacity for 787 rates in the teens, not a development program [BX-1317, B-2336]. It is funded inside the debt lines and the board does not price it. Pope finds the added 787 demand fits: about 117 a year by 2060 (9.75 a month). Charleston also takes skilled people transferred off the 737 line as it steps down.
- **Boeing:** Next-narrowbody engine requirement: a stable, multi-source engine supply (restated) — We tell CFM, P&W and Rolls-Royce again that Boeing's next narrowbody will be planned around a stable supply of parts, engines first, with more than one engine source and durability first [BX-1298]. There is no launch, no date and no engine selection this round. At launch we will request code 7, the only code that names all three makers. This serves Control's engine enabler at no grid cost (Board recommendation 5).
- **Boeing:** Zero-for-zero tariffs in civil aerospace, with the industry — We keep working with industry associations for a tariff-free regime for aircraft and parts. It is good for every company in the industry [B-2290]. No grid effect.
- **Airbus:** Long-range, rate-gated supply signals extended to 2050 (NGSA engine sets only) — We extend the signals to CFM and P&W to cover the NGSA ramp to 2050. The A320neo LEAP-1A and GTF tail ends by 2047.
- Sized to our own needs only: on the delivery path about 41-45 CFM and 54-59 P&W sets a month, gated on rate, with ceilings at capacity (Wagner).
- No payment, advance or take-or-pay, and nothing reserved against other customers.
- Announced in general terms only, with no volumes and no internal plan targets [A-0159, A-0243, A-0360, AX-0005].
- **Airbus:** NGSA engine line: code 7 final, neutral on how the makers organise — NGSA flies CFM and P&W GTF2, so the dual source is met. How the engine makers organise themselves is their decision. We neither invite nor discourage the Rolls-Royce / P&W Joint Venture.
- On Wagner's plain reading, fitting was settled at EIS, so NGSA keeps CFM plus P&W's engine either way. That is two sources, and the Joint Venture counts as one.
- Whether Rolls-Royce would then count as a third maker in the market is a ruling for the game master.
- No payment, advance or take-or-pay to induce any engine launch. Anything above EUR 300m would be its own Board item, and none is proposed. No confidential information is exchanged [A-0243, A-0360, AX-0054].
- **Airbus:** Secure our own materials and work packages — Titanium through Aubert & Duval, additive manufacturing, the raw-materials watchlist, and the Spirit work packages, fully integrated. All of it serves our own ramp only [AX-0060, AX-0061, AX-0062, AX-0063].
- **Airbus:** Recruiting and training for the NGSA ramp and the next generation — Our own hiring, apprenticeships and training, in Europe and across our global footprint. It targets no rival's staff [A-0485, A-0366].
- **Airbus:** Zero-for-zero trade advocacy in an industry coalition — With industry associations on both sides of the Atlantic, Boeing and Embraer included as allies on trade. It stays strictly to public trade policy, with no exchange of pricing, capacity, programme or engine-selection information [AX-0088, AX-0046].
- **CFM/GE:** RISE kept whole as technology with Safran — RISE stays fully funded as a joint CFM technology programme. There is no product launch and no 'program dividend' [CX-0174, CX-0214], and it is never cancelled for cost [CX-0398]. Testing continues with our partners, and RISE technology keeps flowing into LEAP upgrades [CX-0162]. No entry date, and no ground-test or flight-test date [CX-0205, CX-0009]. This fits joint practice: Safran is a full partner in RISE.
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP durability and delivery first — LEAP-1A durability hardware is certified. It is in every new -1A engine and goes into the fleet at shop visits [CX-0225, CX-0209]. We report LEAP time on wing in cycles once the fleet has proven it, and we claim nothing before then (Ali's wording; the -1B fleet outcome is not in his record) [CX-0210, CX-0009]. OE engines come first, and spares never crowd them out [CX-0529]. This is joint practice with Safran. Facts only, no promise dates [CX-0436].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF2 on offer, on standard terms, to any fps or new narrowbody that selects P&W, sole or shared — GTF2 has been ready since 2036 and is available on standard terms to any airframer that selects Pratt & Whitney, sole or shared (codes 2, 6, 7). This answers Boeing's stated call for more than one engine source with durability first. For Embraer, GTF2 or the GTF family at the GTF Advantage standard, at Embraer's choice. Both are installations, not developments. We claim no selection beyond NGSA's code 7, and we say nothing about any airframer's dates or rates [PX-0109, P-0746, PX-0062]. No discount, concession or free upgrade [PX-0055]. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** NGSA and A320neo GTF tail: rate-gated supply agreements continue on standard terms — We continue to sign against Airbus's rate-gated signals on standard terms. Volumes stay inside our castings and forgings plan, with no take-or-pay, no volume promise beyond that plan, no discount and no free upgrade. We announce no volumes, no NGSA share and no Airbus date or rate. The ramp is balanced jointly with Airbus, and the fleet keeps first call on material [PX-0099, PX-0110, PX-0055]. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Respectful answer to Rolls-Royce's standing Joint Venture commitment — We have no Joint Venture to announce and are opening no terms. We share risk and investment with partners, never our core technology [P-1233]. We respect Rolls-Royce's commitment and keep the relationship professional. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF Hot Section kit continues at shop visit, on standard terms — Upgrades are sold, not given [PX-0100]. We give no time-on-wing figure until measured fleet data supports one, per Mitchill's ground-4 advice and Eddy's ground-1 standing condition [PX-0088, PX-0106]. No grid effect.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Next-generation geared technology kept funded as technology only — This is a reallocation inside the existing self-funded R&D envelope of about $6B a year. There is no new bill and no stretch. We name no product, no date, no dollar figure and no performance claim. Mitchill cleared it on ground 4 and on envelope fit. Eddy ranks it last when engineering work collides. Scope and figure go to the Finance Committee at the next annual GTF2 NPV review, as a dated follow-up ranked by payback. 'Miss a cycle' [PX-0071] and 'an and, not an or' [PX-0109] are RTX-wide statements, applied to engines here by inference. No grid effect.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Standing Joint Venture commitment with Pratt & Whitney, restated on standard terms — The 2035 `jv_with_pw: commit` stands. It is for an airframe that has chosen both makers. The terms credit the technology Rolls-Royce brings. That asks for value; it is not a concession. There is no payment to P&W or to any airframer, no launch-style pricing and no free upgrades. The terms are unchanged, so no new joint CEO-CFO sign-off is needed. No dates, and nothing on any airframer's fleet or on when our share would start.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Continue UltraFan technology maturation inside the existing R&D envelope — Demonstrator and geared-architecture work continues inside base R&D and feeds Trent time on wing and durability. No new programme, no new bill, no dates.
- **Embraer:** Deliver the plan and keep building cash to support a potential next move — We keep delivering the E2 family, the business jets, defence and services to plan in the harvest season. Productivity comes before new capacity. Commercial output rises toward about 120 jets a year only where the return justifies it, and we keep supply-chain headroom [EX-0053, EX-0080, EX-0077, EX-0078, EX-0087]. We keep the cash and the low net debt ($0.7B) to support a potential next move. The board does not price this.
- **Embraer:** Keep technology readiness funded for the next product cycle — Studies continue for a new cycle of products. We name no segment and no date. We keep talking to all the engine makers and select none, because engine selection comes before any business case [EX-0112, EX-0141, EX-0133, EX-0044]. Meijer's 2045 readiness advice is logged for any later case:
- about 216 commercial jets a year by 2054 on a supply base Airbus is loading;
- 192 P&W engines a year behind Airbus's rate-gated agreements, with the in-service fleet keeping first call on material;
- a four-year clean-sheet certification, against the E2's roughly five years.
None of these is a veto ground on this brief.
- **Embraer:** Trade: argue on data, not politics — We keep working with both governments for zero-for-zero in aerospace. Our case is more than 40% US content and US jobs: tariffs mean fewer US parts bought and a limited ability to invest in the future [EX-0098, EX-0100, EX-0101, EX-0124]. The 10% share cut is already in the brief. It is no reason for this decision either way. On this grid it is worth about $0.01B.
