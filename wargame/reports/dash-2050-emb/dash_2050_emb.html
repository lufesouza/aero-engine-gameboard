The MAX phase-out game, replayed with Embraer as a sixth player and political terms in the payoffs. Embraer can launch a new 150-180 seat narrowbody with Team Korea in any round. It enters service four years later (2034 for a 2030 launch, before 2035 as you asked). Its campaigns would cut single-aisle prices by 2%, or 2.6% on Boeing's MAX while Boeing has no fps. Embraer's executives and Board were profiled from its own calls to May 2026 and played its seat like every other company. What happened: Airbus launched NGSA in 2030 (in service 2037), as in the previous game. Boeing did nothing in all four rounds. Embraer held in all four: its launch was below zero in every column of every brief (−$1.02B in 2030, −$0.22B in 2050), so its CFO's funding test failed each time. The engine makers moved less than before. Boeing ends at −$0.24B against the status quo (−$0.28B in the previous game), and its share of single-aisle deliveries falls from 39% in 2030 to 22% in 2050 and none in 2060, when the last MAX is built.

How to read the figures. Values are $B, present value at 2026, against the status quo, in which nobody launches anything (and Embraer builds no airliner). Do Nothing is Boeing launching nothing while the others move. Each round every company's ExCo gets a brief: a grid that values each of its plans against the moves its rivals could make that round, one column per move. The CEO names the column it expects. Boeing's Board approves a launch only if it adds more than +$1B in the expected column and loses no more than $1B in any column it finds plausible, and its CFO vetoes any plan more than $2B below Do Nothing in the risk case. P&L figures are the game's commercial-airplane model at 2026 prices; cash and net debt come from the game master's balance-sheet tracker in 2026 dollars.
