## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** A few years ago we said we had three jobs to do before anything else: stabilize the factories, finish the airplanes we'd started, and repair the balance sheet. The 777X is in service, the MAX 7 and 10 are delivering, and our net debt is back where it was before the hard years. I'm proud of this team for that. We still work on safety and quality every day, and that doesn't stop.

People ask me whether now is the time for the next airplane. We will do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready. We're ready on our end. The other two aren't there yet, and I'm not going to launch an airplane to win a news cycle. When we do launch, you'll get an entry-into-service date we know we can meet, with room built into it, and not before.

On the 737 we're holding at 57 a month. The line is stable and the data is good, and the demand in front of us doesn't need another step right now. I'd much rather run a steady line than chase a number. On widebodies, the 787 is the most efficient airplane in its class. We'll keep investing in it and in Charleston.

Going slow is what let us go fast. It's so much more important that we do this right than fast.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Today Airbus launches its next-generation single-aisle aircraft, with entry into service planned in the second half of the 2030s. This is how we prepare the Airbus of the 2030s: on the clock of technology maturity, with a disciplined and controlled ramp-up. To serve our customers through the transition, we will keep the A320 family in production for longer on our proven lines while the new aircraft ramps up. We have invited CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce to power the new aircraft, because robust, multi-source engine supply matters to our customers and to the ramp. We will fund the programme from our own cash generation, within our financial framework, and preserve our financial flexibility and a strong balance sheet. The A350 family, including the A350F, remains our widebody focus. Together with our European industrial base, our suppliers and our partners worldwide, we will continue to work with the whole industry for a tariff-free, zero-for-zero regime for civil aviation.
- **CFM/GE:** Safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. That's how we run this business, and it's how we made this call.

Here's what we know. The LEAP is flying on every 737 MAX and on the A320neo, and our job today is to deliver those engines on time and support that fleet. We'll keep at it with our suppliers, and with our partners at Safran.

Here's what we don't know: when the airframers will launch their next single-aisle, and what they'll want under the wing. That's really not for us to say. We don't have a birthright on that next order. So CFM will offer our engines to both airframers, and we will commit a new engine alongside a committed airframer.

RISE remains a technology programme we're all in on with Safran. The airframers will set the date for that architecture.

On the widebody, we're investing in a GEnx upgrade so the 787 fleet stays competitive for our customers.

It's that simple. Ain't that hard.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Control what you can control. The last few years on the GTF fleet were hard on our customers, and we own that. Time on wing and getting aircraft back in the air come first, ahead of any new engine.

Here is our commitment. In any round an airframer selects Pratt & Whitney for a new single-aisle, as sole source or alongside a partner, we will launch our next-generation geared turbofan that same round. It is planned to be ready six years from launch, so 2036 for a commitment made this year. Its durability will be proven before it enters service. That means time on wing ahead of fuel burn in the specification, life-limited parts at full life at entry into service, and no testing compressed to hold a date. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF.

We will offer it on standard terms, priced for value. We are not in the early entry phase. We are continuing the GTF Advantage hot-section durability kit across the A320neo fleet, also at standard terms. The dates and rates of any new aircraft are the airframer's to give, not ours.
- **Rolls-Royce:** So, plainly. Rolls-Royce will launch UltraFan for a new narrowbody that selects us, in the round it selects us, where seven years of development fit before its entry into service. Entry into service no earlier than seven years from launch. We don't compress maturity for anyone. Partnership is our preference, not our need: the Joint Venture is open to Pratt & Whitney, and we commit the round they do. UltraFan widebody launches in the round a 787 or A350 Re-engine is announced, if it pays. Widebody is our core, and our Trent durability work continues. We won't do anything not profitable. I will say that categorically.
