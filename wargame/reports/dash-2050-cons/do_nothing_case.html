The Do Nothing case, broken down

Boeing launches nothing in 2030: no fps, no 787 Re-engine, no rate increase. Airbus launches NGSA in 2030 on the A320 Alt plan, in service 2037, as played. Nobody moves after 2030. Every value is measured against the status quo, in which nobody launches anything. Below: the market-share rules for Boeing and Airbus, the volumes they produce, the prices and margins, and how each maker's value is built.

Boeing loses nothing until 2047. In Do Nothing Boeing builds and sells exactly what it builds in the status quo, every year to 2047: the 737 MAX line is full (684 a year from 2035) in both. Your 1-point rule would cut Boeing's preference from 2037, but Airbus cannot build the share it wins, so its slack keeps Boeing's line full to 2047. From 2048 Airbus, 12% above its plan, can serve nearly all its customers, and Boeing's deliveries fall to 248 in 2060 (status quo 619). The profit gap is −$0.5B to −$1.4B a year, late and heavily discounted: −$0.83B in all.
The dashboard alone sees a much bigger loss. It sells 2,000 narrowbodies a year and moves Airbus 3 points a year from NGSA's entry into service, so Boeing falls to 20% (400 aircraft) from 2043, against 800 in the status quo. At $3.84M an aircraft that is −$1.54B a year from 2043, after a ramp from −$0.23B in 2037: −$3.64B in all.
Airbus's gain is NGSA's margin. NGSA earns $14.45M an aircraft against $6.83M for an A320neo, and Airbus builds 1,339 a year by 2050 against 1,127 in the status quo. From 2044 that is $10.7B-$12.2B a year more profit. Against it: the $35.52B NGSA bill, the transition dip (831 aircraft in 2038) and the Alt plan's cost. Airbus ends at +$14.09B, against +$45.82B on the dashboard alone, where it sells 1,600 NGSAs a year.

Market-share assumptions

Two kinds of share. Preference is the share of customers who want each maker's aircraft; the rules below set it. Delivered share is what each maker actually builds and sells, once demand, the firm orders and both makers' factories are applied. The game values deliveries.

How the value is built

Each maker's ΔPV is its narrowbody operating profit minus the status quo's, year by year, discounted to 2026, over its programme window, less any programme bill. Airbus counts 2026-2056, to 19 years after NGSA's entry into service. Boeing, with no new narrowbody, counts 2026-2060, to 19 years after the dashboard's default fps date (2041). The dashboard's line is its own 2,000-a-year view; the supply-and-demand line replaces it with what was actually built and sold.
