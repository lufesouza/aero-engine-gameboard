## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** fps is in service and our 737 customers are taking it, but we're turning it, I don't think it's turned: the financials will follow our production performance, and stability comes before rate. The 787 remains the most efficient airplane in its segment, we'll keep improving it with GE and Rolls-Royce, and we're confident we have an answer for anything our widebody customers need. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Our next-generation single-aisle is in service with engines from CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce on a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up, and we will keep growing its family and the A220 family for the customers who know them best. On widebodies the A350 family remains our reference while our Board continues its review of the market, with a commitment to shareholder returns while preserving financial flexibility.
- **CFM/GE:** Our new engine has been flying on both of our customers' new single-aisles since 2037, and the job now is the one that pays for decades: time on wing, shop visits and delivering to Boeing's and Airbus's real rates, with safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. RISE remains our next generation and we're still all in on the physics, but when it goes into service is really for our airframer and airline customers to decide, so we'll keep putting its technology into the engines they fly today. I won't speak to competition: we don't judge ourselves on a relative basis, and we'll keep telling you what we know and what we don't.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** We're controlling what we can control: our next-generation geared turbofan has been flying on NGSA since 2037, and time on wing is the name of the game, so we keep proving it in the fleet every day. Our commitment to a 50/50 narrowbody Joint Venture with Rolls-Royce stands. It follows the V2500 model: each partner owns its modules, and its technology, the gear and UltraFan alike, is credited and not transferred. That is a responsible bet with a responsible payback.
- **Rolls-Royce:** Our UltraFan narrowbody has been flying since 2037 and it is earning: that is a milestone on our journey, not our final destination. We said we would answer Pratt & Whitney, and our answer is that partnership needs to work for both sides: we are bringing a technology that is already in service, so we will not fold it into a 50/50 venture, though we remain open to partners on terms that credit it. On widebody we stay very aligned with Airbus, and we don't commit that easily: we will not spend on a new engine that no airframe needs.

Each partner designs, builds and owns its modules. The commitment stands until the Joint Venture forms [P-1233][P-1240][P-1676][P-1744].

Size the partner MRO network and the pacing parts for the first life-limited-part visits on the NGSA fleet, and keep fleet lift ahead of any new programme. Kits are priced, not given. Terms for compensation and negative engine margin stay private [PX-0084][PX-0118][PX-0123][PX-0100][PX-0113].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** RTX capital framework unchanged (CFO register) — Any Joint Venture share is funded inside the existing E&D and capex envelope and with partners. The dividend comes first and deleveraging continues. 'We do not want to starve the business.' [PX-0081][PX-0353][PX-0268][PX-0192]
- **Rolls-Royce:** Answer to Pratt & Whitney's Joint Venture offer: not on 50/50 terms — As promised in 2035, we answer P&W's standing commitment. UltraFan narrowbody has flown on NGSA since 2037, funded and matured by Rolls-Royce, and we will not fold an in-service programme into a 50/50 venture. We remain open to risk-and-revenue-sharing partners on modules, MRO and future derivatives, on terms that credit UltraFan's technology (RX-0072, RX-0118, R-1394, R-0318).
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan narrowbody fleet support and time on wing — Scale narrowbody TotalCare and MRO capacity with partner shops as the NGSA fleet grows, as in the Singapore MRO Joint Venture. Durability fixes go in at natural shop visits, not through recalls. Keeping the engines flying and earning comes first (RX-0283, RX-0287, RX-0289, R-1212).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Trent 1000 and Trent XWB time-on-wing programme continued; standard terms on Boeing's 787 improvement packages — Durability work on the Trent 1000, 7000 and XWB continues inside a flat R&D envelope. It is not the board's Trent 1000 upgrade. We will support Boeing's 787 performance packages on standard terms, with no price concessions for 787 share (R-1601, RX-0214, R-0998, R-0367).
