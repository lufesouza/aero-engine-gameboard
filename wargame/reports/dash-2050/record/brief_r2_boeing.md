## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** The market, the technology and our balance sheet have come together, so we are launching our next single-aisle airplane, built for our 737 customers on a mature CFM engine, with entry into service planned for 2037. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast: this plan carries headroom, and we will take the 737 up another step only when our KPIs and our suppliers tell us we're ready. We're turning it, and the financials will follow our production performance.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. On that basis our Board has today approved the launch of our next-generation single-aisle, entering service around 2037 on matured technologies and a wing proven buildable at rate, with the engine competition open to CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce. We will keep a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up of the A320 family, and we will fund this preparation of the Airbus of the 30s with a commitment to shareholder returns while preserving financial flexibility.
- **CFM/GE:** We don't have a birthright on that next order, so CFM is committing a new advanced engine. It will be ready in 2036, in time for any new narrowbody launched now, and we will offer it alongside the LEAP to both of our airframer customers and to Embraer. RISE remains our next generation, and when it goes into service is really for our airframer and airline customers to decide; safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** We're controlling what we can control: the GTF fleet is getting the Advantage's durability back-ported, because time on wing is the name of the game. Our next-generation geared turbofan is a responsible bet with a responsible payback, mature gear technology ready six years from launch on standard terms, and we will commit it the moment an airframer selects it for its next single-aisle. We have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF: durability will be proven before entry into service.
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan is the most efficient engine architecture available and it scales from widebody to narrowbody, so Rolls-Royce is ready for the next single-aisle: in the round an airframer selects UltraFan, we launch it, with entry into service seven years from launch. But we need airframers to go there first, and we won't do anything that is not profitable; our preference is partnership, though we don't need partnership for capability. We don't commit that easily, but when we commit, we honour it.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** fps launch-customer campaign with 737 operators — Seek anchor commitments from the large 737 fleets, with commonality, transition and training support. Precedent: the MAX launch built on an anchor customer when share was at risk [B-0413, B-0422].
- **Boeing:** Engine readiness requirement — CFM is the baseline engine. The fps engine must be ready in 2036, a year ahead of EIS, with durability demonstrated before service entry [B-2304, B-2078, B-1597].
- **Boeing:** Early FAA certification-basis agreement for fps — Agree the certification basis and plan with the FAA at launch, in line with Ortberg's push for a faster, reformed process [BX-1309].
- **Airbus:** NGSA launch-customer campaign among A320 family operators — Offer conversion rights from existing A320neo backlog positions and sell cross-crew qualification and family commonality [A-0238, A-0241]. Customers keep their A320neo slots until NGSA arrives.
- **Airbus:** Keep improving the A320neo family to hold about 60% of the backlog until NGSA — A321LR/XLR range and continuous performance packages, the derivative-first doctrine [A-0200, A-0245]. A disciplined and controlled ramp, with no swing in rates.
- **Airbus:** Secure NGSA's industrial supply for our own ramp — Long-term agreements for composite wing capacity, forgings (Aubert & Duval) and titanium, plus long-range rate signals to suppliers [A-0441, A-0457, A-0458, A-0159, AX-0060]. Capacity is for Airbus's own programme only.
- **CFM/GE:** Offer the new CFM engine, with the LEAP derivative, to both Boeing (fps) and Airbus (NGSA) on standard risk-adjusted terms, with no launch pricing — Serve both airframers [CX-0587][CX-0185]; price for a fair risk-adjusted return [CX-0609]; launch pricing 'behind us' [CX-0930]. Narrowbody pricing is a joint decision with Safran [CX-0184]; consent assumed.
- **CFM/GE:** RISE open fan continues as a funded technology programme with no product launch until an airframer commits — Durability and dust testing, plus integration studies offered to both airframers [CX-0216][CX-0193]; no 'program dividend' [CX-0174]; entry date is the airframer's call [CX-0205].
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP durability programme: LEAP-1B kit rolled out at natural shop visits, LEAP-1A held at CFM56 levels — Targets of 8,000 cycles in harsh and 17,000 in neutral conditions [CX-0655][CX-0996]; retrofits take 'years, not months' [CX-0649].
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP-1B output paced to Boeing's actual 737 rate, supporting any rate increase — 'a backlog that we share' [CX-0197]; pace shipments to Boeing's real rate and inventory [CX-0206]. Rate commitments are made jointly with Safran [CX-0160].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF fleet durability: back-port GTF Advantage / Hot Section Plus into the A320neo fleet and expand shop-visit capacity with partner MRO network — Fix-forward on the installed base, kit priced not given; fleet lift and bending the AOG curve come before any new engine [PX-0106][PX-0100][PX-0080][PX-0118][P-1363].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Conditional GTF2 offer to Airbus (NGSA) and Boeing (fps) — Evolved geared architecture (composite fan, CMC hot section, new gear), ready six years from launch, standard terms with pass-through pricing; P&W commits in the round an airframer names it [PX-0067][PX-0117][PX-0055].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** RTX capital framework unchanged (CFO register) — Dividend first, continued deleveraging; any GTF2 programme funded inside the existing E&D and capex envelope with our risk-sharing partners. 'We do not want to starve the business' [PX-0192][PX-0081][PX-0353][PX-0268].
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan narrowbody offer to both airframers — UltraFan is offered for fps and NGSA on standard terms with TotalCare. We launch it in the round an airframer selects it, and entry into service is no earlier than launch plus 7 years. The self-funded narrowbody demonstrator continues so that we are ready when an airframer moves (R-1017, R-1024, RX-0094).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Partnership open, not a precondition — We are open to a risk-and-revenue-sharing partner, P&W included, on terms that credit UltraFan's technology. No unilateral Joint Venture commitment until a partner's commitment is on the public record (R-1010, R-1022, R-1025, RX-0118).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Time-on-wing programme continued — Continue the existing GBP 1bn, four-year durability programme on the Trent 1000, 7000 and XWB inside the flat R&D envelope. This is not the board's Trent 1000 upgrade package (R-1601, RX-0214).
