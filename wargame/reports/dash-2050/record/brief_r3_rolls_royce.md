## Public statements (last round)

- **Boeing:** fps is on plan for entry into service in 2037, and we're not done until we're done: we will finish certification with the FAA before we build at rate, on a CFM engine that proves its durability first. It is so much more important that we do this right than fast. The 787 remains the most efficient airplane in its segment, and we're confident we have an answer for anything our widebody customers need.
- **Airbus:** Our core principles of safety, quality, integrity, compliance and security will remain at the heart of all we do. Our next-generation single-aisle is on track to enter service around 2037 with engines from CFM, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce, and its industrial supply is secured for a disciplined and controlled production ramp-up; until it arrives we will keep improving the A320 family for our customers. On widebodies the A350 family remains our reference, and we continue to prepare the Airbus of the 30s with a commitment to shareholder returns while preserving financial flexibility.
- **CFM/GE:** Both of our airframer customers have launched, and CFM will be there for both: the new engine is on track to be ready in 2036, a year ahead of entry into service, and we'll call it ready when the testing tells us it is, not before. We don't have a birthright on any of these campaigns, so we'll keep earning them on durability and time on wing: safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order. RISE remains our next generation, and when it goes into service is really for our airframer and airline customers to decide.
- **Pratt & Whitney:** We're controlling what we can control: our next-generation geared turbofan is on track to be ready in 2036, a year ahead of NGSA's entry into service, and because we have learned our lesson from the prior iterations of the GTF, time on wing and full-life parts will be proven before it carries a passenger. We have put on the record our commitment to a 50/50 narrowbody Joint Venture with Rolls-Royce, to spread risk and investment as we did on the V2500, with each partner keeping its own technology, and the same engine is available to Boeing on standard terms. These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks.
- **Rolls-Royce:** When Airbus selected UltraFan for NGSA we committed, and when we commit something, we honour it: the engine will be ready for entry into service in 2037, tested to maturity and with its support in place first. That is a milestone on our journey, not our final destination. We remain open to partnership and to any airframer that wants UltraFan, but we don't commit that easily and we won't do anything that is not profitable.

## Other moves made public

- **Boeing:** fps certification before rate, with the FAA — fps stays on plan for service entry in 2037. Certification is completed with the FAA before the production ramp, so production and certification never run concurrently. The CFM engine must show durability before service entry; its 2036 readiness gives the one-year buffer we required [BX-1263, BX-1309, B-2304].
- **Boeing:** Continuous 787 improvement instead of a Re-engine — Performance and product packages with GE and Rolls-Royce keep the 787 the most efficient airplane in its segment. Price and share are pressed where Airbus has no new answer. Precedents: incremental 737NG gains before re-engining, and the 787 as the answer to the A330neo [B-0286, B-0634, B-0934, B-0342, B-0858].
- **Airbus:** NGSA launch-customer campaign among A320 family operators, continued — Convert A320neo backlog positions into NGSA delivery slots from 2037, selling cross-crew qualification and family commonality [A-0238, A-0241]. Customers keep their A320neo slots until NGSA arrives. The aim is to win back the share the 737 rate step took.
- **Airbus:** Keep improving the A320neo family until NGSA enters service — Continue A321LR/XLR range and performance packages under the derivative-first doctrine [A-0200, A-0245]. Hold a disciplined and controlled ramp with no swing in rates, and do not react to Boeing's 737 rate [AX-0010, A-0039].
- **Airbus:** Secure NGSA's industrial supply for EIS and the ramp — Extend the long-term agreements for composite wing capacity, forgings (Aubert & Duval) and titanium, and give suppliers long-range rate signals for the NGSA ramp [A-0457, A-0458, A-0159, AX-0060].
- **Airbus:** A350 family: compete on availability and value; widebody review continues — No re-engine decision this year. Sell the A350 and A350F on availability and price against the 787, as with the A330 bridge [A-0003, A-0091]. The Board's widebody market review continues [A-0302, AX-0019]. We re-test the A350 Re-engine in 2045 if the 787 has not been re-engined.
- **CFM/GE:** New CFM engine on track to be ready in 2036 for both fps and NGSA, with durability shown in test before entry into service — Ready at launch + 6, a year ahead of both 2037 entries, which meets Boeing's stated requirement of an engine ready in 2036. The date is not called ready without 'turn on and turn off' test evidence [CX-0009][CX-0008], and no earlier date is claimed.
- **CFM/GE:** Standard risk-adjusted terms continue for Boeing, Airbus and Embraer, with no launch pricing — A fair risk-adjusted return 'regardless of what our competitors may do' [CX-0609]; launch pricing 'behind us' [CX-0930]; price/cost positive [CX-0929]. Narrowbody pricing is a joint decision with Safran [CX-0184]; consent assumed.
- **CFM/GE:** RISE continues as a funded joint technology programme, with its technologies going into the new engine and LEAP now; no product launch until an airframer commits — 'do we have to wait until RISE ... the answer is no' [CX-0162]; no 'program dividend' [CX-0174]; the entry date is the airframer's call [CX-0205]; joint with Safran [CX-0147][CX-0166].
- **CFM/GE:** LEAP-1B paced to Boeing's actual 737 rate; LEAP durability kits fitted at natural shop visits; CFM shop capacity readied for the new engine — 'a backlog that we share' [CX-0197]; shipments paced to Boeing's real rate [CX-0206]; retrofits take 'years, not months' [CX-0649]; about 70% of LEAP shop visits done by CFM, split equally with Safran [CX-0219].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF2 durability demonstration before readiness — Endurance testing in severe environments at 2-3x the earlier programme, with full-life parts at entry into service. Durability is shown in 2036, a year ahead of NGSA's 2037 entry into service. Fleet statistics are published; terms for compensation and negative engine margin stay private [PX-0128][PX-0073][P-0779][PX-0113].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF2 offered to Boeing for fps as a second source — The engine is already launched and ready in 2036, a year ahead of fps EIS, which meets Boeing's stated readiness requirement. Standard terms with pass-through pricing and no launch-style discounts. A second source gives Boeing castings and supply resilience at rate. We do not speak for Boeing's choice [P-1867][PX-0117][PX-0055][P-1292][PX-0062].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** Joint Venture terms offered to Rolls-Royce — A 50/50 risk-and-revenue-sharing narrowbody Joint Venture on the IAE and Engine Alliance model. Each partner designs, builds and owns its modules. P&W's fan-drive gear system and hot section remain P&W technology, credited to the Joint Venture but not transferred. Our commitment is on the record now; it stands until the Joint Venture forms or we withdraw it [P-1233][P-1676][P-1744][P-1875].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** GTF fleet: continue the Advantage back-port and MRO network expansion — Fleet lift and bending the AOG curve come before any new engine. The kit is priced, not given [PX-0106][PX-0118][PX-0123][PX-0100].
- **Pratt & Whitney:** RTX capital framework unchanged (CFO register) — GTF2 and any Joint Venture share are funded within the existing E&D and capex envelope, with partners. The dividend comes first and deleveraging continues. 'We do not want to starve the business.' [PX-0192][PX-0081][PX-0353][PX-0268]
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan narrowbody readiness for NGSA — Testing is paced to readiness in 2037 (launch plus 7 years), with durability shown before entry into service. No earlier date is offered (RX-0296, RX-0284, R-1476).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Narrowbody support network built before entry into service — Build narrowbody TotalCare and MRO capacity with partner shops before 2037, as in the Singapore MRO Joint Venture, because we have no narrowbody MRO scale today (RX-0283, RX-0287, RX-0279).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Partnership open, no unilateral commitment — If P&W puts a Joint Venture commitment on the public record, we will answer it in the next round on terms that credit UltraFan's technology (R-1010, R-1022, RX-0118).
- **Rolls-Royce:** UltraFan offer to Boeing for fps stands — Standard terms, ready 2037 to match fps entry into service. We will not promise 2036 (R-1476, R-0970).
- **Rolls-Royce:** Time-on-wing programme continued — The durability programme on the Trent 1000, 7000 and XWB continues inside a flat R&D envelope (R-1601, RX-0214).
