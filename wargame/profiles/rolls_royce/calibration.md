# Rolls-Royce (supplier `rolls_royce`): reconciled calibration

## Basis

**Money.** All values are in $B (or $M per engine), in constant 2026 dollars, as PV to 2026 at RR's WACC of 10%.

**FX.** GBP figures are converted at **1.36 $/GBP**, MS's 2026e effective hedged GBP:USD rate (R-1672). The evidence brackets the rate between:
- 1.34: MS 2030e, GBP 8.60M = $11.52M per engine (R-1657);
- 1.48: 2024, GBP 7.748M = $11.47M per engine (R-0125), and MS's aftermarket conversion rate (R-0203).

Aftermarket economics are built directly in USD (hours x $/EFH from the MS model), so they need no conversion. GBP costs from past years are uplifted to 2026 at MS's index (2% a year, 3% in 2023, R-1658).

**Sources.** Evidence is from `wargame/profiles/rolls_royce/evidence.jsonl` only. `wargame/model.py` and the `years` and `suppliers.rolls_royce` blocks of `config/default.json` were read only to confirm the mechanics. No other company profile was read.

**Inputs reconciled.** Two independent calibrations: A (bottom-up) and B (top-down). Where they differed by more than 25%, the cited cells and quotes were re-read. The working scripts were session-only. Every derivation is in the parameter table, with its evidence ids.

### Incumbent widebody value build ($M per installed engine, 2026 $)

1. **Aftermarket gross profit per engine-year, 2025e.** MS hours x $/EFH x (1 - 25% RRSP) x 44.47% underlying margin (R-1623-R-1626, R-1639):

   | engine | 2025e $M per engine-year |
   |---|---|
   | XWB-84 | 0.683 |
   | XWB-97 | 0.832 |
   | Trent 7000 | 0.509 |
   | Trent 1000 | 0.515 |

   Check: XWB-84 $2,217M of cash revenue / 1,082 engines x 0.75 x 0.4447 = 0.683.
2. **2026 level.** x1.03 yield escalator (R-1631) x (47.9% / 44.47%), the margin interpolated between 2025e and 2027e (R-1637). Factor 1.109.
3. **Mix.** The game's status quo is 140 Airbus engines a year (MS 2030e installed mix 166 / 56 / 78 XWB-84 / XWB-97 / T7000, R-1687) plus 44 Trent 1000s (fit 0.22). Result: $0.699M per engine-year.
4. **Life.** 25 years (R-1371, R-1417, R-0630) at 10%: annuity 9.077. RR captures about 90% of lifetime service value (R-1549): MS's 100% LTSA coverage describes young fleets (R-1639), and older engines leave LTSA.
   - Aftermarket PV = 0.699 x 9.077 x 0.9 = **$5.71M**.
   - A combined two haircuts (aging hours and capture, 0.87) on 2025e economics. B used 2026e economics with no capture (0.98). This calibration uses the 2026 base and RR's own capture figure.
5. **OE including spares.**
   - On-wing loss: -GBP 0.5M in 2026e-2030e (R-1653).
   - Spares: 0.14 per installed engine (0.133 in 2019, R-0122/R-0225; 0.143 in 2030e, R-1654/R-1686) x GBP 12.6M profit per spare. GBP 12.6M is the mean of 2026e (GBP 18.3M revenue x about 64% margin = 11.7) and 2030e deflated (707.6/48.4/1.02^4 = 13.5) (R-0121, R-1654).
   - GBP 1.26M x 1.36 = **$1.72M**.
   - RR's OE metric and MS's large-engine OE line both include spares (R-0843, R-0118, R-1652).
6. **Total: $7.43M -> 7.4.**
   - Without spares the value would be $5.0M. That is the comparable figure to the live 4.0, so the main change is the decision to count spares.
   - Low 5.0: 2025e economics, aging hours, capture 0.9, 2024 on-wing -1.0, 2019 spare attach and margin (5.4-5.6). A further allowance covers the Civil cost of capital (R-1464) and the 20-year LTSA life (R-0757).
   - High 9.5: 2030e margin of 53.2% (R-1637), full capture, OE at 2030e (about 9.0), with room for the 1.48 aftermarket FX (R-0203).

## Scale check

**What the game books.** The game's status quo gives RR 170 widebodies x 2 x (0.588 x 0.22 + 0.412 x 1.0) = **184 installed widebody engines a year** (140 Airbus, 44 Boeing) and no narrowbody engines.
- At $7.4M per engine that is **$1.37B a year** of lifecycle value.
- PV 2026-2060 at 10% is about **$14.5B**.
- The live parameters gave 173 engines x $4.0M = $0.69B a year.

**What the evidence implies.**

| year | installed engines | basis | lifecycle value at $7.4M | game volume as share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 221 | 278 deliveries - 57 spares (R-0225, R-0121) | $1.6B a year | 83% |
| 2026e | about 260 | 312 - about 52 (R-1686, R-1654) | $1.9B a year | 71% |
| 2030e | 339 | 387 - 48 | $2.5B a year | 54% |

The gap comes from **volume, not value per engine**:
- The game's Airbus widebody line is 70 aircraft a year, against MS 2030e of 150 (83 A350-900 + 28 A350-1000 + 39 A330neo, R-1687).
- Its Boeing line is 100, against 120 787s (R-1661).
- Per-engine values were not inflated to compensate.

**Per-engine values against MS division totals.**
- **OE:** 339 x $1.72M = $0.58B, against MS 2030e large-engine OE gross profit of GBP 538M = $0.72B nominal, about $0.67B in 2026 $ (R-1652). Ours is slightly lower because spares are taken at a blended margin.
- **Aftermarket:** $0.70M per engine-year before capture, against MS 2030e of GBP 4,473M / 6,288 in-service engines = $0.95M nominal, about $0.88M in 2026 $ (R-1637, R-1644, R-1670). MS's path embeds a 53% margin and real yield growth that we do not count, so our values sit at or below MS's trajectory.
- **RR's 2018 template:** OE -GBP 1.6M plus about GBP 5M of aftermarket per engine over 25 years (R-1461). Restated at today's halved OE loss and roughly doubled aftermarket profit per engine (R-1644), it lands in the same $7-8M zone once spares are counted.

**Stakes in the game.**
- **A350 re-engine on a non-RR engine:** removes about 140 x $7.4M = **$1.0B a year** of booked value.
- **Trent 1000 upgrade (+10pp):** adds 20 engines x $7.4M = $0.15B a year, plus $0.13B a year of installed-base saving.
- **A sole-source NGSA on UltraFan:** 2,400 narrowbody engines a year x $3.1M = **about $7.4B a year** at maturity (fps sole-source: 1,600 x 3.1 = $5.0B). That is roughly 5x RR's whole status-quo widebody value in the game, against $7.5B x 1.6 of alpha-loaded capex. It is far above anything in RR's history (peak of 510 large engines in 2019, R-0225). Narrowbody selection outcomes therefore dominate RR's payoff whenever an airframer can pick UltraFan.

## Open issues

1. **Spares are inside value per engine.** Spares contribute about $2.4M (0.14 x GBP 12.6M x 1.36) to every widebody value. Both A and B count them, because RR's OE metric and MS's OE line include spares and spare demand scales with the installed fleet (R-0843, R-0118, R-1652). Without spares the values would be incumbent 5.0, UltraFan widebody 5.8 and UltraFan narrowbody about 2.2, close to the live 4.0 / 4.5 / 1.6. This choice explains most of the change from the live values.
2. **Gross contribution, not EBIT.** Civil EBIT is only about 57-59% of Civil standard gross profit (2024: 1,505 / 2,649; R-0298, R-0119, R-1652). Loading the non-R&D overhead onto the aftermarket would cut the incumbent value to about $5.7M. The parameters follow the definition (OE margin + PV of aftermarket profit).
3. **Nominal WACC on constant-$ flows.** Flows are in constant 2026 $ but are discounted at a nominal 10%, as for the airframers. Real-rate discounting (about 8%) would raise values by about 15-20%. Post-2026 real yield and margin growth in MS (R-1631, R-1637) is not counted either. Both choices keep the values conservative.
4. **One UltraFan widebody value for two airframes.** The engine is worth about $8.2M on an A350-class airframe but about $6.4M on a 787-class re-engine (Trent 1000-class yields). The 8.0 assumes mostly A350 use. If the 787 re-engine on UltraFan matters in play, the game would need a per-airframe value.
5. **Aggressive terms are one number across segments.** 1.2pp and 0.86 are sized on the widebody. The same value fraction on a narrowbody would be 1.5pp, so narrowbody airframers get slightly too little margin from aggressive RR terms.
6. **uf_nb capex: the industrialisation half rests on proxies.** About $4-5B of the 7.5 comes from GE's $3.5bn plant capex (R-1910 covers all GE sites) or RR's 2016-18 capex bulge scaled by value. The development half is RR-evidenced. Treat 7.5 as calibrated to plus or minus $2B.
7. **The Trent 1000 fix already exists.** The HPT blade (BoM B) was certified in June 2025 and BoM C followed by end-2025 (R-1023, R-1306). The `t1000_upgrade` lever therefore represents a further share-recovery package. Its saving partly overlaps MS's margin path (R-1681), and the 0.13 leans to the lower half of the range for that reason.
8. **A350 exclusivity ends in 2030.** The A350-900 could be dual-sourced from 2031 (R-0360, R-0991). The game holds an Airbus widebody fit of 1.0 until an A350 re-engine enters service, so a dual-source erosion without a re-engine is not modelled.
9. **alpha and WACC trade off.** The 0.6 loading reproduces RR's 15% hurdle at a 10% WACC. If a modeller prefers the Civil 12.5% WACC (R-1464), alpha should drop to about 0.3.
