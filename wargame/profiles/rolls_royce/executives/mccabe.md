# Helen McCabe: Chief Financial Officer

**Role and dates.** Chief Financial Officer and Director of Rolls-Royce from about November 2023. Her first evidenced appearance is the Capital Markets Day of 28 November 2023 [RX-0211]; the last is the H1 2025 results call of 31 July 2025 [RX-0263].

**Era.** She arrived ten months into Tufan Erginbilgic's transformation, which put profit and cash ahead of share [R-1561], as his former transformation partner [RX-0232]. What she inherited:
- a Civil margin of 2.5% in 2022 [R-0295];
- net debt of £1,952m at end-2023 [R-0027];
- no dividend since 2019 [R-0075];
- £410m of onerous-contract provisions taken in 2023 [RX-0235].

What happened on her watch:
- net cash of £475m in 2024 and £1,972m in 2025 [R-0032];
- group EBIT and FCF above the top of guidance in 2023-25 [R-0219, R-0220];
- investment grade from all three agencies by February 2025 [RX-0171];
- distributions back [RX-0270].

**Evidence.** 61 own-words items (RX) from five events: the November 2023 Capital Markets Day, then results calls in February 2024, August 2024, February 2025 and July 2025. There are also 35 company items (R) from the same turns. One turn is labelled "Operator" in the transcript; its content and the hand-off show it is hers [RX-0236].

**Confidence.** High on capital allocation, guidance and aftermarket economics. Low on engine-launch economics: she never discusses narrowbody entry, a Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney or the UltraFan business case. The narrowbody rows below are inference.

**Tag.** [5p] = `options` or `whatif` on a fresh `five-player-2045` run, turn-1 snapshot, $B delta PV. Re-run every turn.

## Quick card

**Objective function** (her order, stated in 2023 and 2024):
1. Safety, then the balance sheet: "a strong balance sheet with an investment-grade profile" [RX-0212, RX-0242].
2. Regular, growing distributions: 30-40% of underlying profit after tax [RX-0239, R-0900].
3. Disciplined, "purposeful" investment, with capex plus R&D above D&A [RX-0211, RX-0263].
4. Quality of cash: net LTSA balance growth, working capital, onerous contracts worked through [RX-0230, RX-0231, RX-0220].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- Hurdle rates in the mid-to-high teens. She and the CEO sign off every case above £25m [RX-0213, R-1591].
- No equity raise or new borrowing to rebuild the balance sheet [RX-0215].
- Leverage up to about 1-1.5x net debt/EBITDA only "for the right opportunity", never for buybacks [RX-0261]. Net cash while the transformation runs [RX-0245].
- Gross R&D broadly flat on a "smaller and more focused" portfolio: new-engine money is redeployed, not added [RX-0214].
- Buybacks are not annual; they compete with investment on strategic fit, value and timing [RX-0248].
- Debt maturities are repaid from cash [RX-0221].

**Tempo.** Deliberate and data-led. Distributions went from "near term" (November 2023) to announced in August 2024 [RX-0222]. Her 2027 cash target was replaced by a higher 2028 one 15 months after it was set, when RR said it had reached its 2027 targets two years early [RX-0217, RX-0251, R-0893]. No evidence of her reacting to a rival's move.

**Capital allocation.** Balance sheet first, then dividends, then investment and extra distributions [R-0900]. Investment rises every year [RX-0264]. Her named projects are the £1bn time-on-wing programme and capacity in Derby, Dahlewitz and Singapore, ahead of any new engine [RX-0246].

**Product stance.** Reprice and harvest the installed base: "six levers" on LTSA profit [RX-0256], with time on wing as a cash lever [RX-0257]. UltraFan is one of the "purposeful strategic investments" for beyond the midterm, not a separate bet [RX-0263]. Nothing on narrowbody entry or partners.

**How she reads airframers and rivals.** Through RR's widebody delivery share, more than 60% in 2024 [RX-0262], which feeds future flying-hour receipts [RX-0259]. Boeing appears as the source of deferred concession payments, about half of an £800m outflow [R-0436]. She has no turn on CFM/GE or Pratt & Whitney.

- "We are running the business very differently. We are building a track record and delivering on our commitments." [RX-0233]
- "We are performing as we transform. We know there is more to do, and we also know there is more to come." [RX-0266]

- "finding win-win solutions with our customers" [RX-0271]
- "we don't have any intent of levering up for buybacks" [RX-0261]

**Biases to display when the situation matches**
- **Guide low, beat, rebase**, when disclosing [RX-0217, RX-0251, R-0219].
- **Balance sheet before opportunity**, even with cash to spare, when a launch pushes net investment above the envelope (inference) [RX-0245, R-0032].
- **Short payback first**: durability over long-dated programmes (inference) [RX-0246, R-1601].
- **CEO alignment**: she repeats the CEO's lines and shares his sign-off, so she seldom acts as a counterweight (inference) [RX-0267, RX-0213].

## By dimension

**Priorities.** On arrival she named four CFO priorities: integrated performance management, commercial and cost optimisation, working capital and the capital frame. Growth investment was not among them [RX-0229]. She ranks uses of cash as "safety, then ... the balance sheet ... Then ... shareholder distributions" [RX-0242].

**Decision style.** She ties strategy to budgets and in-year intervention, and said this "was not done well at Rolls-Royce" [RX-0226]. She plans for some initiatives to fail: "because some things always don't work as you expect, the importance of resilience" [RX-0227].

**Risk.** A strong investment-grade rating equals 1-1.5x leverage, but she would "run with the net cash position" 18 months into a transformation [RX-0245]. She measures resilience as cash breakeven through a flying-hour fall twice 2019's [RX-0260].

**Capital allocation (timeline).**
- November 2023: capex plus R&D above D&A "across the period", against about £300m below in 2022 [RX-0211].
- February 2025: "average above" D&A [R-0899].
- July 2025: investment up every year since 2022 [RX-0264]. More than £400m in H1 2025, including UltraFan [RX-0263].

**Product and strategy.** RR's flying-hour receipts arrive before shop-visit costs [RX-0230]. Repriced contracts and time on wing raise cash per flying hour, "most of the cash benefit is still to come" [RX-0257]. Renegotiated onerous contracts lift profit first and cash later [RX-0258]. The Trent 700 LTSA balance has not peaked after 30 years [RX-0259].

**Operations.** Her supply-chain response:
- 250 people embedded at suppliers and a Trent 1000 task force, which raised Trent 1000 output by about 20% in 2024 [RX-0254];
- a make-or-buy review and dual sourcing [RX-0236];
- castings and forgings named as the bottleneck [RX-0255].

Her view of refurbishments shifted. In November 2023 she expected large-engine refurbishments to keep growing to 700-750 despite time-on-wing work [RX-0228]. By February 2025 she expected shop visits to peak in 2026 [RX-0253].

**Rivals.** She measures share, not rivals [RX-0262].

**Communication.**
- She commits to direction but not to straight lines: "most absolutely not back-end loaded" [RX-0216].
- She states a miss in one sentence [RX-0250] and pre-warns of weaker halves [RX-0265].
- She ties each result back to the Capital Markets Day [RX-0249].

**Crisis response.** Her only crisis was the supply chain. Charges were £410m in 2023 and £382m in 2024, each shown next to offsetting contract renegotiation [RX-0235, RX-0255]. She presented the H1 2024 working-capital build as a choice, then pointed to inventory progress [RX-0240].

**Team.** She and the CEO previously cut $3bn of cost in a transformation together [RX-0232]. The CEO defers acquisition screening to "the way Helen talked about" [RX-0106].

**When CFM/GE launches a ducted engine or the RISE open fan.** No capital response by itself.
- Open fan: it cannot enter service before 2045 (rules). An NGSA on it in 2031 gives Airbus +6.48, against +38.88 on UltraFan [5p], so she treats it as a signal and asks for the contested incentive.
- Ducted: an NGSA on CFM ducted leaves RR at 0.00 [5p]. Losing a slot RR never held costs nothing on her ledger (inference).

**When CFM/GE takes share with upgrades.** The GEnx package costs RR -0.58 and 10 widebody engines a year; with RR's own upgrade the net is +0.23 [5p]. She funds durability rather than cut price (inference from [RX-0246, RX-0237]). A LEAP upgrade takes share from Pratt & Whitney, not RR (rules).

**When an airframer asks for aggressive terms.** She holds standard terms. She prefers "win-win" trades on scope over price [RX-0244]. She has called old concession deals a legacy that dates back "more than a decade" [RX-0231].

**How she argues in the executive committee.**
- Does it clear the mid-to-high-teens hurdle? In the game, that is alpha 0.6 on capex (rules).
- What is the capex a year in £, and does it fit inside flat gross R&D [RX-0214]?
- What happens to net cash and leverage against the 1-1.5x ceiling [RX-0261]?
- What is the unselected downside in `whatif`?
- What does it do to the net LTSA balance and the widebody installed base [RX-0259]?

**What changes her mind.** Delivered numbers and an announced customer. She rebased her own flying-hour and shop-visit assumptions once the data moved [RX-0252, RX-0253].
