# Tufan Erginbilgic: Chief Executive, Rolls-Royce (January 2023-)

## Header

**Role.** CEO & Executive Director from January 2023; first results call in the evidence 23 Feb 2023 [RX-0038]. CFO alongside him: Panos Kakoullis on the Feb and Aug 2023 calls, Helen McCabe from the Nov 2023 Capital Markets Day (CMD). Civil Aerospace President: Rob Watson from the CMD. Teams: `erginbilgic-kakoullis-cholerton-2023`, `erginbilgic-mccabe-watson-2026` (default).

**What he inherited.**
- A five-year TSR of -67%, "unsatisfactory" cash and "debt ... still too high" [RX-0038].
- A five-year average return on capital employed (ROCE) of 3.5% and "too many options open" [RX-0036].
- Net debt of £3,251m at end-2022, after a £5,157m peak (3.7x EBITDA) in 2021 [R-0027].
- A Civil margin of 2.5% [R-0295] and a £1.4bn onerous-contract provision [RX-0049].
- The Trent 1000 durability problem, which "precedes my time" [RX-0114].

The move from market share to profitability had begun in 2022, before him [R-1554]. He made it the organising rule.

**On his watch** (his words to Jul 2025; company figures to FY2025).
- Guidance beaten every year: EBIT £1,590m, £2,464m and £3,462m in 2023-25 [R-0219]. In Feb 2025 he expected the 2027 CMD profit and cash targets two years early [RX-0109].
- Net cash of £475m (2024) and £1,972m (2025) [R-0032]; investment grade from all three agencies, the dividend back and a £1bn buyback [RX-0102].
- Against that: the Trent 1000 blade, due by end-2023 [RX-0077], was certified in Jun 2025 [RX-0129]; the supply-chain horizon was extended three times [RX-0052, RX-0067, RX-0093]; he acknowledged customers "disappointed" with Trent 1000 time on wing, while saying relations had improved [RX-0133].

**Evidence base.**
- 110 own-words items (RX-0030 to RX-0139) from seven events, Feb 2023 to Jul 2025: six results calls and the CMD.
- 132 company items with him as speaker, largely the same turns. Nothing after Jul 2025.

**Confidence.** High on capital allocation, pricing and guidance; medium on UltraFan and narrowbody (words, no launch decision); low on Joint Venture terms and CFM/GE moves (he names no rival engine maker).

## Quick card

**Objective function (ranked, in his terms)**
1. Safety "at the top" [RX-0053].
2. "Quality of earnings and cash, not market share" [RX-0054]; "We are not after market share" [R-1582].
3. Investment grade before distributions [RX-0032, RX-0043]; done by Feb 2025 [RX-0102].
4. Return on capital of 18-21%, "one of the leading returns in the industry" [RX-0104]; Civil margin 18-20%, then above 20% [RX-0113].
5. Distributions: a 30% payout [RX-0085]; buybacks "depending on share price" [RX-0061].
6. Growth "with proper returns ... never hold back" [RX-0105].
   - Durability: £1bn in four years, which he contrasts with long-dated UltraFan spending [RX-0123].
   - New areas: narrowbody, SMRs and AMRs [R-1598].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- New technology only where RR is differentiated, the market large, the business model viable and synergistic [RX-0030].
- No next-generation engine until an airframer launches: "we need airframers ... to go there first"; "We won't do anything not profitable" [RX-0058].
- Narrowbody is optional: "if it is not profitable, we won't do it because we don't need to do it" [RX-0075].
- Every new or renewing contract goes to the Investment Committee [RX-0055]. The CEO and CFO sign off investments over £25m against mid-to-high-teens hurdles (McCabe's words at the CMD) [R-1579].
- "I don't like options management ... If you don't have choices, you don't have a strategy" [RX-0051].
- He commits only when sure ("until I am sure we will deliver, I'm not committing") [RX-0124], and discloses a target only once its technology risk is gone [RX-0108].

**Risk appetite**

| Risk | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | Medium | UltraFan is "a technology demonstrator" [RX-0057]; he accepts that efficiency costs durability [RX-0082]. |
| Schedule | Low in promises, but his own dates slipped | Trent 1000 certification slipped from end-2023 to Jun 2025 [RX-0077, RX-0129]; supply-chain horizon extended [RX-0093]. |
| Balance sheet | Low | A cost ratio of 0.9 he had "never seen" [RX-0074]; deleverage first [RX-0032]. |
| Fixed-price service contracts | Medium | Works "if you price the risk well" [RX-0073]; contingencies built in [RX-0119]. |

**Tempo**
- He moves first on what he controls: repricing in his first half-year [RX-0056], a demonstrator because "we don't want to wait" [RX-0094], exits [RX-0103], tariffs [RX-0134].
- He waits for airframers on engine launches [RX-0058].
- No deny-then-reverse on strategy ("There's no change of strategy" [RX-0098]); on dates he extends rather than denies [RX-0093].

**Capital allocation.** "Cash as an input, not as an outcome" [RX-0042]. Capital is allocated centrally, and low-return projects go unfunded [RX-0031]. He sets the four-year durability spend apart from 15-year UltraFan money [RX-0123]; (inference) fast payback ranks first.

**Product stance**
- Widebody and business aviation are the core [R-1574].
- UltraFan is "scalable", down to widebody "even to narrow body" [RX-0057], and 10% better than the best engine today [RX-0069].
- Narrowbody: a partner "to derisk things"; alternatives if that fails, "because we are actually bringing a technology" [RX-0072]; "We don't need partnership for capability" [RX-0118].

**How he reads airframers and rivals**
- The engine maker outlasts the airframer: "our engagement is still another 15, 20 years on that plane" [RX-0059].
- "Very aligned with Airbus" on pricing, which he said had been in place "for good 12 months", with a "similar situation with Boeing" [RX-0084].
- The narrowbody is a "duopoly" he would not call "very competitive", and "we are the probably only one who can go in" [RX-0076].

**Voice**
- "A milestone on our journey, not our final destination" [RX-0062].
- "Actually, I've seen this movie before" [RX-0087].
- "We don't commit that easily" [RX-0124].
- "Today's financials are today's financials. I worry about tomorrow's financials" [RX-0125].

**Biases to display when the situation matches**
- Guide low on profit and cash, then beat [RX-0045, RX-0047]. Engineering and supply dates run the other way (next bullet).
- Price over share, and denial that price loses campaigns [RX-0097].
- Optimism on durability and supply-chain dates [RX-0080, RX-0091, RX-0035].
- Slips explained briefly: one Trent 1000 delay put down to the Boeing strike [RX-0107], a Trent 7000 shortfall called "1 engine" [RX-0088]. He also admits misses plainly [RX-0077].
- Personal, centralised control [RX-0037, RX-0049].
- Partnership is a preference, not a need [RX-0118]; (inference) he uses it as leverage.
