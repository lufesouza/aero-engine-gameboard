# Rolls-Royce: 2026 analyst-model baseline

This note sets out what two sell-side company models say about Rolls-Royce. They are Morgan Stanley's model (17 Aug 2026; Overweight; price target 2,000p at a 1,541p share price [MS ImportantDisclosures]) and Goldman Sachs's model (published 17 Aug 2026; rating suspended [GS Cover Page]). No Capital IQ consensus was supplied for Rolls-Royce, so none is used. All money is in £ million on the underlying (not statutory) basis, for years to 31 December. 2025 is the last full actual year (A); 1H26 is actual; later years are analyst estimates (E). Morgan Stanley's forecasts stop at 2030 and Goldman's at 2031. Only Goldman's valuation runs on to 2036. The game's board numbers govern the payoff; this note is the real-world context. Tags name the bank, sheet and row (r); "derived" means a sum or ratio of the cited rows; "(inference)" marks judgement beyond the data.

- **The balance sheet is repaired.** Rolls-Royce had net cash every year from 2005A to 2014A (peak £1,939m in 2013A). It had net debt in 2015A-2017A (£111m-£305m) and again in 2019A-2023A, with a trough of -£5,157m in 2021A [MS BS r60]. It returned to net cash of £475m in 2024A, £1,895m in 2025A and £2,136m at 1H26 [MS BS r60, 1H26]. Caveat: the series includes leases from 2019 and uses the older accounting basis before 2017.
- **Forecast cash build.** MS has net cash of £16,330m by 2030E [MS BS r60]; GS has £12,734m in 2030E and £16,920m in 2031E [GS Balance Sheet r117].
- **The downside precedent.** When large-engine flying hours fell from 15.3m in 2019A to 6.6m in 2020A [MS Civil AM r52], free cash flow was -£4,185m in 2020A and -£1,442m in 2021A [MS CFS r111]. Civil trading cash flow alone was -£4,510m in 2020A [MS Civil r125].
- **Cash ahead.** Free cash flow adds up to about £29.6bn over 2026E-2030E in MS [derived: MS CFS r111] and £34.6bn over 2026E-2031E in GS [derived: GS Cash Flow Statement r85]. Free cash flow was £1,964m in 1H26 alone [MS CFS r111, 1H26].
- **Shareholder returns.** Buybacks were £1,008m and dividends £885m in 2025A [MS CFS r67, r72]. MS has £8.0bn of buybacks in 2026E-2028E (£2,500m, £2,750m, £2,750m) and none in 2029E-2030E [MS CFS r67]; GS adds £1,000m a year in 2029E-2031E [GS Cash Flow Statement r61]. MS dividends reach £2,222m in 2030E [MS CFS r72]. MS returns £16.1bn to shareholders over 2026E-2030E (£8.0bn buybacks plus £8.1bn dividends) [derived: MS CFS r67, r72].
- **Customer float.** Airlines pre-pay for TotalCare service contracts (long-term service agreements) before engines come in for overhaul. The Civil net liability for these was £10,397m in 2025A and MS forecasts £13,538m in 2030E [MS BS r85]. Its growth added £501m to cash in 2025A; MS forecasts £1,134m in 2030E [MS CFS r103]. This float exists because of the widebody fleet in service (inference).

- **Answer to the core question.** Civil earned 61.5% of group EBIT in 2025A [derived: MS Civil r14 / Divisional r70]. Widebody (OE losses, aftermarket and spares) earned 75.8% of Civil standard gross profit [derived: MS Civil r114 / r108]. Together that puts roughly 47% of group profit on widebody in 2025A and about 44% in 2030E [derived: MS Civil r14 / Divisional r70 × Civil r114 / r108] (inference: Civil EBIT is not split by business, so the gross-profit share stands in for it). Widebody is also about 39% of group revenue in both 2025A and 2030E [derived: MS Civil r72+r76 / Divisional r23].
- **The cow is the aftermarket.** Widebody services revenue was 2.5 times widebody OE revenue in 2025A [derived: MS Civil r76 / r72]. Across all of Civil, aftermarket standard gross profit was £2,869m against £514m from new engines, both before contract accounting adjustments and operations and engineering costs [MS Civil r84, r83]. GS puts the implied gross margin on long-term service contracts at 62.7% in 2025A, rising to 71.2% in 2030E [GS Civil Aerospace r221].
- **Civil margins have turned round.** The Civil margin was -50.0% in 2020A, 20.5% in 2025A and 25.3% in 1H26 (EBIT £1,567m on revenue £6,186m) [MS Civil r14, r15, r4, 1H26]. GS has Civil EBIT of £2,855m in 2026E and £4,546m in 2031E [GS Civil Aerospace r6].

Civil's share of group trading cash flow falls from 65.0% (2025A) to 52.6% (2030E) [derived proxy: MS Civil r125 / CFS r108]; Power Systems' growth is likely a main reason, but the files forecast no Power Systems cash flow (inference).

## Engines and supply

**What the files show.**
- **Sourcing position.** Rolls-Royce is the only engine on the A350 and the A330neo. It is one of two engines on the 787, where the Trent 1000 competes with GE's GEnx [game brief]. It is not the sole widebody engine overall. In 2025A, 219 of 259 Trent deliveries (85%, incl. spares) were for airframes with no rival engine; MS has 335 of 391 (86%) in 2030E [derived: MS Civil OE r82-r87]. Only the Trent 1000 (40 engines, 15% in 2025A) shares its airframe with a rival [derived: MS Civil OE r82 / r87].
- **No narrowbody engine.** GS shows 319-418 narrowbody engines a year in 2006A-2011A (V2500, through the IAE partnership), 220 in 2012A, and zero from 2013A to 2031E [GS Civil Aerospace r61]. V2500 aftermarket revenue falls from £299m (2025A) to £95m from 2028E as the IAE royalty ends [MS Civil r79].
- **Supply constraints.** Civil supply-chain charges were £382m in 2024A and £161m in 2025A. MS forecasts £175m in 2026E, £75m in 2027E and none from 2028E [MS Civil r19]. MS therefore assumes the problem is resolved by 2028E (inference).
- **Durability.** GS shows Trent 1000 costs of £431m (2018A), £578m (2019A), £524m (2020A), £350m (2021A), then falling to £25m in 2025A: about £2.2bn in total [GS Civil Aerospace r656; derived sum]. Trent 1000 deliveries fell from 126 (2019A) to 5 (2022A) [MS Civil OE r82]. The files do not split that fall between the pandemic and the durability problem.
- **Demand for service.** The company guides large-engine flying hours to 115-120% of 2019 in 2026 and 130-140% in 2028 [MS Civil AM bridge]. MS reaches 149% of 2019 by 2030E [derived: MS Civil AM r52].

**What the files do not show.** There are no capacity figures for castings, forgings or overhaul shops beyond the supply-chain charge. Shop-visit counts exist (MS Civil AM r59-63) but are not in this extract. Nothing shows Rolls-Royce's share of 787 engine orders.

- **R&D spend has fallen.** Company-funded R&D cash fell from £1,106m (2018A) to £603m (2025A) [MS IS r129]. MS assumes 2.5% of revenue from 2026E, about £3.7bn in total over 2026E-2030E [derived: MS IS r129]. Neither model has a line for UltraFan or a narrowbody programme.
- **New engines lose money for years.** Widebody new-engine losses came to about £3.0bn over 2017A-2025A [derived: MS Civil OE r52], starting at £710m in 2017A (about £1.6m per engine) [MS Civil OE r52-53]. MS has these losses only reaching zero in 2028E [MS Civil OE r52].
- **Durability is the second precedent.** About £2.2bn of Trent 1000 costs over 2018A-2025A [GS Civil Aerospace r656; derived sum].
- **Much of the money is already allocated.** Of MS free cash flow of about £29.6bn over 2026E-2030E, £16.1bn (about 54%) goes to shareholder returns [derived: MS CFS r111, r67, r72].
- **How the two banks value it.** GS puts enterprise value at £134.8bn and equity value at 1,730p a share, using an 8.46% discount rate (its cost of equity, with a 100% equity weight) and 3% long-term growth. About 64% of enterprise value (£85.9bn) is terminal value beyond 2036 [GS DCF Valuation D18-D48]. MS's 2028E Civil EBIT is £3,063m / £3,662m / £4,213m in its bear / base / bull cases (margins 21% / 24% / 26%), with price targets of 1,329p / 2,000p / 2,761p [MS Bull Bear Base r17, r18, r48]. The bear case is £599m below base on Civil EBIT [derived: MS Bull Bear Base r17].

## What this means for your decisions

- **Do Nothing is a strong default, not a passive one (inference).** Close to half of group profit rests on widebody [derived: MS Civil r14 / Divisional r70 × Civil r114 / r108], and 85% of Trent deliveries (incl. spares) in 2025A were for airframes where Rolls-Royce has no rival engine [derived: MS Civil OE r82-r87]. Protecting that position through any airframer Re-engine of the 787 or A350 comes first.
- **Widebody upgrades defend the one contested seat (inference).** The 787 is the only widebody where Rolls-Royce fights engine against engine, and it carries the £2.2bn durability history [GS Civil Aerospace r656; derived sum]. A Trent 1000 upgrade or an UltraFan widebody pays only once an airframer commits to a Re-engine. Without one, it buys nothing.
- **An UltraFan narrowbody Solo launch repeats the Trent ramp at bigger volume (inference).** The precedent is about £3.0bn of new-engine losses over nine years [derived: MS Civil OE r52] and £1.4m lost per engine in 2018A [GS Civil Aerospace r108]. Rolls-Royce also has little narrowbody aftermarket to fall back on: V2500 service revenue drops from £299m (2025A) to £95m from 2028E [MS Civil r79]. Launch against a firm airframer engine selection, not on spec.
- **The Joint Venture fits the R&D budget better (inference).** Company-funded R&D runs at only £0.6bn-£0.9bn a year to 2030E [MS IS r129]. Sharing the programme splits the R&D bill and the two risks the files show: years of new-engine losses and durability costs.
- **Do not stack projects in the 2030 round (inference).** Trent deliveries ramp from 259 to 391 [MS Civil OE r87] and flying hours from 17.0m to 22.8m [MS Civil AM r52] between 2025A and 2030E. MS has supply-chain charges ending only in 2028E [MS Civil r19]. A narrowbody launch on top of a widebody project would compete for the same engineers, suppliers and overhaul shops.
- **Read the airframers' narrowbody timing as your clock (inference).** If Boeing or Airbus use Delay Tactics, or launch a new narrowbody late, a later Rolls-Royce launch lands after the widebody ramp and after MS's £8.0bn buyback window (2026E-2028E) closes [MS CFS r67]; GS keeps buying back £1,000m a year in 2029E-2031E [GS Cash Flow Statement r61]. Neither bank models a Rolls-Royce narrowbody engine, and GS narrowbody units are zero to 2031E [GS Civil Aerospace r61], so an airframer selection would be real news to investors.

## What the models do not cover

- **No new programmes.** Neither model has an UltraFan, narrowbody or certification-cost line, and GS narrowbody units are zero to 2031E [GS Civil Aerospace r61]. R&D is split only by division.
- **Short horizon.** The forecasts end in 2030E (MS) and 2031E (GS). GS's DCF adjusted EBIT and sustainable free cash flow run to 2036E (£11,832m and £9,318m) [GS DCF Valuation r6, r13]. Nothing in the files covers the game years 2045-2060.
- **Civil EBIT is not split** between widebody and business aviation. The "share of group on widebody" above is therefore an inference from revenue and gross-profit splits.
- **No segment free cash flow.** Civil's share of cash uses trading cash flow as a proxy (derived).
- **Thin scenarios.** MS bear / base / bull cases cover 2028E only; GS has none. Neither has a widebody-volume shock or a narrowbody sensitivity.
- **Supply chain.** No parts or overhaul-capacity metrics beyond the MS supply-chain charge [MS Civil r19].
- **Valuation inputs differ.** The MS discount rate is not in the file. GS's rating is suspended, and its income statement uses a 1,150p share price [GS Income Statement r108] against MS's 1,541p [MS ImportantDisclosures].
- **Comparability.** Net cash before 2017 is on the older accounting basis and before 2019 excludes leases. The 2015-2016 Civil figures are on a historic basis. No Capital IQ consensus is in this pack.
