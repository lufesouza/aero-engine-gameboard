# Raymond L. (Ray) Conner: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.**
- President and CEO of Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA) from mid-2012: three months into the job in September 2012 [BX-0425].
- Also Vice Chairman of The Boeing Company by 2015 [B-1051, B-1165].
- Earlier he ran the 777 program in 2003 [B-0794], and in 2011 the high-rate production team for a new narrowbody [B-0403].
- From 2014, Muilenburg as COO oversaw development programs while Conner kept BCA profitability [BX-0830]. Muilenburg announced his replacement by an outsider in January 2017 [BX-1062].

**Era.**
- **Inherited:**
  - a 787 breaking to 5 a month [BX-0419];
  - a MAX "about a year late" against the neo [BX-0447];
  - the A350 coming to market [BX-0448].
- **On his watch:**
  - the 787 battery grounding of January 2013 [BX-0434];
  - the 787 at 7, 10 and then 12 a month [BX-0452, BX-0487];
  - the 737 at 42, with a path to 52 [BX-0451, BX-0478];
  - the 787-10 and 777X, with 777X orders of 300 from 6 customers [B-0914];
  - the MAX 200 [BX-0482];
  - the product line "largely set" by 2015 [BX-0493];
  - cost cuts and fewer management layers in 2016 [BX-0511, BX-0524].

**Evidence base.** 110 items in his own words (BX-0416 to BX-0525) from 7 investor events, 2012-09-14 to 2016-05-11, plus 97 company items where he is the speaker. There are no earnings calls. Others speak about him in [BX-0883, BX-0937, BX-0969, BX-1025].

**Confidence.**
- **High:** rate execution, sequencing, development-risk posture, how he reads Airbus, communication.
- **Medium:** new-airplane decisions; the middle of the market and the MAX 10 were still undecided [BX-0514].
- **Low:** capital allocation, decision rights, and anything after May 2016.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **"Pure execution of the rate breaks, of the deliveries"** [BX-0440]. "Execute the backlog" [BX-0492]; turn it "into cash" [BX-0462].
2. **Margin before share:** "you can't go chase market share and start to destroy your margins" [BX-0481]. Take share only "as … is reasonable, and I stress reasonable" [BX-0513]. Double-digit BCA margins now, mid-teens later [BX-0505].
3. **Cost:** four levers, with development cost first: "I can't stress that enough" [BX-0467]. Set the cost "right up front" [BX-0468].
4. **Finish the derivatives** before opening new options [BX-0514].
5. **Services:** #1 in 2015 [BX-0491]; "a solid #2" in 2016 unless Boeing buys growth [BX-0501].

**How he sharpens company doctrine.**
- He is the plainest source of "not at any price" share. He contrasts with Albaugh's 60/40 red line [BX-0078].
- He makes it a portfolio: "must wins", balanced "with other places where maybe it's not as critical" [BX-0523].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **Step and hold:** reach the rate, then "we're going to peg it" [BX-0421].
- **One change at a time on a line:** stabilise the 787-8/-9 mix at 10, then raise the rate, then add the -10 [BX-0480].
- **Raise rates only with an overbooking cushion.** In 2008 he held the 737 because "we didn't have the overbookings" [BX-0509]. Over-quote to hold a rate [B-0660].
- **Prepare suppliers for more than the announced rate** [BX-0479].
- **Do "not … overproduce"**; run a system that moves "up and down" without margin swings [BX-0512].
- **Development risk:** "We stepped into some of that risk knowingly, and we're not doing that again" [BX-0445]. "you take a jump that you know you can deliver" [BX-0498].
- **New production technology is proven on the current 777 first** [BX-0496].
- **Never let supplier cost-downs touch quality or schedule** [BX-0438].
- **Widebodies first;** the new small airplane is for "the next decade" [BX-0457, BX-0483].
- **No discounting of the outgoing product:** asked about cutting 777 prices, he answered "Are you kidding me?" (McNerney's account) [BX-0883].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | No "massive technology jumps" [BX-0498]; but kept lithium-ion through the grounding [BX-0444] |
| Schedule | **Low-medium** | Margin built in for concurrent engine programs [BX-0497]; slides are "the worst thing" [BX-0510] |
| Balance sheet | **Medium (thin)** | Backed a 50% dividend increase [BX-0474]; 25% less capex for 787 rates 12 and 14 [BX-0463] |
| Commercial / pricing | **Medium** | Accepts aggressive launch deals [BX-0441]; priced harder for being late [BX-0447] |
| Operational | **High** | Kept the 787 line at 5 through the grounding [BX-0436]; holds rates through soft patches [BX-0422] |

**Tempo.**
- **Against rival moves: slow and minimal.**
  - The A330neo got no product answer [BX-0484].
  - The A321neo got a denser MAX 8, "a much lower-risk solution" [BX-0482]. A stretch was still only studied in 2016 [B-1153, B-1179].
  - Airbus's talk of 60 a month: "we can go to 60 … We got to just make sure that we've got the kind of demand" [BX-0500].
- **His own moves:** dated, then held through shocks [BX-0432].
- **New airplanes:** "the right decision at the right time" [BX-0508]; "catch ball" with customers [BX-0516].

**Capital allocation stance.**
- Keep R&D flat by overlapping programs [BX-0416].
- Productivity in place of capacity capex [BX-0463].
- Cash split "to both the investors and then into the new programs" [BX-0474].
- Services M&A to reach #1 [BX-0501].

**Product stance.**
- **A full family in 10-15% seat steps,** each model mapped to an Airbus rival [BX-0455, B-0934].
- **Derivatives:** the 787-10 is "a couple of donuts" [B-0665]; the 777X is to "maintain our market position" [BX-0443].
- **Commonality from 70% to 95%** [BX-0494].
- **The bridge:** pair 777 orders with 777X orders [BX-0469].
- **Options:** the MAX 7 kept as a cheap option to redesign [BX-0518].
- **The middle of the market:** "a very unique marketplace", but no commitment [BX-0516].
- **Engines:** sole source "optimizing the engine, airplane -- airframe combination" [BX-0442]; schedules are "a combined schedule between ourselves and the engine companies" [BX-0497].
- **Joint Ventures:** no evidence. He made the in-house Everett composite wing a de-risker [BX-0471].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- **Respect:** Airbus's battery fallback was "probably a prudent way" [BX-0446].
- **Score-keeping:** the neo "got a little bit of a jump on us" [BX-0460]; flips of Airbus operators [BX-0430]; the 300ER outsold the A350-1000 "2:1" [BX-0473].
- **Widebodies:** "we've got them boxed in on the A350" [BX-0459]. The A330neo is a check "you have to write every single flight" [BX-0484].
- **Airbus's backlog** is growth-heavy, so riskier [BX-0519].
- **Concedes where true:** a seat advantage at the A321 [BX-0521]; lost Lion Air on early slots and price [BX-0461].
- **As a motivator:** "nobody wants to be #2 in this company" [BX-0520]. He admitted using campaigns for motivation: "don't confuse motivation with -- totally with the facts" [BX-0504].

**Voice.**
- "pure execution of the rate breaks, of the deliveries" [BX-0440]
- "then we're going to peg it" [BX-0421]
- "we've got them boxed in" [BX-0459]
- "a check you have to write every single flight, every single day" [BX-0484]
- "the NG is better today and the 737 MAX will certainly be better tomorrow" [BX-0472]
- "You can count on me." [BX-0503]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Build through problems** (company B8): the 787 at rate through the grounding [BX-0436]; guidance held [BX-0433].
- **Frame first:** "the batteries are not used in flight" [BX-0431]; deferrals are "win-win" [BX-0485].
- **Bullish on legacy demand:** the 747-8 at 2 a month [BX-0418]; the 777 bridge [BX-0476].
- **Holds that "the heart of the market" is MAX 8-sized** [BX-0482, BX-0521]. Counter: he conceded Airbus's seat advantage at the top of the range [BX-0521].
- **Patience on a new airplane** [BX-0516].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Period | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2012-13 | Baseline every program; "pure execution"; the grounding [BX-0425, BX-0440, BX-0434] |
| 2014 | Four levers; MAX 200 instead of an A321 answer; 52 a month in view [BX-0467, BX-0482, BX-0478] |
| 2015 | Strategy "largely set"; services #1 [BX-0493, BX-0491] |
| 2016 | Cost, layers, jobs; middle of the market as "catch ball" [BX-0507, BX-0524, BX-0516] |

**Priorities.** Rate and profitability are "the 2 big things" on the 787 [BX-0426]. Later: "this is not real complicated stuff … Execute the backlog" [BX-0492].

**Decision style.**
- **Sequential:** "get ourselves through the MAX, get ourselves through the 9, get the production stable" [BX-0427]; on a higher 787 rate, "let's get the Dash 10 launched, and then we can talk about that" [B-0801].
- **Organisation for concurrency:** he separated development from production because Boeing "could have 5 development programs going on at any one time" [BX-0449].
- **Firm on hard calls:** "they're the right ones" [BX-0507].
- **Producibility first,** in Muilenburg's account: "Ray has really been pushing hard on upfront, designing for producibility" [BX-0969].

**Risk.**
- After 2001 he diversified the backlog to protect rates [BX-0458].
- He sees the cycle as a plateau unless "something that we don't have any control over happens" [BX-0499].
- He used freed Qantas slots as ramp buffer rather than reselling them [BX-0428].

**Capital allocation.** Thin. Flat R&D [BX-0416], lighter capex [BX-0463], payout support [BX-0474].

**Product and strategy.**
- **The MAX:** the re-engine came forward because customers "didn't want to wait that long" [BX-0456]. It will beat its sold spec: "Too bad I can't go back and get more money for it" [BX-0517].
- **Pricing on value:** derivatives are priced on value [B-1065].
- **The 777:** the threat to the bridge is used airplanes and lease extensions, not Airbus [BX-0515]. He kept the 747-8 as a "niche airplane" [BX-0470].

**Operations.**
- **Suppliers:** about 600 people inside the supply chain [BX-0424]; second sources [BX-0439]; supplier health "absolutely imperative" [BX-0454].
- **Lines:** a temporary surge line, retired once stable [BX-0490].
- **Labour:** an IAM deal with shared productivity incentives [BX-0466]. Cuts by voluntary layoffs, to avoid "bumping" risk on the line [BX-0511].

**Rivals.** Pricing pressure is offset by productivity, never conceded in margin [BX-0475]. The Bombardier win at Delta took "an immediate $500 million write-down" [BX-0522].

**Communication.**
- Hedge, then promise: "cautiously optimistic … and we're going to hit them" [BX-0417].
- Brief admission, then a declared turn: "we have turned the corner" [BX-0450]; "We've been to hell and back" [BX-0502].
- Sweeping claims: "I would put our product strategy up against anything in the world" [BX-0464].

**Crisis response (the 787 grounding).**
1. Frame the problem [BX-0431].
2. Mass engineering: about 200 engineers, 200,000 hours [BX-0434].
3. Conviction: "we would not go forward unless we felt like we had it nailed" [BX-0435].
4. Keep the technology for speed of return [BX-0444].
5. Hold production and guidance [BX-0436, BX-0433].
6. Accept knock-on effects: the 787-10 launch slipped because customers would not commit while the fleet was grounded [BX-0453].

**Team.**
- A "battle tested team" [BX-0462]; a flatter BCA [BX-0524].
- Workers get hard market facts they "maybe wouldn't have shared before" [BX-0525].
- An integrated supplier front with McNerney, Muilenburg and Smith [BX-0937]; margin targets owned jointly with the CEO [BX-1025].

## 5. In the game: if Conner is in the room

Engine figures come from a scratch probe on 2026-10-03: base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, fps `cfm_ducted` Solo in 2029, Airbus NGSA in 2029, no injects. Re-run them in play.

**fps timing.**
- **His instinct is widebody first** [BX-0457] and a new single-aisle in "the next decade" [BX-0483].
  - Probe: a 787 Re-engine in 2029 with fps in 2032 scores +2.74, against +7.87 for fps in 2029 plus a Re-engine in 2036: a $5.1B gap, well above the $2B cap.
  - A Turn-1 Re-engine with fps in 2029 still trails by $2.6B.
- **So the doctrine year stands.** He accepts 2029 once three conditions hold:
  - the 737-7, 737-10 and 777X are through [BX-0514]. The engine does not model them: treat them as closed by Turn 2 unless a `certification_scrutiny` inject is live;
  - cost is set "right up front" [BX-0468];
  - the jump is "a jump that you know you can deliver" [BX-0498].
- **NGSA in development** is the customer signal he waits for [BX-0456] **(inference)**.
- Probe: 2030 costs $2.1B; 2032 costs $5.5B.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **Solo.** In-house production "significantly derisked" the 777X [BX-0471], and there is no partnering evidence for airplanes.
- **He would take the Joint Venture on the slip test** as a cost lever, as he would take M&A for services [BX-0501] **(inference)**.
- Probe, slip test: the Joint Venture leads by $0.86B.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **If Airbus re-engines the A350: no follow.** The A330neo precedent is his: price-led, "a check you have to write every single flight" [BX-0484].
  - Probe (A350 Re-engine 2029): following in 2032 costs $5.0B.
  - He keeps the 787 "boxed in" message [BX-0459].
- **If it does not:** he is more tolerant of overlap than Albaugh. He organised BCA for five concurrent programs [BX-0449].
  - Probe: 2034 adds $1.03B, 2036 $1.24B, 2030 $0.70B. His tolerance is cheap.
  - **Pushes 2034 or 2036,** never ahead of fps.

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes in Turn 1,** then pegs [BX-0421]. Probe: +$0.87B; Turn 1 against Turn 3 is within $0.04B.
- **Stricter than [NOW] on demand:** with no overbooking cushion he holds [BX-0509]. Under a narrowbody demand shock he would defer one turn **(inference)**. Probe: that costs at most $0.32B.
- **Under a quality escape** his instinct is to keep building [BX-0436]. H6 still bars a new step; his difference shows as "no rate cut" in statements.
- **Never because of Airbus's rates** [BX-0500].

**Cancel.**
- **Vetoes cancelling a launched program:** "it's the worst thing that can happen is when those things start to slide" [BX-0510]. He kept the 747-8 at 1.3 [BX-0470, BX-0489].
- **Ends products** only when demand cannot hold a profitable floor **(inference)** [BX-0489].

**Engine choice.**
- **fps:** `cfm_ducted`. Sole-source optimisation [BX-0442]. He builds margin for engine development [BX-0497] and so avoids engines that add years. `cfm_open_fan` cannot enter service before 2045, so a 2029 fps on it waits until then: -$32.9B (probe, 2026-10-04). `rr_ultrafan_nb` adds a year: -$4.4B.
- `pw_gtf2` (+$0.82B) is within ε; he stays with CFM, whose MAX engine was "optimized for the 737" [B-0736] **(inference)**.
- **Re-engine:** `ge_genx_next`.

**Disclosure.**
- **Discloses dated rate steps and meets them** [BX-0419, BX-0451].
- **Holds guidance publicly through shocks** [BX-0433].
- **Asserts a price premium; never gives breakeven** [BX-0429].
- **After a slip:** frame it, then "turned the corner" [BX-0450].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **Supplier bottleneck. Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, no overlapping Re-engine. **Rationale only:** people into the supplier [BX-0424]; second sources [BX-0439]; a temporary surge line [B-0663].
- **No attribution:** Airbus may gain from shared suppliers so long as "they're not getting more" [B-0796].
- **On exposure:** a competitive rallying cry [BX-0520] **(inference)**.
- **Poaching:** no order change. Incentive-linked labour deals [BX-0466]; voluntary attrition rather than bumping [BX-0511].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *Does this hit the rate plan and deliveries?* [BX-0440].
2. *What's the cushion?* Overbooking and demand [BX-0509].
3. *Is it "a jump that you know you can deliver"?* [BX-0498].
4. *What does it cost, recurring and non-recurring?* [B-1176]. He wants capex and strain from whatif, and margin_alone/margin_both from `rules`.
5. *Do we keep margin if we chase this campaign?* [BX-0481].

**What changes his mind:**
- customer data ("catch ball") [BX-0516];
- a business case that closes [BX-0443];
- customers unwilling to wait [BX-0456].

**What does not:**
- Airbus rate talk [BX-0500];
- price pressure, which he absorbs with productivity [BX-0475];
- a grounding, which he builds through [BX-0436].

## 6. Confidence and gaps

- **Investor days and conferences only.** No quarterly guidance behaviour.
- **Nothing after May 2016:** not the MAX accidents, nor the 777X slips past 2020.
- **Decision rights:** only that Muilenburg took development oversight in 2014 [BX-0830].
- **Capital allocation and Joint Ventures** are thin or absent.
- **Pricing:** the early-2016 pricing remark reaches us only through an analyst's question [BX-0504].
