# James F. (Jim) Albaugh: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.**
- President and CEO of Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA); the sources tag him "BCA CEO" and later print the data provider's title "Former Executive Vice President" [BX-0001, BX-0026].
- McNerney moved him from the defense business to BCA in 2009, replacing Carson [BX-0641], so that he "had to catch his own punt" [BX-0709].
- His successor was three months into the job by September 2012 [BX-0425], so he left around mid-2012. No source covers the exit.

**Era.**
- **Inherited:**
  - a 787 years late, first delivered on 26 September 2011 [B-0526];
  - over-outsourced fuselage work, bought back in Charleston [B-0497];
  - a 747-8 "a little short on the guarantees" [B-0643];
  - the A320neo, launched in December 2010 [BX-0019];
  - 2010 orders about double plan [B-0381], with the 737 sold out through 2015 and the 787 through 2019 [B-0395].
- **On his watch:**
  - the 787 entered service [BX-0003];
  - the 737 MAX was launched in August 2011, after American Airlines split its order [BX-0043, B-2415];
  - about 60% more output over three years: 737 from 31.5 to 42, 767 and 747 from 1.5 to 2 [B-0536, B-0537], 777 to 8.3 [B-0641];
  - a five-year IAM extension [BX-0058];
  - the 767 line kept for the tanker [B-0642];
  - the 787-10 and 777X studied; both launched in 2013, after he left [BX-0057].

**Evidence base.** 82 items in his own words (BX-0001 to BX-0082) from 5 investor events, 2011-05-24 to 2012-05-15, plus 134 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.**
- **High:** rate discipline, the derivative-versus-clean-sheet choice, pricing and share, supply-chain doctrine.
- **Medium:** how he reads Airbus, and capital beyond BCA.
- **Low:** crisis response (no major crisis falls in the window), Joint Ventures, and 2009-10.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Leader "by all measures":** revenues, deliveries, earnings [B-0349]. That means half the narrowbody market [B-0639]. "I'm not going to set our goals low" [BX-0001].
2. **Profit, not volume:** "we're in the business of making money selling airplanes" [BX-0048]. He would rather win on capability and relationships "and maybe lose on the first one [price]" [BX-0049]. "Program margins, never good enough" [BX-0072].
3. **Protect the cash engines:** the 737 and 777 "drive the cash that we need to do our development programs" [BX-0032]. Build "the bridge to the 737 MAX" with no delivery gap [BX-0047].
4. **Capacity matched to demand:** "7 years of backlog is too much" [BX-0010]; "We're giving up market share if we don't" [BX-0073].
5. **Make the 787 pay:** "not satisfied with the zero-margin airplane" [BX-0011].
6. **Keep the decade "de-risked"** [BX-0037].

**How he sharpens company doctrine.**
- The company profile puts share fifth, "not at any price" (profile.md, objective 5), and prices for scarcity [B-2331]. He gives share a number: Airbus at 60% and Boeing at 40% is "a slippery slope", because the leader comes down the learning curve faster and gains pricing power [BX-0078].
- **(inference)** The game's narrowbody status quo is exactly 40/60 [RULES]. He would read 2026 as sitting on his red line.

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **Rates:**
  - "break rate and you stabilize" [BX-0007];
  - no rate breaks on two programs at once [BX-0008];
  - 24 months of planning [BX-0069] and about 6 months of readiness review [BX-0009];
  - demand must hold "for 2 to 3 years" [BX-0051];
  - never up "before we have stability at the rate that we're operating at" [BX-0044].
- **Downturns:** never cut rates; burn down backlog instead [BX-0061, B-0505].
- **Capital:** "tens of millions" is a quick yes; more needs several years of demand confidence [BX-0027]. "you don't tell me you need to have more capital to go up in rate" [BX-0064].
- **New airplane:**
  - mature the technology first, then a gated process that does not "travel design risk into the next phase" [BX-0005];
  - design the production system with the airplane [BX-0013];
  - go to the board only when the non-recurring cost is known and the risk acceptable [BX-0057];
  - "not going to be driven by a date" [BX-0004].
- **Scope:** everything beyond the core change must "earn its way onto the airplane" [BX-0036], past "Delaney first and then me next" [BX-0075].
- **One derivative at a time:** "We got to finish the -9 first" [B-0632].
- **Price:** "We're not going to give airplanes away" [BX-0053].
- **Intellectual property:** "build something of everything" [BX-0014]; "very high walls around … critical technologies" [BX-0050].
- **Contracts:** cap liquidated damages; supplier failures fall on suppliers [BX-0076].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | Turned down a clean sheet 20% more efficient over non-recurring cost and 60-a-month composite producibility [BX-0030, B-0589] |
| Schedule | **Low (padded)** | Set the MAX for 2017 while driving the team earlier [BX-0028] |
| Balance sheet | **Medium-low** | Would extend the 777 at 8.3 rather than spend capital [BX-0018]; cut R&D by $300-500M [BX-0039] |
| Fixed price / commercial | **Low**, one exception | Capped damages [BX-0076]; but promised a 787 "profitable from the beginning" [BX-0029] |
| Capacity through the cycle | **High** | Waved off bubble warnings as lessor self-interest [BX-0077]; holds rates in a soft market [BX-0061] |

**Tempo.**
- **Narrowbody:** deliberate in words, fast once an anchor customer is at risk. In May 2011 nobody was "ringing my phone off the hook" [BX-0006] and he was "leaning towards a new airplane" [BX-0012]. About three months later he announced the MAX "before we went to the board" [BX-0043].
- **Widebody:** reactive. Major 777 upgrades were held "in reserve" [BX-0016] and timed to whether the A350-1000 was "a real airplane" [BX-0052, B-0546].
- **After losses:** "go play in their sandbox, too" [BX-0080].

**Capital allocation stance.**
- Money not spent on the clean sheet went to the 787-10 and the 777 [BX-0026, B-0590].
- Lean and flow before capital [B-0508].
- Bring critical work back in: in-house engineering rose from about 50% to about 70% [B-0404], but "We're not going to totally vertically integrate" [B-0624]. A 500-person supplier oversight team [B-0513].

**Product stance.**
- **Derivative over clean sheet** when non-recurring cost or producibility is unresolved [BX-0030, BX-0060]. Customers wanted "fuel burn now and date certainty" [BX-0034].
- **"The most efficient airplane out there in every category"** [B-0634].
- **Engines:** sole source with GE/CFM, a 68-inch fan, a custom core [B-0501]; "a lot of people … get over-enamored with fan size" [BX-0035].
- **Joint Ventures:** no supporting evidence. Outsource only "to get access to capital … technology … low cost" [B-0386], and beware paying partners "to develop IP that they now own" [BX-0015].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- **A share player:** "it is a market share game" [BX-0078]. Its launch pricing is "predatory", but "what we're not doing is losing margin" [BX-0079].
- **Credit where due:** better at financing, a steadier producer [B-2392, B-2393]. "They build a good airplane" [BX-0024].
- **Underrated the neo:** "parity with the 737-800NG" [BX-0019]; switching costs would hold customers [BX-0020]. American split its order within weeks [B-2415].
- **Shared suppliers:** watches what Airbus asks of them [B-0495, B-0587].
- **The wider field:** "The days of the duopoly are over" [BX-0021].

**Voice.**
- "we're in the business of making money selling airplanes" [BX-0048]
- "it's not about having the best airplane, it's about having the best airplane that's available" [BX-0010]
- "you break rate and you stabilize" [BX-0007]
- "I want to under-promise and I want to over-deliver" [BX-0028]
- "anything else we do will have to earn its way onto the airplane" [BX-0036]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Discounting the rival's first move:** "parity" and switching costs [BX-0019, BX-0020] (company B5, sharpened).
- **Waiting until a rival widebody is "real"** [BX-0052] (B7).
- **Optimism on program economics and share,** beside padded dates: "profitable from the beginning" [BX-0029]; MAX/neo order parity "by the end of this year, or sometime in the middle of next year" [BX-0068].
- **Capacity optimism:** "no evidence … that we have a bubble" [BX-0077].
- **Candour about misses:** "we have failed miserably" at burning down backlog [BX-0040]; "We did outsource too much" [BX-0074].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Date | Posture |
|---|---|
| May 2011 | Clean sheet favoured; re-engine kept alongside; no rush [BX-0012, BX-0006] |
| Jul-Aug 2011 | American at risk: MAX announced before the board [BX-0043, B-2415] |
| Nov 2011 | "De-risked this decade"; savings go to widebody derivatives [BX-0037, BX-0026] |
| 2012 | Rate ramps on every program; share red line; counter-attack on Airbus customers [B-0622, BX-0078, BX-0080] |

**Priorities.** Leadership with a premium price [B-0538]. The backlog is a weapon only while it is short: too long, and "you push them to go buy airplanes they don't necessarily want" [B-0503].

**Decision style.**
- **Gated and data-driven:** he drills down "to every work cell" and second-sources before a rate step [B-0406].
- **He reversed his own preference** once the unknowns could not be closed: "I'm an engineer, and I was looking forward to the opportunity to build that airplane" [BX-0030].
- **Shared calls at the top:** McNerney presented rate calls as joint: "the judgment of Jim Albaugh and I" [BX-0692].

**Risk.** Program risk is the enemy: program managers should be "very paranoid about what can go wrong" [BX-0025]. Market risk is accepted [BX-0038].

**Capital allocation.** Rates rise through lean flow, not plant: the 737 flow time fell from 22 to 11 days [B-0389].

**Product and strategy.**
- **The 2011 choice:** the clean sheet's 20% gain lost to three unknowns: 60 composite airplanes a month, non-recurring cost, and a guaranteed first delivery [B-0589, B-0532].
- **The re-engine** kept roughly an 8% cost-of-ownership edge [B-0352] and "about 1/2 the market" [B-0580].
- **Widebodies:** no clean-sheet 777: "It's a huge investment, time and money" [BX-0060]. The composite-wing 777X was sized against the A350-1000 [B-0633].

**Operations.**
- **Suppliers run "like they're our factories"** [BX-0033]: 1,200 monitored, about 500 able to stop a line [B-0631]. A rescued supplier stays under watch for a "long tail" [BX-0071].
- **Labour:** strikes are "lose-lose" [BX-0046]; the early IAM deal was "a huge accomplishment" [BX-0058].
- **Concurrency** was the 787 rate killer [B-0636].

**Rivals.** He matches Airbus "deal per deal" while keeping "the most competitive airplane" [B-0400]. He holds the premium against launch pricing [B-2416] and weighs Airbus's capacity in his own 737 rate decisions [B-0540]. COMAC will "eventually … get it right" [BX-0062].

**Communication.**
- Candid about misses [BX-0040, BX-0074]. Firm near-term calls, none beyond the visible horizon [BX-0055].
- He rejected the "sandbagging" charge: "we will sneak up on these rate changes" [BX-0054].
- "we don't put schedules out if we don't think that those are schedules that we can meet" [BX-0041].

**Crisis response.** Thin. He named the 787-9 slip and its cause [BX-0065], and held 2012 guidance through a shimming issue [BX-0056].

**Team.** He put people where their strengths fit, "nothing to do with dissatisfaction" [BX-0063]. He set up the high-rate production team under Conner [B-0403]. The 777X call goes to McNerney once BCA has done its homework [BX-0082].

## 5. In the game: if Albaugh is in the room

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03; base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, fps `cfm_ducted` Solo in 2029, Airbus NGSA in 2029, no injects. Re-run them in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes:** 2029, the H2 year; his gates are matured technology [BX-0005], known non-recurring cost [BX-0057] and producibility at rate [BX-0013]. "Not driven by a date" in words only: H2 keeps the order at 2029.
- **Share at risk:** if NGSA launches in Turn 1, he would push hardest for the H1 exception, as he moved within three months in 2011 [BX-0043, BX-0078] **(inference)**.
  - Probe against NGSA 2026: Solo in 2028 scores +4.06 against +1.95 for Solo in 2029.
  - But Solo 2028 fails the slip test: -7.38 against -4.32 for Do Nothing under the same Airbus orders.
  - The Joint Venture in 2028 (-5.75) passes.
  - So his instinct gets the timing right; the team rule must set the form.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **Default: Solo.** Reasons: IP walls [BX-0015, BX-0050] and "build something of everything" [BX-0014].
- **He would accept a Joint Venture framed as "access to capital"** [B-0386], with the critical technology kept in-house **(inference)**.
- Probe, slip test: the Joint Venture leads by $0.86B (NGSA 2029) and $1.63B (NGSA 2026, 2028 launch). Nominal: Solo leads by $2.74B.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **If the A350 is not re-engined:** a staggered launch, one development at a time [BX-0008, B-0632]. Probe: 2036 adds $1.24B to fps alone; 2034 adds $1.03B; 2030 adds $0.70B.
- **His bias runs against H3:** "we don't want to be disadvantaged on the 777 and won't be" [B-0591]; a real rival "will drive us to making a decision sooner" [BX-0052].
  - Probe (A350 Re-engine in 2029): following in 2032 costs $5.0B; the same turn costs $8.1B; even 2036 costs $2.3B.
  - H3 holds. His pressure goes into statements about efficiency in every category [B-0634].

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes in Turn 1:** capacity is a weapon [B-0494], and share is lost without it [BX-0073]. Probe: +$0.87B with fps; Turn 1 and Turn 3 lie within $0.04B, Turn 2 is $0.32B lower.
- **The $1.2B capex** [RULES] is above his "tens of millions" quick-yes, so he wants 2-3 years of demand [BX-0027, BX-0051]. The 737 backlog supplies that.
- **Demand shock:** keep the step and burn backlog [BX-0061]. This matches [NOW].
- **Quality escape or FAA scrutiny:** no step until stable [BX-0044], as H6 requires.

**Cancel.**
- **Vetoes cancelling a launched program:** he kept the 767 [B-0642] and promised a profitable 787 [BX-0066].
- **Ends a line only when its anchor customer is gone** [B-0588].

**Engine choice.**
- **fps:** `cfm_ducted`. Fan-size skepticism [BX-0035] and the GE/CFM sole source [B-0501].
  - He vetoes `cfm_open_fan` and `rr_ultrafan_nb`. The open fan cannot enter service before 2045, so a 2029 fps on it waits until then (probe, 2026-10-04: -$32.9B). The UltraFan adds a year (-$4.4B).
  - `pw_gtf2` (+$0.82B) is within ε, so he stays with CFM **(inference)**.
- **Re-engine:** `ge_genx_next`.

**Disclosure.**
- **Padded public dates** with an earlier internal target [BX-0028].
- **No air-show announcements** [BX-0004].
- **When share is at risk, disclose early to freeze customers.** In 2011 he told them a better re-engine or new airplane was coming [B-0402], and announced before the board [BX-0043].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **Supplier bottleneck. Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, no overlapping Re-engine. **Rationale only:** weekly metrics and early intervention [BX-0070]; rescue or insource [BX-0014]; charge the supplier [BX-0076].
- **He would ask what the shared supplier is doing for Airbus** [B-0587], but would make no attribution before exposure **(inference)**.
- **Poaching:** no order change. Boeing has the only active US design teams [B-0625], though half its engineers could retire within five years [B-0390].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *What is the non-recurring cost, and how sure are we?* He wants capex and the delay cost from `whatif` [BX-0057].
2. *Can we build it at rate?* [BX-0013].
3. *Where does this leave us against 60/40?* He wants the `shares` path [BX-0078].
4. *Is their airplane "real"?* Firm launches only [BX-0052].
5. *Does demand hold "for 2 to 3 years"?* [BX-0051].

**What changes his mind:**
- an anchor customer at risk, or customers asking for date certainty [B-2415, BX-0034];
- unresolved non-recurring cost [BX-0030];
- a rival product proven real [BX-0052].

**What does not:**
- air-show calendars [BX-0004];
- lessors' bubble talk [BX-0077];
- analysts' sandbagging charges [BX-0054].

## 6. Confidence and gaps

- **Fourteen months of evidence** (May 2011 to May 2012). Nothing on the 787's first flight, the Charleston second line or his exit.
- **No major crisis falls in the window,** so his crisis behaviour is inferred.
- **Outcome notes:** many outcomes come from readers' later knowledge, not his window.
- **Attribution:** some 2011-11-03 turns labelled McNerney read as his (e.g. [B-0511, B-0520]). They are not used here.
- **Decision rights:** only that the 777X launch sits with McNerney [BX-0082].
