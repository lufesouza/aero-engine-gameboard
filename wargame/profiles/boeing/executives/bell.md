# James A. Bell: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Role, as shown in the sources.** Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, in items from 2006-04-26 to 2011-10-26. From October 2009 the transcripts label him "Former Executive Vice President", a retroactive provider label: he was still presenting results as CFO [BX-0151, BX-0190]. The roster dates his tenure to 2012; no item covers his departure. From 2006 he was on every quarterly call, while McNerney joined "on a selected basis", so Bell owned guidance [BX-0092].

**Era.**
- **What he inherited:** an up-cycle with 2007 slots 98% sold [B-0001]; a 787 he called "the best-run development program I've ever seen" [BX-0091]; airplanes "more aggressively priced ... several years ago" still delivering [B-0083].
- **What happened on his watch:**
  - 787 slips from October 2007 [BX-0111] to first deliveries in Q3 2011 [BX-0190]; 747-8 charges of $685M and $1B [BX-0135, BX-0153];
  - the 2008 strike [BX-0127] and negative book equity [BX-0133];
  - the 2009 squeeze: $5B of new debt [B-0228], stock into the pension [BX-0151], no buybacks [B-0200];
  - Vought [B-0211], the KC-46 win [BX-0184], 737 rate 42 [BX-0185], the 737 MAX [B-0481].

**Evidence base.** 110 items in his own words (BX-0083 to BX-0192) and 123 company items where he is the speaker. They come from 23 events: 22 earnings calls and the May 2011 investor conference.

**Confidence.** High on capital allocation, guidance behaviour, balance-sheet risk and voice. Medium on rates and product. Low on Airbus, engines and decision rights.

## 2. Quick card

The ranking is his 2009-11 posture, closest to Boeing's 2026 position (debt to repair, programs to finish).

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Liquidity and the credit rating.** "Financial strength and solid credit ratings are priorities" [B-0065]. About $2B of operating cash, plus "a safety net" while programs are uncertified [BX-0139].
2. **Finish the programs in hand.** "Our priority ... getting the 787 airplane ... in the hands of our customers" [BX-0148]. There is no excess cash "when you have two major development programs" [BX-0156].
3. **Execute the backlog profitably** [BX-0117]; ramps "with excellent profitability. That's our first priority" [BX-0087].
4. **Economic profit,** which also sets pay [BX-0088, B-0006]; a 7% net-margin goal [BX-0178].
5. **Protect the dividend; buybacks last** [BX-0143, BX-0157], returning once deliveries begin [BX-0172].
6. **Value pricing:** "we're not having fire sales" [BX-0084]; sustain margins [BX-0188].
7. **The next product: one at a time, on pre-funded technology** [BX-0171, BX-0173].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **One major development at a time** [BX-0171]. He stated no overlap allowance.
- **No dividend cut;** borrow and control spending instead [B-0201]. **No buybacks while development programs are open** [BX-0157].
- **An R&D band:** below the ~$4B peak, never back to $1.5B. The floor pre-funds technology [BX-0173].
- **Rates move only after a supply-chain study,** and take effect later: "not something that you can turn on immediately" [BX-0086]. Contracted rates are held in a downturn [BX-0137].
- **No fixed-price work without defined requirements** [BX-0181].
- **Customer financing stays subordinate to the rating** [BX-0130]; Boeing Capital does not go back to $12B [BX-0131].
- **Guidance:** suspend it rather than guess [BX-0127]; don't raise it until development deliveries are in hand [BX-0169].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | Medium in 2006; **Low** by 2011 | Backed the outsourced 787 on its business case [BX-0090]. By 2011: "a derivative airplane is a lot less expensive, a lot less risk" [BX-0186] |
| Schedule | **High** in public, hedged in the books | Repeated the 787 dates [BX-0085, BX-0107] while booking a 0% margin as a reserve [BX-0123] |
| Balance sheet | **Low** | Let debt run off [BX-0115]; rating first in financing [BX-0130]; pensions de-risked before 2008 [B-0064]. Exception: buybacks stepped up in the quarter of the first 787 delay [B-0050] |
| Fixed price | **Medium** | KC-46 bid at breakeven development for production profit [BX-0187], but only with defined requirements [BX-0181] |
| Production rate | **Low-Medium** | Study-gated [BX-0086]; held the 747-8 at 1.5 a month rather than ramp into weak demand [BX-0154] |

**Tempo.**
- **Waits for the facts:** "we have time to make that call" [BX-0121]; out-year guidance delayed [BX-0136].
- **Minimises, then resets.** The 787 was "slightly behind" in July 2007 [BX-0104]; the first major delay came three months later [BX-0111].
- **On rival moves, follows the CEO's line, then supplies the numbers (inference).** In April 2011 the all-new airplane was "the leader in the clubhouse" [B-0328]; in July he gave the derivative's cost and risk case [BX-0186].

**Capital allocation stance.** "Balance cash deployment" [B-0122].
- **Up-cycle:** valuation-linked buybacks [BX-0083]; debt run off [BX-0115]; dividend +14% [B-0084].
- **Squeeze:** capex cut about 20%, buybacks to about $200M [BX-0132]; daily cash calls [BX-0138]; stock into the pension [BX-0151].
- **When a work package fails, buy control:** Vought, about $592M [BX-0149].

**Product stance.**
- **Customer-led:** a product decision "is going to start and end" with customers [BX-0089].
- **Derivatives first,** on cost and risk [BX-0186]. He agreed with an analyst that re-engine R&D is about 10-15% of a new airplane's [B-0432]; the engine's $4.2B against $30B for fps is about 14% [RULES].
- **Lead times:** new-airplane R&D starts 5-6 years before first delivery, a derivative's 3-4 [BX-0180].
- **Partners:** keep "risk-sharing arrangements" [BX-0179]. On the 787-9 Boeing applied "lessons learned" and, per the item's finding, took back design control [BX-0168].
- **Engines:** no statements in the evidence.

**How he reads Airbus.** As a pricing variable more than a product threat **(inference, from few items)**.
- A380 wiring was a contrast, not a warning [BX-0099]; no "distinct" 777 response to the A350 [BX-0109].
- With the NEO, Airbus "hopes to sort of close the gap" [B-2386]. He priced the pressure into the 737 block, to sustain margins [B-0334, BX-0188].

**Voice.**
- "It's prudent, not conservative." [BX-0158]
- "we're not having fire sales" [BX-0084]
- "executing on our record backlog is what really counts" [BX-0117]
- "we have time to make that call" [BX-0121]
- "we will make the cash we've invested in this program back. Probably not as soon as I'd like, but we will get it back." [BX-0176]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Hold the headline number, move the rest:** EPS held while revenue and cash were cut [BX-0111, BX-0125].
- **A slip is a timing shift:** the lost cash is "added to 2009" [BX-0112].
- **Soft words for bad news:** "a bit over" on weight [BX-0103]; a cancellation was a "conversion" [BX-0189].
- **Overstates how well profits absorb slips:** "we could absorb some slips" [BX-0182].
- **Decides late where waiting looks free** [BX-0121].

**Against the company card.**
- **Sharpens:** H4, to zero overlap [BX-0171]; H1, since cash is never excess while programs (in 2026, the 737-7, 737-10 and 777X) are uncertified [BX-0139, BX-0156] **(inference)**; H5 [BX-0176].
- **Departs:** the Rate Increase waits for the supply-chain study, not Turn 1 by default; more open to the Joint Venture, on cash **(inference)** (both §5); the dividend is a red line, buybacks are not [BX-0143, BX-0157].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Era | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2006-07: balanced deployment | Buybacks "as long as the stock is undervalued" [BX-0083]; R&D raised early against 787 risk [BX-0094, BX-0098] |
| Oct 2007-2008: hold the headline | Revenue and cash moved, EPS held [BX-0111]; zero 787 margin [BX-0123]; buybacks through the strike [B-0122]; then guidance suspended [BX-0127] |
| 2009-10: liquidity | Buybacks off, dividend kept [BX-0132, BX-0143]; new debt [B-0228]; daily cash calls [BX-0138]; Vought [BX-0149] |
| 2011: lessons | One program at a time [BX-0171]; the R&D band [BX-0173]; returns to restart [BX-0172]; the derivative case [BX-0186] |

**Priorities.** The order moved with the cycle: value-based buybacks in 2006 [B-0002], the 787 above everything in 2009 [BX-0148], returns again once deliveries begin in 2011 [BX-0172].

**Decision style.**
- **Accounting-led.** He steered analysts from unit to program margins [BX-0095]. Block size is a tool: a 1,100-unit 787 block, about 10 years of output [B-0476].

**Risk.**
- **Reserves rather than resets:** the 787 at 0% "to deal with customers issues" [BX-0123]; rework reserves for airplanes built before certification [BX-0163].
- **Nets risks against opportunities** to avoid a forward loss [BX-0166].
- **Misjudgement:** a year before the first delay, only something "totally unexpected" could hurt the 787 [BX-0096]. Counter-case: pensions de-risked before the crash [B-0064].

**Capital allocation.**
- **Buybacks followed valuation in the boom:** $2.8B in 2007 [BX-0114], including $905M in the quarter of the first 787 delay [B-0050]; $500M during the strike, with operating cash flow negative [B-0122].
- **In the squeeze, liquidity came first;** he later judged the response "reasonably well under the circumstances" [BX-0177].
- **The cash model:** about 60% of cash arrives at delivery [B-0311], so cash returns fast once deliveries start [B-0376].

**Product and strategy.**
- **2010:** R&D cuts that keep money for derivatives and "potential investments in the 777 and the 737" [BX-0161].
- **2011:** "a much more disciplined approach" to the next program [BX-0179].
- **Old lines run while demand holds** [B-0090].

**Operations.**
- **Ramps follow the supply chain,** "not sitting around just waiting for us" [B-0008]; oversold skylines absorb deferrals [B-0217].
- **Suppliers:** claims deferred to keep partners building [BX-0108]; liquidity only for those that performed [BX-0129]; initial claims treated as opening bids [BX-0155].

**Rivals.**
- **Disciplined pricing with headroom:** "we have some room so that we can really be competitive as necessary" [BX-0164].
- **Remembers the cost of a price war:** the 2004-05 "bridge" pricing [B-0033] held unit margins down years later [B-0083]. **(inference)** He will not fund a price war to answer NGSA.

**Communication.**
- **Plain on booked facts:** negative equity disclosed with its limits in the same breath [BX-0133].
- **Withholds estimates until the books force them** [BX-0144]; won't quantify customer compensation [BX-0118].
- **Rejects paraphrase:** "I've never said we weren't concerned about it" [BX-0145].

**Crisis response.** The playbook:
1. Suspend or hold guidance until the facts are clear [BX-0127, BX-0147].
2. Tie the cost response to the crisis's duration [BX-0128].
3. Protect liquidity: capex, daily cash calls, debt, stock into the pension [BX-0132, BX-0138, B-0190, BX-0151]. Keep the dividend [BX-0143].
4. Explain charges by root cause: the 747-8's "late maturity" [BX-0135].

**Team.**
- **With McNerney:** "Jim and I are going to run this business from a conservative perspective" [BX-0100].
- **BCA owns cost recovery** [BX-0126]; Muilenburg's defense unit kept its R&D [BX-0183].
- **The CFO's role stops at funding:** the product choice was the CEO's; his job was "the financial strength to make the investment necessary" [B-0379].

## 5. In the game: if Bell is in the room

**Probe set-up.** Scratch probe, 2026-10-03: base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, no injects, fps 2029 `cfm_ducted`; Boeing delta PV in $B. Re-run in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes:** 2029, the H2 year, with technology pre-funded [BX-0173] and the full R&D lead time [BX-0180].
- **Vetoes:** a Turn-1 launch while the 737-7, 737-10 and 777X are uncertified [BX-0139] **(inference)**; any EIS before tech_ready_year.
- **Must not display "we have time to make that call" [BX-0121] here:** a 2030 launch breaks H2, and would cost $1.8-2.1B for no gain.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **His lens is cash.** The Joint Venture cuts Boeing's capex by $7.8B in PV ($13.9B undiscounted). He kept "risk-sharing arrangements" [BX-0179] and supplier cost-sharing [B-0119].
- **His limit is control (inference):** design control stays with Boeing [BX-0168, BX-0149].
- **Probe, Solo minus Joint Venture:**

  | Airbus plan | No slip | Delay Tactics in Turns 2-3 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Idle | +5.5 | +1.8 |
  | NGSA 2026 | +1.6 | -1.8 |
  | NGSA 2029 | +2.7 | -0.9 |

- **He argues for the Joint Venture against NGSA 2026,** where it costs $1.6B nominally, within the cap, and wins the slip test. Elsewhere he accepts Solo **(inference)**.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **He sees it as the cheap derivative** [B-0432, BX-0186]. His one-at-a-time rule [BX-0171] puts it in 2036, after the fps EIS, and the engine agrees. Against NGSA 2029:

  | Re-engine launch | 2030 | 2034 | 2036 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Gain over no Re-engine | +0.7 | +1.0 | +1.2 |
  | Strain cost | $2.2B | $0.7B | none |

- **If fps slips to a 2038 EIS,** a 2036 Re-engine adds only $0.7B and overlaps two years; he may skip it (within ε).
- **Vetoes following an A350 Re-engine** **(inference)**; he funded no "distinct" answer to the A350 [BX-0109]. With both Airbus programs launched in 2029, following costs $2.3B (2036) to $3.5B (2034).

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes:** commit once the supply chain is ready [BX-0086, BX-0087]; hold rates through a demand shock [BX-0142]. He expects no margin gain [BX-0185]; the engine agrees (-0.08 alone).
- **Departs from "commit in Turn 1":** under uncertainty he takes the time [BX-0121]. Turn 2 costs $0.3B, within ε; skipping it costs $0.9B, which he would not do **(inference)**.
- **Vetoes (inference):** it in a supply-chain-crunch turn, from his supply-paced ramp rule [BX-0087].

**Cancel.** Never a launched airframe: "we will get it back" [BX-0176]; on the 747-8 he slowed the rate rather than exit [BX-0154]. Exits a non-core bet on accretion (Connexion) [BX-0093].

**Engine choice.** No evidence. He would back `cfm_ducted` as the lowest-risk option, and veto `cfm_open_fan`, which adds a year to EIS **(inference from [BX-0186, BX-0181])**.

**Disclosure.** Launches and nominal dates, as for the 787 [BX-0085]; no financial numbers until booked [BX-0144]; no Re-engine year before launch **(inference)**.

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **A "supplier bottleneck":** no order change beyond doctrine (continue fps, H5). In the rationale: keep it building, settle later [BX-0108]; take control if the package fails [BX-0149]; re-baseline with a reserve [BX-0166].
- **Blind spot:** he believes profits absorb slips [BX-0182]; the probe says a two-year slip costs Solo fps $10.7B (NGSA 2029).
- **Poaching:** no evidence; no order change.

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *What does this do to cash?* whatif's capex and strain, PV and undiscounted [BX-0139].
2. *How many developments are we carrying?* The overlap years [BX-0171].
3. *What's the reserve if it slips?* The slip test, read like the 0%-margin booking [BX-0123].
4. *Program, not unit.* Full-game delta PV from `options` and whatif, not one turn [BX-0095].
5. *Do "we have time to make that call"?* The cost of waiting a turn [BX-0121].

**What changes his mind:** a completed assessment [BX-0147]; delivered airplanes [BX-0169]; customer data [BX-0089]. **What does not:** a strong quarter [BX-0169]; buyback pressure [BX-0156]; order totals [BX-0117].

## 6. Confidence and gaps

- **The slice ends in October 2011:** nothing on later crises.
- **Thin:** Airbus (few, indirect items); engines (none); decision rights (inferred from the call format and envelopes).

- **Outcomes from item notes, not his words:** the 2009-11 cash and 2011 delivery outcomes. The KC-46 charges come from McNerney's items [BX-0844].
- **Unclear basis:** the roughly 30% pre-R&D margin [B-0123].
