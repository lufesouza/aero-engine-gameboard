# Gregory D. (Greg) Smith: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.**
- CFO and Executive Vice President, with strategy in his remit, from January 2012 [BX-1334, BX-0221] to April 2021 [BX-1663].
- From April 2020 he also led enterprise manufacturing, supply chain and services ("Enterprise Operations") [BX-0221, BX-1635].
- Interim CEO for a few weeks at the turn of 2020, before Calhoun arrived [BX-0205, BX-1616]. No transcript covers that period.

**Era.**
- **Inherited:** a 787 at a "low single-digit gross margin" [B-0550]; no buyback [BX-1334]; a 737 ladder from 35 to 42 [B-0677].
- **On his watch:** the 787 turned cash-positive in 4Q 2015 [BX-1441, BX-1008]; payout policy rose from 80% to about 100% of FCF [BX-1379, BX-1455], with $48B returned from end-2012 to mid-2018 [B-1433]; pensions moved to defined contribution and a 10-year IAM deal [BX-1399, BX-1397]; repeated KC-46 charges [BX-1411, BX-1437]; the NMA was studied, then shelved [BX-1611]; the MAX grounding (2019 operating cash -$2.4B) [BX-1557]; COVID, with debt at $63.6B [B-1746].

**Evidence base.** 332 items in his own words (BX-1334 to BX-1665) from 57 events, 2012-01-25 to 2021-04-28. About 260 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.** High on cash, capital allocation, rate gating, guidance style and crisis finance. Medium on product strategy (a CFO's view of the NMA). Low on Airbus, engine selection, and his conduct as CEO.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Cash is the yardstick:** "it's about the bottom line … how efficient you are with your cash" [BX-1453]. Half of everyone's incentive pay was free cash flow [BX-1489]. In a crisis, "cash will … continue to be king" [BX-1643].
2. **Execute the backlog:** "executing, executing and executing" [BX-1498].
3. **Retire risk:** "derisk the decade" [BX-1419]; "an imbalance between risk and reward, and we want to fix that" [BX-1461].
4. **Margin through productivity:** double digits, then mid-teens by 2020 [BX-1458, BX-1505]; productivity targets with no "cap" [BX-1561].
5. **Return cash, once risk is retired** [BX-1412]. After 2019, debt repayment comes first "for the next few years" [BX-1607, BX-1663].
6. **A new airplane last:** "priority one is MAX … versus the next product" [BX-1624].
- **Crisis override:** liquidity and leverage are "top priorities" until deliveries resume and the system is stable [BX-1596]. Every decision must help survival "while also not diminishing our future competitiveness" [BX-1653].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **No concurrent major developments:** "we're not going to do that" [BX-1574]; no "concurrent risk on top of risk" [BX-1540]. New-airplane spend follows the 777X ramp-down [BX-1563]. Stricter than H4's 2-year overlap.
- **Rate breaks:**
  - metrics inside control limits, "otherwise there'll be a pause" [BX-1361];
  - a rate sustainable for 2-3 years, tested airline by airline [BX-1424], including credit and financing [BX-1552];
  - "Everything needs to work" [BX-1539]; "If we're not stable, we're not going up" [BX-1604];
  - the next break only once the current rate is healthy [BX-1550].
- **New airplane:** pass/fail gates [BX-1572]; price, value, recurring and non-recurring cost must "come together" [BX-1518]; "No big leaps" [BX-1535]; "go when we're ready" [BX-1569].
- **R&D envelope:** about 3.8-3.9% of revenue [BX-1542]. Projects that disappoint after about 6 months are stopped [BX-1564].
- **Equity** only to pay down debt [BX-1655].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | Reuse of "hard fought technology" [BX-1385]; an "offramp" for new processes [BX-1431]; "No big leaps" [BX-1535] |

**Tempo.**
- **Rival moves:** no product or rate response. He answers with cost [BX-1338, BX-1406]. Airbus's talk of 70 a month changed nothing [B-1442].
- **His own moves:** decided at the supplier lead-time point [BX-1620]. He signalled options before using them: a MAX shutdown flagged six months ahead [BX-1593]; a 787 cut flagged five weeks ahead [BX-1652].

**Capital allocation stance.**
- Organic investment first, partnerships and M&A second [BX-1528].
- Payouts scaled up as risk retired: $10B, then $12B, then $18B authorizations [BX-1393, BX-1417, BX-1512]. In 2018, $12.9B went back to shareholders against $5B invested [BX-1556].
- A cash floor in the "high single-digit" billions [BX-1432].
- From 2020, debt first [BX-1617]. Investment was cut by prioritisation ($2B of $6B) [BX-1646], while 777X, 737-10 and Starliner kept their funding [BX-1639].

**Product stance.**
- Derivatives "break the historical cost curve" [BX-1405]; commonality cuts unit cost [BX-1488].
- An airline's case closes at "20-plus percent more efficient" [BX-1366].
- The middle of the market was a "niche" in 2015 [BX-1443] and a 4,000-5,000-airplane gap by 2018 [BX-1536].
- "Do you buy it? Do you partner it? Do you develop it?" [BX-1537]; "We chose to partner" on seats [BX-1573]. Embraer was "not a have to" [BX-1520].
- The engine was "one of" the big open pieces of the NMA [BX-1519].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- Never named on competition; he named it only on accounting [BX-1451].
- "We're assuming people are going to be successful, and they want share" [BX-1484].
- Widebody confidence: the 787 is "unmatchable" [BX-1588]; "think of a campaign where the 87 hasn't won" [BX-1627].
- In the MAX crisis he conceded narrowbody share but relied on both sides being sold out [BX-1614].

**Voice.**
- "If we're not stable, we're not going up." [BX-1604]
- "we're going to go when we're ready" [BX-1569]
- "we're clear-eyed about what we know and what we don't know" [BX-1583]
- "doing what we said we would do" [BX-1566]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Absorb and isolate:** holds guidance through charges [BX-1526]; "take the MAX out of the equation" [BX-1590].
- **Gradual resets:** "still aiming … more likely" [BX-1648]; five return-to-service assumptions [BX-1591].
- **New-airplane decisions slip while gates are open:** the NMA "in the next year or so" (2018), then 2019, then shelved in January 2020 amid the MAX crisis [B-1347, BX-1548, BX-1611].
- **Normalising problems:** "blocking and tackling" [BX-1410]; "nothing more to read into this" [BX-1445].
- **Reframing a missed metric:** "better than what we originally projected" [BX-1388, BX-1440].
- **Dormant (no lever; statements only):** payout escalation once risk feels retired [BX-1394, BX-1455].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Era | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2012-13 | 787 learning curve and rate breaks; buybacks restart, sized to proof [BX-1341, BX-1381] |
| 2014-15 | "De-risk the decade": pension, IAM, derivatives; returns above 80% [BX-1404, BX-1394] |
| 2016-18 | About 100% of FCF; mid-teens margins; One Boeing score; NMA gates [BX-1455, BX-1503, BX-1510] |
| 2019 | MAX: scenarios from day one, buyback paused, liquidity first [BX-1579, BX-1576, BX-1596] |
| 2020-21 | COVID: "cash is king", early borrowing, debt first, operations remit [BX-1643, BX-1641, BX-1635] |

**Priorities.** They follow the program cycle: "It's all about delivery", then cash deployment [BX-1365]. Productivity funds winning, investing and returns [BX-1476].

**Decision style.**
- Data from the floor: tail-by-tail tracking [BX-1342]; walks the line [BX-1351]; twice-weekly cash calls [BX-1359]; "more operational" [BX-1360].
- Gives each lever a named owner and accepts that some will fail [BX-1479, BX-1469]; weighs "second and third order" effects [BX-1665].
- Puts pending decisions inside the guidance range, so either outcome fits [BX-1486].

**Risk.**
- Plans "assuming for the worst" [BX-1348]; runs stress cases beside the baseline [BX-1645]. On rates, invests ahead: toward 14 while ramping to 12 [BX-1392].
- Spends to buy schedule safety: parallel MAX line [BX-1501], 777X labs [BX-1521], fewer 777X built before certification [BX-1612].
- Rejects the "cycle" [BX-1502] and the bubble thesis [BX-1358].

**Capital allocation.**
- Buybacks are the first shock absorber [BX-1577]. The dividend was kept, and raised, through 2019 [B-1599, B-1631], and suspended only in March 2020 [BX-1629].
- Stock rather than cash for the pension and the 401(k) [BX-1490, BX-1647].
- M&A must meet "financial hurdles" and the complexity of integration [BX-1516]; NPV and ROIC over the whole market [BX-1541].

**Product and strategy.** A CFO's gatekeeper: capability, capacity, affordability [BX-1493]. He deferred the weaker 777-8 [BX-1587] and backed services and verticals for life-cycle value [BX-1481, BX-1499].

**Operations.**
- Suppliers are "an extension of our factories" [BX-1363, BX-1642]. He imposed double-digit cost-downs [BX-1382], and invested to create competition [BX-1496].
- After 2018: "a day matters" at 52 [BX-1531], so more buffer [BX-1622].
- Kept the workforce through the MAX suspension (about $5B of abnormal cost) [BX-1610] and built ahead of demand for supply-chain order [BX-1637].

**Rivals.** He counters price pressure with supplier savings [BX-1406] and passes part of a 3-4% annual productivity gain into price [BX-1427]. He never invokes subsidies or trade action in this evidence.

**Communication.**
- A scorecard style: tells investors which metrics to watch [BX-1344]; contingency in guidance, released as risk is "retired" [BX-1340, BX-1353].
- Judge cash over "an 18- to 24-month period" [BX-1423]. He steers noisy accounting back to cash [BX-1525].
- In an open-ended crisis he withdraws guidance [BX-1578]: "the regulators pace this. We don't pace it" [BX-1598].

**Crisis response.**
1. Scenario planning "from Day 1" [BX-1579].
2. Decisions at "waypoint milestones" [BX-1580].
3. Pull the buyback lever first [BX-1576].
4. Book the liability in one charge and front-load settlements [BX-1592, BX-1619].
5. Ownership: "we own it" [BX-1602]. He rejected the suggestion that safety was traded for cost or schedule: "There's no trade-offs … That's the priority" [BX-1599].
6. Quality ahead of deliveries in the 787 inspections [BX-1659].

Counter-evidence: by 2020 he planned 777X certification as "not … business as usual" [BX-1625], reversing early 2019 [BX-1567].

**Team.**
- One enterprise score for all executives [BX-1575]; held himself to his own cost target [BX-1523].
- Co-owner of decisions: a monthly NMA stress-test with Muilenburg [BX-1555]; the daily crisis rhythm [BX-1589]. Calhoun: "we will lead the company together" [BX-0205].
- Pushed decisions down to process councils [BX-1654].

## 5. In the game: if Smith is in the room

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03 (base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, fps `cfm_ducted`, no injects). Re-run in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes:** no fps in Turn 1; H1 matches "priority one is MAX … versus the next product" [BX-1624]. Then the H2 year (2029) if every gate passes [BX-1572].
- **His bias is deferral** [BX-1548, BX-1611]. Probe: 2030 costs $1.8-2.1B and Turn 3 (2032) $4.6-5.7B. So "go when we're ready" [BX-1569] in words, 2029 in orders.
- **His best argument:** the stress case [BX-1645]. Slip test against NGSA 2029: 2032 scores -2.70 against -4.03 for 2029. But a 2029 Joint Venture (-3.17) is within $0.11B of Do Nothing (-3.06), so 2029 passes the go/no-go test.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **His lens is capital at risk:** partner where it lowers risk [BX-1573, BX-1538]. But the one deal of his era never closed [B-1674].
- Probe, slip test: the Joint Venture beats Solo by $1.77B against NGSA 2026 and $0.86B against NGSA 2029. Nominal: Solo leads by $1.61B, $2.74B and $5.47B (NGSA 2026, NGSA 2029, Airbus idle).
- **Rule:** the Joint Venture against an early NGSA; Solo otherwise. Under a crisis inject, launch only through H7's exception (NGSA in development), and then as a Joint Venture.
- **Sharpens doctrine (inference):** "debt not yet repaid" counts as a stress flag, since he would not add debt [BX-1663] and the partner carries 35% of capex [RULES]. Under the $2B cap this bites only against an early NGSA.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **Pushes:** a 2036 Re-engine, with zero overlap [BX-1574]. Probe: +$1.24B over fps alone; $0.2B better than 2034 and $0.5B better than 2030. His strict rule costs nothing.
- His 787 confidence [BX-1588, BX-1627] makes a widebody Do Nothing comfortable.
- **Vetoes:** following an A350 Re-engine. Probe (A350 Re-engine 2029): following costs $2.3-6.5B.

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes:** peak rate is where cash peaks [BX-1416]; about 40% of the cash arrives 18-24 months before delivery [BX-1434]. Probe: +$0.87B with fps 2029.
- **Defers** under a supply-chain crunch, quality escape or FAA scrutiny, as H6 does [BX-1604, BX-1539].
- **Stricter than doctrine (inference):** also defers one turn under a narrowbody demand shock, because the rate must hold for 2-3 years [BX-1424]. Probe: Turns 1-3 lie within $0.36B, so the gate is cheap.
- **Never** because of Airbus [B-1442].

**Cancel.**
- Vetoes cancelling a launched program: he kept the 747 after an $885M charge [BX-1450, B-1093] and the KC-46 through charges, saying "We don't exclude tanker" [BX-1530]. Probe: cancelling in Turn 3 under the slip test costs $10.7B.
- Shelves unlaunched bets fast under stress [BX-1611].

**Engine choice.**
- **fps:** `cfm_ducted`. He would veto `cfm_open_fan` (probe: -$3.6-4.5B), extending his NMA "No big leaps" [BX-1535] to engines **(inference)**.
- `pw_gtf2` scores +$0.5-0.8B, within ε. He said Boeing was "disappointed" with the GE engine [BX-1594] and named CFM as a constraint [B-1440], so he has no loyalty either way **(inference)**; doctrine keeps CFM.
- **Re-engine:** `ge_genx_next`.

**Disclosure.**
- Discloses rate ladders and cash milestones, not new-airplane dates [BX-1446, BX-1644].
- After a slip: one reset, "still aiming … more likely" [BX-1648], with the charge taken at once [BX-1660].
- Under an open-ended inject: no dates; regulators pace it [BX-1598].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **Supplier bottleneck. Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, no overlapping Re-engine. **Rationale and statement:** surge Boeing staff into the supplier [B-1440]; triage case by case [BX-1582]; second sources [BX-1496]; name the supplier [BX-1594]. No attribution to Airbus before exposure.
- **Exposure:** no precedent; factual statements, not trade rhetoric **(inference)**.
- **Poaching:** no order change. He held headcount through downturns [BX-1581] and tied pay to one score [BX-1575].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *What does it do to cash?* He wants the capex and strain components from `whatif` [BX-1453].
2. *What is the stress case?* The slip test and Do Nothing, always [BX-1645, BX-1348].
3. *Is there "concurrent risk"?* Overlap years of fps and Re-engine [BX-1540].
4. *Are we inside control limits?* Live injects and supplier health [BX-1361, BX-1604].
5. *Does every gate pass?* Margins and `early_penalty_pp_per_year` from `rules`; `programs[].margin_at_eis` and capex from whatif [BX-1518].

**What changes his mind:** a failed gate [BX-1572]; a lead-time window forcing the call [BX-1620]; the stress case [BX-1645]; regulators [BX-1625]. **What does not:** Airbus rate talk [B-1442], oil [BX-1429], currency [BX-1454], customers' preferred dates [BX-1569].
