# W. James (Jim) McNerney: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.** Chairman, President and CEO from mid-2005 (first item July 2006) until July 2015, when Dennis Muilenburg became CEO and he stayed Chairman [BX-0886]. Transcripts from October 2009 mislabel him "Former Executive Chairman"; he was CEO.

**Era.**
- **What he inherited:**
  - "an era of management turmoil" [BX-0580];
  - a 787 selling at record pace [B-0031], on a global risk-sharing supply model that he championed for its cash economics [BX-0562, B-0024].
- **What happened on his watch:**
  - 787 first delivery slipped from May 2008 to September 2011 [BX-0574, BX-0671];
  - the 2008 strike [BX-0608];
  - the 2009 recession cuts [BX-0621, BX-0616];
  - the tanker protest and re-win [BX-0601, B-0594];
  - the 737 MAX in 2011 [BX-0715];
  - the 787-10 and 777X launches [B-0807, B-0848];
  - the battery grounding [BX-0781];
  - 15 rate increases in four years [B-1042];
  - large buybacks [BX-0825, BX-0853].

**Evidence base.**
- 330 items in his own words (BX-0557 to BX-0886) from 43 calls, investor days and conferences, 2006-07-26 to 2015-07-22, densest in 2011-13.
- 483 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.** High on rates, product, Airbus and voice; Medium on decision rights; Low on the Chairman period.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Keep commitments, to customers and investors alike.**
   - "The best customer service is to deliver on your promises" [BX-0576].
   - Financial commitments are "as important as" customer ones [BX-0577].
2. **"Growth and productivity simultaneously"** [BX-0569].
   - Productivity covers "stumbles in innovation" [BX-0605].
   - It must stay "slightly bigger than our R&D number" [BX-0859].
3. **Execution: "de-risking the decade"** [BX-0802, B-0363].
4. **Margin and cash inside a market-share "corridor"** [BX-0809]. "We don't let pricing get in the way of margins" [B-0596].
5. **Convert the backlog**: move "from worshiping backlog to managing backlog" [B-0608].
6. **Return cash once it is earned.**
   - Buybacks are "lagging indicators" [BX-0835].
   - About 80% of free cash flow [BX-0857].
7. **Organic growth "first, second and third"**; no "Hail Mary" M&A [BX-0858, BX-0814].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **Re-engine vs clean sheet.**
  - Re-engine only if the new airplane is 10-15 years away [BX-0655], i.e. 2025 or later: doing both puts "our backlog at risk twice" [BX-0672].
  - **Override:** re-engine at once if waiting puts "meaningful market share at risk" [BX-0715].
- **Never two big developments on top of each other** [BX-0676]. R&D should have "no major year-over-year spikes" [BX-0841].
- **Rate increases** need all of these:
  - "at least 2 years of stability" at "95% certainty" [BX-0699, BX-0700];
  - suppliers that are ready and fund their own capacity [BX-0663, B-0268];
  - stability at each step [BX-0818];
  - partner terms locked before the announcement [BX-0823];
  - at least 10 months' lead [BX-0623].
- **Launch gates:**
  - technology, demand, anchor customers and the supply base [BX-0684];
  - about 100 orders [BX-0624];
  - a business case that closes [BX-0865];
  - a production system able to reach the target rate [BX-0722].
- **Kill test:** revenue must outweigh the remaining cost, with a competitive margin [BX-0618].
  - Applied fast to a non-core venture (Connexion) [BX-0558].
  - Applied to the 747-8, which he judged passed [BX-0618, B-0192]; never used to cancel a launched airplane.
- **Never "outrun your board"** [B-0438]: the American Airlines MAX commitment was "pending launch approval" [B-0419]; Albaugh's account is that it was announced before the board met [BX-0043].
- **Fixed price: stated, then broken:** no "big fixed price development programs" (2008) [BX-0607]; the tanker bid (2010) [BX-0656, BX-0707].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | High to 2008, then **Low** | "Bit off more than we could chew" [BX-0611]; "avoid the moonshot, unless we have to" [BX-0839]. Exception: the 777X composite wing [B-0693] |
| Schedule | **High** in public dates; lower after 2011 [BX-0817] | A 787 plan with 20% fewer flight-test hours than the 777: "aggressive but it hangs together" [BX-0585] |
| Balance sheet | **Low** | A cash cushion "in case something does go really, really bad" [BX-0581]; Boeing Capital kept as a backstop [BX-0805]; pensions moved to defined contribution [B-1040]. But buybacks at the cycle peak [BX-0591] |
| Fixed price | **Medium-High**, against his own vow | The KC-46 is "closer to fixed-price development" [BX-0707]; charges followed [BX-0844] |
| Production rate | **Medium, rising** | "A little more aggressive this decade" [BX-0754] |

**Tempo.**
- **Waits until a rival product is "real":** "what the A350 is or isn't" [BX-0575, BX-0658].
- **Denies, then reverses on a core franchise.** "The market will wait for us" in January 2011 [BX-0675] became the MAX about seven months later [BX-0716].
- **Discounts Airbus rate claims** [BX-0876]; his rate gate is supplier readiness [BX-0819].

**Capital allocation.**
- R&D comes down after a peak [BX-0634, BX-0795].
- Boeing Capital held in reserve as a backstop [BX-0805]; customer financing committed when "very manageable" [BX-0711].

**Product stance.**
- **Derivatives that "harvest" matured technology** [BX-0773, BX-0792].
- **The "sweet spot":** "a lot of value but with manageable financial and technical risk" [BX-0723].
- **Partners:** open in principle [BX-0579], but "we delegated too much" [BX-0698], so engineering came back in-house [BX-0685].
- **Engines:** single source, chosen on "the depth and length of their technical maturation" [BX-0753].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- **Claims:** discount them until firm. The A350-1000 was "pretty close" to a paper airplane [BX-0658].
- **Products:** it catches up rather than leapfrogs. The NEO was "closing a gap" [BX-0690]; the A330neo is "a 1970s design with new engines" [BX-0868].
- **Subsidies** let it "take more risks" [BX-0640].
- **Rates:** it ramps harder [BX-0625].
- **Clean sheets:** it has no appetite for one against the 777 [BX-0804].
- **Share:** expect 50-50 in narrowbodies; win in widebodies [BX-0810, BX-0811].

**Voice.**
- "growth and productivity simultaneously, we mean it" [BX-0569]
- "I would rather be paranoid now than deeply disappointed later" [BX-0557]
- "meet demand without getting beyond our headlights" [BX-0610]
- "aggressive, but responsible" [BX-0656]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Confident dates, then serial resets** on new technology [BX-0568, BX-0598, BX-0648].
- **Minimise, then concede:**
  - "a local issue … a local fix" [BX-0626];
  - "part of the plan" [BX-0681];
  - "slightly higher", said only when pressed [BX-0778].
- **Deny, then reverse** [BX-0691, BX-0715].
- **Persist on launched programs:** the 747-8 at zero margin [BX-0645].
- **"Hold rate" optimism on weak lines** [BX-0780, BX-0816].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Era | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2005-07, ambition | Defended outsourcing and confident dates: "we can push the envelope a little bit" [BX-0563] |
| 2007-10, recovery | Recentralised BCA [BX-0620]; bought the Charleston 787 work [BX-0631]; cut back in the recession [BX-0609] |
| 2011-12, pivot | MAX and the rate ramp: "we've booted 2 developments … we are not going to do that again" [BX-0697] |
| 2013-15, harvest | Derivatives, supplier cost-down, buybacks, succession [BX-0792, BX-0797, BX-0857, BX-0886] |

**Priorities.** The ranking tracked the crisis of the moment:
- 2009: the 787 first and financial strength last [BX-0630];
- 2011: development, then rates [BX-0674];
- 2013: the 787 "back into service" [BX-0784];
- 2014: build the backlog out "quickly and profitably" [BX-0836].

Unlike today's "returns off" doctrine, he would restore returns once programs stabilise, as in 2013 [BX-0796] **(inference)**.

**Decision style.**
- **Deliberate.** Rate calls "are big business decisions … not simply a reaction to today's view of the market" [BX-0622].
- **Watches leading indicators:** financeability tracked "a year or 2" out [BX-0762].
- **Leads in person:** "I'm leading it" [BX-0797].
- **Admits lateness:** "we waited too long" [BX-0617].

**Risk.**
- **Internal targets** sit "significantly within the business case" [BX-0572].
- **Development risk causes "lumpy earnings"** [BX-0845].
- **The 787 lesson:** "a bridge too far" [BX-0638]; "the financial characteristics were not fun" [BX-0866].
- **The reference case for rates is 1997** [BX-0705].

**Capital allocation.**
- **Buybacks:**
  - $3B in 2006 [BX-0566];
  - $7B in January 2008, with the 787 already late [BX-0591];
  - $10B in 2013 [BX-0825];
  - $12B in 2014 [BX-0853].
- **Sustained demand gets the capital:** capital would not "stand in our way for a nanosecond" [BX-0752].
- **M&A comes after shareholders and "seed corn"** [BX-0750].

**Product and strategy.**
- **2006-10:** "book-ends" around the A350, and wait [B-0010, BX-0594].
- **2011:** the MAX, "containing the work statement to reduce risk" [BX-0746].
- **2013:**
  - the 787-10, only once the factory was stable, despite demand [BX-0790];
  - the 777X, a new wing and engines on the current airframe [BX-0774].
- **2015:** no middle-of-market airplane [BX-0865].
- **Widebody work before the next narrowbody** [B-0289, B-0447], the reverse of today's doctrine.

**Operations.**
- **Rates:** over-booking is the buffer [BX-0619]; supply-chain readiness is the binding limit [BX-0819].
- **Suppliers:** re-compete or insource [BX-0783]; a "no fly list" [BX-0798]; volume traded for margin [BX-0863].
- **Labour:**
  - held out for management rights in 2008 [BX-0608];
  - Charleston to diversify labour risk [BX-0637];
  - "prepared to move the work" [BX-0828].
- **Builds through problems** [BX-0782, BX-0712].

**Rivals.**
- **Walks away** from Ryanair-type deals [BX-0724].
- **Matches profitably:** at American, "aggressively priced, but not irresponsibly priced" [BX-0725].
- **Prices high where uncontested:** "shamelessly" on the 777 [BX-0856, BX-0883].
- **No price war** [BX-0813].
- **Litigates:** a GAO protest [BX-0601] and the WTO [BX-0657].

**Communication.**
- **Prudent financial guidance:** "not because we're trying to be sandbaggers" [BX-0861].
- **No victory claims:** "never to declare victory" [BX-0815]; "we don't mention commitments unless things are largely done" [BX-0870].
- **Candid in hindsight:** "the baseline was too ambitious" [BX-0628]; "a wildebeest" [BX-0698].

**Crisis response.**
1. Re-plan with margin and embed staff at suppliers [BX-0586, B-0073].
2. Change the leader or the structure [BX-0590, BX-0620].
3. In a downturn, protect the balance sheet and cut costs [BX-0609].
4. In a safety event, defer to investigators and refuse hypotheticals [BX-0781, BX-0777, BX-0789].
5. Hold the production plan [BX-0782].

**Team.**
- **Mandate:** fix leadership "software" [BX-0580].
- **CFO:** Bell called the pair "conservative" [BX-0100].
- **BCA heads:**
  - rate calls shared with Albaugh [BX-0692], who was sent to BCA to "catch his own punt" [BX-0709];
  - backed Conner on 777 pricing [BX-0883].
- **Accountability:** shared [BX-0642]; the top bears "equal brunt" of cuts [BX-0659].
- **Incentives:** reward cost and productivity as highly as a new wing [BX-0884].
- **Succession:** Muilenburg as COO [BX-0830]. The test he set: business fundamentals "as strong as the technical" [BX-0869].

## 5. In the game: if McNerney is in the room

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03 (base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, no injects); re-run in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes:** no fps until the gates close: production system [BX-0722], anchor customers, and a business case that closes in whatif [BX-0865]. H1 suits him **(inference)**.
- **Trigger (inference):** NGSA in development read as "meaningful market share at risk" [BX-0715]; in 2011 that override meant re-engining, not a new airplane. He would deny in that turn and launch the next.
- **Vetoes:** an EIS before tech_ready_year, from his technology-readiness logic [BX-0654] **(inference)**. A clean sheet without a closed case is a "moonshot" [BX-0838].

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **Default:** Solo, keeping systems engineering in-house after the "wildebeest" [BX-0698, BX-0685].
- **When NGSA forces an early launch:** the Joint Venture, sharing capex as in his 2006 risk-sharing logic [BX-0562] **(inference)**.
- **Probe** (with a 2036 Re-engine; Delay Tactics in Turns 2-3): the Joint Venture beats Solo by $2.0B against NGSA 2026 and $1.1B against NGSA 2029, and loses by $1.6B with Airbus idle.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **Pushes:** a harvesting derivative, as with the 777X, whose wing and engines carried over 90% of its value [B-0762].
- **His rule, "make sure they don't come together"** [BX-0676], puts the Re-engine after fps development. Probe: 2036 beats 2034 by $0.2B, within ε, so display it.
- **Must not display:** his widebody-first instinct [B-0289]. A Re-engine in 2030 with fps in 2032 costs $3.9-5.0B against fps 2029 plus a 2034 Re-engine, above the $2B cap.
- **Vetoes:** following an A350 Re-engine. He answered the A330neo with value pricing, not a product [BX-0848, BX-0868]. Probe (NGSA and A350 Re-engine in 2029): following costs $2.3-3.5B.

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes:** commit once suppliers are ready, with "a bias to production increases" [BX-0799]. In 2009 he held the narrowbody rate and cut weak lines early [B-0199, BX-0621], so he would keep it through a demand shock **(inference)**.
- **Vetoes:** it in a supply-chain-crunch turn [BX-0819], and as an answer to Airbus rates, which he discounts [BX-0876].
- **Differs from H6:** he kept the 787 ramp through the 2013 grounding [BX-0782], so he would argue to keep a Rate Increase through a quality escape **(inference)**. H6 is a hard rule for every team: the instinct goes into the public statement or a logged note, never into the orders.

**Cancel.**
- **Never cancel** a launched program [B-0192].
- **The kill test** applies [B-0153].
- **End a legacy line** while demand still exceeds supply [BX-0874].

**Engine choice.**
- **fps:** `cfm_ducted`, on its maturity [BX-0753].
  - He rejected Pratt & Whitney for the MAX [B-0600].
  - Probe: `cfm_open_fan` costs $3.6-4.5B.
- **787 Re-engine:** `ge_genx_next`, the doctrine default (Rolls-Royce is within ε). His GE pick for the 777X [BX-0791] fits it **(inference)**.

**Disclosure.**
- **Launches:** disclosed only with an order book in hand: 102 commitments for the 787-10, 259 for the 777X [B-0807, B-0848]. Before that, he offers "conditional offerability" [B-0691].
- **Rates:** disclosed once supplier terms are locked [BX-0823].
- **Targets against Airbus:** none [B-0060].
- **EIS dates:** stated firmly in public (bias B1) [BX-0574, BX-0759].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **A "supplier bottleneck."**
  - **Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, and launch no overlapping Re-engine.
  - **Statement and rationale only:** embedded staff, re-competition, the "no fly list" [B-0073, BX-0783, BX-0798]. No attribution to Airbus before exposure.
- **Once exposed:** a "level playing field" complaint and litigation, in statements [B-0017, BX-0601].
- **Poaching:** no order change. In statements: engineers moved across units [B-0059]; "we don't cut too deep" [BX-0679].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *What is NGSA, or isn't it?* He wants the event log and firm orders [BX-0575].
2. *Are we "putting meaningful market share at risk by waiting"?* He wants whatif share paths for launching vs waiting [BX-0715].
3. *Do two developments pile up?* He wants the overlap years and the strain cost [BX-0841].
4. *Does the case close inside our internal targets?* He wants the `options` best response and the `whatif` nominal and slip-test numbers [BX-0572].
5. *Is the supply base ready, two years at 95%?* He checks the injects [BX-0700].

**What changes his mind:** customer evidence, as with American Airlines [BX-0716]; a deeper technical review [BX-0717]; supplier readiness. **What does not:** Airbus rate claims [BX-0876], press reports [BX-0681], analysts' upside cases [BX-0567].
