# Brian J. West: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.** Executive Vice President of Finance and CFO, in items from 2021-10-27 to 2025-07-29 [BX-1666, BX-1874]. Calhoun hired him from outside in July 2021 for "broad operational expertise" [BX-0293].
- **The "2019-2025" label is not supported.** Nothing in the evidence predates October 2021, and the 2019-21 CFO was someone else [BX-0221].
- Jay Malave was CFO by September 2025 [BX-1316].

**Era.**
- **What he inherited:** 2021 free cash flow of -$4B, after -$20B in 2020 [BX-1726]; 787 deliveries halted, with 110 aircraft in inventory [B-1855]; 335 MAX in inventory [B-1861]; $58.1B of debt [B-1863].
- **Under Calhoun (to August 2024):**
  - positive free cash flow in 2022 [BX-1726];
  - the Investor Day target of $10B FCF in 2025-26 [BX-1718];
  - Spirit quality escapes [BX-1747, BX-1762];
  - the Alaska 1282 door plug and the FAA cap [BX-1777].
- **Under Ortberg:**
  - the IAM strike [BX-1836];
  - 2024 free cash flow of -$14.3B [B-2232];
  - a $24B capital raise [B-2231];
  - the $10.55B Digital Aviation Solutions sale [BX-1867];
  - the 737 at 38 a month in May 2025 [BX-1872].

**Evidence base.**
- 209 items in his own words (BX-1666 to BX-1874), from 28 events: 16 earnings calls, 11 conferences and the 2022 Investor Day.
- 151 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.** High on capital allocation, liquidity, rate gating, disclosure and his track record. Medium on new-airplane gates (qualitative only). Low on Airbus, engines, Joint Ventures and decision rights.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked (his terms).**
1. **A stable factory:** "The path to stable financials is a stable factory" [BX-1801]. The productivity gain is "dwarfed by the quality benefit" [BX-1788].
2. **The investment-grade rating, as a constraint and not a trade-off:** "the #1 priority ... we're going to do what it takes to protect that rating, period" [BX-1824].
3. **Free cash flow,** "the #1 metric ... through the lens of all of our decision-making" [BX-1694].
4. **Finish development programs:** "-7, -10, 777X ... that's job one" [BX-1863].
5. **Invest within the envelope:** R&D rises every year [BX-1734]; capex planned to rise about $500M in 2025 while the company was still burning cash [BX-1847].
6. **A new airplane only as a "step function"**, 20-30% better [BX-1740, BX-1803].
7. **Shareholder returns:** "too early ... it'll be there someday" [BX-1737].

**How he sharpens the company doctrine.**
- **The rating is a hard floor.** He will change the funding instrument to hold it [BX-1816, BX-1842].
- **He plans on the downside, where doctrine plans on nominal dates and caps the downside.** He builds contingency into targets [BX-1775], plans without China [BX-1763] and sets "a conservative plan" [BX-1868]. In the game he would treat the slip test as the planning case **(inference)**.
- **He reads Airbus less than doctrine does.** Demand is a given; the contest is on supply [BX-1789].
- **His deny-then-reverse is on capital structure, not product** [BX-1795, BX-1842].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **Rates follow supply, not demand:** "a function of our confidence in our supply chain, not the demand signals" [BX-1690]. Proof points first [BX-1722]; then the six FAA-agreed KPIs [BX-1860, B-2283].
- **Don't rush:** "We can't push the factory too hard" [BX-1820]. Boeing itself chose to stay below the FAA's cap of 38 [BX-1799].
- **No traveled work:** accept only fully conforming fuselages [BX-1800].
- **Keep the supply chain hot:** "no intention of changing the master schedule" [BX-1762]. He reversed this only when cash became binding [BX-1838].
- **No fixed-price development:** "no appetite" [BX-1742]; none signed [BX-1774].
- **Liquidity:** a floor of about $10B of cash plus revolvers [BX-1843]; maturities pre-funded 12, later 18, months ahead [BX-1808, BX-1831].
- **Growth must be "accretive to profit" and "relatively capital light"** [BX-1728, BX-1793].
- **New airplane:** it needs advanced engines [BX-1803], nothing niche [BX-1740], current certifications finished first [BX-1863], and spending "within the envelope" [BX-1792, B-2279].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | "Advanced engine technology" for 20-30%; "will not be rushed" [BX-1803] |
| Schedule | **Low** in plans, **High** in public dates | Holds rates back [BX-1799], yet the 777X date was reset twice on his watch, then again by his successor (§3) |
| Balance sheet | **Medium** | "If inventory spikes ... we can handle it" [BX-1790]; the rating is the floor [BX-1824] |
| Fixed price | **Very low** | Legacy programs aim only to break even [BX-1854] |

**Tempo.**
- **Does not move on rival moves.** He disputes the premise [BX-1743].
- **Waits a long time, then switches.** China slots were deferred "for a long time", then remarketed [BX-1706, BX-1756].
- **Cuts in steps:** "high conviction on the low end" [BX-1748], then "the low end" [BX-1771], then a cut [BX-1767].
- **Shocks:** cash conservation from the strike's first day [BX-1836].
- **Capital structure: reversed in about seven months.**
  - "we would not use equity" (March 2024) [BX-1795];
  - "Nothing is off the table" (May) [BX-1816];
  - an equity offering (October) [BX-1842].

**Capital allocation stance.** Excess cash goes to debt [BX-1714]. He buys stability with working capital: buffers [BX-1741], $87.5B of inventory [BX-1852], cash for Spirit [BX-1747]. Returns are for the Board [BX-1759].

**Product stance.**
- **Clean sheet:** "next decade" (2023) [BX-1740], then "no timetable" for a 50-year product (2025) [BX-1864].
- **Derivatives as cash tools:** "We don't need the 10" [BX-1684]; more 8s and 9s [BX-1815]; 777Fs during the 777-9 pause [BX-1685].
- **Programs are J-curves:** the 777X turns cash-positive "about a year after EIS" [BX-1830].
- **Integration:** rejected in 2023 [BX-1739]; adopted in 2024 to "run it as a factory" [BX-1802].
- **Engines:** the LEAP was "the real gating item", and "CFM has been very collaborative" [BX-1710].

**How he reads Airbus.** Rarely by name.
- He disputed an analyst's premise of Airbus narrowbody ascendancy: the MAX "holds its own" [BX-1743].
- Slips across the industry help pricing, but "every campaign, we compete head to head" [BX-1751].
- "The product line holds up really well competitively" [BX-1822].

**Voice.**
- "Deliver airplanes, generate cash, pay down debt." [BX-1720]
- "The path to stable financials is a stable factory." [BX-1801]
- "We can't rush. We can't push the factory too hard or the system too hard" [BX-1820]
- "the investment grade is the #1 priority ... period." [BX-1824]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **W1. Hold the target, then move it "to the right".** Reaffirm it once ("no what-ifs" [BX-1731]; unchanged after a Spirit escape [BX-1761]), then concede that it "moved to the right quite a bit" [BX-1834].
- **W2. First cost estimates come in low.** The 787's abnormal costs went from $1B to $3B [BX-1668, BX-1769]. Privately budget for cost growth **(inference)**.
- **W3. Reframe in cash terms:** "cash margins ... remain positive" [BX-1672]; "immaterial" [BX-1749].
- **W4. Pledge, then reverse** under pressure on the rating [BX-1795, BX-1842].
- **W5. Look only at supply.** Under-react to Airbus product moves in statements [BX-1789] **(inference)**.
- **Counter-evidence:**
  - the 2022-23 cash commitments were kept [BX-1726, BX-1753];
  - one estimate was cut [BX-1752];
  - the conservative 2025 plan was beaten [BX-0552].

## 4. Sections by dimension

| Era | Dates | Stance |

| Turnaround (Calhoun) | Oct 2021-Dec 2023 | Cash as the lens; dated targets; no-equity pledges |
| Door plug (Calhoun) | Jan-Aug 2024 | Stability over financials; no guidance; Spirit brought back in-house |

**Priorities.**
- 2022: three milestones: 787s, 737s including China, and cash [BX-1693]. "Return value" meant the balance sheet [BX-1724].
- September 2024: recover commercial, derisk defense, then an investment-grade capital structure [BX-1839].

**Decision style.** Milestones, not dates: "the first step is getting the first one out the door" [BX-1677]. One rate step at a time: "let that take care of itself" [B-2040].

**Risk.**
- **The fixed-price lesson hardened** from "took the medicine, move on" (2022) [BX-1696] to "no appetite" (2023) [BX-1742].
- He said the risk had been retired; more charges followed [BX-1733].

**Capital allocation.** Capex of $1-1.5B (2022) [BX-1686] grew to $5B of R&D plus capex, up 20%+ (2023) [BX-1792]. In 2022 he held $7-9B of cash above baseline [B-1956].

**Product and strategy.**
- "We're going to make an airplane someday ... but it's not right now" [B-2277].
- Slower certification is "the new reality. We are going to resource it" [B-2032].

**Operations.**
- **Supply binds, not demand** [BX-1707]. The supply chain went from "not a constraint" [BX-1678] to the main explanation for misses.
- **Fixes:** on-site teams and safety stock [BX-1700].
- **Cost model:** parts are 60-70% of an airplane, labor under 15% [BX-1787]; the IAM settlement is "less than 5%" [BX-1851].
- **China:** redirect its aircraft to other customers rather than cut rates [BX-1871].

**Rivals.** Price discipline while slots are scarce [B-2044]. C919: "not a new competitive entry ... Let's go compete and win" [BX-1744].

**Communication.** "it's a turnaround, make no mistake about it" [BX-1703]; "I promise you" in place of numbers [BX-1673]; transparency, "sometimes it's painful" [BX-1735].

**Crisis response.**
- **787 halt:** "the right thing to do for our future" [BX-1676]; "FAA decides" [BX-1689].
- **Door plug:** "full accountability ... we welcome it" [BX-1786]; "all of that wasn't enough" [BX-1798].
- **Strike:** cuts to supplier spend and the workforce [BX-1846].

**Team.**
- He backed Calhoun's small center [BX-1697, BX-1745] and "more engineers, more mechanics" [BX-1746]. Calhoun credited his CFO with "the same intensity" about "knowing everything" [BX-0328].
- He named the cultural fault: schedule had come before conformance [BX-1805].
- He left bargaining to the teams [BX-1806] and aligned with Ortberg [BX-1841].

## 5. In the game: if West is in the room

Engine figures: a scratch probe, 2026-10-03, base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, `cfm_ducted`, no injects. The "slip" case adds Airbus Delay Tactics in Turns 2-3. Re-run in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes for gates, not a year:**
  - the 777X delivered (2027 [BX-0550]);
  - the MAX 7/10 certified (2026 [BX-1873]);
  - the rating secure [BX-1824];
  - spending "within the envelope" [BX-1792, B-2279].

  The engine models none of these: treat them as closed by Turn 2 unless a `certification_scrutiny` inject is live, so he accepts 2029 **(inference)**.
- **"Next decade" [BX-1740] read as 2030** would cost $1.8-2.1B, and H2 keeps it out of the orders; he had dropped the marker by 2025 [BX-1864].
- **Vetoes:** any Turn-1 launch, H1's exception included: it "will not be rushed" [BX-1803]. Also any launch in a strike or crisis turn [BX-1836] **(inference)**.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **His lens:** with Airbus idle, the Joint Venture cuts alpha-loaded capex in PV from $23.8B to $16.0B but gives away $13.3B of operating value [probe]. A CFO who protects the rating "period" sees the saving first **(inference)**.
- **The slip case decides:**

  | Airbus | Solo | Joint Venture | Do Nothing | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Idle | 6.83 | 4.99 | 0.07 | Solo |
  | NGSA 2026 | -8.10 | -6.33 | -4.37 | Joint Venture; only it passes the $2B slip leg, by $0.04B (fragile) |
  | NGSA 2029 | -4.03 | -3.17 | -3.06 | Joint Venture by 0.86, within ε |

- **Rule:**
  - Joint Venture when NGSA was launched in 2026-27: the nominal cost is $1.6-2.0B, within the cap.
  - Otherwise Solo. The nominal cost of the Joint Venture is $2.74B against NGSA 2029 and $5.47B against an idle Airbus.
  - His added stress flag: a crisis inject in the launch turn while debt is unrepaired [BX-1842] **(inference)**.
  - Counter-weight: he insourced Spirit for control [BX-1862].

**787 Re-engine.**
- **Vetoes it in Turns 1-2.** The 777X comes first [BX-1863]. The 787 is a cash engine to ramp: 10 a month [BX-1809]; cash margins forecast above 2018 levels by 2025-26 [BX-1783], below them near term [BX-1835].
- **Probe:**
  - 2034 adds +1.03 to a 2029 fps; 2032 adds +0.78; 2029 is a wash.
  - Alone in 2030, it adds +2.90.
  - Following an A350 Re-engine of 2029 costs $3.5-5.0B: never.
- He asks when the cash turns [BX-1830].

**Rate Increase.**
- **Turn 1, yes.** 38 → 50 is the "biggest mover" toward $10B [B-2086, BX-1811].
  - Probe: +0.86 with fps; Turn 1 beats Turn 2 by 0.31.
- **Never in response to Airbus or demand** [BX-1690].
- **Defer it** in a quality, FAA or supply-crunch turn [BX-1799].
- **When deferring:** keep suppliers on the master schedule [BX-1773]; stop those holding safety stock if cash binds [BX-1838].

**Cancel.**
- **Never a launched program.** He paused the 777-9 instead of cancelling it [BX-1681]: it is "going to be on for decades" [BX-1695].
- **Probe:** cancelling a slipped Solo fps in Turn 3 against NGSA 2026 scores -16.05, against -8.10 for continuing.
- He prunes only non-core assets [BX-1867].

**Engine choice.** `cfm_ducted`, by doctrine; it adds no development years. He called CFM's engines "the real gating item" on the 737, but CFM "very collaborative" [BX-1710]; that he stays with CFM is **(inference)**.
- `pw_gtf2` scores +0.48 to +0.82, within ε.
- `cfm_open_fan` loses $3.6-4.5B: veto.

**Disclosure.**
- **No regulator-gated dates:** "out of respect for the process ... steer away from specificity" [BX-1779].
- **Ranges with a defensible floor** [BX-1715].
- **Owns harm early:** "we will, at times, frustrate both our customers and our shareholders" [BX-1817].
- **At an fps launch:** the 20-30% bar [BX-1803], the 50-year horizon [BX-1864] and "within the envelope" [B-2279].
- Never names Airbus [BX-1822].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **A "supplier bottleneck." Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, no overlapping Re-engine. **Rationale only:** staff and fund the supplier [BX-1747]; hold the schedule [BX-1762]; carry buffers [BX-1861]; insource if it recurs [BX-1802].
- **After exposure:** a factual statement only **(inference)**.
- **Poaching:** no order change; in statements, hiring (15,000 people in 2022 [B-2035]).

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *When does the cash turn?* He wants `whatif` capex and strain [BX-1694, BX-1830].
2. *Does the rating survive?* No overlap strain **(inference)**.
3. *What if not every piece goes right?* He wants the slip test [BX-1775].
4. *Is the supply chain ready?* He checks the injects [BX-1690].
5. *Step function or niche?* He wants tech_ready_year [BX-1740].

**What changes his mind:** a binding constraint, such as the strike's cash drain [BX-1838] or a threat to the rating [BX-1842]; evidence that keeps failing [BX-1706].

**What does not:** demand signals [BX-1690], analysts' premises about Airbus [BX-1743] or upside cases [BX-1764].
