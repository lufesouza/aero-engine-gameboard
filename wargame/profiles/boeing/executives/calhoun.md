# David L. (Dave) Calhoun: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.** President, CEO and Director from January 2020 until he handed over to Kelly Ortberg in August 2024 (items 2020-01-29 to 2024-07-31) [BX-0193, BX-0415]; before that a long-time director, "a front-row seat" [BX-0194].

**Era.**
- **What he inherited:** a grounded MAX with about 450 aircraft parked [B-1892]; 737 production suspended [B-1609]; an FAA that had told Boeing to "stop with your predictions" [BX-0345]; a pending NMA and Embraer Joint Venture [B-1611].
- **What happened on his watch:**
  - COVID and a $25B bond raise [BX-0350]; the Embraer exit, the NMA shelved, the 747 ended [BX-0216, BX-0217, BX-0227];
  - the MAX ungrounded in November 2020 [BX-0222]; 787 deliveries stopped for over a year [B-2134]; three 777X slips [B-1694, BX-0239, BX-0311];
  - engine castings capping the 737 rate [BX-0331]; positive free cash flow in 2022 [BX-0359];
  - the Alaska 1282 door plug, the FAA cap of 38 a month [BX-0402], and the Spirit buy-back [BX-0414].

**Evidence base.**
- 223 items in his own words (BX-0193 to BX-0415) from 24 events: earnings calls, the 2021 shareholder meeting, conferences and the November 2022 investor day.
- 274 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.** High on rates, new-airplane gates, Airbus, crisis response and voice. Medium on capital thresholds. Low on decision rights and on Joint Ventures for a new airplane.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Safety and quality "above all else"; the regulator decides** [BX-0202, BX-0403].
2. **Stability as the differentiator:** "stability is the watch word … We want to be predictable" [BX-0334]. It ranks above share [BX-0326].
3. **Free cash flow, "our #1 metric"** [BX-0306], to repay crisis debt [B-1934]: reinvest, then debt, then dividends [BX-0248].
4. **Return on capital:** "We're not in the value destruction game" [BX-0260].
5. **Finish the derivatives:** "We'll have a 7. We'll have a 10. We'll have a 777 family" [B-1947].
6. **"Rightful share", not 50-50:** "If stable is at 40 without China, then that's what we want to do" [BX-0358, BX-0258].
7. **A new airplane only as a step change, in the mid-2030s** [BX-0355, B-1942].

**How he sharpens the company doctrine.** He states the share trade-off openly where doctrine says "not at any price"; he would put fps a year later, which H2 keeps out of the orders (§5); he is more negative on widebody development; and the NGSA trigger moves him less.

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **New-airplane gates:**
  - at least 20% better, "hopefully … closer to 30%", in efficiency and emissions [BX-0375];
  - "It can't be a niche" [BX-0377];
  - a "sustainability wrapper" [BX-0356];
  - digital tools proven first: "We don't do it the other way around" [B-1901, BX-0269];
  - in service "not before '35" [BX-0376]; "not … to the drawing board this decade" [B-1977].
- **Rates:**
  - "plan for rate increases, but only pull the trigger when the supply chain is ready" [BX-0321];
  - the trigger is demonstrated weekly engine deliveries: "we'll announce at that day" [BX-0352];
  - "We won't rate up … until it is under control" [BX-0406].
- **No traveled work.** A paused line is "a badge of courage" [BX-0353, BX-0354].
- **Never press the regulator on timing** [BX-0268, B-2099].
- **No fixed-price development:** "never do them again" [BX-0378].
- **No equity for liquidity** [BX-0317].
- **Never abandon a launched airplane:** "I don't care, how many charges we take" [BX-0320].
- **Plan without upside:** guidance assumes "the best things don't happen" [BX-0366], and China is excluded [BX-0370].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | Next engines give "around the 10% at the high end" [BX-0357]; open rotor is "not a short putt" [BX-0382] |
| Schedule | **Low** in plans, **Medium** in public dates | Pads plans for regulator pauses [BX-0302], yet many public dates missed (§3) |
| Balance sheet | **Medium** | $25B of debt, not equity [BX-0316]; spare capacity [B-2081]; ~$1B for Wisk "when you're starving for cash" [B-1905] |
| Fixed price | **Very low** | "That fixed-price development world has to stop" [BX-0378], though he defended the T-7A and MQ-25 bids [BX-0324] |
| Production rate | **Low** | "Every metric gets better when you slow things down" [BX-0412] |

**Tempo.**
- **Does not answer rival products:** "why would I rush? No good reason" [BX-0322]; "1 year or 2 years more in the market to learn more is not going to hold us back" [BX-0242].
- **Cuts structure fast in a downturn:** the Embraer exit and a ~10% workforce cut were both announced in April 2020 [BX-0216, BX-0215].
- **Holds, then reverses when the evidence fails:** China slots held in April 2022, remarketed in October [BX-0313, BX-0339]; the MAX 7 exemption withdrawn [BX-0401]; 130,000 headcount became 140,000 [BX-0229, B-1823].
- **No deny-then-reverse on product (inference):** no Airbus move changed his new-airplane stance.

**Capital allocation stance.**
- R&D protected but flat; development money goes to certifying derivatives [BX-0253, BX-0282].
- Deleverage to "close" to the 2017-18 balance sheet [BX-0372], then "I want nothing more than to return money to you" [BX-0343].
- Prunes return-blind targets and bets: the $50B services target [BX-0344], supersonic [BX-0270], low-value services lines [BX-0261].

**Product stance.**
- **Derivatives now:** MAX 7/10, the 777X and a 777X freighter [BX-0288].
- **A clean sheet is a 50-year bet** with "massive" losses if wrong [B-2060]; his candidate is the Transonic Truss-Braced Wing [B-2077].
- **Partners:** walked away from Embraer [BX-0216]; insourced Spirit to "course correct the decision made decades ago" [BX-0414].
- **Engines:** "heavily weighted to one of those suppliers" (CFM) [B-2047]; wants "a bigger fan diameter ultimately and maybe even open rotor someday", but "I don't want to make choices too early" [BX-0386].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- **Rough parity:** "niches where we have advantage and niches where the other guy has advantages" [B-2520].
- **The A321:** concedes its route advantage [BX-0234], but the XLR is "not a meaningful competitor" [BX-0387].
- **Its rate announcements** create "trouble for our supply chain" [BX-0277]; supply chains, not plans, set output [BX-0381]. Pricing is "pretty disciplined" [BX-0365]; delivery slots decide campaigns [B-2041].
- **Widebodies:** "we'll just go up against that all day long" (A350-1000) [B-2051]; the A350F is "a direct competitor" [BX-0278].
- **China** wants "an even split" [BX-0379], and "a single provider from France" cannot meet its widebody needs [BX-0341].
- **COMAC** reaches at most 10% by 2050 [BX-0380].

**Voice.**
- "Getting decisions right is way more important than getting them fast" [BX-0200]
- "We won't predict timing. We won't get ahead of our regulator. We will go slow to go fast" [BX-0395]
- "late discussion on trouble is worse than trouble itself" [BX-0318]
- "This is Boeing getting tough on Boeing" [BX-0286]
- "It can't be a niche … there is no return" [BX-0377]
- "I can't give you certainty until I'm certain" [BX-0294]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **No regulator dates, but optimistic delivery dates** (B1 variant): the "lion's share" of 787 inventory [BX-0263]; 450 737s [BX-0367]; "this moment will accelerate recovery" [BX-0397].
- **Recasts setbacks as strength:** the 777X slip came from "a position of strength" [BX-0311]; quality escapes show "exactly the opposite" of a lost step [BX-0388]; the Spirit escape was "a signal of a healthy supply chain" [BX-0369]. Counter: plain ownership in 2024 [BX-0399, BX-0398].
- **Patience that cedes share** [BX-0258, BX-0358].
- **Sunk-cost persistence** on launched programs [BX-0274, BX-0320].
- **Points to Airbus's own troubles under pressure, then commits to improvement** (one instance) [BX-0254]. Counter: [BX-0399, BX-0398].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Era | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2020, triage | MAX first, then liquidity: dividend suspended, buyback ended [BX-0207]; managed to "unit margins, cash margins" [BX-0226] |
| 2021, stability over speed | "3 mountains": the MAX, COVID, China [BX-0272]; 787 halt; NMA deferred to sharpen the "point design" [BX-0232] |
| 2022-23, cash and ramp | FCF first; engine-gated rates; investor-day targets; 2035 airplane [BX-0347, BX-0376] |
| 2024, door plug | Quality above cash; no guidance; Spirit insourced; succession [BX-0403, BX-0395, BX-0414] |

**Priorities.** Survive each crisis "while not diminishing our future competitiveness" [BX-0244]; "I've never appreciated slow growth more in my life" [BX-0273]. China is "the difference between commercial aerospace leadership or not" [BX-0333], yet he planned without it [BX-0370].

**Decision style.**
- **Evidence-gated:** he sized the debt raise from 9/11 and SARS data [BX-0350].
- **Patient by design:** "patience. A word I bet you've never heard from a CEO" [BX-0351].
- **Sequences before choosing:** "I'm sequencing these 2 programs before I call out a point design" [BX-0269].
- **Capacity early, rates late:** a fourth line "a year or 2" early [BX-0374].

**Risk.**
- **Schedules include regulator time,** which "prior to the 737 MAX recert, we would never have included" [BX-0302].
- **Asymmetric:** no rate above 31 without clarity on China, because "if I … get it wrong, it's a big wrong" [BX-0276].
- **Development is where mistakes last:** "you pay it for a long time" [BX-0410].

**Capital allocation.** Liquidity first: "We went big, we raised $25 billion" [BX-0316]. About $5B of structural cost cuts, to accrue "to our airplanes" [BX-0236].

**Product and strategy.** Engines will not carry the next airplane, so value must come from "the way it's engineered and the way it's manufactured" [BX-0257]. "I don't want to fill a gap in a product line" [BX-0355].

**Operations.**
- **Inventory first:** "move the finished goods first" [BX-0225]; the rate is "hostage" to inventory [B-1727].
- **Suppliers stay on the master schedule** through Boeing-side disruptions [BX-0368, BX-0409]: "rates solve most of the supply chain's problems" [BX-0393].
- **Quality pushed upstream:** inspection in Wichita; only clean fuselages shipped [BX-0407].
- **Contested record:** his January 2023 no-traveled-work claim [B-1992] gave way to the 2024 crackdown [BX-0408].

**Rivals.** He competes on product, not price: "we let the airplanes perform and sell itself" (200 of 270 at United) [BX-0292]. He backs trade enforcement against launch aid [BX-0291].

**Communication.** Self-critical: "I apologize for guessing that the last issue was our last" [BX-0284]. Guidance resumed in November 2022 after nearly three years [BX-0346] and was withdrawn again in January 2024 [BX-0395].

**Crisis response.**
1. **Own it at once:** "We caused the problem" [BX-0399]; "Boeing is accountable" [BX-0398].
2. **Stop the line or deliveries** and pay the schedule cost [BX-0243, BX-0255].
3. **Defer to the regulator:** "I wish I had called that out in the first day" [BX-0400].
4. **Fix quality while demand is slack** [BX-0289], and **keep the destination** (rate 50) [BX-0405].

**Team.**
- **CFO as operating partner:** Smith also ran manufacturing and supply chain [BX-0221]; the next CFO was picked for operating experience [BX-0281, BX-0293].
- **Engineering under one leader** reporting to him [BX-0252]; operators back in the businesses [BX-0327]; safety and quality in incentives [BX-0251].
- **Succession:** "not … a large leadership overhaul" [BX-0415].

## 5. In the game: if Calhoun is in the room

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03, base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, `cfm_ducted`, no injects. Re-run them in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Prefers 2030**, from "not … to the drawing board this decade" [B-1977] and EIS "not before '35" [BX-0376].
- **Orders: 2029.** H2 is a hard rule; the preference never enters the orders.
  - **Log it:** "Calhoun 2030 preference: $1.8-2.1B" (probe: $2.11B with Airbus idle, $1.80B against NGSA 2026, $2.12B against NGSA 2029).
  - **Voice it** in `public_statement` as a conservative EIS; "not … this decade" stays in the rationale, since it contradicts a 2029 launch.
  - The slip test agrees: against NGSA 2026 a 2030 Joint Venture would fail the slip leg (-6.84 vs Do Nothing -4.37); the 2029 one passes (-6.33).
- **Vetoes:** the H1 exception (fps in 2028): "why would I rush?" [BX-0322]; accelerating for NGSA, which he would read as no step change (engines give ≤10% [BX-0357]) **(inference)**; any launch in a crisis turn [BX-0403], subject to the H8 cap where H7's exception applies.

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **Solo by default (inference).** No statement on a development Joint Venture; the Embraer exit was over unmet deal conditions [BX-0216], and Spirit [BX-0414] and seats [B-2055] were supplier insourcing.
- **A Joint Venture only on the slip test.** With Delay Tactics in Turns 2-3, the Joint Venture beats Solo by $1.8-1.9B against NGSA 2026 and $0.9-1.1B against NGSA 2029; it loses by $1.5-1.8B with Airbus idle.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **Vetoes it** while the product line is "loaded" [B-1947]; the 787 already holds "a strong advantage" [B-1897]. **(inference)** He would extend to a Re-engine his view that new-propulsion gains are "narrow" [B-1900].
- **Probe:** with fps in 2029, a 2036 Re-engine gains $1.24B, above ε, so his veto is a logged soft premium of $1.24B, within the cap. Following an A350 Re-engine costs about $2.6B: never.

**Rate Increase.**
- **Turn 1, yes.** It is the 38 → 50 ladder [BX-0391, BX-0405], with capacity built ahead [B-2188]. Probe: +$0.9B with fps.
- **Vetoed** in a quality, FAA or supply-crunch turn [BX-0406, BX-0402].
- **Never as a share signal** against Airbus [BX-0262].
- **Sharpens H6:** in a deferral turn, keep suppliers on the master schedule and re-order the Rate Increase in the first clean turn [BX-0409] **(inference)**.

**Cancel.** Never a launched fps or Re-engine [BX-0320, BX-0274]; quick exits only from unlaunched or non-core bets [BX-0217, BX-0270].

**Engine choice.** fps: `cfm_ducted` [B-2047]; `pw_gtf2` is +$0.4-0.7B, within ε. His open-rotor interest [BX-0386] costs $3.3-4.1B as `cfm_open_fan`: voice it in statements only. Re-engine: `ge_genx_next` [B-2095] **(inference)**.

**Disclosure.**
- No regulator-dependent dates [BX-0395, B-2099]; rates announced only for supplier capacity [BX-0262]; problems disclosed early [BX-0318, B-1945].
- Derivatives trailed before board approval [B-1813]; a clean-sheet point design called out only "when we call it out" [BX-0269].
- At an fps launch: the 20-30% gain and a conservative EIS.

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **A "supplier bottleneck." Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, and launch no overlapping Re-engine. **Statement and rationale only:** embed staff [B-2097], inspect upstream [BX-0407], cash support [BX-0369], insourcing [BX-0414], engine allocation checked against Airbus [BX-0340]. No attribution to Airbus before exposure (fog of war).
- **Once exposed:** a trade-enforcement statement only [BX-0291] **(inference)**.
- **Poaching:** no order change. In statements: hiring, as with the 10,000 engineers [B-1941].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *Is it 20% better, on mature technology?* He wants tech_ready_year and the engine's eis_add [BX-0375].
2. *What if "the best things don't happen"?* He wants the slip test and the `options` worst case [BX-0366].
3. *What does it do to cash and debt?* He wants Do Nothing against the plan in `whatif` [BX-0306, BX-0249].
4. *Is the supply chain ready?* He checks the injects [BX-0321].
5. *Are we chasing share?* He rejects share-driven plans [BX-0326].

**What changes his mind:** evidence that fails, such as China's silence [BX-0339]; a better principled argument [BX-0401]. **What does not:** Airbus rate claims [BX-0277], single campaign losses [B-1868], analysts' premises [BX-0253].

## 6. Confidence and gaps

- **Joint Ventures for a new airplane:** untested; the only evidence is the Embraer exit.
