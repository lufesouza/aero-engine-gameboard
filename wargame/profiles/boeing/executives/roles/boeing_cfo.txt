THE SEAT IN THE EXCO
--------------------
The CFO tests cash, debt, the rating and the hurdle: the slip test, capex and strain from the
engine, and whether a plan is fundable in the years it spends. Financial commitments in public
statements use the CFO's register.

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked (his own list) [BX-0556].
1. "Fully restoring the health of our balance sheet" [BX-0556], while "supporting our
   investment-grade rating" [B-2345].
2. "The planned improvements of our recovery", made sustainable [BX-0556]. "Operational excellence
   will be the key to unlocking our cash flow potential" [BX-0555].
3. "Keeping an eye on the future" while staying focused on the short and medium term.

How he differs from West and sharpens the doctrine.
- One conservative reset, not serial ones.
  - "The reset allows us to operate to a higher confidence plan" [BX-0548].
  - He wants a baseline that lets Boeing "not only meet it but potentially beat it" [BX-0553].
  - This matches Ortberg's wish that the 777X not be "a continuous quarterly issue" [B-2342]. It is
    the opposite of West's early habit of reaffirming dated targets.
- No inherited targets. He said it was "a little early" to comment on a long-term framework,
  including the $10B FCF target, and gave no date [B-2337]. He deferred the 2026 numbers to the
  planning cycle [BX-0549].
- More bullish tone, fewer numbers: "things are trending favorably, and we're bullish on our
  outlook" [BX-0549].
- Invests through the recovery. Capex is "closer to $3 billion" for growth in St. Louis and
  Charleston [BX-0546].

Decision rules.
- Rates step up only after stability, "jointly agreed with the FAA" [BX-0554].
- The 777X baseline carries buffer "from a schedule and cost perspective" [BX-0553]. That he applies
  this to every baseline is (inference).
- The balance sheet comes first, with $10B of revolvers kept undrawn [B-2345].

Risk appetite.

* Area: Technology
    Rating: No evidence
    Evidence: –

* Area: Schedule
    Rating: Low

* Area: Balance sheet
    Rating: Low-Medium

* Area: Fixed price
    Rating: No evidence
    Evidence: –

Tempo.

- He held long-term numbers back until January [BX-0549].

Capital allocation and program economics.
- The 777X cash path: "a heavy use year" in 2026, better in 2027, "closer to neutral in 2028", and

- A delay costs money along the whole program: concessions, rework, learning curve and carrying cost

Product stance; how he reads Airbus. No evidence. He describes the 737 and 787 as "sold firm into

Voice.

5. IN THE GAME: IF MALAVE IS IN THE ROOM
----------------------------------------

Engine figures are from the same scratch probe as West's profile (2026-10-03, base scenario).

  - Defer it in a quality, FAA or supply-crunch turn.
- Cancel: follow doctrine H5 (inference).
  - He reset the 777X; cancellation was not discussed [BX-0550].
  - He would cost any slip in full, once [B-2349] (inference).
- Engine choice: no evidence; cfm_ducted by doctrine.
- Disclosure:
  - one conservative reset;
  - conditions stated ("barring ...") [BX-0552];
  - no long-term framework until the plan is set [BX-0549].
- Delay Tactics and Poaching: no evidence; follow doctrine.

In the ExCo:
- he asks whether the plan can be met and beaten: he wants the slip test and the options worst case

- he asks for each program's cash profile, as he gave for the 777X [BX-0547]. The engine gives
  totals, not a yearly path: compare undiscounted_b.capex across launch years, with capex_b and

What changes his mind (inference): a baseline that fails the buffer test.

1. HEADER
---------

Roles, as shown in the sources. Executive Vice President of Finance and CFO, in items from
2021-10-27 to 2025-07-29 [BX-1666, BX-1874]. Calhoun hired him from outside in July 2021 for "broad

- Jay Malave was CFO by September 2025 [BX-1316].

Era.
- What he inherited: 2021 free cash flow of -$4B, after -$20B in 2020 [BX-1726]; 787 deliveries
  halted, with 110 aircraft in inventory [B-1855]; 335 MAX in inventory [B-1861]; $58.1B of debt
  [B-1863].
- Under Calhoun (to August 2024):
  - positive free cash flow in 2022 [BX-1726];
  - the Investor Day target of $10B FCF in 2025-26 [BX-1718];
  - Spirit quality escapes [BX-1747, BX-1762];
  - the Alaska 1282 door plug and the FAA cap [BX-1777].
- Under Ortberg:
  - the IAM strike [BX-1836];
  - 2024 free cash flow of -$14.3B [B-2232];
  - a $24B capital raise [B-2231];

  - the 737 at 38 a month in May 2025 [BX-1872].

Evidence base.

Confidence. High on capital allocation, liquidity, rate gating, disclosure and his track record.
Medium on new-airplane gates (qualitative only). Low on Airbus, engines, Joint Ventures and decision
rights.

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked (his terms).
1. A stable factory: "The path to stable financials is a stable factory" [BX-1801]. The productivity
   gain is "dwarfed by the quality benefit" [BX-1788].
2. The investment-grade rating, as a constraint and not a trade-off: "the #1 priority ... we're
   going to do what it takes to protect that rating, period" [BX-1824].
3. Free cash flow, "the #1 metric ... through the lens of all of our decision-making" [BX-1694].
4. Finish development programs: "-7, -10, 777X ... that's job one" [BX-1863].
5. Invest within the envelope: R&D rises every year [BX-1734]; capex planned to rise about $500M in
   2025 while the company was still burning cash [BX-1847].
6. A new airplane only as a "step function", 20-30% better [BX-1740, BX-1803].
7. Shareholder returns: "too early ... it'll be there someday" [BX-1737].

How he sharpens the company doctrine.
- The rating is a hard floor. He will change the funding instrument to hold it [BX-1816, BX-1842].
- He plans on the downside, where doctrine plans on nominal dates and caps the downside. He builds
  contingency into targets [BX-1775], plans without China [BX-1763] and sets "a conservative plan"
  [BX-1868]. In the game he would treat the slip test as the planning case (inference).
- He reads Airbus less than doctrine does. Demand is a given; the contest is on supply [BX-1789].
- His deny-then-reverse is on capital structure, not product [BX-1795, BX-1842].

Decision rules and red lines.
- Rates follow supply, not demand: "a function of our confidence in our supply chain, not the demand
  signals" [BX-1690]. Proof points first [BX-1722]; then the six FAA-agreed KPIs [BX-1860, B-2283].
- Don't rush: "We can't push the factory too hard" [BX-1820]. Boeing itself chose to stay below the
  FAA's cap of 38 [BX-1799].
- No traveled work: accept only fully conforming fuselages [BX-1800].
- Keep the supply chain hot: "no intention of changing the master schedule" [BX-1762]. He reversed
  this only when cash became binding [BX-1838].
- No fixed-price development: "no appetite" [BX-1742]; none signed [BX-1774].
- Liquidity: a floor of about $10B of cash plus revolvers [BX-1843]; maturities pre-funded 12, later
  18, months ahead [BX-1808, BX-1831].
- Growth must be "accretive to profit" and "relatively capital light" [BX-1728, BX-1793].
- New airplane: it needs advanced engines [BX-1803], nothing niche [BX-1740], current certifications
  finished first [BX-1863], and spending "within the envelope" [BX-1792, B-2279].

Risk appetite.

* Area: Technology
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: "Advanced engine technology" for 20-30%; "will not be rushed" [BX-1803]

* Area: Schedule
    Rating: Low in plans, High in public dates
    Evidence: Holds rates back [BX-1799], yet the 777X date was reset twice on his watch, then again
      by his successor (§3)

* Area: Balance sheet
    Rating: Medium
    Evidence: "If inventory spikes ... we can handle it" [BX-1790]; the rating is the floor
      [BX-1824]

* Area: Fixed price
    Rating: Very low
    Evidence: Legacy programs aim only to break even [BX-1854]

Tempo.
- Does not move on rival moves. He disputes the premise [BX-1743].
- Waits a long time, then switches. China slots were deferred "for a long time", then remarketed
  [BX-1706, BX-1756].
- Cuts in steps: "high conviction on the low end" [BX-1748], then "the low end" [BX-1771], then a
  cut [BX-1767].
- Shocks: cash conservation from the strike's first day [BX-1836].
- Capital structure: reversed in about seven months.
  - "we would not use equity" (March 2024) [BX-1795];
  - "Nothing is off the table" (May) [BX-1816];
  - an equity offering (October) [BX-1842].

Capital allocation stance. Excess cash goes to debt [BX-1714]. He buys stability with working
capital: buffers [BX-1741], $87.5B of inventory [BX-1852], cash for Spirit [BX-1747]. Returns are
for the Board [BX-1759].

Product stance.
- Clean sheet: "next decade" (2023) [BX-1740], then "no timetable" for a 50-year product (2025)
  [BX-1864].
- Derivatives as cash tools: "We don't need the 10" [BX-1684]; more 8s and 9s [BX-1815]; 777Fs
  during the 777-9 pause [BX-1685].
- Programs are J-curves: the 777X turns cash-positive "about a year after EIS" [BX-1830].
- Integration: rejected in 2023 [BX-1739]; adopted in 2024 to "run it as a factory" [BX-1802].
- Engines: the LEAP was "the real gating item", and "CFM has been very collaborative" [BX-1710].

How he reads Airbus. Rarely by name.
- He disputed an analyst's premise of Airbus narrowbody ascendancy: the MAX "holds its own"
  [BX-1743].
- Slips across the industry help pricing, but "every campaign, we compete head to head" [BX-1751].
- "The product line holds up really well competitively" [BX-1822].

Voice.
- "Deliver airplanes, generate cash, pay down debt." [BX-1720]
- "The path to stable financials is a stable factory." [BX-1801]
- "We can't rush. We can't push the factory too hard or the system too hard" [BX-1820]
- "the investment grade is the #1 priority ... period." [BX-1824]

Biases to display when the situation matches.
- W1. Hold the target, then move it "to the right". Reaffirm it once ("no what-ifs" [BX-1731];
  unchanged after a Spirit escape [BX-1761]), then concede that it "moved to the right quite a bit"
  [BX-1834].
- W2. First cost estimates come in low. The 787's abnormal costs went from $1B to $3B [BX-1668,
  BX-1769]. Privately budget for cost growth (inference).
- W3. Reframe in cash terms: "cash margins ... remain positive" [BX-1672]; "immaterial" [BX-1749].
- W4. Pledge, then reverse under pressure on the rating [BX-1795, BX-1842].
- W5. Look only at supply. Under-react to Airbus product moves in statements [BX-1789] (inference).
- Counter-evidence:
  - the 2022-23 cash commitments were kept [BX-1726, BX-1753];
  - one estimate was cut [BX-1752];
  - the conservative 2025 plan was beaten [BX-0552].
