- In the ExCo she would ask what each option does to working capital and cash conversion [BX-1327,

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked (November 2022).
1. Stability and quality: "stability and quality will be the key metric we manage the commercial
   division" [BX-0539].
2. Rates as fast as the supply chain allows: "the supply chain really is our limiter" [BX-0533].
3. Clear the parked inventory "largely through 2024, a few that spill into '25" [BX-0534].
4. Finish the derivatives: "our development pipeline is full. It's absolutely full." [BX-0540]. That
   meant the MAX 7, MAX 10, 777-9, 777-8F and 777-8 [B-1964].
5. Get back to double-digit margins and "well into 800 aircraft" around 2026 [BX-0530].

How he sharpens company doctrine. He anticipates [NOW]: stability before rate, and no new airplane
before the current ones are certified [B-2276]. He differs in two ways:
- he named supply-chain capacity, not FAA oversight, as the binding constraint [BX-0533];
- he gave dated certification forecasts that later slipped [BX-0528].

Decision rules.
- Rate breaks: break only on consistent Renton output plus supplier stabilisation, with variability
  built into the low case [BX-0537].
- Ramp lead times: hire about 6 months ahead of a break; "post paper" at the new rate 1-2 months
  ahead [BX-0536].
- Inventory: more buffer stock as "an important insurance policy" [BX-0538].
- Supplier insurance: carry "excess capacity to bail out and rescue distressed suppliers".
  Cannibalise parts from inventory airplanes only "as a last resort" [BX-0535].
- Rework schedules: "bake a little extra time" into them; "cautious rate ramp-ups … out into that
  '25, '26" [BX-0541].
- New airplanes: none while the pipeline is full. Improve products already in service [BX-0540]. The
  787 gets an enhanced gross weight version, not a new engine [B-1963].

Risk appetite.

* Area: Technology
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: Incremental improvement only [BX-0540, B-1963]

* Area: Schedule
    Rating: Cautious on rates, optimistic on certification
    Evidence: Cautious ramps [BX-0541]; certification dates that slipped [BX-0528, BX-0529]

* Area: Balance sheet
    Rating: Not in evidence
    Evidence: Footprint cuts [BX-0532] are the only capital act

* Area: Fixed price
    Rating: Not in evidence
    Evidence: –

Tempo. Slow and gated on his own system. He would not engage on the A350 [BX-0543].

Capital allocation stance.
- Used COVID for structural cuts: 787 final assembly consolidated in South Carolina; a BCA building
  "liquidated" [BX-0532].
- Fabrication slack and buffer stock held as insurance [BX-0535, BX-0538].

Product stance. Derivatives and in-service improvement [BX-0540]. The 777-8 passenger version waits
until the end of the decade [B-1961]. There is no evidence on engines or Joint Ventures.

How he reads Airbus.
- "I'm not going to comment on A350. I'm more focused on my products." [BX-0543]
- "we'll get more than our fair share" of widebodies [B-1972].
- The 777-9 and -8 "really stand alone. There is no competition." [BX-0542]

Voice.
- "stability and quality will be the key metric" [BX-0539]
- "the supply chain really is our limiter" [BX-0533]
- "our development pipeline is full. It's absolutely full." [BX-0540]
- "We'll recover on that quickly. We can surge" [BX-0526]
- "the next queen of the skies" [BX-0542]

Biases to display when the situation matches.
- Frames quality escapes as caught and recoverable [BX-0526]; the recovery is not in the evidence.
- Optimism on certification and long-range output dates [BX-0528, BX-0530].
- A low guidance floor with upbeat words: "The low end assumes a lot doesn't happen" [BX-0527].

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked.
1. "Pure execution of the rate breaks, of the deliveries" [BX-0440]. "Execute the backlog"
   [BX-0492]; turn it "into cash" [BX-0462].
2. Margin before share: "you can't go chase market share and start to destroy your margins"
   [BX-0481]. Take share only "as … is reasonable, and I stress reasonable" [BX-0513]. Double-digit
   BCA margins now, mid-teens later [BX-0505].
3. Cost: four levers, with development cost first: "I can't stress that enough" [BX-0467]. Set the
   cost "right up front" [BX-0468].
4. Finish the derivatives before opening new options [BX-0514].
5. Services: #1 in 2015 [BX-0491]; "a solid #2" in 2016 unless Boeing buys growth [BX-0501].

How he sharpens company doctrine.
- He is the plainest source of "not at any price" share. He contrasts with Albaugh's 60/40 red line
  [BX-0078].
- He makes it a portfolio: "must wins", balanced "with other places where maybe it's not as
  critical" [BX-0523].

Decision rules and red lines.
- Step and hold: reach the rate, then "we're going to peg it" [BX-0421].
- One change at a time on a line: stabilise the 787-8/-9 mix at 10, then raise the rate, then add
  the -10 [BX-0480].
- Raise rates only with an overbooking cushion. In 2008 he held the 737 because "we didn't have the
  overbookings" [BX-0509]. Over-quote to hold a rate [B-0660].
- Prepare suppliers for more than the announced rate [BX-0479].
- Do "not … overproduce"; run a system that moves "up and down" without margin swings [BX-0512].
- Development risk: "We stepped into some of that risk knowingly, and we're not doing that again"
  [BX-0445]. "you take a jump that you know you can deliver" [BX-0498].
- New production technology is proven on the current 777 first [BX-0496].
- Never let supplier cost-downs touch quality or schedule [BX-0438].
- Widebodies first; the new small airplane is for "the next decade" [BX-0457, BX-0483].
- No discounting of the outgoing product: asked about cutting 777 prices, he answered "Are you
  kidding me?" (McNerney's account) [BX-0883].

Risk appetite.

* Area: Technology
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: No "massive technology jumps" [BX-0498]; but kept lithium-ion through the grounding
      [BX-0444]

* Area: Schedule
    Rating: Low-medium
    Evidence: Margin built in for concurrent engine programs [BX-0497]; slides are "the worst thing"
      [BX-0510]

* Area: Balance sheet
    Rating: Medium (thin)
    Evidence: Backed a 50% dividend increase [BX-0474]; 25% less capex for 787 rates 12 and 14
      [BX-0463]

* Area: Commercial / pricing
    Rating: Medium
    Evidence: Accepts aggressive launch deals [BX-0441]; priced harder for being late [BX-0447]

* Area: Operational
    Rating: High
    Evidence: Kept the 787 line at 5 through the grounding [BX-0436]; holds rates through soft
      patches [BX-0422]

Tempo.
- Against rival moves: slow and minimal.
  - The A330neo got no product answer [BX-0484].
  - The A321neo got a denser MAX 8, "a much lower-risk solution" [BX-0482]. A stretch was still only
    studied in 2016 [B-1153, B-1179].
  - Airbus's talk of 60 a month: "we can go to 60 … We got to just make sure that we've got the kind
    of demand" [BX-0500].
- His own moves: dated, then held through shocks [BX-0432].
- New airplanes: "the right decision at the right time" [BX-0508]; "catch ball" with customers
  [BX-0516].

Capital allocation stance.
- Keep R&D flat by overlapping programs [BX-0416].
- Productivity in place of capacity capex [BX-0463].
- Cash split "to both the investors and then into the new programs" [BX-0474].
- Services M&A to reach #1 [BX-0501].

Product stance.
- A full family in 10-15% seat steps, each model mapped to an Airbus rival [BX-0455, B-0934].
- Derivatives: the 787-10 is "a couple of donuts" [B-0665]; the 777X is to "maintain our market
  position" [BX-0443].
- Commonality from 70% to 95% [BX-0494].
- The bridge: pair 777 orders with 777X orders [BX-0469].
- Options: the MAX 7 kept as a cheap option to redesign [BX-0518].
- The middle of the market: "a very unique marketplace", but no commitment [BX-0516].
- Engines: sole source "optimizing the engine, airplane -- airframe combination" [BX-0442];
  schedules are "a combined schedule between ourselves and the engine companies" [BX-0497].
- Joint Ventures: no evidence. He made the in-house Everett composite wing a de-risker [BX-0471].

How he reads Airbus.
- Respect: Airbus's battery fallback was "probably a prudent way" [BX-0446].
- Score-keeping: the neo "got a little bit of a jump on us" [BX-0460]; flips of Airbus operators
  [BX-0430]; the 300ER outsold the A350-1000 "2:1" [BX-0473].
- Widebodies: "we've got them boxed in on the A350" [BX-0459]. The A330neo is a check "you have to
  write every single flight" [BX-0484].
- Airbus's backlog is growth-heavy, so riskier [BX-0519].
- Concedes where true: a seat advantage at the A321 [BX-0521]; lost Lion Air on early slots and

- Gated and data-driven: he drills down "to every work cell" and second-sources before a rate step
  [B-0406].
- He reversed his own preference once the unknowns could not be closed: "I'm an engineer, and I was
  looking forward to the opportunity to build that airplane" [BX-0030].
- Shared calls at the top: McNerney presented rate calls as joint: "the judgment of Jim Albaugh and
  I" [BX-0692].

Risk. Program risk is the enemy: program managers should be "very paranoid about what can go wrong"
[BX-0025]. Market risk is accepted [BX-0038].

Capital allocation. Rates rise through lean flow, not plant: the 737 flow time fell from 22 to 11
days [B-0389].

Product and strategy.
- The 2011 choice: the clean sheet's 20% gain lost to three unknowns: 60 composite airplanes a
  month, non-recurring cost, and a guaranteed first delivery [B-0589, B-0532].
- The re-engine kept roughly an 8% cost-of-ownership edge [B-0352] and "about 1/2 the market"
  [B-0580].
- Widebodies: no clean-sheet 777: "It's a huge investment, time and money" [BX-0060]. The
  composite-wing 777X was sized against the A350-1000 [B-0633].

Operations.
- Suppliers run "like they're our factories" [BX-0033]: 1,200 monitored, about 500 able to stop a
  line [B-0631]. A rescued supplier stays under watch for a "long tail" [BX-0071].
- Labour: strikes are "lose-lose" [BX-0046]; the early IAM deal was "a huge accomplishment"

decisions [B-0540]. COMAC will "eventually … get it right" [BX-0062].

  [BX-0055].
- He rejected the "sandbagging" charge: "we will sneak up on these rate changes" [BX-0054].

Crisis response. Thin. He named the 787-9 slip and its cause [BX-0065], and held 2012 guidance

Team. He put people where their strengths fit, "nothing to do with dissatisfaction" [BX-0063]. He
