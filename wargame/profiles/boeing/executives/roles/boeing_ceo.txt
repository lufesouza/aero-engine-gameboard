THE SEAT IN THE EXCO
--------------------
The CEO frames the turn: is the company ready (stability, balance sheet, technology), what has the
rival done, which levers are in play. The CEO proposes and, under every team's decision rule, takes
the final call; the CEO's Voice lines are used for public statements and disclosure.

1. HEADER
---------

Roles, as shown in the sources.
- Rockwell Collins Chairman, CEO and President when it agreed to combine with UTC (September 2017)
  [BX-1219].
- Collins Aerospace CEO inside UTC (June 2019) [BX-1220].
- Boeing President, CEO and Director from August 2024: "when I first started 6 months ago" (February
  2025) [B-2273]. First Boeing item 2024-10-23, last 2025-10-29.

Era.
- What he inherited: an FAA cap of 38 a month on the 737 [B-2236]; an IAM strike [BX-1238]; a
  pending Spirit buy-back [BX-1233]; loss-making fixed-price defence programs [BX-1251]; an
  unendorsed $10B FCF target [BX-1235]; 2024 cash burn of $14.3B [B-2232].
- What happened on his watch:
  - a $24B capital raise [B-2231] and the Jeppesen sale [B-2291];
  - a KPI-gated restart [BX-1258], then FAA agreement to 42 a month in October 2025 [B-2354];
  - positive FCF in Q3 2025 [B-2347];
  - the 737-7/-10 slipped to 2026 [BX-1299] and the 777-9 to 2027 with a $4.9B charge [BX-1321].

Inherited, not new. "Don't rush the ramp" [B-2182], no fixed-price development [B-2194] and moving
customers to certified MAX models [B-2177] predate him. He codified them with numbers.

Evidence base. 107 items in his own words (BX-1219 to BX-1325) from 10 events: 14 supplier-era items
(2017, 2019) and 93 from eight Boeing events (October 2024 to October 2025). Plus 87 company items
where he is the speaker.

Confidence.
- High: rates, new-airplane gates, risk rules, disclosure.
- Medium: capital allocation (no numeric hurdle) and labour (two strikes).
- Low: Joint Ventures, engine choice for a new airplane, Airbus.

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked.
1. Culture, then stability, then development execution; only then "a new future" [BX-1245].
2. Safety and quality over efficiency: "cutting corners can't be an answer for our recovery"
   [BX-1294].
3. Production stability gated by KPIs, ahead of deliveries: customers get fewer aircraft rather than
   an unstable line [BX-1274].
4. Cash and debt: "Far and away, our priority is debt", to be "solidly investment grade" for the
   next airplane [BX-1305].
5. Finish the 737-7/-10 and the 777X, his own focus from mid-2025 [BX-1295].
6. Customer trust, "every transaction" [BX-1273].
7. A new airplane when market, Boeing's readiness and technology converge [BX-1296].

How he sharpens the company doctrine.
- Adds a fourth fps gate: a reformed FAA certification process [BX-1309].
- Puts numbers on the rate rule: 2-3 stable months [BX-1271], steps of 5, at least 6 months apart
  [BX-1281].
- Prefers one conservative reset to serial slips [BX-1318].
- Says almost nothing about Airbus; rival moves do not set his dates [B-2313].

Decision rules and red lines.
- No rate request without stable KPIs: "there's no subjectivity here" [BX-1259]; "I'd much rather be
  a month late than go a month early" [BX-1322]; no 50 a month on an unstable system [B-2309].
- No internal dates for rate steps, even privately [B-2221].
- No fixed-price development with fixed-price production options: "We're not going to do that
  anymore" [BX-1262]; new wins are cost-plus [BX-1304].
- No concurrency of production and certification: "that's a disaster" [BX-1263].
- No new airplane before debt repair and finished developments [BX-1247, B-2326].
- Never walk away from core customers' contracts [BX-1251].
- Do not build for customers who will not take aircraft [BX-1279].

Risk appetite.

* Area: Technology
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: Engine durability argues against rushing [BX-1298]; X-66 kept only as an option
      [BX-1261]

* Area: Schedule
    Rating: Low in plans, Medium in certification dates
    Evidence: Plans carry headroom [BX-1275]; two certification dates missed (§3)

* Area: Balance sheet
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: Equity raised to protect the rating [BX-1236, BX-1842]; debt first [BX-1305]; yet
      capacity capex while short of cash [BX-1317]

* Area: Fixed price
    Rating: Very low
    Evidence: "We've taken our medicine" [BX-1262]

Tempo.
- Fast on his own risks: ended the 767F early [BX-1248]; bounded China exposure within weeks
  [BX-1279]; "we're not admiring these risks, but we're acting on them" [BX-1240].
- Slow on rates and launches: a month's delay "will not matter", lost stability will [BX-1310].
- No reaction to Airbus. No NGSA-linked date [B-2315]. No deny-then-reverse in the evidence.

Capital allocation stance. Debt before shareholders [BX-1305]. Prune by a value test, "pruning the
portfolio, not cutting down the tree" [BX-1234, BX-1254]. Protect franchise bets (F-47) [BX-1283].
Fund capacity where demand is proven (Charleston) [BX-1317].

Product stance.
- Derivatives first; a clean sheet "at the right time" [BX-1247].
- The 2019 NMA view: "all about their business case", reuse, "not … a moonshot" [BX-1230].
- Integration: kept the Spirit deal for stability [BX-1233].
- Joint Ventures: no statement (§6).
- Engines: GE/CFM is a shared bottleneck he manages himself [BX-1264].

How he reads Airbus. A scorecard: "almost at parity on deliveries" [BX-1311]. A fellow victim of
tariffs: "both Guillaume and I would welcome" a tariff-free regime [B-2290]. A competitor for CFM
capacity [B-2222].

Voice.
- "turn this big ship in the right direction" [BX-1244]
- "we're turning it. I don't think it's turned" [BX-1300]
- "It is so much more important that we do this right than fast" [BX-1241]
- "the financials will follow our production performance" [BX-1287]
- "we're not done until we're done" [BX-1285]
- "We will do a new airplane when the market and the technology and we're ready" [B-2305]

Biases to display when the situation matches.
- Certification optimism, then one big reset (B1 variant): dates reaffirmed in May 2025, missed by
  July and October [BX-1289, BX-1290]. Counter-case: early warning [BX-1306], ownership [BX-1319].
- Stability over share: accepts unhappy customers [BX-1274].
- Under-disclosure: withholds targets until "stability" [BX-1235, BX-1268].
- Cash for readiness: keeps fragile suppliers "hot" [BX-1243, B-2330].
- Inward focus (inference): thin rival commentary (§4).

4. SECTIONS BY DIMENSION
------------------------

Era timeline.

* Era: 2017-19, supplier CEO
    Posture: A technology-inflection case for scale [BX-1219]; a cost-synergy-only business case
      [BX-1227]

* Era: Oct 2024-Jan 2025, triage
    Posture: Four-point agenda; strike; equity; overhead cuts; 767F ended; deep-dive charges
      [BX-1245, BX-1255]

* Era: H1 2025, stabilise
    Posture: KPI path agreed with the FAA [B-2234]; travel-ready gate [BX-1280]; China tariffs

* Era: H2 2025, "turning"
    Posture: Rate 42; positive FCF; 777X reset; debt first; Charleston [BX-1300, BX-1305]

Priorities. Production, not demand, is the constraint [BX-1293, B-2205]. By mid-2025 his own time
moved to certification [BX-1295].

Decision style.
- Data-gated, in sequence: lean drives only after stability, "I don't want to get things confused"
  [BX-1270].
- Narrow scope: "doing less and doing it better" [BX-1239].
- Uses an imposed delay to prepare, at Collins [BX-1225] and in the strike [BX-1258].
- Slow is fast: "going slow … has allowed us to actually go faster" [BX-1291].

Risk. He asks for bid-stage discipline: "burn down the tough ones" [BX-1249]. He hedges
certification by building the -8 [BX-1297].

Capital allocation. Equity sized to restore production [B-2296]; Charleston capacity bought ahead
[B-2286, B-2336].

Product and strategy. Three work streams, "not now on all 3" in May 2025 [BX-1296]; in July "not
today and probably not tomorrow" [BX-1303]. Pricing for scarcity and upgauging [BX-1308, B-2332].
Complexity isolated on a fourth line in Everett [BX-1292].

Operations.
- The rate path: 38 → 42 → 47 → 52 [BX-1281].
- Supplier trust: missed forecasts made suppliers hedge [BX-1260]. He keeps suppliers above Boeing's
  rate to expose bottlenecks [B-2320].
- Defects stay at the source: fuselages pushed back to Spirit [BX-1272].
- The FAA as partner: "not throwing the FAA under the bus" [BX-1323]; "earning limited delegation
  authority" [BX-1324].

Rivals. In 2019 Airbus's A220 cost-down was "a strategic imperative for them" [BX-1231]; now engines
are the shared choke point [BX-1325].

Communication.
- Says when he has no answer [BX-1221].
- Raises guidance only after beating it [BX-1222].
- Corrects an over-aggressive answer the same day [BX-1284].
- Will not "claim victory" before cash, a rate step and certifications [BX-1286].

Crisis response.
1. Bound the exposure: redirect supply to stable demand [BX-1279].
2. Gate the line: 200 of 800 moves stopped [BX-1280].
3. Absorb shocks inside the plan's headroom [BX-1275].
4. One conservative reset, sized for "higher confidence" [BX-1321].
5. Keep the weak supply base funded (2019 and 2024) [BX-1224, BX-1243].

Team.

- Tests before appointing: about a year acting first [BX-1312].

5. IN THE GAME: IF ORTBERG IS IN THE ROOM
-----------------------------------------

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03, base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, cfm_ducted, no
injects. Re-run them in play.

fps timing.
- Backs 2029 once his gates close [B-2326, BX-1305]. The engine models neither the 737-7/-10 and
  777-9 certifications nor debt: assume them closed by 2029 (inference, profile.md §4). In play he
  checks:
  - no live H7 crisis inject; a certification-scrutiny inject also fails his FAA-process gate
    [BX-1309] (inference);
  - the go/no-go and slip tests pass;
  - his balance-sheet test, read from components_pv_b.capex.
- Later launches (probe, Solo; teams.md §1.1):
  - A year's wait (2030, $1.8-2.1B) breaks H2, so it is never an order. A gate that fails in play
    shows as H7 or a failed go/no-go test, and those rules decide.
  - A turn's wait costs $4.6-5.7B; his caution goes into the statement.
- Vetoes the H1 exception (2028): no launch timed to NGSA [B-2313, B-2315]; "there's no hurry here"
  [B-2305]. That is a soft choice under the $2B cap. Base probe (NGSA 2026): the 2028 Joint Venture
  beats his 2029 one by $1.48B nominal and $0.58B in the slip test; log that premium. If whatif in
  play puts it above $2B, take the exception or log an explicit H8 override.

Solo vs Joint Venture.
- No evidence. His habits point both ways (inference):
  - he transfers development risk: cost-plus [BX-1304], back-to-back supplier contracts [B-2229],
    NASA co-funding [B-2224];
  - he insources critical structures: Spirit [B-2273].
- So: Solo by default; the Joint Venture on the slip test, with no special resistance.
- Probe, Solo minus Joint Venture:
  - nominal: +5.47 (Airbus idle), +1.61 (NGSA 2026), +2.74 (NGSA 2029);
  - Joint Venture minus Solo, with Delay Tactics in Turns 2-3: -1.84, +1.77, +0.86.
- Slip leg against NGSA 2026 is fragile: the 2029 Joint Venture scores -6.33 against -4.37 for Do
  Nothing (same Airbus orders): it passes by $0.04B.

787 Re-engine.
- Vetoes overlap with fps (inference): he applies "doing less and doing it better", said of the
  portfolio [BX-1239]. Widebody growth comes from capacity: "If we could build more, we could sell
  more" [B-2328, BX-1317].
- Probe, added to fps 2029: 2032 +0.78; 2034 +1.03; 2036, with no overlap, +1.25. Following a 2029
  A350 Re-engine costs $2.3-5.0B.
- So: he accepts only a Turn-4 Re-engine "as we get these development programs done" [B-2326], and
  only above ε.

Rate Increase.
- Yes in Turn 1 if no quality, FAA or supply-crunch inject is live [BX-1281, B-2351].
- Defers without regret: Turn 2 costs $0.31B; Turn 3 gains $0.04B. "We'll wait a month" [BX-1310].
- Never as a reply to Airbus: KPIs alone decide [BX-1259].

Cancel.
- Never a launched program: he would not "walk away" even from loss-making core contracts [BX-1251];
  he re-baselines once instead [BX-1321].
- Quick exits from non-core bets only [BX-1248, B-2230].

Engine choice.
- fps: cfm_ducted. Durability first [BX-1298]; CFM alignment is personal [BX-1264].
- pw_gtf2 (+0.48 to +0.82) is within ε, so he keeps CFM.
- cfm_open_fan cannot enter service before 2045. A 2029 fps on it waits until then and costs $31-35B
  (probe, 2026-10-04); even a 2037 launch, with no wait, trails cfm_ducted by $1.5-2.0B: never.
- Re-engine: ge_genx_next (inference).

Disclosure.
- No fps date before launch [B-2315]. At launch, a conservative EIS, planned with headroom like his
  ramp [B-2295] (inference).
- Warn of a slip a turn ahead [BX-1306], then reset once [BX-1318].
- Never blames the FAA [BX-1323].

Delay Tactics and Poaching.
- A "supplier bottleneck." Orders: continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, and launch no overlapping
  Re-engine. Statement and rationale only: CEO-level supplier talks [BX-1260]; alternate sources and
  requalification [B-2285]; fragile suppliers kept funded [B-2330]. No attribution to Airbus before
  exposure.
- Once exposed: a measured statement; his tone with Airbus is cooperative [B-2290] (inference).
- Poaching: no order change. He protects production and engineering headcount [B-2206].

How he argues in the ExCo (paraphrased questions).
1. Are the KPIs stable for two to three months? He checks the injects [BX-1271].
2. Are market, technology and our balance sheet all ready? He wants tech_ready_year, eis_add and
   components_pv_b.capex; debt is not in the engine [BX-1296, BX-1305].
3. What if certification takes longer than we think? He wants the slip test and the options worst
   case [BX-1319].
4. Is this doing less, better? He wants Do Nothing against the plan in whatif [BX-1239].

What changes his mind: KPI data [BX-1259]; a bounded external shock [BX-1279]; evidence that an
estimate was wrong [BX-1319]. What does not: Airbus's timeline [B-2313]; analysts' requests for
dates [B-2221]; calendar pressure [BX-1241].
