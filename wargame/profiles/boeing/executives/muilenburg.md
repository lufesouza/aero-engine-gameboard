# Dennis A. Muilenburg: executive profile

## 1. Header

**Roles, as shown in the sources.**
- CEO of Boeing Defense, Space & Security (BDS), in items from May 2011 to December 2013 [BX-0887, BX-0930].
- Vice Chairman, President and COO from early 2014 [BX-0948, BX-0830].
- CEO from July 2015 [BX-0886, BX-0984]; Chairman as well by April 2016 [B-1142]. The board split the roles on 11 October 2019 [BX-1217]; he left in December 2019 (annotation) [BX-1176].
- Transcript titles are retroactive; roles follow the dates.

**Era.**
- **Inherited:** the MAX and 777X in development, a 787 deferred balance forecast to peak at about $25B (§3) [BX-0940], the fixed-price KC-46 [BX-0893] and a 737 ladder to 52 [BX-0959]. His theme: "strategic consistency" [BX-0975].
- **On his watch:** 737 at 47 and 52 [BX-1083, BX-1144]; 787 to 14 [BX-1092]; 777 from 8.3 to 5 [BX-0999, BX-1059]; the NMA studied, never launched [BX-1210, B-1613]; the Embraer Joint Venture [B-1432]; $10-13B a year returned [BX-1055, B-1467]; KC-46 charges [BX-0979, BX-1136]; two MAX accidents and the March 2019 grounding [BX-1167, BX-1171].

**Evidence base.** 332 items in his own words (BX-0887 to BX-1218) from 43 events, 2011-05-24 to 2019-10-23, densest in 2016-19. 340 company items where he is the speaker.

**Confidence.** High on rates, cash, sequencing, the NMA and crisis voice. Medium on Airbus (he usually declined comment). Low on CFO decision rights and engine selection.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked.**
1. **Deliver existing commitments: "building strength on strength"** [BX-1001, BX-0984].
2. **Cash: "a growing cash business year-over-year"** in every scenario [BX-1045], with about 100% of free cash flow returned [BX-1043, B-1426].
3. **Margin: double digits in 2017, 15% by 2020,** "not … a slogan" [BX-1028, BX-1082], never withdrawn [BX-1216].
4. **Productivity as offense** [BX-1019]; it funds price, earnings and R&D, "all 3" [BX-1012].
5. **Predictable development:** time-phased R&D, no spikes [BX-1004, BX-1070].
6. **Adjacencies:** $50B of services [BX-1060]; selective verticals [BX-1145].
7. **A new airplane only when "the business case closes"** [BX-1110, BX-1192].
- **Crisis override:** "our top priority is the safe return to service" [BX-1175], while other priorities stay "unchanged" [BX-1174].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **One development at a time.** A new airplane comes "on the backside of 777X … We and you can't stand a spiky R&D profile" [BX-1004]. R&D stays at 3.7-3.8% of sales, and a launch must fit inside it [BX-1159].
- **No technology push** [BX-1048]: existing engines [BX-1128]; never the 787's "all at once" [BX-1118].
- **Launch gates:** a closed business case, "not … date-driven" [BX-1124]; a two-step authority to offer, then launch [BX-1156]; services and verticals counted in the case [BX-1147]; no cannibalising the 787 [BX-1065].
- **Not for share:** "We are not in business just to chase market share" [BX-1010]; no "drastic" price cuts [BX-1042].
- **Rates:** one break at a time; "step to 57 a month when we're ready to step" [BX-1149]. Supply-chain readiness is the gate [BX-1100].
- **Rate cuts:** an order hurdle and a deadline, then act [BX-1011, BX-1047]. Below 42 a month, "in some cases" a temporary shutdown beats a lower rate [BX-1193].
- **Partners and M&A** come third and are "not a must do" [BX-1116]. They never dent the payout [BX-1115].

**Risk appetite.**

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | **Low** | "10 intermediate steps … as opposed to one big 20-year step" [BX-0954]; "the right set of technology" [BX-0953] |
| Schedule | **High** in public | End dates held while milestones slip, then reset in small steps [BX-0978, BX-1063, BX-1188] |
| Balance sheet | **High** | A $20B buyback and dividend +20% after Lion Air [BX-1154]; no longer "a cyclical business" and a 100% FCF return "unwavering" [BX-1114, BX-1115]; debt for Embraer during the grounding [BX-1196] |
| Fixed price | **High** | KC-46 as a "50-, 60-year … franchise" [BX-1033]; T-X and MQ-25 charges, "a commercial investment mindset" [BX-1151] |

**Tempo.**
- **Rival moves:** "don't change our plans" after the C Series deal (October 2017) [BX-1099, B-1317]; Embraer talks two months later [B-1326], a counter **(inference)**. His account: "something we've been working on for some time" [BX-1120].
- **Never matches Airbus rates** [B-1428, B-1400].
- **Decides at the last lead-time point** [BX-1214]: held the 787-at-14 decision 14 months [BX-1038, BX-1092].
- **Acts on dated triggers:** the 777 cut came three months after his "2 to 3 months" warning [BX-1047, BX-1059].
- **Pre-empts on cost:** sizes for the worst case, invests for the central case [BX-0905, B-0728].

**Capital allocation.** Stated order: organic investment, about 100% FCF return, then targeted M&A [BX-1112, BX-1072]. Dividends "communicate confidence" [BX-1044].

**Product stance.**
- **Derivatives**, "spiraling technology in" [BX-0947]; over 90% reuse [BX-0955, B-0907]; the MAX 10, "not a technology stretch" [BX-1064].
- **Clean sheet:** the NMA at 220-270 seats and 5,000 nm [BX-1126]. The innovation is the production system [BX-1127].
- **Verticals:** the composite wing in-house [BX-0965]; propulsion integration [BX-1129].
- **Joint Venture:** Embraer for products, verticals, services and engineers [B-1328, B-1420, B-1432]; Joint Ventures "where they make sense" [B-1379].
- **Engines:** "existing engine technology packaged in a new way" [BX-1128].

**How he reads Airbus.**
- "A strong and aggressive competitor"; the answer is to "out-innovate" [BX-1095]. "A respected competitor" [BX-1186].
- Narrowbodies are 50-50, but the MAX backlog's "quality" is better [BX-1018].
- Widebodies: Airbus "can't match" the 777 [BX-1017]; 485 repeat 787 orders against "90-some" for the A350 and A330neo [BX-1132].
- Airbus rate moves confirm the market [BX-1131]; he declines to comment on them [BX-1140].

**Voice.**
- "building strength on strength" [BX-1001]
- "it's not going to be a date-driven decision" [BX-1124]
- "well understood and defined" [BX-1058]; "we have our arms around this" [BX-0980]
- "a defining moment … we're going to come out even stronger" [BX-1177]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
- **Firm dates, small resets:** intermediate milestones slip; the end date is held, then re-dated without "slip" [BX-0978, BX-1063, BX-1098].
- **Normalising strain:** "normal ramp-up pressures" [BX-0994]; "a great problem to have" [BX-1141]; "normal ebb and flow" [BX-1097].
- **"Not cyclical"** rate optimism [BX-1015, BX-1114].
- **Repeated deferral of the NMA decision**, May 2018 to October 2019 [BX-1124, BX-1156, BX-1210].
- **Hold course in words, then partner (inference; see Tempo)** [BX-1099, B-1326, BX-1120].
- **Attributes slips to external dependencies** (GE's engine, regulator approvals) while holding the end date [BX-1188, BX-1201]. Counter: [BX-1189, BX-1167].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**

| Era | Posture |
|---|---|
| 2011-13, BDS | Worst-case cost sizing; R&D ring-fenced; closed the C-17 line [BX-0897, BX-0928, BX-0931] |
| 2014-15, COO | Enterprise supplier cost-down; development gates [BX-0952, BX-0951] |
| 2015-17, CEO | "Strategic consistency", then "play offense"; widebody triage; cash return [BX-0975, BX-1019, BX-1047] |
| 2018-Feb 2019 | "Global industrial champion": 57+, NMA, Embraer, $20B buyback [BX-1165, BX-1146, BX-1154] |
| Mar-Oct 2019 | Return to service first; "we have changed" [BX-1175, BX-1206] |

**Priorities.** Debt repair never appears among them; cash return rose every December, even after Lion Air [BX-1154], unlike today's debt-first doctrine [B-2259, B-2327] **(inference)**. In the game: statements only (no payout lever).

**Decision style.**
- Scenario-bounded: order-of-magnitude estimates [BX-0901]; "a bounding case" [B-1215].
- Line decisions 1.5-2 years ahead of the last delivery [BX-0932]; hard development gates [BX-0951].
- Centralised functions [BX-1034] but left unit heads to tailor cuts [BX-1026].

**Risk.**
- A 7-year backlog only needs to be "directionally right" [BX-1009].
- His lesson from the 787: stage innovation [BX-1016].
- Fixed price needs "technology maturity upfront" [BX-0892]. He kept bidding anyway [BX-1053, BX-1151]; later T-X and MQ-25 losses appear only in annotations [BX-1152].

**Capital allocation.**
- Ranking conflict: returns were "the top priority" in prepared remarks, investment "#1" in Q&A the same day [BX-0972, BX-0973].
- Cash returned exceeded operating cash in 1Q 2017 and 1Q 2018 [B-1262, B-1388]. The raised dividend was paid on negative operating cash in 2Q 2019 [B-1576].

**Product and strategy.**
- Middle of the market as three options: Do Nothing, a MAX stretch, or all-new [BX-1014]. He took the stretch [BX-1086].
- The NMA case: cannibalisation [BX-1162]; separate from the 737 fight [BX-1103]; a stepping stone to a later narrowbody [B-1480].

**Operations.**
- Partnership backed by threat: second sources and insourcing [BX-0945, BX-1067]; longer payment terms [BX-1040]; patience with Spirit [BX-1076].
- The 2018 step to 52 broke: 29 737s delivered in July [BX-1144]. The lesson: staff up earlier [BX-1150].
- In the grounding: 42 a month, not a halt; some suppliers kept at 52 [BX-1171, BX-1182].
- Labour: respect, plus "multiple sites" [BX-0983]; attrition, not layoffs [BX-1068].

**Rivals.**
- Competes on cost, not price: productivity buys "pricing flexibility" [BX-1027].
- His hardest words: Bombardier's "illegal subsidies … dumping behavior" [BX-1105]. He kept the trade case despite customer strain [B-1319].
- COMAC is competitor and partner [BX-1095].

**Communication.**
- Directional claims without numbers [BX-0920]; "executable" guidance [BX-0939]; no "big corrections quarter-to-quarter" [BX-0914].
- Early warnings inside a strong message [BX-0958, BX-1035].
- Charges framed as "proactive" [BX-1037].
- Withdrew guidance in April 2019 [BX-1169].

**Crisis response.** The playbook:
1. States safety first [BX-0915]; defends the product [BX-1170].
2. Keep building as a signal of confidence [BX-0915, BX-1171].
3. Pull enterprise talent onto the problem [BX-0916, BX-1205].
4. "Well defined and understood"; hold the end date [BX-0979, BX-0978].
5. Ownership language: "We own it" [BX-1167]; "chain of events" [BX-1166].
6. Governance: a board review within weeks; a safety organisation within about 7 months [BX-1172, BX-1212].

Counter-evidence: he accepted the FAA's added requirement [BX-1189], set up a $100M fund [BX-1190], conceded safety issues must surface faster [BX-1195], and by October said "we have changed" [BX-1206].

**Team.**
- "One Boeing": lent BDS talent to the 787 [BX-0907]; a joint supplier front with McNerney, Conner and Smith [BX-0937].
- Credits unit heads [BX-1025]. Brought in an outsider to run BCA [BX-1062], and replaced him in 2019 as "leadership plans" [BX-1218].
- Defended the combined Chairman-CEO role [BX-1176], then backed the split [BX-1217].
- Cash is managed daily with the unit CFO [BX-0919].

## 5. In the game: if Muilenburg is in the room

Engine figures: scratch probe, 2026-10-03 (base scenario, Turn-1 Rate Increase, fps `cfm_ducted`, no injects). Re-run in play.

**fps timing.**
- **Pushes:** no fps in Turn 1; H1 suits him [BX-1124, BX-1004]. In Turn 2, the H2 year, if the case closes in whatif.
- **His bias is deferral:** an authority to offer promised for 2019 never came [BX-1156, BX-1210].
  - Probe: one year late (2030) costs $1.8-2.1B; Turn 3 (2032) costs $4.6-5.7B.
  - So defer in words only: "not ready to be at a decision point" [BX-1210].
- **His best argument:** in the slip test against NGSA 2029, fps 2032 (-2.70) beats 2029 (-4.03). But 2029 passes go/no-go, so it stands.
- **NGSA in development:** he would not accelerate **(inference)**, as after the C Series deal [BX-1099].

**Solo vs Joint Venture.**
- **He signed the game's partner:** the Embraer Joint Venture [B-1432], for engineering and manufacturing capacity [B-1567], but "not a must do" [BX-1116].
- **Pushes:** the Joint Venture once NGSA is in development **(inference)**.
  - Probe, slip test: the Joint Venture beats Solo by $1.77B against NGSA 2026 and $0.86B against NGSA 2029.
  - Probe, nominal: Solo leads by $1.61B against NGSA 2026, $2.74B against NGSA 2029 and $5.47B with Airbus idle.
- **Rule:** the Joint Venture against an early NGSA (within the $2B cap); Solo otherwise.
- **Differs from doctrine:** the Solo default leans on the Embraer exit [B-1674]. He would discount it **(inference)**.

**787 Re-engine.**
- **Pushes:** a derivative [BX-0947] feathered after fps: "a much more sequential approach" [BX-1139].
  - Probe: a 2036 Re-engine adds $1.2B to fps 2029 and beats 2034 by $0.2B, within ε: display it.
- **Vetoes:** following an A350 Re-engine **(inference; H3 governs)**; he dismissed the A330neo against the 777 [BX-0966]. Probe: following costs $2.3-3.5B.

**Rate Increase.**
- **Pushes it hard:** commit in Turn 1 [BX-0998, BX-1125]. Probe: worth +$0.86B with fps 2029.
- **Supply-chain crunch:** he would call it "normal ramp-up pressures" [BX-0994], but his own gate is supplier health [BX-1149]. Defer: Turns 1-3 lie within $0.36B, so H6 costs little.
- **Quality escape or FAA scrutiny:** his sequence is certify, re-deliver, stabilise, then re-ramp [BX-1181]. Defer, and say the long-term ladder holds [BX-1185].
- **Never** because of Airbus rates [B-1428].

**Cancel.** Vetoes cancelling a launched fps or Re-engine: a "franchise" [BX-1033]; "investment that's now behind us" [B-1135]. Ends legacy lines without orders [BX-0931], but kept the 747 as a niche [BX-1041].

**Engine choice.**
- **fps:** `cfm_ducted`, existing technology [BX-1128]. Probe: `cfm_open_fan` costs $3.6-4.5B. Against `pw_gtf2` (within ε) he would cite LEAP supply over Airbus's engine troubles [BX-1096] **(inference)**.
- **Re-engine:** `ge_genx_next`, though he attributed a 777X slip to the GE9X [BX-1209].

**Disclosure.**
- Discloses rate ladders and dated targets years ahead, as confidence signals [BX-0959, BX-0998].
- New airplane: the market and specification, never a date [BX-1126, BX-1124].
- Warnings early, with a bounded worst case [BX-1035, BX-1052].
- After a slip, re-dates without calling it a slip [BX-1063].

**Delay Tactics and Poaching.**
- **Supplier bottleneck. Orders:** continue fps (H5), re-baseline once, and launch no overlapping Re-engine. **Statement and rationale only:** names the supplier [BX-1188], embeds staff [B-1474], runs suppliers above Boeing's rate [B-1583], second-sources [BX-1067]. No attribution to Airbus before exposure.
- **Once exposed:** the hardest fair-trade language, in statements only [BX-1105, BX-1133].
- **Poaching:** no order change. In statements: cross-unit transfers [BX-0903]; $300M for talent [B-1376]; recalled retirees [BX-1097].

**How he argues in the ExCo** (paraphrased questions).
1. *Does it fit a flat R&D profile?* He wants overlap years and `components_pv_b.capex` and `strain` [BX-1004, BX-1159].
2. *Does the case close, whatever the date?* He wants `options` and whatif nominal vs Do Nothing [BX-1192].
3. *What is the bounding case?* He reads the slip test as the worst case [BX-1052].
4. *Are we chasing share?* He rejects share-only arguments [BX-1010].
5. *Is the supply chain ready to step?* He checks the injects [BX-1149].

**What changes his mind:** a dated order trigger that fails [BX-1047, BX-1214]; supplier health [BX-1149]; a regulator's call [BX-1189]. **What does not:** Airbus moves [BX-1099], rate talk [BX-1140], cycle worries [BX-1078].
