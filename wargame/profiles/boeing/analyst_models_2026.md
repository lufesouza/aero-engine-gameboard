# Boeing: 2026 analyst-model baseline

This note sums up what the analysts' company models say about Boeing. The sources are the Morgan Stanley model dated 29 July 2026 (MS) [MS BA ImportantDisclosures r8], the Goldman Sachs model dated 28 July 2026 (GS) [GS BA Cover r8] and the S&P Capital IQ consensus printed around 10-11 October 2026, before the third-quarter results on 21 October 2026 [Capital IQ Recent Changes r16; Consensus r13]. Money is in US dollars, billions ($B), nominal, rounded from the models' $ millions. Years are calendar years to 31 December. A = actual, E = estimate. Both broker models stop at 2030. Consensus runs to 2030 for cash flow and interest and to 2028 for net debt; consensus figures for 2031-2035 rest on a single estimate. The game's board numbers govern the payoff. This note is real-world context. The one exception is the GM's balance-sheet tracker (below), which scores the debt objective.

## Engines and supply

- **What the files show on engines: nothing.** None of the three files has a row or note on engines, engine shortages, airframes waiting for engines, or named suppliers.
- **Public structure, not from the models:** the 737 MAX has one engine supplier (CFM, LEAP-1B). The A320neo family offers two (CFM and Pratt & Whitney).
- **Related evidence, possibly indirect signs of supply strain (inference).** 737 production ran 397 aircraft ahead of deliveries in 2019, then behind by 48-136 a year in 2021-2025 as inventory unwound [MS BA Inventory r43]. The 787 ran 18-49 a year behind in 2023-2025 [MS BA Inventory r7]. Unabsorbed overhead cost $1.1-3.7B a year in 2019-2023 [GS BA BCA margin r88].
- **Customer compensation.** Cash concessions on the 737 and 787 totalled $17.0B over 2019-2025 (computed). GS assumes none from 2026 [GS BA FCF build r23].
- **Supply base.** Boeing brought Spirit AeroSystems in-house in December 2025; GS books $0.9B of acquired revenue inside BCA in 2026E [GS BA SEG r7, r48]. Goodwill rose from $8.1B to $17.3B in 2025 [MS BA Balance_Sheet_Annual r20].
- **Durability:** the files hold no engine durability data and no engine-related charges.

- **R&D:** the extract carries no R&D line (R&D history is in this profile's financials note). Capital spending was $1.7B in 2018 and $2.9B in 2025. Both models put it at $4.0B in 2026. By 2030 MS has $3.7B and GS $3.0B [MS BA Cash_Flow_Annual r33; GS BA CFLO r55]. Boeing guides $4.0B for 2026 [Consensus capex guidance r208].
- **No new airplane is in any forecast.** None of the broker or consensus cash flows includes a new airplane programme [finance_inputs.json].
- **Debt service.** Interest cover was 25.2x in 2018. GS has it at 0.88x in 2026 and 9.18x in 2030 [GS BA BS r85]. Interest stays about $2.0-2.1B a year in 2030 in both models (MS $2.1B, GS $2.0B) against $1.4B in consensus (2 of 3 est.) - **Charge precedents.** MS records BCA charges of $8.2B for the 737 MAX in 2019, $6.5B for the 777X in 2020, $4.35B in 2024 and $4.95B ("Other") in 2025 [MS BA BCA_Annual r40]. GS counts $45.6B of one-time and abnormal BCA costs over 2019-2025 (computed sum). It still carries $4.0B in 2026, falling to $0.5B in 2030 [GS BA BCA margin r87].
- **Defense losses.** Defense, Space & Security lost $3.5B in 2022, $1.8B in 2023 and $5.4B in 2024 [MS BA Income_Statement_Annual r14].

## The balance-sheet objective and the GM's tracker

Control's objective for this game is to repair the balance sheet and bring debt back to historical levels. The GM tracks it as follows [finance_inputs.json]:

- **Measure:** net debt = total debt minus cash and short-term investments. Both broker models and consensus forecast it on this basis.
- **Start:** $22.53B at end-2026E, the consensus mean (5 est.) [Consensus 'Net Debt']. GS has $22.55B [GS BA BS r64] and MS $23.01B [MS BA Balance_Sheet_Annual 'Net Debt']. Gross debt at mid-2026 is $45.9B in both models [GS BA BS r63, 2Q26A], the board's $45B [finance_inputs.json].
- **Historical level: $0**, standing for the 2010-2018 average of about -$0.6B, a small net cash. The yearly figures for 2010-2018, $B, are 1.90, 1.10, -3.15, -5.62, -4.02, -2.09, -0.08, 1.13, 5.28 [GS BA BS r64; finance_inputs.json].
- **Deadline:** end-2030, counting from 2027. **Hold years:** 2035, 2040, 2045 and 2050 [finance_inputs.json].
- **Capital allocation is debt first.** Every dollar of free cash flow goes to net debt until it is back at the historical level. After that, surplus cash goes to the owners. Boeing's Board reserves shareholder returns for itself until the credit rating is solid [finance_inputs.json].

- **How game moves enter.** The tracker adds 79% of any profit the game adds or takes away against its baseline. It subtracts programme cash as it is spent, spread over the development years on the CFO's planning curve. It also charges 3% real interest on any debt the game adds. The tracker is for objective scoring only. The board's payoff and its own penalty on $45B of gross debt are unchanged [finance_inputs.json].
- **Why net debt, not gross debt.** Gross debt does not come back to its ~$11B pre-crisis level in either broker model by 2030: it is still $36.3B (MS) or $38.6B (GS), 3.3-3.5 times the old average (computed) [MS BA Balance_Sheet_Annual 'Total Debt'; GS BA BS r63]. A gross-debt target would measure the bond calendar, not the Board's choices (inference).

## What this means for your decisions (inference)

- **The analyst path is a Do Nothing path.** No forecast includes a new narrowbody or a Re-engine. Every dollar of a launch comes on top of the consensus cash flows.
- **Before 2030 the slack is thin in the stress case.** On base cash flow net debt hits zero in 2029. With cash flow one-third lower it clears only at end-2030, with about $4.8B to spare in 2026 dollars (computed: $27.32B of stress cash flow less the $22.53B start) [finance_inputs.json]. Spending much beyond that before end-2030 risks missing the deadline.
- **A launch in the 2030 round mostly tests the hold years.** The CFO's curve puts 5% and 7% of the bill in the first two years and peaks at 14% in year 7 [finance_inputs.json]. The heavy spending falls in the mid-to-late 2030s, so it bears on the 2035 and 2040 hold years, not on the 2030 deadline.
- **A launch competes with buybacks, not with debt repair.** Consensus free cash flow for 2026-2030 is $45.8B (computed) [Consensus 'Free Cash Flow']. GS's $42B of buybacks [GS BA CFLO r45] would use almost all of it. Under debt first, programme money after 2029 comes out of what would otherwise go to owners.
- **Waiting has volume cover.** The 4,404 737s in backlog are about 7 years of output at MS's 624 a year (computed) [GS BA Data r219; MS BA Inventory r41]. Staying with Do Nothing for another round does not empty the line before the mid-2030s. The risk of waiting is competitive, not cash.
- **Engine selection is a supply-risk choice the models do not price.** The 737 MAX depends on one engine supplier, while the A320neo family has two. The 2019-2025 record shows what production disruption costs: $17.0B of concessions and $45.6B of one-time BCA costs (computed) [GS BA FCF build r23; GS BA BCA margin r87]. A second engine source, or a Joint Venture engine with committed capacity, buys resilience. These files cannot put a number on it.
- **The models' biggest disagreement is capital allocation:** GS's $42B of buybacks against none in MS [GS BA CFLO r45; MS BA Cash_Flow_Annual r51]. Customer advances also differ by $17.4B by 2030 (computed) [MS BA Balance_Sheet_Annual r33; GS BA BS r29]: a launch that brings early deposits could help cash, and a slip in 777X or 737 deliveries hurts it twice, through advances and through concessions.
