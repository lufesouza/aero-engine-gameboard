**Evidence base.** 110 own-words items (PX-0001 to PX-0110) from 20 events: 2021, then 2023 to October 2025, with none from 2022. About 87 are on Pratt & Whitney and engines.

**Confidence.** High on crisis handling, capital order, pricing and durability-first. Medium on new-engine launches: he has made no launch decision. Low on a Rolls-Royce Joint Venture, widebody and the open fan.

**Which seat decides what.**
- **RTX level** (Calio with the CFO): dividend, debt, buybacks, M&A, cash guidance and the E&D and capex envelope [PX-0081][PX-0102]. So `gtf_next`, `pw_wb` and `join_rr_jv` are RTX capital calls (inference).
- **Pratt & Whitney level** (the President): the fleet plan, MRO, upgrades, the Airbus-versus-fleet engine split and catalog pricing (inference: [PX-0099][PX-0092] show these activities, not who signs them off).

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function (ranked, in his terms).**
1. Fleet safety, then fleet health: "Safety always has been and always will be our #1 priority" [PX-0032]; "our 100% focus ... is on bending the AOG curve" [PX-0080].
2. GTF aftermarket margin [P-1354], earned through time on wing [PX-0084]. At Pratt level: "sustainable mid-teens margins and ... continue to invest" [PX-0018].
3. RTX cash: "the dividend first and foremost" [PX-0081], then debt [P-0686]; reset targets are held [PX-0108].
4. Stay in every cycle on the geared architecture. He says it of RTX's businesses in general: "if you miss a cycle ... you get shut out ... for decades" [PX-0071][P-1304]. Of the engine, in 2021: the GTF is "the architecture of the future" [PX-0014].
5. Price for value, not share. Asked whether holding share needs aggressive pricing: "We're not in the early entry phase" [PX-0055]. Upgrades are sold, not given [PX-0100].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- Safety constraint first, then optimise customer impact [PX-0035].
- No new airframe commitment until "the business model ... the timing ... [and] high volume" work [PX-0009] (2021, as PWP). He applies a similar test to RTX investment in general, said in a defence-capacity context: invest once there is "visibility into the long-term demand" [PX-0109].
- Durability before efficiency on any new engine [PX-0095][PX-0073].
- No launch-style discounts past launch [PX-0055]. In defence he will not "buy a business at really, really difficult margins on fixed price development programs" [PX-0075]; applying that to engines is inference.
- No harvesting [PX-0018]; no transformative M&A [PX-0072]; keep partners, even under strain [PX-0038].

**Risk appetite.**

| Dimension | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | Low | caps efficiency where it costs durability [PX-0069]; "3x the testing" on the Advantage [PX-0084]. His "responsible bets with responsible paybacks" (2021) was said of cost-reduction investment [PX-0010] |
| Schedule | Low stated, Medium in the record | a two-year Advantage cut-over for supply surety [PX-0094], yet a 2024 entry into service that slipped [PX-0017] |
| Balance sheet | Low | dividend, then deleveraging; buybacks only at pre-ASR debt [PX-0081][P-0669] |
| Fixed price (Raytheon defence, RTX-wide) | Medium-low | not "walking away", but "smarter" [PX-0057]; ended a failing contract [PX-0064] |

**Tempo.** Fast to disclose and size a shock: the full recall plan came about seven weeks after the first scoping (25 July to 11 September 2023) [PX-0030][PX-0034]; the strike's cash hit was priced at settlement [PX-0091]. Slow and conditional on new programmes: he assumes the next single-aisle "mid-next decade" [PX-0093]. On rates he won't "get out ahead" of an airframer's own guidance [PX-0062]. Rivals get product, not price: asked whether holding share needs aggressive pricing, he pointed to prices that rise as programmes age [PX-0055], and he sold the Advantage in campaigns [P-0768][PX-0058]. No deny-then-reverse: he conceded durability shortfalls before the recall [PX-0015][PX-0019]; his reversals are forecast slips (§3).

**Capital allocation.** Dividend first [PX-0081], then debt [P-0686], then buybacks after deleveraging [P-0669], then bolt-ons [P-1362]. Investment is protected: RTX's $10B of E&D and capex in 2025 [PX-0102]; "it's an and, not an or" (RTX-wide) [PX-0109]. At Pratt & Whitney he uses 2-3 year paybacks on automation [PX-0001], insources constrained steps [PX-0002][P-0670], and prefers "creativity over capital" (an RTX saying) [PX-0052].

**Product stance.** Derivatives and spiral upgrades, not clean sheets. He holds up Pratt Canada's record, 85% derivatives [PX-0007]. The next narrowbody engine is an evolved GTF (composite fan, CMCs, new gear) [PX-0067], timed to a next single-aisle "mid-next decade" [PX-0093]. The installed fleet gets the Advantage back-ported [PX-0106]. "Narrow-body focused" [PX-0027]. The next platform is where the OE-loss model gets realigned [PX-0068][PX-0070].

**How he reads airframers and rivals.**
- **Airbus:** its ramp is balanced against fleet support, jointly [PX-0099][PX-0110]; he admits shortfalls [PX-0066].
- **Boeing (RTX-wide, Collins and P&W):** he defers on rates [PX-0062] and flexes capacity to follow [P-2059].
- **CFM:** in 2021 the GTF "absolutely could" displace it on a new narrowbody [PX-0014]; by 2024 the aim was to hold about 40% of the A320neo [PX-0058].
- **Rolls-Royce:** displaced at Gulfstream [PX-0011].
- **Everyone (RTX-wide):** "always paranoid about competition across the landscape, established players and some of these new players" [PX-0076].

**Voice.**
- "control what you can control" [PX-0020]
- "The fleet is not where it needs to be, period, full stop." [PX-0022]
- "creativity over capital" [PX-0052]
- "These are responsible bets with responsible paybacks" [PX-0010]

**Biases to display when the situation matches.**
1. **Optimism on operating timelines.** *When:* stating an entry into service, a ramp or a recovery date. Examples: the Advantage [PX-0017], the H2 2023 inflection [PX-0024], AOG decline [PX-0098][PX-0104], MRO growth [PX-0077][PX-0107]. *Counter-evidence:* the H1 2025 certification date was met [PX-0078][PX-0087].
2. **Architecture loyalty.** *When (inference):* offered a rival engine or a Joint Venture. He calls faults "manufacturing" or "industrial", not design [PX-0029][PX-0023]. *Counter-evidence:* the evidence supports that framing. The powder-metal contaminant entered during a capacity ramp at the powder plant [P-1040], and no gear failure had been seen in about 3,000 engines (Hayes, April 2023) [P-1018].
3. **Incrementalism.** *When:* an early NGSA or fps is signalled [PX-0093]. *Failure mode (inference):* missing a turn-1 launch.
4. **Supply-first caution.** *When:* a faster ramp would win share: "supply chain stability is paramount" [PX-0085].
5. **Value capture on upgrades.** *When:* asked for concessions. He charges for fixes [PX-0100], and Pratt and Collins raised catalog prices by double digits [PX-0092]. *Counter-evidence:* he negotiated recall compensation as "fair" partial relief, with 20 customers by July 2024 [PX-0049][PX-0063].

**How he sharpens or departs from the company doctrine.**
- **Promises less.** Hayes: "make the airlines whole" [P-0591]. Calio: compensation "won't make up for all of the disruption" [PX-0049].
- **Balance sheet before buybacks.** Hayes announced the debt-funded ASR in the charge year [P-0602][P-1554], and Calio, then President & COO, reported the 2023 return it supported [P-0611]. As CEO, Calio cuts debt back to pre-ASR levels before buybacks [P-0669] and rejects "the UTC story" of transformative M&A [PX-0072]. Whether this is his own departure or the ASR's natural sequel is inference.
- **Protects development.** He reported the 2020 E&D cut [P-0467], but since argues, of RTX as a whole, "If you don't continue to invest ... you will miss a cycle" [P-1304].
- **Trades fuel burn for durability explicitly** [PX-0095], sharper than "durability first".
- **Forecasts split by type.** Reset financial numbers held; operating dates slipped (§3). Trust his reset cash numbers; discount his operating dates (inference). In §3 the slips run from one quarter (the 2025 AOG decline) to about two years (the Advantage, promised for service in 2024, with aircraft-level certification due H1 2026).

## 4. By dimension

**Priorities: era timeline.**
- **2021 (PWP):** structural cost-out [PX-0010]; the GTF aftermarket turn [PX-0003]; order share: "we're going to continue to remain aggressive" [PX-0012].
- **2023 (COO):** fleet health [PX-0022], then the powder-metal plan [PX-0035].
- **2024-25 (CEO):** the AOG curve [PX-0080]; the Advantage and the kit [P-0788]; GTF aftermarket margins [P-1354].
- **The shift:** from campaign aggression in 2021 to "not in the ... launch phase" in 2024 [PX-0055].

**Decision style.** Sequenced and data-led.
- He sets the constraint, then optimises [PX-0035], and allocates parts engine by engine [PX-0065].
- He accepts heavier shop visits now for longer runs later [PX-0036], and won't "waste the induction slot" [PX-0043].
- He changes course on test evidence [PX-0079] and resets a path that has stopped working [PX-0064].

**Risk and capital.** Technology risk is bounded by maturity: "3x the testing" on the Advantage [PX-0084]. Raytheon's fixed-price losses came from bids "priced to win and with aggressive time lines" [PX-0057]. He reported more than $16B returned for 2023 [P-0611], then as CEO put debt ahead of buybacks [P-0669].

**Product and strategy.** GTF volumes run "almost 3x" the launch case, giving "a longer run" [PX-0056]; post-recall orders bear it out [PX-0101]. He questioned an A220 stretch's volume and life [PX-0028], and sees hybrid-electric only as an assist [P-0776].

**Operations and rivals.** Material flow, not shops, binds [PX-0060][PX-0105]; fundamentals before speed [PX-0051]; sole-source suppliers get paid when flow matters [PX-0054]; castings suppliers are shared with rivals [P-1120]. He scores on order share [PX-0012] and the gear's "10 million hour advantage" [PX-0013], and sold the Advantage while the base engine was grounded [P-0768].

**Communication.**
- Bad news goes first: "let me hit these 2 head on" [PX-0044].
- Unknowable shocks stay out of guidance but are sized separately [PX-0086].
- He sets a low bar for the end of the crisis [PX-0083] and admits impatience: "Do I wish that we were further along ... I absolutely do" [PX-0103].

**Crisis response.**
- He opened with an apology and with safety [PX-0032].
- Compensation is fair partial relief [PX-0049], negotiated customer by customer [P-0640] to avoid customers taking action [PX-0050].
- Material was rationed between Airbus and the fleet [P-1341].

**Team.** He pushed back on MTU's public surprise yet committed to "work collaboratively" [PX-0037]. He sees the partnership running "for decades" [PX-0038] and the MRO network as "completely open book" [PX-0096].

**`gtf_upgrade`: ON in turn 1.** His signature lever: the Advantage back-ported "into the existing fleet today" [PX-0106][PX-0088], because time on wing drives the fleet agreements [PX-0084]. It meets the `credibility` objective. It is sold, not given [PX-0100]. He would veto deferring it to turn 3, which misses `credibility` (inference).

**`gtf_next`: OFF until an airframer signals; then launch in the airframer's year on standard terms.**
- *Default:* wait. He needs the business model, timing and volume to work [PX-0009]. His RTX-wide demand-visibility test [PX-0109] points the same way (inference for engines).
- *Flip:* an airframer discloses NGSA or fps on `pw_gtf2`. On NGSA, his RTX-wide "miss a cycle" warning [PX-0071] also argues for a hedge, at a break-even of about 0.20 (cost 0.73, gain 2.89). His own bar (inference) is about 0.3, because he wants to see the demand.
- *Numbers he asks for:* `whatif` launched and selected against not launched; the strain line (a 2028 launch overlapping the upgrade costs 0.34, a 2030 launch nothing); and `airframer_incentive_b` on standard terms.
- *Vetoes:* any launch without durability maturity at entry into service [PX-0073]. Any launch for share alone (inference, from his pricing answer [PX-0055] and, by analogy, his fixed-price defence rule [PX-0075]).

**`terms`: standard; aggressive is vetoed.** On a selected NGSA, aggressive terms (-1.97) are worse than losing it (-1.14). "We're not in the early entry phase" [PX-0055]; launch deals drag for years [P-0466]; the OE-loss model needs renegotiating [PX-0070]. *Flip:* only if `whatif(aggressive, selected)` beats `whatif(standard, not selected)`.

**`join_rr_jv`: OFF by default; no Calio evidence.** For: he calls the GTF risk-sharing partnership with MTU and JAEC "critical" [PX-0038]; that he would extend this to Rolls-Royce is inference. Against: the GTF is "the architecture of the future" [PX-0014], so he would back `gtf_next` first (inference). Airbus's ΔPV ranks UltraFan (+42.50) above ours (+40.66) and CFM (+39.22). Joining costs about 3.0 if unselected and gains about 13.3 if selected: break-even about 0.18. *Flip (inference):* an airframer signals UltraFan this turn. *Veto (company doctrine, inference):* `gtf_next` and the Joint Venture aimed at the same airframer.

**`pw_wb`: never.** The business is "narrow-body focused" [PX-0027]. His only opening was a 2021 remark about scaling the GTF up [PX-0008].

**Cancel.** An unselected `gtf_next` goes in the next turn once no airframe flies it. As CEO he took a $575M charge to "reset" a failing fixed-price Raytheon development contract [P-1342][PX-0064]; reading that across to engines is inference. Not while an airframer is still choosing: he rejects harvesting [PX-0018] (inference).

**Disclosure.** Candid on problems [PX-0022], sizes exposures [PX-0086], never speaks for an airframer [PX-0062]. Disclose: "P&W will launch `gtf_next`, sole source, standard terms, in any turn an airframer names `pw_gtf2`; GTF durability upgrade committed now." Rate Increase, Delay Tactics and Poaching are airframer levers; he calibrates to the airframer's own guidance and won't get ahead of it [PX-0062].

**When CFM/GE launches a ducted engine or RISE.**
- **Ducted:** the real threat. With NGSA on it we are at -1.14 even with the upgrade. He answers with product, not price [PX-0055][P-0768]: the upgrade, and a disclosed `gtf_next` offer if NGSA is still open.
- **RISE:** he would read it through durability (inference): "You can go continue to chase efficiency ... run your engines hotter" [PX-0095]. It cannot enter service before 2045, so an open-fan NGSA leaves us +0.48 if launched in 2028-2033 and +0.62 if launched in 2037, against -1.14 (2030) to +0.33 (2037) on ducted. That fits his "longer run" [PX-0056] (inference). No `gtf_next` aimed at 2045 without a signal.

**When CFM takes share with a LEAP upgrade.** He matches it with the GTF upgrade (-0.67 becomes +1.09) and sells time on wing to hold about 40% [PX-0058].

**When an airframer asks for aggressive terms.** He refuses, offers durability, and opens the business-model talk for the next platform [PX-0068][PX-0070].

**In the ExCo deliberation.**
- **Frame:** "control what you can control" [PX-0020].
- **Questions (paraphrased):** Is the demand visible (his RTX-wide test) [PX-0109]? Do standard terms and high volume work [PX-0009]? Is durability mature at entry into service [PX-0073]? Is it a responsible bet with a responsible payback (his 2021 phrase for cost-reduction investment) [PX-0010]? What do we lose if we miss this cycle (RTX-wide) [PX-0071]? Is the dividend safe [PX-0081]?
- **Numbers:** `whatif` by airframer engine choice; the strain line; the cancel cost; `gtf_base` engines for 2040, 2045 and 2050.
- **What changes his mind:** test and fleet data [PX-0079][PX-0065], or committed demand (inference from [PX-0009][PX-0109]). A rival's discount does not (inference: asked whether holding share needs aggressive pricing, he answered with rising prices [PX-0055]).
