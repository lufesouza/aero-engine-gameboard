# Pratt & Whitney: calibration of the supplier parameters

These are the calibrated parameters for the `pratt_whitney` supplier player in the Boeing vs Airbus war game. Money is $B in constant 2026 dollars. Per-engine values are lifecycle values in $M (OE margin plus the PV of aftermarket profit at the 8.5% WACC), P&W-net of risk-sharing partners. All evidence ids refer to `wargame/profiles/pratt_whitney/evidence.jsonl`.

The values to merge are in `calibration.json`, which holds only the CALIBRATED leaves; the PLACEHOLDER strain leaves are left at their live values. I checked the merge in memory: every key exists in `wargame/config/default.json`, and all 44 tests in `wargame/tests/test_engine.py` pass with the merged values. The repo was not edited.

## How the two calibrations were reconciled

Two independent calibrations were run: A (bottom-up) and B (top-down). The table shows what each gave and what was chosen.

| parameter | A | B | gap | chosen | how it was resolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| alpha | 0.30 | 0.40 | 33% | **0.35** | The mapping was re-checked (`alpha_chk.py`). With the reconciled ramp, alpha is 0.28 for a 10% hurdle and 0.39 for 10.5%. The revealed GTF hurdle is about 10% all-in on an 8% cost of capital, i.e. about +2pp. |
| ramp.start_frac | -0.30 | -0.50 | 67% | **-0.35** | Re-fitted independently (`ramp_chk.py`). The analogue PV shortfall is 3.8-6.1 mature engine-years. A's ramp gives 5.3 and B's 6.6; -0.35 / 10y gives 5.9. |
| ramp.years | 9 | 10 | 11% | **10** | The calendar evidence (aftermarket parity about 10y, mature OE loss at 12y, EBIT breakeven at 11-12y) and slower fps / pw_wb volumes favour 10. |
| upgrade.capex_b | 1.3 | 1.0 | 30% | **1.0** | A's extra $0.3bn was MRO shop capacity (16 to 19 shops). That serves the powder-metal visit surge, not the upgrade, so it is excluded. |
| upgrade.installed_base_saving_b_per_year | 0.50 | 0.40 | 25% | **0.45** | Re-derived from shop visits avoided: $0.3-0.6bn a year. The midpoint is taken. |
| upgrade.saving_years | 8 | 10 | 25% | **9** | Contract terms are 8-10y (early) and 10-15y (FMP). From a 2029 start, the remaining term is about 6-10y. |
| programs.gtf_next.value_m_per_engine | 1.0 | 1.1 | 10% | **1.05** | Midpoint. |
| programs.pw_wb.value_m_per_engine | 2.8 | 3.0 | 7% | **2.9** | 1.05 x the 2.77 widebody/narrowbody ratio. |
| terms.aggressive | 1.0pp / 0.70 | 1.1pp / 0.72 | <10% | **1.1pp / 0.71** | A $0.30M concession: 100 x 2 x 0.30 / 55 = 1.09pp, and 1 - 0.30 / 1.05 = 0.71. |

Every other parameter had the same central value in A and B. Where their ranges differed, the range given here is the union of the two.

## Scale check

### Status quo

The game delivers 2,000 narrowbodies x 0.60 Airbus share x 2 engines x 0.40 fit = **960 GTF engines a year on the A320neo**. At $0.6M each, that books **$0.58bn a year** of lifecycle value, worth **about $6.9bn** in PV over 2026-60 (annuity factor 12.0 at 8.5%). With the live parameters it was 840 engines x $0.8M = $0.67bn a year, about $8.1bn.

The evidence gives a much smaller A320neo flow:
- GS's platform build ('A320neo @ 40%') has about 441-464 GTFs a year going onto A320neos over 2019-23 (P-1771).
- The game's A320neo engine count is therefore **about 2.1x real A320neo installs**.
- It is instead close to P&W's **total** GTF output across all platforms (A220, E2 and spares): 900 in 2023, 1,008 in 2024, 1,176 in 2026E and 1,371 in 2028E (P-0024, P-1788). Large commercial engine shipments were about 875 in 2023 (P-1064).
- At evidence scale the A320neo flow is about 460-720 engines a year, or **$0.28-0.43bn a year** at $0.6M.

### Cross-checks on value

- UTC valued its 7,000-engine GTF order book at more than $7bn of NPV, about $1.0M per engine in 2016 PV, before the durability problems (P-0182).
- GS's net GTF P&L impact is -$0.46bn (2024), -$0.1bn (2025E), about 0 (2027E) and +$0.31bn (2028E) (P-1782). That is the young-fleet drag the ramp represents.
- P&W's adjusted operating income is $2.28bn (2024) and $3.64bn (2028E) (P-0044, P-1793). The game's $0.58bn a year of A320neo lifecycle value is therefore about 15-25% of segment profit, which is plausible.
- Undiscounted lifecycle profit per engine: about -0.6 + 3.5 visits x $0.86M = about $2.4M (B).

### New programmes, at game scale

**gtf_next on a sole-source NGSA.** The game has 2,400 engines a year x $1.05M = **$2.5bn a year** at maturity. A real sole-source NGSA would deliver about 1,300-1,500 engines a year (about $1.5bn a year), so the game is about 1.7x. The ramp (-0.35 / 10y) removes about 5.9 mature engine-years of value, about **$14.9bn** in PV at EIS; the live ramp removed 2.4 engine-years. Own capex is $4.5bn, x1.35 alpha = $6.1bn. For comparison, GTF family spend was about $16bn over about 12 years (P-0298), and P&W E&D peaked at about $1bn a year (P-0217).

**pw_wb on a 787 Re-engine.** 170 x 0.588 x 2 = **200 engines a year** (versus about 168 real at 787 rate 7, P-2048) x $2.9M = **$0.58bn a year** at maturity, against $6.0bn of capex (x1.35). That is a weak case, consistent with P&W having no widebody ambition (P-1313).

**gtf_upgrade.** The installed-base saving of $0.45bn a year over 9 years from 2029 is worth about $2.3bn PV. The +5pp of fit adds about 120 engines a year, about $0.07bn a year. Alpha-loaded capex is about $1.25bn PV.

## Open issues

1. **Whether GS's per-engine figures are P&W-net.** GS's per-engine GTF economics (the $3M overhaul and 35% margin plug) are not reconciled to its segment model (P-1789). OE losses reconcile to P&W's reported NEM (P-0308), which is P&W-net. Aftermarket visit costs are taken at a 51% P&W share (P-1747) for the installed-base saving. If GS's figures were whole-programme, incumbent and new-engine values would fall by up to about half; the low ends partly cover this.
2. **Aftermarket margin drives the incumbent value.** The plausible range of 10-35% (P-0667, P-0478, P-1776, P-1754) moves the incumbent value from 0.1 to 1.3. This is the single largest uncertainty in P&W's payoff.
3. **Negative start_frac. Resolved in the engine.** Under the old formula, value x value_mult x ramp, aggressive terms shrank the early loss while the ramp was negative. The engine now books a new engine at mature value x ramp, minus a fixed concession of (1 - value_mult) x mature value per engine. Aggressive terms therefore always cost value, and the negative start_frac of -0.35 stands as calibrated.
4. **The ramp is shared.** One ramp applies to gtf_next and pw_wb. Widebody volumes of about 200 engines a year would learn more slowly, so the ramp is optimistic for pw_wb.
5. **Powder-metal costs are excluded.** These were a $2.9bn P&W-net charge (P-1563), left out of both the ramp and the incumbent value because the `gtf_durability_crisis` inject models them. Including them would push a GTF-repeat ramp fit to about -1.5.
6. **Shared terms across segments.** The aggressive-terms pair is set for narrowbody (1.1pp and 0.71, a $0.30M concession). On widebody the same multiplier implies a $0.84M concession, which is 0.93pp rather than 1.1pp.
7. **Alpha depends on the hurdle definition.** If P&W's hurdle is applied to actual aftermarket cash-flow timing (7-18 years after delivery) rather than lifecycle value booked at delivery, the equivalent alpha is much higher (0.5-1.2 at 10%). The booked-value mapping is used because that is what the game discounts.
8. **Strain is PLACEHOLDER.** No evidence isolates overlap strain. `full_overlap_b` 1.5 and `norm_years` 5 are kept, and `strain.background` is left empty. A possible background window for the 2026-27 GTF fleet recovery and the GTF Advantage cutover (P-1666, P-1824, P-1826) was not added.
9. **The JV split is not in this parameter set.** The `uf_nb` `jv_pw` partner shares sit in Rolls-Royce's parameters. P&W's precedent supports about 50/50: the Engine Alliance with GE (P-1676, P-1700), the 2012 midsize JV plan with RR (P-1870), and partnering out 'probably half' of a new engine (P-0713).
10. **Scale.** The game's narrowbody volumes are about 2x real A320neo installs. Per-engine values were deliberately not distorted to offset this, so P&W's absolute payoffs in the game are about 1.7-2x evidence scale.
