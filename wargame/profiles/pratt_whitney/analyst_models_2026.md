# Pratt & Whitney (RTX): 2026 analyst-model baseline

This note sets out the real-world financial position of Pratt & Whitney (P&W), drawn from two broker company models of its parent, RTX. They are Morgan Stanley (MS, Kristine Liwag, 27 July 2026) and Goldman Sachs (GS, Noah Poponak et al., dated 26 July 2026). No Capital IQ consensus was available for RTX, so the note has no consensus figures. Money is in US dollars, millions unless stated. RTX's year ends on 31 December. "A" means actual and "E" means a broker estimate. Both models stop at 2030E, and the GS GTF sheet stops at 2028E. Nothing covers the 2035-2050 rounds. P&W is a segment of RTX, so debt and cash are shown for RTX as a whole. In the game, the board's numbers decide the payoff. This note is the real-world context behind them. Source tags give file, sheet and row. "P&W_Annual" is short for MS's "Pratt & Whitney_Annual" sheet.

- **Leverage peaked in 2023 and is now falling.** In 2023A RTX bought back $12.9bn of stock [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r41] and issued $12.9bn of long-term debt [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r35]. It then repaid a net $2.5bn in 2024A and $3.4bn in 2025A, and GS models a further $2.8bn in 2026E with nothing after that [GS RTX CFLO r31]. Both brokers put net debt below 1x EBITDA by 2030E [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r82; GS RTX BS r80].
- **Free cash flow recovered as the GTF cash drain eased.** The 2026 company outlook is "~8.5bn to 8.75bn" [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Qtr BJ72]. Actual free cash flow in the first half of 2026 was 1,309 in 1Q26 plus 2,878 in 2Q26 [GS RTX CFLO r47, quarterly].
- **Powder-metal cash payments end in 2027-2028.** MS records the 2026 outlook as a "~$700mn powder metal impact" [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Qtr BJ15], which matches GS's 2026E figure. GS then has -200 in 2027E and nothing after that [GS RTX CFLO r65]. *Caveat:* this GS line sits in a "GS FCF build" whose total does not reconcile to reported free cash flow. Read it as GS's estimate of the yearly cash cost.
- **Shareholder returns are mostly dividends for now.** Both brokers assume no buyback in 2026E and $4bn in 2027E [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r41; GS RTX CFLO r35]. MS shows 45.6% of free cash flow returned to shareholders in 2025A and 44.5% in 2026E [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r77].
- **Cash left after dividends and buybacks over 2026E-2030E.** On MS it is about $15.6bn: $53.8bn of free cash flow, less $22.2bn of dividends and $16.0bn of buybacks. On GS it is about $10.4bn: $52.4bn, less $23.5bn and $18.5bn [calc. from MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r72/r40/r41; GS RTX CFLO r47/r34/r35].
- **Debt headroom.** Assuming a 3x leverage target, MS shows $32.0bn of "Dry Powder for M&A" in 2026E, rising to $65.0bn by 2030E [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r87].

- **P&W is RTX's largest segment but its least profitable.** In 2025A it made 36.1% of RTX sales but only 24.7% of segment adjusted operating profit [GS RTX SEG r108, r181]. Its 8.3% margin compares with 16.4% in 2010A [MS RTX P&W_Annual r46]. For contrast, in 2025A Collins earned 16.2% [MS RTX Recast Collins Aerospace_Annual r32] or 16.8% [GS RTX SEG r190], and Raytheon earned 11.5% [MS RTX Recast Raytheon_Annual r31].
- **Both brokers bring the margin back only to about 10% by 2030E.** By 2030E Collins reaches 19.0% [GS RTX SEG r190], with adjusted operating profit of 7,915 [MS RTX Recast Collins Aerospace_Annual r29] or 8,044 [GS RTX SEG r169]. P&W's is 4,481 [MS RTX P&W_Annual r43] or 4,490 [GS RTX SEG r168].
- **2026 outlook.** The company guides to P&W sales growth in the high single digits and adjusted operating profit up 275 to 350 [MS RTX ProForma_IS_Qtr BJ4, BJ13]. MS's 2026E is +341 on 2025A and GS's is +386 (+383 on the sum of its 2025 quarters), which puts GS above the top of the range [calc. from MS RTX P&W_Annual r43; GS RTX SEG r168]. Actual sales were 8,173 in 1Q26 and 8,881 in 2Q26, with adjusted operating profit of 711 and 740 [MS RTX P&W_Qtr r26, r43].
- **Aftermarket is overtaking new-engine sales.** Services were 43.9% of sales in 2025A and reach 54.4% in 2030E. They pass products in 2028E [calc. from MS RTX P&W_Annual r12, r21]. *Caveat (inference):* the 2023A products figure appears to carry the $5.4bn sales cut [MS RTX P&W_Annual r29], because products plus services equals reported sales.
- **The latest quarters.** Commercial aftermarket sales grew 19% in 1Q26 and 25% in 2Q26. Commercial OE sales fell 1% and 8%. Military sales grew 7% and 23% [GS RTX Metrics tracker cols J, K, I]. *Caveat:* for 3Q25 the GS tracker and MS's quarterly P&W "End market" rows appear to have the OE and aftermarket growth rates swapped [MS RTX P&W_Qtr r65-r66; GS RTX Metrics tracker cols J, K].
- **Backlog is not split out for P&W after 2019.** Commercial aerospace backlog (Collins plus P&W) was $125bn at end-2024, $161bn at end-2025 and $170bn at 2Q26 [GS RTX Data r207]. Total RTX backlog was $268bn at end-2025 and $289bn at 2Q26 [GS RTX Data r211].

## Engines and supply

**What the files show.**
- **Engine shipments, up to 1Q23 only.** Company-sourced quarterly shipments of large commercial engines peaked at 247 in 4Q18. They fell to 92 in 2Q20 and recovered to 224 in 4Q22 and 167 in 1Q23 [GS RTX Metrics tracker col E]. The series stops at 1Q23, and the "GTF" column is empty.
- **OE sales are falling while aftermarket sales grow.** This was the pattern in 1H26 (see The business). The files do not say why. One reading is that spare engines and repairs are taking priority over new engines (inference).
- **Engine problems show up only as money.** The only engine-problem data in the files are financial: the 2023 powder-metal charge and the customer compensation payments (next section). The only aftermarket inputs are generic ones: "Engine on-wing" 0.98 and "PBH rate / hour ($)" 150 [GS RTX GTF Analysis r37-r42].

**What the files do not show.**
- Durability measures: aircraft-on-ground counts, grounded A320neo or A220 fleets, shop-visit volumes, time on wing, or GTF Advantage performance.
- Supply-chain limits: castings, forgings, or how spare engines are allocated against new-engine deliveries to Airbus and Embraer.
- Deliveries by airframe after 2023: these rows stop or hold placeholders [GS RTX GTF Analysis r47-r50]. The MS "Pratt Canada (PW800)" row is all zeros [MS RTX P&W_Annual r6].
- How GTF costs are split with programme partners (MTU, JAEC).

## Investment capacity and programme risk

- **R&D is not visible.** Neither file gives P&W's R&D, capex or segment free cash flow. Only RTX-wide capex is shown: 2,627 in 2025A, 3,120 in 2026E (outlook "~$3,100" [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Qtr BJ24]) and 3,628 in 2030E [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r24].
- **Group capacity is strong by 2030.** About $10-16bn of cash is left after dividends and buybacks over 2026E-2030E [calc. from MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r72/r40/r41; GS RTX CFLO r47/r34/r35], net debt falls below 1x EBITDA [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r82; GS RTX BS r80], and MS shows $65.0bn of debt headroom at a 3x leverage target in 2030E [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r87]. For context, RTX sales grow from 88,603 in 2025A to 120,916 in 2030E [MS RTX ProForma_IS_Annual r9].
- **Precedent 1: the 2023 powder-metal charge (profit and loss).** P&W's 2023A sales were cut by 5,401, giving reported sales of 18,296 against adjusted sales of 23,697 [MS RTX P&W_Annual r29, r26, r30]. It reported an operating loss of -1,455 against adjusted operating profit of 1,688 [MS RTX P&W_Annual r36, r43]. MS adds back a further 3,069 [MS RTX P&W_Annual r41], and GS's one-time items total -3,143 [GS RTX SEG r156]. Neither model labels these rows "powder metal".
- **Precedent 1: the 2023 powder-metal charge (cash).** Customer compensation is -300 in 2023, -2,500 in 2024, -1,000 in 2025, -700 in 2026E and -200 in 2027E. That is $4.7bn in total [calc. from GS RTX CFLO r65], with the same caveat about the GS build as above. The files give neither the programme's lifetime cost nor the partners' share.
- **Earlier charges, not labelled in the files.** MS adds back 947 in 2015A and 810 in 2020A [MS RTX P&W_Annual r41], and adjusts sales by 142 and 425 in the same years [MS RTX P&W_Annual r29]. GS's one-time items were -990 in 2020A [GS RTX SEG r156]. The files do not say what these charges were.
- **Precedent 2: the long GTF ramp.** On the GS legacy model (modelled figures, not company-reported), the net GTF impact was negative every year from 2014 to 2027E, at its worst -975 in 2018 [GS RTX GTF Analysis r24]. The modelled OE loss was $4.2m per engine in 2016 [GS RTX GTF Analysis r14]. Over a similar period P&W's adjusted margin fell from 16.4% in 2010A to 8.1% in 2018A and 2.5% in 2020A [MS RTX P&W_Annual r46]; the files do not split that fall by cause.

## What this means for your decisions

- **Cash does not limit a new engine launch in the 2030 round; the return test does (inference).** By 2030E RTX's net debt is below 1x EBITDA [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r82; GS RTX BS r80] and $10-16bn is left after shareholder returns over 2026E-2030E [calc. from MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r72/r40/r41; GS RTX CFLO r47/r34/r35]. A new engine (GTF2, solo) would compete with buybacks, not with the dividend or the credit rating (inference).
- **Launch against a committed airframer, not on speculation (inference).** On the GS legacy model (modelled, not company-reported), the GTF loses money on every new engine sold and turns net positive only in 2028E [GS RTX GTF Analysis r14, r24]. Both brokers already have P&W at only about a 10% margin in 2030E, while GS has Collins at 19% [GS RTX SEG r189, r190; MS RTX P&W_Annual r46]. Starting a second loss-making ramp before the first one pays back would hold P&W's margin down for longer (inference). Prefer the "launch only if an airframer selects P&W" form of the move (inference).
- **Do Nothing earns well in the near term, but its costs come later (inference).** Services pass products in 2028E [MS RTX P&W_Annual r12, r21], and on the GS legacy model the installed base reaches about 10,400 engines by 2028E [GS RTX GTF Analysis r19]. The cost is losing a place on the next narrowbody, which falls after the models end in 2030E (inference).
- **A Joint Venture with Rolls-Royce shares the new-engine risk (inference).** P&W has the lowest margin of RTX's segments (see The business), so sharing new-engine losses with a partner would suit the parent (inference). The price is an equal share of the market with the partner. The files cannot test the terms, including whether a partner would lower P&W's own development bill.
- **Airframers will price in durability (inference).** The 2023 precedent cost $5.4bn of sales [MS RTX P&W_Annual r29] and, on GS's estimate, $4.7bn of compensation cash over 2023-2027E [calc. from GS RTX CFLO r65; same caveat about the GS build]. Airframers choosing an engine for a new narrowbody will weigh this, so expect to offer durability guarantees (inference).
- **Timing: 2030E is the strongest balance-sheet year the models show.** By then the powder-metal payments have ended on GS's estimate (0 from 2028E [GS RTX CFLO r65]), buybacks have restarted [MS RTX Cash_Flow_Annual r41; GS RTX CFLO r35] and leverage is at its lowest in either model [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r82; GS RTX BS r80]. The models say nothing about the later rounds. If airframers use Delay Tactics, the GTF aftermarket lasts longer, but returns on an early launch come later (inference).
- **Widebody: the models carry no P&W widebody engine.** The files give no basis for a widebody move, and the Joint Venture route is a narrowbody move (inference).

## What the models do not cover

- **No forecasts after 2030E.** The GTF sheet stops at 2028E. The 2035, 2045 and 2050 rounds rest on board numbers alone.
- **No P&W segment cash flow, capex or R&D.** Every cash and balance-sheet figure here is for RTX as a whole.
- **No P&W backlog after 2019, and no current dollar split of P&W sales.** There is no company split into commercial OE, commercial aftermarket and military; the company data come only as growth rates.
- **No GTF durability or supply data.** There are no company-reported GTF deliveries after 1Q23, and no total powder-metal cost or partner split.
- **Nothing on next-generation engines.** That covers the GTF Advantage beyond its name, open-fan designs, a P&W answer to a new Boeing narrowbody and dual sourcing on a new airframe.
- **No consensus figures.** No Capital IQ consensus was available for RTX.
- **The two models disagree in places.** Collins 2025A adjusted operating profit is 4,893 [MS RTX Recast Collins Aerospace_Annual r29], 5,085.9 [MS RTX Financial Summary r46] or 5,072 [GS RTX SEG r169]; the MS recast notes an actuation divestiture [MS RTX Recast Collins Aerospace_Annual]. P&W 2025A adjusted operating profit is 2,722 in the GS annual figures but 2,725 when its own quarters are added up [GS RTX SEG r168]. 2Q26 P&W sales are 8,881 [MS RTX P&W_Qtr BF26] against 8,889 [GS RTX SEG DQ14]. MS's annual debt sheet labels 2020-2025 as estimates [MS RTX Debt_Annual], so this note uses its balance-sheet rows instead [MS RTX Balance_Sheet_Annual r80-r82].
