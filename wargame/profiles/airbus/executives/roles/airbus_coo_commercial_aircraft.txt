1. HEADER
---------

Role. CEO of the Commercial Aircraft business. He is listed on the Executive Committee directly
after the group CEO [AX-0109].
- He joined "the business in November" and took the role on 1 January 2026 [AX-0108, A-0493]. The
  year of his arrival is cut off in the OCR. The handover is reported in the FY2025 report, so the
  arrival was probably November 2025 (inference).
- He overlapped with his predecessor, Christian Scherer, who handed over "institutional knowledge,
  ongoing projects, and stakeholder relationships" [AX-0100]. The Board credits the CEO with the
  "seamless transition" of the CEO of Commercial Aircraft [AX-0093]; that quote refers to the role
  and names neither man.
- Operating chain: there is no group COO. An EVP Operations and an EVP Programmes and Services sit
  under the Commercial Aircraft CEO [AX-0031]. A group COO existed in May 2022 [AX-0032].
- His prior career is not in the sources.

What he inherited (end-2025):
- Rate commitment: A320 family at "between 70 and 75 aircraft a month by the end of 2027,
  stabilising at rate 75". The Board Report attributes the slip to an engine supplier whose name is
  illegible [AX-0054].
- Delivery record: 793 deliveries in 2025, up from 766 in 2024 but 3% below the initial projection,
  after a 3 December guidance reset that the Board ties to supply-chain challenges [AX-0052,
  AX-0053, AX-0009].
- Quality: two technical issues at end-2025, with root-cause investigations under way [AX-0056], and
  A320 quality reports [AX-0057].
- Capacity: new A321 lines in China and the US, and the Spirit work packages for the A220 and A350
  [AX-0060].
- Programmes: the A350F in its industrial phase [AX-0082], and NGSA technologies being matured, with
  no launch [AX-0020].
- Market position: about 60% of the single-aisle backlog [AX-0025].

Evidence base. 2 AX items [AX-0108, AX-0109], plus 2 that name him in the handover [AX-0100, A-0493]
and 1 that refers only to the role [AX-0093]. Confidence: Very low. The profile is derived from his
role.

4. IN THE GAME: IF WAGNER IS IN THE ROOM
----------------------------------------

He is the ExCo's production, quality and supply-chain test. (inference throughout)

* Lever: NGSA launch
    Pushes for: Launch only once the production system and the rate-capable wing are on track
      [A-0441, A-0225]. Game proxy: no NGSA launch before 2028, the company default.
    Vetoes: A launch in 2026-27 that competes with the end-2027 ramp for engineering talent and
      suppliers, unless whatif shows at least $1B. An NGSA launch in 2028 passes.

* Lever: Engine choice
    Pushes for: No added EIS years; full capture
    Vetoes: Open fan or UltraFan on NGSA without at least $1B, given the engine-supply history
      [AX-0054]. In the game the open fan cannot enter service before 2045 and failed this in every
      case tested: NGSA 2028 on it is $54.13B below ducted (base, no fps).

* Lever: A350 Re-engine
    Pushes for: Not while the A350F and the Spirit A350 packages are being absorbed [AX-0082,
      AX-0060]. Game proxy: no A350 Re-engine before turn 3 (the ../profile.md default plan).
    Vetoes: Concurrency with NGSA under a supply-crunch inject

* Lever: Rate (Boeing's Rate Increase)
    Pushes for: Our own ramp: "disciplined and controlled" [AX-0010]
    Vetoes: Reacting to Boeing's rate

* Lever: Poaching
    Pushes for: On while a programme is in development: talent binds both the ramp and new
      programmes [A-0366]
    Vetoes: n/a

* Lever: Delay Tactics
    Pushes for: No view in evidence. The rules come from ../profile.md hard rule 4; the quality and
      integrity pillars apply [AX-0073].
    Vetoes: A second turn

What he asks for. The overlap years and strain dollars in whatif, the engine's eis_add, and whether
an inject (supply crunch, engine maturity slip) changes concurrency costs.

Evidence. 6 AX items, all [outside: Boeing], 2011-2018, plus company items repeating them [A-0072,

What the outside views say:
- A public market caller. In 2011 Boeing's commercial chief said there were "a few things that John
  and I agree on and certainly where the market is going is" (the quote is cut off) [AX-0094]. In
  2012 a Financial Times journalist on a Boeing call said Leahy "thought the order cycle had

  my guess is John would feel that he was -- got carried away with himself, which can happen, but
  you have to ask him" [AX-0096]. This is one instance.
- Fought on fuel-burn numbers. Boeing's commercial chief pitched the MAX as "8% better than a NEO"
  and planned to "say that to John Leahy" [AX-0098].
- A superb but solo dealmaker. In March 2018, discussing the change at the top of Airbus's sales
  team, Boeing's marketing chief said he "was a superb sales leader, but he did a lot of it on his
  own". Boeing expected Airbus to become "more disciplined" while remaining "a very aggressive
  competitor" [AX-0099, A-0128]. No item links Leahy to any compliance matter.

Context, not attributed to him. Airbus's commercial conduct in the years Boeing describes him as
sales chief included:
- neo pricing reported at or below A320ceo prices [A-0064];
- launch pricing Boeing called predatory [A-0066];
- the American Airlines breakthrough [A-0045];

- the Japan Airlines A350 win [A-0086].

No item names him as the decision-maker in these campaigns (inference: these fall in his era only).
