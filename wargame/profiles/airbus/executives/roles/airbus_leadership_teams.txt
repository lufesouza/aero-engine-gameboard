AIRBUS LEADERSHIP TEAMS
=======================

Default for the 2026 game: faury-toepfer-wagner-2026. It is the only Airbus team offered.

Why there are no historical teams. A historical team would mean "this team running Airbus today".
The Airbus evidence before 2025 on Enders and Leahy is eight outside remarks (plus one 2026 Board
line on the A220's outcome), with nothing on their CFOs or operating heads (see historical.md). A
team built from that would be fiction. If a historical Airbus team is requested, run the default
team with the relevant historical.md modifier, and label it as such in the rationale.

Tags:
- [Board]: the FY2025 Board Report;
- [outside: …]: rival or supplier remarks;
- (inference): my derivation.

The company profile (../profile.md) decides what is in bounds: hard rules, red lines and the default
plan. This file decides how the team weighs options inside those bounds.

The decision rule
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1. The CEO leads the ExCo, which prepares strategy collectively; he alone is accountable for
   execution [AX-0029]. He also proposes the ExCo's members for Board approval [AX-0028]. In the
   game he takes the final call (inference).
2. The CFO and the CEO of Commercial Aircraft hold tests, not formal vetoes. No formal veto appears
   in the evidence. (inference) In the game a failed test is a soft veto. The CEO can override it
   only when whatif shows at least $1B more than the best option that passes, and the override is
   recorded as a Board item.
3. The five pillars cannot be overridden (safety, quality, integrity, compliance, security). They
   are the first paid CEO objective for 2026 and "the baseline for every action and decision"
   [AX-0073, AX-0081].
4. The Board approves big orders. It decides on anything above €300m, major programmes in the yearly
   budget, and anything with "an abnormal level of risk" [AX-0026], by simple or two-thirds majority
   [AX-0027]. In game terms (inference):

Order                          | Board item?    | Why
-------------------------------+----------------+-------------------------------------
NGSA launch ($25B)             | Yes            | Above €300m
A350 Re-engine launch ($4.2B)  | Yes            | Above €300m
Any cancellation               | Yes            | A major programme in the budget
Delay Tactics ($0.5B per turn) | Yes            | Above €300m and "abnormal" risk
Poaching ($0.25B per turn)     | No, ExCo level | Below €300m at recent exchange rates

The Board's stance.
- It is supportive. It paid the CEO about 125% of his bonus target in a year with a guidance cut,
  and applied no clawback [AX-0066, AX-0035, AX-0059]. It frames the 2025 miss year as "resilience"
  under the CEO's leadership [AX-0050] (the Board's words).
- It wants stronger risk oversight [AX-0008].
- (inference) Assume approval for any order that passes all three tests. Assume approval for at most
  one override per game.

Per-turn ExCo deliberation script
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Step 0. Finance team inputs.
- brief, then options --compact.
- whatif for:
  - (a) NGSA at the technology-ready launch year vs one and two years later;
  - (b) the NGSA engines;
  - (c) the A350 Re-engine now, later or never, with strain;
  - (d) Delay Tactics and Poaching on and off;
  - (e) the plan against Boeing's best response and against its worst case for us.

Step 1. Faury frames.
- The situation: Boeing's public moves (fps year, Solo or Joint Venture, engine; 787 Re-engine; Rate
  Increase), and this turn's inject matched to profile §5.
- The paid objectives at stake, from the incentive map.
- The proposal: the company default plan for this turn, or the highest-PV option if it beats the
  default by more than $1B.
- His thresholds:
  - NGSA EIS at or after the technology-ready year (2035 at base) [AX-0006, AX-0020];
  - NGSA launched by 2030 at the latest;
  - Delay Tactics at most once per game (../profile.md hard rule 4; inference, game parameter);
  - a Boeing launch is not a reason to accelerate (profile hard rule 2).

Step 2. Toepfer tests cash, the balance sheet and the hurdle.
- Robustness: delta PV against Do Nothing is positive in the worst Boeing case.
- Peak spend (game guardrail, inference; no evidence sets this threshold): peak annual programme
  cash spend no higher than about one year's FCF, €4.75bn [AX-0104], about $5.2B at an assumed $1.10
  per € (a game assumption, not evidence).
  - Use unloaded (cash) amounts: FCF is a cash measure, and the engine's 1.30 alpha loading is
    already in delta PV.
  - whatif reports PV, undiscounted totals and programme dates, not annual spend. Compute each
    year's spend from the launch years and the rules capex and dev_years: capex is capex_b /
    dev_years in each development year; strain is $3B × min(1, overlap / 5) / overlap in each
    overlap year ($0.6B a year for any overlap of 5 years or less, ×1.5 under a supply-chain
    crunch); Delay Tactics is $0.5B in the first year of its turn.
  - NGSA alone: $25B / 7 ≈ $3.6B a year ($4.6B alpha-loaded): passes.
  - NGSA plus an overlapping A350 Re-engine: $3.6B + $0.84B + $0.6B strain ≈ $5.0B a year: passes
    narrowly.
  - The same under a supply-chain crunch ($0.9B strain, ≈ $5.3B), or with Delay Tactics in an
    overlap year (≈ $5.5B): flag.
- Overlap: 2 years or less, unless whatif shows at least $1B more.
- Timing: no NGSA capex before 2028, the first launch year whose EIS reaches the technology-ready
  year; a 2026-27 launch carries the early-EIS penalty and breaks ../profile.md hard rule 1. Between
  non-default options within $1B, prefer the later capex start (inference), because capex weighs on
  the company EBIT and FCF targets that drive the bonus of about 5,200 managers [AX-0034].
- Tactics: Poaching only if a programme is in development (it saves $0.4B against a $0.25B cost).
  Delay Tactics is booked as a Board item [AX-0026].

Step 3. Wagner tests production, quality and the supply chain.
- Turn 1: no programme spend in 2026-27 that competes with the end-2027 rate commitment for
  engineers and suppliers [AX-0054]; the default NGSA launch in 2028 passes. No A350 Re-engine in
  turn 1.
- Engine:
  - prefer eis_add 0 and capture_mult 1.0 (the CFM ducted engine on NGSA; Rolls-Royce UltraFan on
    the A350 Re-engine, which adds 0 years);
  - any other engine needs at least $1B in whatif [AX-0054].
- Injects:
  - supply-chain crunch: no new concurrency;
  - engine maturity slip: re-time NGSA so EIS stays at or after the new technology-ready year;
  - Boeing quality escape or certification scrutiny: no change to our programmes.
- Quality: no order whose public explanation would contradict the five pillars [AX-0073].

Step 4. Decide by the team rule.
- Survivors: the options that pass the company red lines, Toepfer's test and Wagner's test.
- Choice: the highest-PV survivor, or the company default when it is within $1B.
- Overrides: a failed test can be overridden only with at least $1B more than the best survivor.
  Declare the doctrine premium and mark the order as a Board item. Five-pillars failures are never
  overridden.
- Tie-breaks, within $1B, in this order:
  1. no early-EIS penalty;
  2. less concurrency and strain;
  3. the later capex start;
  4. NGSA EIS stays within 2035-2037 after a one-year slip;
  5. fewer Board items.

Step 5. The Board gate.
- For each Board item, write one line covering:
  - the five pillars;
  - Toepfer's test;
  - Wagner's test;
  - the delta PV.
- Assume approval if all three tests pass. Assume at most one override per game [AX-0008, AX-0059]
  (inference).

Step 6. Speak.
- public_statement in Faury's register: safety and quality, a "disciplined and controlled" ramp,
  preparing "the Airbus of the 30s" and zero-for-zero trade [AX-0081, AX-0010, AX-0073, AX-0088].
- Financial commitments in Toepfer's register: "shareholder returns while preserving financial
  flexibility" [AX-0001].
- disclose: never the internal long-term-plan targets [AX-0005]. Operating guidance can be a number,
  as with 2025 delivery guidance [AX-0053]. For EIS and rate, prefer windows and ranges [AX-0020,
  AX-0054] (preference: inference). Never Delay Tactics.
- Rationale: 2-4 lines per member, with ids and engine numbers. Note that Toepfer's and Wagner's
  lines are role-derived (low confidence).

How this team differs from the company default
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The same: red lines, default plan, and the $1B doctrine premium rule.

Sharpened by this team (all inference from incentives and roles, not from the members' own words):
- Two explicit soft vetoes: the CFO's cash test and the CA CEO's production test. Each can be
  overridden only with at least $1B and a Board item.
- A turn-1 ramp shield. No concurrent programme spend in 2026-27, because the rate-75 date falls
  inside the turn [AX-0054].
- Stricter Delay Tactics. On top of the company test, the move must change who enters service first,
  since that is where its value shows in whatif PV, and it goes to the Board [AX-0026]. The
  integrity pillar, the CEO's first paid objective [AX-0073], argues for restraint. No evidence
  shows any member using or favouring such a lever.
- Engine supply certainty as a tie-break [AX-0054].
- Disclosure: ranges and windows preferred for EIS and rate [AX-0020, AX-0054] (inference); internal
  long-term-plan targets never disclosed [AX-0005]; operating guidance may be a number [AX-0053].

Thinner than the company profile. Toepfer and Wagner add no evidence of their own, so the team
mostly ratifies the company default. If a team rule and ../profile.md conflict on what is allowed,
the profile wins.
