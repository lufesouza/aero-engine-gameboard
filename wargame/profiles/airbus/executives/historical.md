# Historical Airbus leaders: Enders, Leahy, Scherer (outside-view cards)

**Read this first.**
- **Outside views only.** Each card rests on a handful of remarks by rivals (Boeing), a supplier (the CEO of Pratt & Whitney's parent) or journalists on their calls, plus a few lines from the FY2025 Board Report. None of these leaders' own words are in the sources.
- **Not playable teams.** The evidence is too thin to build a decision rule, so no historical Airbus team is offered (see `teams.md`). Use these cards for flavour, or as a "what if this person sat in today's ExCo" modifier, and say so in the rationale.
- **Tags:**
  - **[outside: Boeing]**: Boeing executives, analysts or journalists on Boeing calls;
  - **[outside: P&W]**: the CEO of Pratt & Whitney's parent (UTC);
  - **[Board]**: the FY2025 Board Report;
  - **(inference)**: my derivation.

## Tom Enders: CEO (as named in 2017-2019 remarks)

**Evidence.** 3 AX items: 2 [outside: P&W], 1 [Board]. **Confidence: Very low.** The sources do not give his tenure dates. He is addressed as Airbus's chief in October 2017 and January 2019 [AX-0036, AX-0037].

**What the outside views say:**
- **The bold CSeries deal (October 2017)** [outside: P&W]. UTC's CEO called it "a great deal for them", adding that "Airbus has high confidence or they wouldn't have done this deal". He read it as a vote of confidence in the GTF engine [AX-0036]. An analyst noted that Airbus and Bombardier had talked about 6,000 aircraft over about 20 years in the below-150-seat market [A-0126] [outside: Boeing].
- **Wariness of supplier power (January 2019)** [outside: P&W]. Enders, like Boeing's CEO, pushed back on supplier consolidation (UTC buying Rockwell Collins). UTC described the relationship as "a little bit of show-me" [AX-0037].

**Outcome on the record** [Board]:
- **A220 (the CSeries):** 482 delivered and 467 in backlog by end-2025, 93 of them delivered in 2025 [AX-0038].
- **Status:** a programme still in production, with 949 aircraft delivered or in backlog [AX-0038]. No cited quote gives an Airbus unit target for the programme, so no judgement against an ambition is made.
- **Ownership:** Airbus now holds 75% of the programme, with the Quebec government's 25% redeemable in 2035 [A-0265].
- **Supply:** his successor bought Spirit's A220 work packages to secure supply [AX-0060].

**Not attributed to him in the sources.** The A380 exit, announced in February 2019 [A-0143], comes after the January 2019 remark that names him. No item attributes the decision to him.

**If he sat in today's ExCo (inference, weak):**
- **Acquisition as a route into a segment,** as with the CSeries [AX-0036, A-0265].
- **Wariness of supplier consolidation,** making suppliers prove themselves ("a little bit of show-me") [AX-0037].

The evidence supports nothing on deal price, sourcing policy, disclosure style, NGSA timing, A350 Re-engine, Delay Tactics or Poaching.

## John Leahy: chief commercial officer, Airbus's sales chief (as described 2011-2018)

**Evidence.** 6 AX items, all [outside: Boeing], 2011-2018, plus company items repeating them [A-0072, A-0083, A-0128]. **Confidence: Low.** Four of the six are Boeing executives characterising a rival. The other two are a journalist's questions on Boeing calls.

**What the outside views say:**
- **A public market caller.** In 2011 Boeing's commercial chief said there were "a few things that John and I agree on and certainly where the market is going is" (the quote is cut off) [AX-0094]. In 2012 a Financial Times journalist on a Boeing call said Leahy "thought the order cycle had probably peaked" at Farnborough [AX-0095]; these are the journalist's words, not Leahy's. Boeing's CEO's reply is summarised in the item but not quoted. The sources do not show how either call turned out.
- **Publicly questioned the 787's reliability after the July 2013 Heathrow fire** (as quoted by a Financial Times journalist [AX-0097]): "it's pretty obvious this airplane is not reliable and does not have mature systems". Boeing's CEO answered with a hedged guess: "you'd have to ask him, but my guess is John would feel that he was -- got carried away with himself, which can happen, but you have to ask him" [AX-0096]. This is one instance.
- **Fought on fuel-burn numbers.** Boeing's commercial chief pitched the MAX as "8% better than a NEO" and planned to "say that to John Leahy" [AX-0098].
- **A superb but solo dealmaker.** In March 2018, discussing the change at the top of Airbus's sales team, Boeing's marketing chief said he "was a superb sales leader, but he did a lot of it on his own". Boeing expected Airbus to become "more disciplined" while remaining "a very aggressive competitor" [AX-0099, A-0128]. No item links Leahy to any compliance matter.

**Context, not attributed to him.** Airbus's commercial conduct in the years Boeing describes him as sales chief included:
- neo pricing reported at or below A320ceo prices [A-0064];
- launch pricing Boeing called predatory [A-0066];
- the American Airlines breakthrough [A-0045];
- the Lion Air win with early slots [A-0080];
- the Japan Airlines A350 win [A-0086].

No item names him as the decision-maker in these campaigns **(inference: these fall in his era only)**.

**If he sat in today's ExCo (inference):**
- **Public statements:** willing to question a rival's product in public after an incident, as with the 787 in 2013 [AX-0097] (one instance).
- **Disclosure:** public market-cycle calls, as at Farnborough 2012 [AX-0095].
- **Integrity limit:** the 2026 integrity and compliance pillar [AX-0073] limits how far that voice goes.
- **Delay Tactics:** the rules come from `../profile.md`; nothing in Leahy's record bears on it.

## Christian Scherer: former CEO, Commercial Aircraft

**Evidence.** 1 AX item [Board] that names him [AX-0100], plus 2 that describe the handover without naming him in the quoted text: AX-0093 refers to the role, and the A-0493 quote names only Wagner. **Confidence: Minimal.**

**What the sources say:**
- **An orderly handover.** He "worked closely with Lars Wagner", ensuring "a comprehensive transfer of institutional knowledge, ongoing projects, and stakeholder relationships" [AX-0100]. The Board calls the transition "seamless" [AX-0093].
- **His last year at the head of Commercial Aircraft (inference from the 1 January 2026 handover; no item attributes these outcomes to him personally):**
  - 2025 deliveries rose from 766 to 793 but came in 3% below the initial projection [AX-0052];
  - the 3 December guidance reset [AX-0053];
  - rate 70-75 re-dated to end-2027 [AX-0054];
  - the Board attributes the slips to the supply chain and an engine supplier [AX-0009, AX-0054].
- **Not in the sources:** his tenure dates, his earlier role as chief commercial officer, and his reasons for leaving.

**In the game.** Do not use him as a decision-maker. His card exists to explain what Wagner inherited (see `wagner.md`).
