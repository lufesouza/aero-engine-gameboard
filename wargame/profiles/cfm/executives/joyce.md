# David L. Joyce: operating seat (President & CEO, GE Aviation), GE's half of CFM

## 1. Header

**Role(s) and dates, as the sources print them.**
- President & CEO of GE Aviation and Vice Chairman of GE in every turn, July 2016 to March 2020 [CX-1109] [CX-1136]. The March 2019 page prints "Vice Chair" [CX-1129].
- In July 2020 Culp introduced John Slattery as "a worthy successor to David" [CX-0403].
- **His place under three GE CEOs:**
  - **Immelt** put the additive businesses under him as "our biggest practitioner" [CX-1043].
  - **Flannery** aligned Global Research under him [CX-0877].
  - **Culp:** "David Joyce made me take a Hippocratic oath when I came on board ... First, do no harm" [CX-0304].

**CFM.** GE holds 50% of CFM International [CX-0231]. Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Joyce calls CFM "of huge importance to both GE and the aviation industry" and says "the partnership between GE and Safran continues to deliver" [CX-0133]. Safran's leaders are not in the sources.

**Era.**

- **What happened on his watch:**
  - **the LEAP ramp:** 77 engines revenue-recognised in 2016 (plus 8 shipped), against a forecast of 110 [CX-1109] [CX-0770]; 459 in 2017 (actual), inside the 450-500 range [CX-1168] [CX-0842]; about six weeks late in March 2018 [CX-1118];

  - **Boeing's NMA** (the middle-of-market concept), studied [CX-1122]; that Boeing later shelved it is a reader note [CX-0143];
  - **the GE9X**, slipping [CX-1131] [CX-1135];
  - **the 737 MAX grounding** [CX-1132] [CX-1136].
- **Context:** he served under three GE CEOs in that time (above).

**Evidence base.**
- 28 `joyce` items and 7 `cfm_jv` items in his own words [CX-0130] [CX-0131] [CX-0132] [CX-0133] [CX-0134] [CX-0141] [CX-0142], from seven events, 2016-07-22 to 2020-03-04.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked in his own terms.**
1. **Secure the CFM56-to-LEAP transition:** "securing this transition to LEAP and successfully executing on the ramp secures the future of this business" [CX-0133].
2. **Hold the margin through the ramp:** "op profit rate held flat as we go through both the LEAP and the Passport ramp", without getting "a little bit fat on SG&A" [CX-1124].
3. **Use the CFM56 and services to pay for LEAP dilution.** "every time we trade a CFM for a LEAP, it's a negative" [CX-1111]. He sells the CFM56 tail at firm prices [CX-1117].
4. **Safety of flight first in a crisis:** "no more important imperative than getting to root cause and corrective action" [CX-1132].
5. **Product picking:** "probably most important skill of any business that works in this industry" [CX-1125].

**Decision rules and red lines.**
- **A new engine passes a business-case test,** not engineering enthusiasm: "How many engines are going to be underwing? How big is the market going to be? ... What's the competitor's response to it?" [CX-1122]
- **With the technology stack in hand,** a new product "comes down to a decision on whether we like the market" [CX-1121].
- **Derivative first.** For the NMA: "LEAP the baseline ... not a full generation, but a half generation" [CX-1126].
- **Engine suppliers per airframe:** "3 is completely out of the question ... Two needs a market big enough to offset the expenses" [CX-1127].
- **No geared product for the NMA:** "We would not offer a geared product in that marketplace" [CX-1128].
- **Acquisitions:** a stand-alone return above 20% before internal savings [CX-1110].
- **Ramp resilience:**
  - 90% of hardware dual-sourced;
  - suppliers able to run at 2x;
  - the system run at the new rate 6 months early [CX-1113].
- **On a quality escape:** shut the supplier and surge the second source [CX-1123].
- **Capacity follows the airframer's real rate,** as with the MAX [CX-1136] [CX-0141].

- **Deliberate on new airframes.** "can we get comfortable with the business case?" [CX-1122]
- **Instant on red lines:** "I can give you that assurance right now" [CX-1128].
- **Not deny-then-reverse,** but confident in advance. His "more ready ... than ever" was followed six months later by an open admission of delinquency [CX-1112] [CX-1118].

**Capital allocation stance.**

**Product stance.**

- **The GE9X** as sole source on the 777X [CX-1131].
- **The CFM56** extended while demand lasts [CX-0131] [CX-1117].

**How he reads the airframers and rivals.**

- **Position:**

## 5. In the game: if Joyce is in the room

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- **Business-case test first:** units under wing, market size and the competitor's response [CX-1122].
- **Offer a derivative:** a LEAP-based half-generation step, i.e. `cfm_ducted` **(inference)** [CX-1126]. RISE was launched after his time [CX-0147], so his view of `cfm_open_fan` is not in the evidence.
- **Price:** launch concessions to win, recovered from the second tranche of customers and from services [CX-1116] [CX-1111].
- **Exclusivity:** sole source preferred. He would bid as one of two only if the market is big [CX-1127].
- **Ramp:** dual sourcing, 2x surge, rate break [CX-1113].
- **Safran:** "a CFM engine" [CX-1126].
- **Refuses:**
  - a three-supplier airframe [CX-1127];
  - a geared design, his NMA red line [CX-1128] **(inference** for fps or NGSA**)**.

**Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody, Solo or Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney.**
- UltraFan has a gearbox. On his NMA red line he would not answer with a geared CFM product [CX-1128] **(inference)**.
- He would compete on utilisation and time on wing [CX-1133].

**Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.**
- The utilisation argument: 8.4 points, 120 flights [CX-1133].
- "We would not offer a geared product in that marketplace" (the NMA) [CX-1128]; for a GTF response this is an **(inference)**.

**An airframer asks for concessions.**
- **Launch concessions:** accepted as a phase [CX-1116].
- **Last-of-model engines:** no discounting [CX-1117].
- **A Boeing crisis:** negotiated payment terms instead of halting deliveries [CX-0142].

**A durability or supply-chain inject hits.**
- **Quality escape:** inspect, shut the supplier and surge the other [CX-1123].
- **Report the lateness in weeks** [CX-1118].
- **A recovery path with a run rate** [CX-1119].
- **Cut capacity to the airframer's real rate** [CX-1136].
- **An fps slip publicly labelled "supplier bottleneck":** no evidence. He would publish CFM's own delinquency rather than let it be implied **(inference)** [CX-1118].

**A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender.** GE alone, with no Safran gate.
- **The offer:** a derivative built on reused technology (CMCs on the GE9X) [CX-1114], i.e. `ge_genx_next` **(inference)**.
- **Sole source** [CX-1131]. Two suppliers only if the market is big [CX-1127].
