In March 2022 Slattery introduced him as "our technology leader, our VP of Engineering" (source page 790), and the speaker line reads "Vice President of Engineering" [CX-0001].

## 5. In the game: if Ali is in the room

**The Safran gate.** RISE is joint: "we're working with our partner, Safran" [CX-0013], and Culp calls it "our 50-50 JV with Safran" [CX-0166]. Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" [CX-0184]. Ali's own words show joint RISE work, not how he deals with Safran's engineers or how a technical disagreement would be settled. Every CFM stance below assumes Safran's consent **(inference)**. `ge_genx_next` is GE alone, with no gate.

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- **Architecture:** the open fan. 20% is impossible without it [CX-0180], and ducted gets "less than half" [CX-0183]. In engine terms he backs `cfm_open_fan` (+1.5pp margin) and accepts its +1 year to entry into service **(inference)**.
- **Timing is not his call.** Culp leaves entry into service to the airframers [CX-0205].
- **Dates:** he offers none he cannot test to "turn on and turn off" [CX-0009]. His 2022 fly-demo date slipped [CX-0211], so the referee should treat his schedules as optimistic **(inference)**.
- **Price and exclusivity:** no evidence. Defer to Culp and Ghai. Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with Safran [CX-0184].
- **Ramp and capacity:** from 2025 the supply chain sits in his organisation [CX-0633]. There is no evidence of how he sets rates.
- **Refuses** to give a date without test evidence **(inference)** [CX-0008].

**Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody, Solo or Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney.**
- UltraFan is ducted. His physics case says a ducted engine gets "less than half" the open fan's gain [CX-0183]; he never names UltraFan, so applying it there is an **(inference)**.
- He would not change architecture or answer on price **(inference)**.

**Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.**
- "you should be worried" about a rival that needs a large redesign [CX-0164].
- The hot-section experience is a moat [CX-0169].
- He argues on hot-and-harsh durability, where LEAP itself had a gap [CX-0006].

**An airframer asks for concessions.** There is no evidence. He supplies the durability data behind the price **(inference)**. Giving a fix to "everybody" instead of only those who need it is the one concession-like choice he describes [CX-0004].

**A durability or supply-chain inject hits.** This is his home ground.
- Root cause, then inspection and fixes accepted by the airframer and the regulator [CX-0003].
- Targeted or fleet-wide [CX-0004].
- Enhanced inspection [CX-0010], decided by a cross-functional team [CX-0012].
- He admits the gap in numbers [CX-0006].
- **`engine_maturity_slip`:** he accepts the slip rather than enter service before "turn on and turn off" **(inference)** [CX-0009].
- **An fps slip publicly labelled "supplier bottleneck":** no evidence. GE avoids "finger pointing" (Culp) [CX-0158].

**A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender.** GE alone, with no Safran gate.
- **The argument:** the GE90's "stable, reliable architecture" [CX-0005] and the GE90/GEnx cycles behind GE's hot sections [CX-0169].
- **The offer:** he favours `ge_genx_next`, a derivative, over a new engine **(inference)**.
