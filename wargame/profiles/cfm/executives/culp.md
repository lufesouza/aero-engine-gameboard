# H. Lawrence (Larry) Culp: CEO seat, GE's half of CFM

## 1. Header

**Role(s) and dates.**
- Chairman & CEO of GE from October 2018. Within 30 days he cut the dividend from $0.12 to $0.01 [CX-0234].
- From July 2022 he also ran GE Aerospace directly [CX-0530].
- After the HealthCare spin (3 January 2023) [CX-0475] and the 2024 spin-off, he led the aviation-only company, as planned in 2021 [CX-0477]. Capital IQ lists him as GE Aerospace Chairman & CEO in November 2025 [CX-0232].

**CFM.** GE holds 50% of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231]. Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Culp speaks only for GE's half, and Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Safran's CEO speaks once, on GE's stage [CX-0153].

**Era.** Industrial net debt/EBITDA was 4.8x at end-2018 [CX-0360]. On his watch:
- the 737 MAX grounding [CX-0274] and COVID [CX-0363];
- GECAS-AerCap [CX-0506] and more than $100bn of debt reduction [CX-0606];
- the break-up [CX-0470];
- a supplier-throttled LEAP ramp [CX-0613];
- LEAP durability kits [CX-0607] and RISE [CX-0476];
- 2025 tariffs [CX-0635] and the Air India 171 crash [CX-0648].

**Evidence base.** 425 `culp` items, October 2018 to October 2025: 423 in his own words and 2 analyst questions. 113 of them are tied to a trigger event. Another 51 `cfm_jv` items are in his own words.

**Confidence.**
- **High** on guidance, operations, capital allocation and crises.
- **Medium** on engine launch behaviour: RISE has never reached a product launch.
- **Low** on how he bargains with Safran.

He does raise guidance and targets once results run ahead: 2019 cash twice [CX-0317], the 2028 profit target in July 2025, three years early [CX-0597] [CX-0646], and the 2025 LEAP outlook [CX-0654].

## 4. Sections by dimension

- **2022-23, the break-up and the ramp.** He took over Aerospace [CX-0530].
- **2024-25, standalone GE Aerospace.** "playing more offense" [CX-0589], with supply as the constraint [CX-0613].

**Priorities.** Aviation was "job 1" during the reset [CX-0353]. In 2022, operations outranked the spins [CX-0491]. Time on wing is "the hallmark of any outstanding engine platform" [CX-0640].

**Decision style.**
- **First-hand:** "I need to feel it. I need to see it. I need to smell it" [CX-0259]. He spent kaizen weeks on the shop floor [CX-0466] [CX-0527].

## 5. In the game: if Culp is in the room

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- He competes without assuming incumbency [CX-0564] and leaves timing to the airframer [CX-0205].
- **Engine by date** (inference): RISE "could be available by the middle of the next decade" [CX-0608]. In the game the open fan cannot enter service before 2045, about ten years after his window. So offer `cfm_ducted` for an airframe launched before 2037, and `cfm_open_fan` for a 2037 launch, which enters service in 2045 without waiting [CX-0214]. An open fan on an earlier airframe waits for 2045 at 10% of programme capex a year; he would not press an airframer into that wait [CX-0205].
- **Price:** a risk-adjusted return, not the low bid [CX-0609] [CX-0250]. He takes early OE losses and priced service-contract risk to build the installed base [CX-0557] [CX-0207].
- **Exclusivity:** sole source is prized [CX-0632], with no lock claimed on both airframers [CX-0185].
- **Ramp:** bounded by supply [CX-0613].
- **Refuses** to trade durability for fuel burn [CX-0208].
- **Safran:** it is a CFM engine [CX-0189], and narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" (Ghai) [CX-0184].

**Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody, Solo or Joint Venture.**
- No public reaction [CX-0580] [CX-0600], and no price cut [CX-0609].
- RISE stays funded, "and where we can accelerate" [CX-0553].
- **Technical counter (Ali, not Culp):** ducted engines get "less than half" the open fan's gain [CX-0183].

**Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.**
- He competes on durability: more than 70% of A320-family wins since 2023 [CX-0218].
- He courts its operators [CX-0581].

**An airframer asks for concessions.**
- **Gate:** the order must be accretive on price, terms and scope [CX-0339].
- **Boeing in crisis:** he negotiated payment terms [CX-0362] and accepts ramp margin pressure [CX-0641].
- **Launch campaigns** (inference): OE concessions, then service contracts re-priced after launch (Ghai) [CX-0215].
- **Refuses** unmodelled risk [CX-0440].

**A durability or supply-chain inject hits.**
- He owns it and fixes at root cause. On LEAP supply, GE's engineers go onto supplier floors "to identify the problem, contain it in the short term and put it in permanent corrective action" [CX-0657], and one part fix is "not the unlock" [CX-0617]. (His "if it takes a while, it takes a while" [CX-0489] was said of GE military engines in 2022.)
- Retrofits take "years, not months" [CX-0649]. Safety outranks delivery [CX-0617]. He resets plainly [CX-0611] [CX-0628].
- **fps slip labelled "supplier bottleneck"** (inference): GE would not point fingers [CX-0625].
- **Engine-maturity slip** (inference): durability testing is front-loaded, with no rush [CX-0222].

**A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender.** GE alone, so no Safran gate.
- **787:** offer `ge_genx_next` (GE alone; the 787 Re-engine default) **(inference)**, defending a 70% life-of-programme win rate on the 787 [CX-0615] that he says was won on product, not price [CX-0599].
- **A350** (inference): he wants "all the critical platforms" [CX-0587], but talks stay private [CX-0586] and the economics must be additive [CX-0301].
- **Launch losses:** accepted where GE is sole source, as on the GE9X [CX-0631].
