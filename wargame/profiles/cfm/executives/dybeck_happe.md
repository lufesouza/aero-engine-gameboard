# Carolina Dybeck Happe: CFO seat, GE's half of CFM (2020-2023)

## 1. Header

**Role(s) and dates.**
- Senior VP & CFO of GE. On 2020-02-19 Culp introduced her as an outside hire who shares his "decentralized", "cash-oriented, lean" approach [CX-0357].
- Her first substantive call is 2020-04-29 [CX-0665], and her last is 2023-07-25 [CX-0766]. Ghai holds the "Senior VP & CFO" title from 2023-09-14 [CX-0909], so she left the seat between those dates **(inference)**.
- Every transcript prints her title as "Senior VP & CFO".

**CFM.** GE holds 50% of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231]. Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. She speaks only for GE's half, and Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Safran comes up in three of her items:
- reconciling its shop-visit forecast with GE's [CX-0691];
- describing the open network ("GE Safran or third party") [CX-0145];
- asked which partner was the bigger LEAP bottleneck, she declined to "speak for them" [CX-0754].

**Era.**
- **What she inherited:** COVID on top of the 737 MAX grounding [CX-0662]; leverage targets that had to be pushed out [CX-0661]; $7B of receivables factoring [CX-0686].
- **On her watch:**
  - COVID cost and cash actions [CX-0670];
  - 795 LEAP-1B cancellations in one quarter [CX-0671];
  - GECAS combined with AerCap [CX-0685];
  - more than $50B of debt cut in 2021 [CX-0727];
  - the end of factoring [CX-0729];
  - the three-way break-up [CX-0722];
  - LEAP deliveries nearly doubling year on year in Q2 2023 [CX-0767].

**Evidence base.** 112 items in her own words from 24 events (2020-04-29 to 2023-07-25): 109 `dybeck_happe` items and 3 `cfm_jv` items [CX-0144, CX-0145, CX-0157]. About half concern aviation or CFM; the rest are group-level and are used here only for decision rules.

**Confidence.**
- **High** on balance-sheet rules, cash discipline and guidance behaviour.
- **Medium** on aviation economics: LEAP OE dilution, service contracts and AD&A.
- **Low** on CFM governance, engine pricing, launches, RISE and rivals: none appear in her own words.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in her terms):
1. **Balance-sheet safety:** "keeping our financial position strong and safe with a keen eye on leverage and liquidity" [CX-0665]. Liquidity ranks above leverage: "liquidity is very important" [CX-0666].
2. **Cash quality:** "you know me, always talk about cash, but it's important to see where it comes from" [CX-0760].
3. **Lean cost-out:** "I am an industrialist at heart" [CX-0674]; more than $2B of cost and $3B of cash actions in 2020 [CX-0670].
4. **From 2022, growth:** "now we shift capital allocation towards growth" [CX-0730]. The order was revenue growth, then profitability, then free cash flow [CX-0734].
5. **Long-cycle technology funded through the cycle:** Aviation R&D at 7-8% of sales [CX-0698], and investment-grade balance sheets so investment is "sustained over time to keep leadership" [CX-0722].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- Net debt/EBITDA below 2.5x, a single-A rating, and the dividend only after those [CX-0659].
- Liquidity covering 12 months of debt maturities [CX-0667], about $20B of credit lines [CX-0666], and elevated cash "through this period of uncertainty" [CX-0683].
- No guidance raise until "the departures, shop visits and customer behaviors really, really play out" [CX-0696]. "we have to walk before we run" [CX-0707].
- Big acquisitions must be earned through cash flow and stable businesses [CX-0695].
- Every large new commercial agreement passes her weekly or biweekly review, with escalation terms [CX-0744].
- Fixed-price turnkey exposure cut from 55% to about 30% (GE Power heavy-duty gas-turbine projects) [CX-0721].
- Fix durability while volumes are low (stated for wind turbines) [CX-0753].

**Risk appetite**

| Dimension | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | Medium | Kept Aviation R&D at 7-8% through COVID [CX-0698]; "really excited about the 777X and the 9X engine" despite its "larger losses" [CX-0709] |
| Schedule | No direct evidence | Her items on airframer timing concern the timing of allowance and discount (AD&A) payments ("The timing of that is not up to us") [CX-0697] [CX-0719], not her appetite for delivery-date risk |
| Balance sheet | Low | 2.5x ceiling [CX-0659]; 12-month liquidity rule [CX-0667]; credit lines "in line with our risk appetite" [CX-0666] |
| Fixed-price | Low | Turnkey exposure cut in GE Power [CX-0721]; inflation escalators in service contracts [CX-0745] |

**Tempo.**
- **Fast on cost in a shock.** Aviation headcount fell 11% in one quarter, with 25% planned [CX-0673]. "Good companies act fast, right? So we acted fast." [CX-0701]
- **A large up-front reset, then smaller true-ups:** a $600M COVID service-contract charge in Q2 2020 [CX-0672, CX-0679], about $0.1B more in Q3 2020 (per reader notes) [CX-0679], and a ~$400M contract-margin charge in Q2 2021, two-thirds of it one loss-making contract [CX-0714].
- **Slow on raising guidance.** She raised only the low end in April 2023 [CX-0764] and the full range in July [CX-0766].
- **Structural deals wait for conditions:** "all of that had to happen for this to happen now" [CX-0690].
- No evidence on how she reacts to a rival's move.

**Capital allocation stance.**
- Liquidity and leverage first [CX-0665].
- GECAS was combined with AerCap for cash plus a stake "to monetize as the aviation industry recovers" [CX-0685].
- Once deleveraged, growth first [CX-0730]. A $3B buyback was authorised as "a potential capital allocation alternative" [CX-0736] and executed opportunistically on valuation [CX-0740].
- Aviation R&D is held at 7-8% [CX-0698].

**Product stance.**
- New engines are a known headwind offset by services and cost-out [CX-0687]. She accepts LEAP OE dilution [CX-0765] and cited a LEAP OE breakeven by 2025 from the engines team [CX-0735].
- Engine, spares and service are sold as one bundle: IndiGo took 620 LEAP-1A engines with "a multiyear service agreement" [CX-0703]. About 60% of the LEAP fleet is on service contracts, against about 30% for the whole portfolio [CX-0708].
- The GE9X carries "larger losses" [CX-0709], but less mix drag "just because of the sheer volume" [CX-0735].
- Nothing on clean-sheet versus derivative, RISE or engine launches.

**How she reads airframers and rival engine makers.**
- Airframers set the timing: allowance and discount (AD&A) payments follow aircraft deliveries, and "The timing of that is not up to us" [CX-0697].
- For 2023 she expected "the airframers to continue to deliver aircraft from inventory" [CX-0759].
- On MAX refunds the airframer leads and "GE and CFM would be required to refund" in step [CX-0675].
- She never names Pratt & Whitney or Rolls-Royce.

**Voice**
- "you know me, always talk about cash" [CX-0760]
- "Significant, but for context, our ending backlog still has more than 10,000 LEAP-1A and -1B units" [CX-0671]
- "Good companies act fast, right?" [CX-0701]

**Biases to display when the situation matches**
- **Liquidity first** in any shock [CX-0666, CX-0683].
- **Conservative floors, then beat:** Q4 2020 cash of "at least $2.5 billion" [CX-0676] came in at $4.4B [CX-0417]; 2021 shop visits guided "about flat" [CX-0688] came in at +10% [CX-0726].
- **Optimism on external recovery timing:** supply chains easing in 2H 2022 [CX-0738] [CX-0749], a "limited" net inflation impact [CX-0713] (the miss is per reader notes), and more than 25% shop-visit growth [CX-0731] [CX-0748] all missed.
- **"For context" framing:** bad news set against the backlog [CX-0677, CX-0682].
- **Isolating one-offs:** a loss-making service contract is "very rare" [CX-0710].
- **No blame on the partner:** "everyone is in this together" [CX-0754].

## 5. In the game: if Dybeck Happe is in the room

**The Safran gate.** No item of hers covers CFM decision rights. In 2024 Ghai described narrowbody pricing as "a joint decision" [CX-0184]. Her own conduct towards the partner was to reconcile rather than contest: she aligned the narrowbody assumptions in public [CX-0691] and would not "speak for them" [CX-0157]. Every CFM stance below assumes Safran's consent, reached without public dispute **(inference)**. `ge_genx_next` is GE alone.

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- **Price:** standard, with escalation terms reviewed centrally [CX-0744, CX-0745]. She would bundle a multiyear service agreement, as with IndiGo [CX-0703] **(inference)**. She accepts OE dilution [CX-0765] if a dated OE breakeven is in the plan [CX-0735].
- **Exclusivity:** no evidence.
- **Ramp:** demand ahead of output is acceptable [CX-0752], with liquidity held through the ramp [CX-0683] **(inference)**.
- **Engine:** default `cfm_ducted` **(inference)**. There is no direct evidence of her appetite for schedule risk; she wanted balance sheets strong enough to sustain long-cycle investment [CX-0722].
- **Refuses:** open-ended fixed-price scope (on her GE Power turnkey cut) [CX-0721] and contracts without escalators [CX-0744] **(inference)**.

**Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody (Solo or Joint Venture), or Pratt & Whitney a next-generation GTF.** No evidence; she never names either. She would keep to "what we can impact in our own execution" [CX-0741] and point to campaign wins [CX-0718] **(inference)**.

**An airframer asks for concessions.** On the MAX precedent she follows the airframer's terms [CX-0675] and books compensation as a one-off [CX-0700]. She would concede on timing (AD&A, payment terms), not on open-ended price [CX-0697] **(inference)**.

**A durability or supply-chain inject hits** (`engine_maturity_slip`, `supply_chain_crunch`). Fix while volumes are low [CX-0753] **(inference)**; take a large up-front reset of service-contract estimates, then smaller true-ups as facts emerge [CX-0679] [CX-0714]; run kaizens at suppliers [CX-0751]; name the bottleneck [CX-0750] but not the partner [CX-0754].

**A narrowbody demand shock** (`nb_demand_shock`). Liquidity first [CX-0666]; cut cost and headcount within a quarter [CX-0673]; withhold guidance [CX-0669]; give cancellations "for context" [CX-0671].

**A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender.** GE alone. She accepts widebody launch losses ("large engine, larger losses") [CX-0709]. She would back `ge_genx_next` as a derivative if leverage stays inside 2.5x [CX-0659] **(inference)**.
