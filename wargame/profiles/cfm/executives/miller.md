# Jamie Miller: GE Senior VP & CFO, 2017-2020

## Header

- **Roles in the sources.** In 2016-17 she spoke as CEO of GE Transportation (the headers misprint "Senior VP & CFO"); those four items give only general decision rules [CX-1141, CX-1144]. Her first call as GE CFO was 2017-10-20 [CX-1145], the call on which Jeff Bornstein stepped down from the role [CX-0117]. Her last item is 2020-01-29 [CX-1246].
- **CEOs she served.** John Flannery to September 2018, then Larry Culp, who "joined October 1" [CX-1231]. Teams: `flannery-miller-joyce-2018` and `culp-miller-joyce-2019`.
- **CFM.** GE holds half of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company; Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227, CX-0231]. Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Miller never names Safran or CFM governance in her own words. On her watch Culp sized GE's MAX exposure from Safran's disclosure [CX-0136], and an analyst benchmarked her $400M quarterly drag against Safran's figure [CX-0140].
- **What she inherited.** The 2017 industrial cash outlook was already down from $12B to about $7B [CX-1148]. LEAP was ramping from 77 engines in 2016 [CX-0073] to 459 in 2017 [CX-1168]. Capex had run at a 1.4-1.5x reinvestment rate through the launch years [CX-1149].
- **On her watch.** The dividend was halved [CX-1154], then cut to $0.01 a quarter [CX-1190]. An insurance charge pre-flagged and then given on a special call (the $6.2B figure is in reader notes only) [CX-1163]; a downgrade from A to BBB+ [CX-1194]. The new revenue standard booked each LEAP at its own cost, exposing early-programme losses [CX-1174]. LEAP output reached 1,118 in 2018 [CX-1195] and about 1,736 in 2019 (the sum of quarters, per reader notes) [CX-1192]. The 737 MAX grounding cost Aviation about $1.4B of 2019 cash [CX-1242]; the GE9X certification delay added cost [CX-1237].
- **Evidence base.** 110 items in her own words from 21 events (2016-06-09 to 2020-01-29). About 49 quote aviation directly. Cross-references come from Flannery [CX-0837, CX-0865], Culp [CX-0234, CX-0299], Joyce [CX-1129] and CFM items [CX-0150, CX-0194].
- **Confidence.** Medium-high on cash, leverage, guidance and capital allocation. Medium on Aviation economics, because many of the numbers come from prepared segment read-outs she delivered. Low on CFM governance, RISE, pricing decisions and rival engine makers: none appear in her words.

## Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in her terms):
1. **The balance sheet.** "We've got too much debt at the company, and we're taking actions to tackle that very directly and very swiftly" [CX-1208]. Her policy is a single-A rating, industrial leverage below 2.5x net debt to EBITDA and "a high cash balance" [CX-1198].
2. **Cash and cost, not EPS.** "EPS is ... no longer the metric we manage to" [CX-1201]. Her first-year priorities, in order, were cash and deleveraging, cost, "a cash and returns focus", then simplicity and transparency [CX-1173].
3. **Aviation as the protected franchise.** It gets "the right R&D dollars, the right CapEx dollars to grow" [CX-1225]. Its job is to launch LEAP in a "really clean way" and to lean into "all of the services flow" [CX-1229].
4. **LEAP down the cost curve to breakeven.** More than 40% of the engine's cost had been taken out, with breakeven "around 2021" [CX-1193]. Aviation margins were to hold about 20% through the ramp [CX-1226].
5. **"Few surprises"** [CX-1232]: "back to the basics", consistent and transparent [CX-1145].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- Deleverage first; "pivoting to play offense" comes afterwards [CX-1219]. No equity raise [CX-1167].
- Underwrite on "risk-adjusted cash returns and margins, no longer comping on share" (said of GE Power's underwriting framework, April 2019) [CX-1217]. Tighten the "strike zone" and test "how the cash profile really looks on these contracts going out several years" [CX-1165]. For aviation campaigns she described discounts "across both engines and services" [CX-1216]; using the Power rule for CFM terms is an **(inference)**.
- Plan on self-help, not the market [CX-1206]. Build contingency into both the operating plan and the deleveraging plan [CX-1223].
- Do not build to plans that are "too optimistic for the market" (said of GE Power) [CX-1158]. Do build supply-chain inventory ahead of a committed ramp [CX-1151].
- Engine-programme capex is judged over the whole life cycle, under a board capital-allocation committee [CX-1153]. Capex falls to a 0.9x reinvestment rate once launches roll off [CX-1149].
- Raise guidance only at the half, once visibility improves [CX-1233]. Pay business leaders 50% on free cash flow [CX-1211].

## Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**
- **2016-17, Transportation CEO.** She bid thin and designed cost out after award: "10 points of incremental margin to the as-bid deal" [CX-1141].
- **October 2017 to September 2018, under Flannery.** The reset, "back to the basics" [CX-1145], with joint reviews of contract assets [CX-0837] and multi-year cash and leverage scenarios [CX-0865].
- **October 2018 to January 2020, under Culp.** Deleveraging, bottom-up planning ("we started completely fresh again") [CX-1231], then the MAX grounding.

**Priorities.** The balance sheet and Power came first [CX-1208]. Aviation's priorities were a clean LEAP launch, then the aftermarket [CX-1229]. Running the businesses better and changing the portfolio were "an and equation", not alternatives [CX-1169].

**Decision style.** Deep and bottom-up: "going deep, coming back up" [CX-1157], with no "flyby" reviews [CX-1228]. She waits for several clean quarters before calling a turnaround [CX-1221], and puts decision rights in the operating businesses [CX-1218], because staff who "don't own it" lose touch with cost and strategy [CX-1210].

**Risk.** She treats cancellable engine orders as firm, because airframers move cancelled slots to other customers [CX-1175]. She counts on the services annuity being "very stable" through cycles [CX-1222], and in March 2019 called Aviation "recession-resilient for the next 12 to 18 months" [CX-1203]; the slice ends before that was tested.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is a payout-ratio question [CX-1154]. GECAS "will remain as it is today" in January 2018 [CX-1162], then became "something we could think about" by October [CX-1184].

**Product and strategy.**
- LEAP OE losses are a cost-curve problem, "closer and closer to that each year" [CX-1230], funded meanwhile by the aftermarket [CX-1250]. Service agreements run 10-20 years: "predictable maintenance costs in exchange for performance guarantees" [CX-1152].
- Airshow wins are reported at list price, outside orders: $12B of LEAP at Farnborough 2018 [CX-1183]. LEAP orders rose 77%, on both airframers, during the grounding [CX-1238].

**Operations.** She flagged shop turnaround times and costs early [CX-1182], reported LEAP lateness in weeks [CX-1180, CX-1195], and tracked LEAP against CFM56 units quarter by quarter [CX-1249]. She accepted a working-capital build to protect the 2018 ramp [CX-1151].

**Rivals.** She is respectful and redirects to her own execution: "They're a great competitor" [CX-1143]. She sees pricing power in Aviation, unlike Power [CX-1160]. The evidence on engine rivals is thin: one win-rate item [CX-1197].

**Communication.** She pre-flags charges and calls a special call "when we knew it" [CX-1163], and reports a downgrade with its contained cost [CX-1194]. She defended the contract-asset model, "nothing I have seen that gives me an indication of an accounting issue"; within three months the SEC opened an investigation [CX-1146].

**Crisis response (737 MAX).** She kept shipping engines [CX-1207, CX-1243] and quantified the hit per quarter: $300M, then $400M while grounded [CX-1235, CX-1236]. She absorbed it inside guidance, offset by services collections and the timing of discount payments [CX-1248], warned that more than half of that offset would reverse in 2020 [CX-1239], and would not forecast beyond the current year [CX-1242].

**Team.** She asked for "transparency and candor and pushback" [CX-1161]. Corporate headcount fell from about 26,000 to about 13,000 as functions moved into the businesses [CX-1218].

## In the game: if Miller is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with the CFM partner (2024) [CX-0184]. In her era the partners split the MAX exposure about evenly [CX-0136]. No evidence shows her handling Safran. Every CFM stance below assumes the partner's agreement, sought through the Aviation chief. Her own Joint Venture template is pre-agreed supply at market prices and defined market sharing [CX-1171] **(inference** that she would apply it to CFM).

### 1. As the market cell's CFM/GE today

None of these situations arose in her own words, so each line applies her cited rule **(inference)**.

- **fps or NGSA launch and engine tender.**
  - *Price:* offer `cfm_ducted` with discounts on engine and services [CX-1216], inside a strike zone of risk-adjusted cash returns (her GE Power underwriting rule [CX-1217], applied here by inference), with the multi-year cash profile tested [CX-1165].
  - *Exclusivity:* seek sole source, which she cites as a strength on the MAX and C919 [CX-1197].
  - *Ramp:* pre-build supply-chain inventory once the ramp is firm [CX-1151], not before (her GE Power lesson) [CX-1158].
  - *Refuse:* a share-buying price (the Power rule, by inference) [CX-1217]; any spend that breaks the 2.5x ceiling [CX-1198].
- **Rolls-Royce narrowbody UltraFan (Solo or Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney).** Downplay it publicly and redirect to execution [CX-1224]. Keep price inside the strike zone rather than chase (the Power rule, by inference) [CX-1217]. Track win rate as the scoreboard [CX-1197].
- **Pratt & Whitney next-generation GTF.** The same scoreboard: the 58% A320neo win rate and above 95% utilisation [CX-1197]. Respond on cost-down after award [CX-1144], not on headline price.
- **Concession request.** Prefer timing to price: discount and allowance payments are a managed cash lever [CX-1244, CX-1248]. As a precedent from just after her tenure (Joyce, March 2020), CFM agreed payment terms with Boeing rather than stopping deliveries [CX-0142]. Refuse open-ended price cuts outside the strike zone (the Power rule, by inference) [CX-1217].
- **Durability or supply-chain inject.** Disclose early in numbers: weeks late [CX-1195] and shop cost [CX-1182]. Commit to a recovery quarter [CX-1215]. Draw on contingency [CX-1223]. Keep guidance unchanged where the cost is contained, as with GE9X [CX-1237].
- **787 or A350 Re-engine widebody tender** (GE alone, no Safran gate). Offer `ge_genx_next` as a derivative. Her expectation that Aviation cash improves as the LEAP and GE9X investment cycle rolls off [CX-1176] argues against opening a new widebody capex cycle.
