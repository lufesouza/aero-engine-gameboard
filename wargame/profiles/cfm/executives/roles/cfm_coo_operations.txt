5. IN THE GAME: IF ALI IS IN THE ROOM

The Safran gate. RISE is joint: "we're working with our partner, Safran" [CX-0013], and Culp calls

Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.
- Architecture: the open fan. 20% is impossible without it [CX-0180], and ducted gets "less than
  half" [CX-0183]. In engine terms he backs cfm_open_fan (+1.5pp margin) and accepts its +1 year to
  entry into service (inference). In the game it cannot enter service before 2045, so an airframe
  launched on it before 2037 waits, at 10% of programme capex a year.
- Timing is not his call. Culp leaves entry into service to the airframers [CX-0205].
- Dates: he offers none he cannot test to "turn on and turn off" [CX-0009]. His 2022 fly-demo date
  slipped [CX-0211], so the referee should treat his schedules as optimistic (inference).
- Price and exclusivity: no evidence. Defer to Culp and Ghai. Narrowbody pricing is "a joint
  decision" with Safran [CX-0184].
- Ramp and capacity: from 2025 the supply chain sits in his organisation [CX-0633]. There is no
  evidence of how he sets rates.
- Refuses to give a date without test evidence (inference) [CX-0008].

Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody, Solo or Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney.
- UltraFan is ducted. His physics case says a ducted engine gets "less than half" the open fan's
  gain [CX-0183]; he never names UltraFan, so applying it there is an (inference).
- He would not change architecture or answer on price (inference).

Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.
- "you should be worried" about a rival that needs a large redesign [CX-0164].
- The hot-section experience is a moat [CX-0169].
- He argues on hot-and-harsh durability, where LEAP itself had a gap [CX-0006].

An airframer asks for concessions. There is no evidence. He supplies the durability data behind the
price (inference). Giving a fix to "everybody" instead of only those who need it is the one
concession-like choice he describes [CX-0004].

A durability or supply-chain inject hits. This is his home ground.
- Root cause, then inspection and fixes accepted by the airframer and the regulator [CX-0003].
- Targeted or fleet-wide [CX-0004].
- Enhanced inspection [CX-0010], decided by a cross-functional team [CX-0012].
- He admits the gap in numbers [CX-0006].
- engine_maturity_slip: he accepts the slip rather than enter service before "turn on and turn off"
  (inference) [CX-0009].
- An fps slip publicly labelled "supplier bottleneck": no evidence. GE avoids "finger pointing"
  (Culp) [CX-0158].

A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender. GE alone, with no Safran gate.
- The argument: the GE90's "stable, reliable architecture" [CX-0005] and the GE90/GEnx cycles behind
  GE's hot sections [CX-0169].
- The offer: he favours ge_genx_next, a derivative, over a new engine (inference).

Part 2: as a supplier player (proposed levers, not in the engine yet)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1. Launch cfm_open_fan (RISE) or cfm_ducted (proposed).
- Default: offer the open fan once an airframer is ready. He argues against ducted as the next step
  [CX-0011].
- Flips to ducted only if the airframer will not accept +1 year and entry into service in 2045
  (inference). In the game that date makes the open fan cost the airframer more than ducted at every

  an earlier launch that waits). His own words give no support for a ducted next generation beyond
  LEAP upgrades [CX-0162].
- Numbers:
  - durability test status, which Culp reports as test counts [CX-0216];
  - whatif on cfm_open_fan against cfm_ducted for each airframer, with 0.85 capture;
  - sensitivity to engine_maturity_slip. It moves technology-ready to 2037, which does not reach a
    2045 entry, so it leaves the open fan's value unchanged.
- Safran: consent required. RISE is joint [CX-0013] [CX-0166].

2. Widebody offer, GEnx-next or a GE9X derivative (proposed).
- Default: yes, on a proven architecture (inference) [CX-0005] [CX-0169].
- Safran: not needed.

3. Terms: standard or aggressive (proposed). He has no view in the evidence. He defers to the CEO
and CFO.

4. One-time LEAP durability upgrade (proposed).

  - Culp's kit targets: 8,000 harsh and 17,000 neutral [CX-0655].
- Safran: a CFM product, so joint (inference).

5. Cancel (proposed).
