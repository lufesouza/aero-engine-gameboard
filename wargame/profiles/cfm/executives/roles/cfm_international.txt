CFM INTERNATIONAL: THE JOINT VENTURE AND ITS GOVERNANCE
====================================================================================================

Plain-text copy of wargame/profiles/cfm/executives/cfm_international.md, built 2026-10-04 by
wargame/profiles/build/make_role_txt.py. The Markdown file is the source of truth.

What this file is. The institution behind the GE executive profiles here; not an Output 1 profile,
since CFM's own officers never speak in the sources.

Evidence. 110 cfm_jv items (100 from GE calls, 2017-2025; 10 from Capital IQ, November 2025), plus a
few GE executive items. One Safran executive speaks: CEO Olivier Andries, on GE's stage in March
2022 [CX-0153].

Confidence. High on structure and GE's public account; low on how decisions are reached with Safran.

1. WHAT CFM IS
--------------

- Owners. A Joint Venture of Engine Investments Holding Company, a GE subsidiary, and Safran
  Aircraft Engines [CX-0227] [CX-0231]. Culp: "our 50-50 JV with Safran" [CX-0166].
- A thin entity. Founded 1974, 64 employees per Capital IQ [CX-0228]. Engineering, production and
  most shop work sit in the parents, so the parents decide (inference, from the headcount and the
  joint work GE describes [CX-0186] [CX-0219]). It holds subsidiaries such as Shannon Engine Support
  Limited [CX-0224].
- Term. Extended to 2050, announced by Andries "together with my partner John Slattery" [CX-0153].
- Framing. Andries: "a unique partnership" that brought the "disruptive" CFM56 to market [CX-0154].
  Culp: "probably the most successful transatlantic joint venture in history" [CX-0198].

2. GOVERNANCE AND THE SAFRAN GATE
---------------------------------

What the parents share.
- Pricing. Narrowbody price increases are "a joint decision", worked by GE's commercial engines head
  "with his partners at CFM" (Ghai, March 2024) [CX-0184]. A mid-single-digit gross rise nets to
  "low single-digit ... given the revenue-sharing agreement" [CX-0184]; spares rises are diluted the
  same way [CX-0201]. Whether the caps sit with Safran, revenue-sharing suppliers or both is not
  stated.
- Delivery promises. In March 2022 Andries committed to more than 2,000 LEAPs in 2023 [CX-0149], as
  did Slattery on the same day [CX-0148]. By February 2023 the joint commitment was +50%: "Both GE
  and Safran ... are committed", "and we've set as much to both our major airframer customers"
  [CX-0160]. In July 2023 the target was 1,700 [CX-0172].
- Losses. For the MAX grounding Safran cited "a EUR 200 million exposure in the second quarter", and
  GE had "something in that same range" (Culp, April 2019) [CX-0136]. That suggests an even split of
  the loss (inference).
- Shop visits. About 70% of LEAP shop visits by 2030 to be done by CFM, "split equally between our
  JV partner, Safran and us" (July 2025), up from a 60/40 plan in 2024 [CX-0219] [CX-0187].
  Customers choose "GE Safran or third party" [CX-0145]: the parents share and compete.
- Working level. Joint supply-chain reviews (2017) [CX-0127]; GE's Bill Fitzgerald spoke to his
  Safran counterpart "3 or 4 days a week ... sometimes 7" [CX-0128]; a "joint GE Aerospace and
  Safran team" cut LEAP test-cell hours by 30% (2024) [CX-0186]. Tom Levin, who "runs CFM programs"
  for GE, "facilitates the signing" of the MRO partner deals [CX-0170] (the transcript credits the
  answer to Culp; the speaker is uncertain).

What each keeps. Separate technology lines: Safran has "their own technology development" [CX-0129].
Tariffs go to fixed-price suppliers, but with Safran "it's a different dynamic" [CX-0202]. GE does
not comment on Safran's own results or its share of supply constraints: "we'll leave their reports
to them" [CX-0146]; "everyone is in this together" (Dybeck Happe) [CX-0157]. But GE does state joint
commitments [CX-0160] and has named a Safran labour disruption [CX-0220].

Public reconciliation. Narrowbody assumptions "pretty aligned" [CX-0144]; the CFM56 outlook "echoed"
by Safran [CX-0199]; a 2028 target of 2,500 LEAPs that an analyst said "your partners put out there"
[CX-0195], adopted by Culp in July 2025 [CX-0213].

The Safran gate (rule for the game). A CFM programme, pricing or capacity decision needs both
parents. The evidence shows joint pricing [CX-0184], rate commitments [CX-0160] and RISE launch
[CX-0147], but never a disagreement, vote or deadlock. Treat the gate as consent with no observed
veto (inference). GE's widebody engines (GEnx, GE9X) sit outside CFM and pass no gate.

3. PROGRAMMES AS THE EVIDENCE SHOWS THEM
----------------------------------------

CFM56: the cash engine. A franchise that "fuels our investments in the rest of this business"
[CX-0130]; production ran past 2019 as LEAP ramped slowly [CX-0131]. Licensed third parties do about
70% of its shop visits [CX-0165]. The fade keeps moving out: a peak "2025-ish" in March 2024
[CX-0177]; a plateau "in that 2,300 range" in May 2025, "and we've been wrong" [CX-0204].

LEAP. Three engines: -1A (Airbus), -1B (Boeing), -1C (COMAC C919) [CX-0168]. The -1A and -1B share
only about 10% of their roughly 2,500 parts, so two ramps run in parallel [CX-0159].

* Date: Mar 2019
    Commitment: About 2,200 LEAPs in 2020
    Outcome: Capacity cut to 1,400 for 2020 after the MAX grounding (March 2020); the 2020 actual is
      not in the evidence; 845 in 2021 (MAX grounding, COVID)
    Ids: [CX-0132] [CX-1136] [CX-0148]

* Date: Apr 2019
    Commitment: OE breakeven in 2021
    Outcome: 2025 (2022 view); OE profit 2026 (2025 view)
    Ids: [CX-0135] [CX-0150] [CX-0194]

* Date: Mar 2022
    Commitment: 2,000 LEAPs in 2023 (GE and Safran)
    Outcome: Target cut to 1,700 by July 2023. 2023 closed at 1,570 engines, +38% (Culp, January
      2024), or +25% (Culp, March 2024); the two records conflict
    Ids: [CX-0148] [CX-0149] [CX-0172] [CX-0590] [CX-0176]

* Date: Mar 2024
    Commitment: Deliveries +20-25% in 2024
    Outcome: "didn't ship as many leaps ... as we had intended"
    Ids: [CX-0179] [CX-0196]

* Date: Mar 2024
    Commitment: -1A kit into production 2024; -1B 2025
    Outcome: -1A certified 6 Dec 2024; -1B certification 1H 2026
    Ids: [CX-0178] [CX-0225] [CX-0210]

* Date: Jul 2025
    Commitment: 2,500 LEAPs in 2028; profit parity with CFM56 by ~2030
    Outcome: Open
    Ids: [CX-0213] [CX-0212]

Durability is the central story: a time-on-wing shortfall "particularly in the Middle East and
India" [CX-0161], which the -1A kit aims to fix "more than twofold" [CX-0209]. Early-life risk sat
with the parents through service agreements GE tends "to underwrite internally" [CX-0163], priced
"accordingly" to win share [CX-0207]; later contracts are re-priced up [CX-0215].

RISE.
- Launch. Launched in 2021 "in concert with our partners at Safran" as a technology programme, to
  become product "as our airframer and airline customers deem appropriate" [CX-0147]. Andries set
  the aim: a disruptive engine "by 2035" with 20% better fuel burn [CX-0152].
- GE's markers. A ducted engine gets "less than half the fuel burn improvements" [CX-0183]
  [CX-0180]. "We're all in, Ron, on open fan" [CX-0214]; customers need "at least a 20% reduction in
  fuel burn" [CX-0217]; the next CFM engine "could be available by the middle of the next decade"
  [CX-0189].
- Joint work. Over 200 wind-tunnel hours "with our partner, Safran" [CX-0191]; 350-plus programme
  tests by July 2025 [CX-0216]. GE's workshare: Avio Aero's gearbox and low-pressure turbine
  [CX-0181].
- Slip. A fly demo "by the middle of this decade" (2022) [CX-0151] became flight test "this decade"
  with Airbus [CX-0211].
- LEAP lessons. Earliest-ever dust testing on RISE HPT blades [CX-0222]; a Chief Mechanic and
  Architect for open-fan durability [CX-0223]; RISE technology feeds LEAP upgrades [CX-0162].

4. CUSTOMERS AND RIVALS AS GE DESCRIBES THEM
--------------------------------------------

- Boeing. Sole source on the MAX [CX-0182], "a backlog that we share" [CX-0197]. GE paces -1B
  shipments to Boeing's actual rate and engine inventory [CX-0206]. In the grounding it cut LEAP MRO
  capex to match [CX-0141]. On the NMA, talks stayed "between partners and between the customer",
  judged on "the long-term economics for GE" [CX-0139].
- Airbus. About 55-60% of the A320neo [CX-0168] [CX-0182]; over 70% of A320 family wins since 2023,
  credited to durability [CX-0218]. On rate 75, "there is no daylight there" (the transcript renders
  the Airbus CEO as "[ Gilman ]") [CX-0200]. On an A220-500 second engine: "we'll keep our comments
  private" [CX-0171].
- COMAC. LEAP-1C sole source on the C919 [CX-0134]; nothing more.
- Demand. Recent orders span both airframers: Riyadh Air and Avolon (-1A), Ryanair (-1B spares)
  [CX-0226]. LEAP is "sold out in effect ... through the rest of this decade" [CX-0221].
- Rivals. Against the GTF, utilisation "8.4 points better" (2019) [CX-0134]. Ali: rivals that "need
  to redesign a large portion of the engine" should worry customers [CX-0164]; a hot-section moat
  makes it "nearly impossible for anybody to come from behind" [CX-0169]. Culp will not "speak to
  competition" [CX-0580] and welcomes GTF operators [CX-0581].

6. WHAT THIS MEANS IN THE GAME
------------------------------

Today CFM/GE is not a player. cfm_ducted is the narrowbody default for fps and NGSA; cfm_open_fan is
RISE (+1.5pp margin, +1 year to EIS, 0.85 capture); ge_genx_next is GE alone and the 787 Re-engine
default. Airframes fall back to CFM or GE when a rival maker has not launched. The referee can test
the market cell's CFM/GE reactions against these rules:
1. Serve both airframers. Culp aligns RISE with both airframers' road maps [CX-0185]; GE wants "to
   be on all the critical platforms" [CX-0171] and works the open fan with Airbus and Boeing
   [CX-0193]. Refusing fps or NGSA, or locking one airframer out, is implausible (inference).
2. The airframer sets the launch date. "It's really not for us to say when" RISE enters service
   [CX-0205]. CFM does not force cfm_open_fan.
3. Ramp promises are joint, and in 2023-24 they ran late. Both parents commit [CX-0160]. 2023
   delivered +38% (January 2024) or +25% (March 2024) against +50% [CX-0590] [CX-0176]; 2024 ended
   down 10% against +20-25% [CX-0954] [CX-0196]. The 2025 guide of +15-20% was raised in October
   2025 to above 20% [CX-0654]; the full-year outcome is outside the evidence. For a promise made in
   the 2023-24 style, expect a shortfall of a quarter to a half of the promised growth, and allow
   for a 2024-type reversal when suppliers fail; a low 2025-style guide needs no haircut
   (inference).
4. Price for return, not to match rivals. "A fair risk-adjusted return ... regardless of what our
   competitors may do" [CX-0188]; launch-era pricing, "those days are behind us" [CX-0930]. Yet GE
   took early-life risk to win share [CX-0207]. Narrowbody price moves need Safran [CX-0184].
5. In a crisis, absorb and share; don't blame. Payment terms rather than halted deliveries
   [CX-0142]; GE put its MAX exposure in the same range as Safran's [CX-0136] (inference: an even
   split); "avoid the finger pointing" [CX-0158]; a Safran disruption named but guidance held
   [CX-0220].
6. Widebody tenders are GE alone, with no Safran gate. GE claims a 70% 787 life-of-programme win
   rate [CX-0615], not won "because of price" [CX-0599].

As a future supplier player (proposed levers, not in the engine).

* Lever (proposed): Launch cfm_open_fan (RISE)
    Safran gate: Yes: RISE is joint [CX-0147] [CX-0166]
    Default from the evidence: Launched when an airframer commits [CX-0205], for at least 20% better
      fuel burn [CX-0217]

* Lever (proposed): Launch advanced cfm_ducted
    Safran gate: Yes (inference)
    Default from the evidence: GE says it gives less than half the gain [CX-0183]: a fallback

* Lever (proposed): Widebody offer (GEnx-next, GE9X derivative)
    Safran gate: No
    Default from the evidence: GE alone

* Lever (proposed): Aggressive terms
    Safran gate: Yes on narrowbody [CX-0184]
    Default from the evidence: Only to win share at launch, re-priced later [CX-0207] [CX-0215]

* Lever (proposed): One-time LEAP durability upgrade
    Safran gate: Yes (inference): joint GE-Safran teams exist [CX-0186]; certification is with the
      airframer [CX-0210]
    Default from the evidence: Under way: -1A done [CX-0225], -1B 1H 2026

* Lever (proposed): Cancel RISE
    Safran gate: Yes
    Default from the evidence: Unlikely: R&D held at 6-8% with no "program dividend" [CX-0174];
      future bets protected in 2020 [CX-0143]

A GE-side player would book half of CFM's narrowbody value and capex, and all of the widebody
(inference from the 50/50 structure [CX-0166]; no CFM financial model exists yet). It would ask
whatif for cfm_open_fan against cfm_ducted on fps and NGSA, and options --side boeing / --side
airbus for each airframer's incentive to pick CFM (the engine has no --side cfm today).

7. GAPS
-------

- Safran's side. Its leaders speak once, at GE's 2022 investor day [CX-0149] [CX-0152] [CX-0153].
  Its pricing, capacity, workshare, view of GE and internal disagreements cannot be known. Its
  labour disruption [CX-0220], sole-source suppliers [CX-0126] (analyst question) and MAX exposure
  [CX-0136] come second-hand.
- Governance mechanics. Board, voting, deadlock rules and the revenue-sharing formula are not
  disclosed. No source names GE's representatives on CFM's governance; EIHC lists no officers
  [CX-0231].
- CFM's officers. Names and titles only [CX-0229] [CX-0230].
- Financials. No CFM-level P&L, cash flow or concession data; all LEAP economics are GE's half. Culp
  said LEAP aftermarket margins stay "below the 56" [CX-0175]; "about half" is an analyst's reading.
- RISE. Investment and partner split unknown; Safran's share appears only as joint wind-tunnel
  testing [CX-0191].
- Customers. Nothing on C919 volumes or China risk; the A220-500 and NMA talks were kept private
  [CX-0171] [CX-0139].
- Attribution. Some 2023 answers credited to Culp read like Stokes's [CX-0170]. 2018 has one item
  [CX-0131].
