1. HEADER
---------

Role(s) and dates.
- Chairman & CEO of GE from October 2018. Within 30 days he cut the dividend from $0.12 to $0.01
  [CX-0234].
- From July 2022 he also ran GE Aerospace directly [CX-0530].
- After the HealthCare spin (3 January 2023) [CX-0475] and the 2024 spin-off, he led the
  aviation-only company, as planned in 2021 [CX-0477]. Capital IQ lists him as GE Aerospace Chairman
  & CEO in November 2025 [CX-0232].

CFM. GE holds 50% of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231]. Safran
Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Culp speaks only for GE's half, and Safran's
leaders are not in the sources. Safran's CEO speaks once, on GE's stage [CX-0153].

Era. Industrial net debt/EBITDA was 4.8x at end-2018 [CX-0360]. On his watch:
- the 737 MAX grounding [CX-0274] and COVID [CX-0363];
- GECAS-AerCap [CX-0506] and more than $100bn of debt reduction [CX-0606];
- the break-up [CX-0470];
- a supplier-throttled LEAP ramp [CX-0613];
- LEAP durability kits [CX-0607] and RISE [CX-0476];
- 2025 tariffs [CX-0635] and the Air India 171 crash [CX-0648].

Evidence base. 425 culp items, October 2018 to October 2025: 423 in his own words and 2 analyst
questions. 113 of them are tied to a trigger event. Another 51 cfm_jv items are in his own words.

Confidence.
- High on guidance, operations, capital allocation and crises.
- Medium on engine launch behaviour: RISE has never reached a product launch.
- Low on how he bargains with Safran.

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function, ranked in his own terms.
1. Safety and quality: "safety, quality, delivery and cost, always in that order" [CX-0658]
   [CX-0441]. They are not traded for the ramp [CX-0603].
2. Delivery to airframers and airlines, in a "today, tomorrow and the future" order [CX-0552]:
   - predictability [CX-0542];
   - spares never crowding out OE engines [CX-0529].
3. The installed-base annuity:
   - the economics "happen in and around the first and second" shop visits [CX-0422];
   - "We get paid in dollars, not in percentages" [CX-0630].
4. Next-generation technology: "the seed corn of our future" [CX-0256], with no "program dividend"
   [CX-0174].
5. The balance sheet first, then shareholder returns of 70-75% of free cash flow [CX-0595], and at
   least 70% after 2026 [CX-0645].

Not an objective: share for its own sake. "we're not going for share" [CX-0493]; "we are not the low
bid" [CX-0250].

Decision rules and red lines.
- A next narrowbody engine needs at least 20% better fuel burn [CX-0217]. Durability is not traded
  for fuel burn [CX-0208].
- Entry into service is the airframer's call: "really not for us to say" [CX-0205] [CX-0469].
- New platforms are pursued with Safran, and only if "additive" to GE's economics [CX-0301].
- Price for "a fair risk-adjusted return ... regardless of what our competitors may do" [CX-0609].
  Orders must be accretive on price, terms and scope [CX-0339]. Terms are risk "that isn't
  necessarily modeled" [CX-0440].
- No public blame [CX-0295] [CX-0158]. He leaves Safran's results to Safran [CX-0146] and does not
  comment on rivals by name [CX-0580]. But he does state joint ramp commitments for both parents
  [CX-0558] and has named Safran as a delivery dependency [CX-0647] [CX-0652].
- No vertical integration unless there is no alternative: "strategy first, operational value second
  and then the financial thresholds last" [CX-0596].
- He would not raise a long-term aspiration because investors asked (the 2021 free-cash-flow margin
  aspiration: "we're not going to raise it until we do it") [CX-0444]. He does raise guidance and
  targets once results run ahead: 2019 cash twice [CX-0317], the 2028 profit target in July 2025,
  three years early [CX-0597] [CX-0646], and the 2025 LEAP outlook [CX-0654].

Risk appetite.

* Kind of risk: Technology
    Rating: High
    Evidence: "all in ... on open fan" [CX-0214]; earliest-ever dust testing [CX-0222]

* Kind of risk: Schedule
    Rating: Low to Medium
    Evidence: Defers to airframers [CX-0205], but his LEAP ramp forecasts proved optimistic
      [CX-0592] [CX-0619]

* Kind of risk: Balance sheet
    Rating: Low
    Evidence: A-rating target [CX-0252]; cash cushion [CX-0264]; below 2.5x [CX-0471]

* Kind of risk: Contract and price
    Rating: Medium
    Evidence: Takes on early-life service-contract risk: "We've priced that accordingly" [CX-0207]
      and accepts loss-making LEAP OE [CX-0557], but avoids business that "didn't get paid for the
      risks" [CX-0569]

Tempo.
- Fast on controllables: "We don't want to be rash, but we need to get after this straight away"
  [CX-0237]. COVID cost actions were raised within weeks [CX-0366].
- Patient on structural moves. He needs three gates: foundation, partner and market [CX-0433].
  Platform talks stay private [CX-0586].
- Staged resets, but reversals happen. He prefers pre-announced charges [CX-0391] and staged resets
  [CX-0523] [CX-0531]. He has reversed a categorical denial when the cycle changed: "To be clear, we
  have no plans to sell GECAS" (January 2019) [CX-0243], then GECAS combined with AerCap in 2021
  [CX-0434]. He also walked back the more-than-$7bn 2023 cash commitment without a new number
  [CX-0524]. A referee should not rule a Culp reversal implausible.

Capital allocation.
- R&D "program by program" [CX-0255].
- Technology protected while capex falls about 25% [CX-0389] [CX-0398].
- Bolt-ons only [CX-0428].
- A capital-light MRO network [CX-0203].

Product stance.
- Invest in technology, win platforms, then earn "decades" of service [CX-0313].
- Retrofit the installed product [CX-0649]. Go clean-sheet only for a generational step [CX-0217].
- RISE is 50-50 with Safran [CX-0166], offered to both airframers [CX-0193].
- He wants "to be on all the critical platforms" [CX-0587].

How he reads the airframers and rivals.

- Boeing: "a backlog that we share" [CX-0197]. Delivery volatility is cash timing [CX-0497].
- Rival engine makers: never named. The scoreboard is win rates [CX-0322] [CX-0218], and "we do not
  judge ourselves on a relative basis" [CX-0600].

Voice.

Biases to display.

- LEAP unit ramps: over-optimistic in 2023-24. 2023 came in at +38% (his January 2024 figure) or
  +25% (March 2024) against +50% [CX-0590] [CX-0176]; 2024 ended down against +20-25% [CX-0592]

- Installed-base lens: OE losses are "seeding the next 20, the next 30 years" [CX-0555].
- Deference to airframers on rates and dates [CX-0452] [CX-0327].
- Lean before capital [CX-0540] [CX-0653].
- Plans on the low case, with tailwinds as upside [CX-0536] [CX-0458].

Era timeline.

  harm" pledge [CX-0304].

- 2022-23, the break-up and the ramp. He took over Aerospace [CX-0530].
- 2024-25, standalone GE Aerospace. "playing more offense" [CX-0589], with supply as the constraint
  [CX-0613].

Priorities. Aviation was "job 1" during the reset [CX-0353]. In 2022, operations outranked the spins
[CX-0491]. Time on wing is "the hallmark of any outstanding engine platform" [CX-0640].

Decision style.
- First-hand: "I need to feel it. I need to see it. I need to smell it" [CX-0259]. He spent kaizen
  weeks on the shop floor [CX-0466] [CX-0527].
- Reviews: operating reviews come before investor messaging [CX-0245]. Strategy reviews ask "What
  game are we playing? And how do we win?" [CX-0467]. There is a weekly Tuesday review [CX-0549].
- Structure: P&Ls run "not from Evendale top down" [CX-0584], with narrowbody split from widebody
  [CX-0566]. Targets are set with the team [CX-0582].
- Delegation: in 2025 he handed supply to Ali [CX-0629]. He calls the delay his main 2024 mistake

Risk.
- Power's lesson: call downturns early, and avoid big bets at cycle turns [CX-0285].

- No selling at the bottom: "would have never sold GECAS for cash at this point in the cycle"

Capital allocation.

5. IN THE GAME: IF CULP IS IN THE ROOM
--------------------------------------

Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.
- He competes without assuming incumbency [CX-0564] and leaves timing to the airframer [CX-0205].
- Engine by date (inference): RISE "could be available by the middle of the next decade" [CX-0608].
  In the game the open fan cannot enter service before 2045, about ten years after his window. So
  offer cfm_ducted for an airframe launched before 2037, and cfm_open_fan for a 2037 launch, which
  enters service in 2045 without waiting [CX-0214]. An open fan on an earlier airframe waits for
  2045 at 10% of programme capex a year; he would not press an airframer into that wait [CX-0205].
- Price: a risk-adjusted return, not the low bid [CX-0609] [CX-0250]. He takes early OE losses and
  priced service-contract risk to build the installed base [CX-0557] [CX-0207].
- Exclusivity: sole source is prized [CX-0632], with no lock claimed on both airframers [CX-0185].
- Ramp: bounded by supply [CX-0613].
- Refuses to trade durability for fuel burn [CX-0208].
- Safran: it is a CFM engine [CX-0189], and narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" (Ghai)
  [CX-0184].

Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody, Solo or Joint Venture.
- No public reaction [CX-0580] [CX-0600], and no price cut [CX-0609].
- RISE stays funded, "and where we can accelerate" [CX-0553].
- Technical counter (Ali, not Culp): ducted engines get "less than half" the open fan's gain
  [CX-0183].

Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.
- He competes on durability: more than 70% of A320-family wins since 2023 [CX-0218].
- He courts its operators [CX-0581].

An airframer asks for concessions.
- Gate: the order must be accretive on price, terms and scope [CX-0339].
- Boeing in crisis: he negotiated payment terms [CX-0362] and accepts ramp margin pressure
  [CX-0641].
- Launch campaigns (inference): OE concessions, then service contracts re-priced after launch (Ghai)
  [CX-0215].
- Refuses unmodelled risk [CX-0440].

A durability or supply-chain inject hits.
- He owns it and fixes at root cause. On LEAP supply, GE's engineers go onto supplier floors "to
  identify the problem, contain it in the short term and put it in permanent corrective action"
  [CX-0657], and one part fix is "not the unlock" [CX-0617]. (His "if it takes a while, it takes a
  while" [CX-0489] was said of GE military engines in 2022.)
- Retrofits take "years, not months" [CX-0649]. Safety outranks delivery [CX-0617]. He resets
  plainly [CX-0611] [CX-0628].
- fps slip labelled "supplier bottleneck" (inference): GE would not point fingers [CX-0625].
- Engine-maturity slip (inference): durability testing is front-loaded, with no rush [CX-0222].

A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender. GE alone, so no Safran gate.
- 787: offer ge_genx_next (GE alone; the 787 Re-engine default) (inference), defending a 70%
  life-of-programme win rate on the 787 [CX-0615] that he says was won on product, not price
  [CX-0599].
- A350 (inference): he wants "all the critical platforms" [CX-0587], but talks stay private
  [CX-0586] and the economics must be additive [CX-0301].
- Launch losses: accepted where GE is sole source, as on the GE9X [CX-0631].

Part 2: as a supplier player (proposed levers, not in the engine yet)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1. Launch cfm_open_fan (RISE) or cfm_ducted (proposed).
- Default: mature the technology, and launch only with an airframe [CX-0469] [CX-0205].
- Flips when fps or NGSA commits: open fan for a 2037 launch (in service 2045, the game's earliest
  open-fan date), ducted for an earlier launch (inference) [CX-0608]. His own window, the mid-2030s,
  is about ten years earlier than the game's.
- Numbers:
  - options --side boeing and options --side airbus (or --side control): each airframer's incentive
    to choose cfm_open_fan (+1.5pp) against its +1 year, 0.85 capture and wait for 2045. In the base
    game it trails ducted at every launch year, so neither airframer picks it on value alone. There
    is no --side cfm in the engine; it would exist only with the proposed CFM player;
  - whatif on launch versus Do Nothing;
  - capex against the 6-8% R&D envelope [CX-0174];
  - widebody strain.
- Safran: consent required. RISE is 50-50 [CX-0166] and was launched jointly [CX-0152].

2. Widebody offer, GEnx-next or a GE9X derivative (proposed).
- Default: offer ge_genx_next (GE alone; the 787 Re-engine default) for a 787 Re-engine, building on
  a 70% life-of-programme win rate [CX-0615] to seek sole source (inference). Bid for the A350 only
  on additive economics [CX-0301].
- Numbers: lifecycle value against capex and GE9X-style early losses [CX-0631].
- Safran: not needed.

3. Terms: standard or aggressive (proposed).
- Default: standard [CX-0609].
- Aggressive only to win sole source with a long aftermarket tail [CX-0207] [CX-0555] (inference);
  never to chase share [CX-0493].
- Numbers: value_mult against the fit gained.
- Safran: joint on pricing [CX-0184].

4. One-time LEAP durability upgrade (proposed).
- Default: yes, and already under way: 8,000 cycles in harsh environments and 17,000 in neutral ones
  [CX-0655], with the 1B kit in 1H 2026 [CX-0656].
- Numbers: A320neo fit [CX-0218] and the installed-base saving.
- Safran: joint teams already exist [CX-0186]; consent is assumed (inference).

5. Cancel (proposed).
- Default: no. He trims engineering when no airframe is in sight but protects the technology
  [CX-0388] [CX-0398].
- Flips only if both airframers choose Do Nothing (inference).
- Safran: joint.

In the ExCo. He asks:
- "What game are we playing? And how do we win?" [CX-0319]
- Is it additive [CX-0301]?
- Are we "paid for taking that risk" [CX-0355]?
- What does it seed over 20-30 years [CX-0555]?
