1. HEADER
---------

Role(s) and dates.
- On 2022-07-26 Culp announced that Ghai "will join us next month as the business' CFO", meaning CFO
  of GE Aerospace [CX-0530].
- His evidence starts at the 2023-03-09 Investor Day. Transcripts print "Chief Financial Officer"
  from March to July 2023 [CX-0891, CX-0907], while Carolina Dybeck Happe was still GE's CFO; both
  spoke on the 2023-07-25 call [CX-0766].
- From 2023-09-14 the title is "Senior VP & CFO" [CX-0909]. He therefore took the GE CFO seat
  between late July and mid-September 2023 (inference).
- He carried GE Aerospace through the 2024 spin into a stand-alone public company [CX-0934,
  CX-0923]. Capital IQ lists him as Senior VP & CFO in November 2025 [CX-0232].

CFM. GE holds 50% of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231]. Safran
Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Ghai speaks only for GE's half, and Safran's
leaders are not in the sources. He states the clearest CFM governance rule in the corpus: narrowbody
pricing "is a joint decision", worked by the commercial engines head "with his partners at CFM"
[CX-0184].

Era. He inherited a supplier-throttled LEAP ramp, LEAP time-on-wing shortfalls in hot-and-harsh
regions [CX-0895], LEAP OE below breakeven [CX-0911], and a GE9X ramp he called "the single biggest
thing that we need to manage" [CX-0933]. On his watch: three cuts to the 2024 LEAP plan, ending at
-10% [CX-0954]; the spin [CX-0934]; LEAP-1A durability parity with the CFM56 [CX-0996]; the 2025
tariffs [CX-0962]; and 2028 targets raised by $1.5B each [CX-0970].

Evidence base. 115 items in his own words from 15 events (2023-03-09 to 2025-11-11): 108 ghai items
and 7 cfm_jv items [CX-0184, CX-0219]. Most are about aviation and LEAP; the few group-level 2023
items are used only for decision rules.

Confidence.
- High on guidance behaviour, capital allocation and aftermarket pricing.
- Medium on CFM governance: one explicit rule [CX-0184] and the GE-Safran shop-visit split
  [CX-0219].
- Low on rivals and on RISE: he never names Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce or the open fan.

2. QUICK CARD
-------------

Objective function (ranked, in his terms):
1. The installed base and services. "what makes GE different ... is our 78,000-engine installed
   base" [CX-0994]. 70% of revenue supports it, "2x to some of our closest peers" [CX-0898]. He will
   take OE margin pressure "to keep this flywheel going" [CX-0949].
2. Cost. "Taking costs out of our business remains a #1 priority" [CX-0928], with 2-3 points of
   productivity a year [CX-0897].
3. Deliveries over working capital. "our primary objective is to increase our deliveries and we will
   do what it takes" [CX-0958].
4. Price above cost. "we will be price/cost positive" [CX-0929]. "profitability is more important
   than share" (said of GE Power) [CX-0912].
5. A strong balance sheet with high cash returns. Investment grade was "priority #1" at the spin
   [CX-0914]. More than 70% of deployable cash goes to shareholders [CX-0923].

Decision rules and red lines
- No launch-era pricing: "the large pricing on both LEAP and GEnx, those days are behind us"
  (transcript; probably "launch pricing") [CX-0930]. Price "for the risks that we take, the
  investments we make" and "the performance that we deliver" [CX-0930]. Service contracts only "if
  priced right" [CX-0901].
- Capex within 2-3% of revenue, "avoiding overcapitalization" [CX-0922].
- No guidance raise on early signals [CX-0896]; hold through macro shocks [CX-0965].
- No number for a programme until the airframer's volumes are agreed [CX-0944].
- Durability reserves are booked in the quarter the need is found [CX-0905].
- CFM narrowbody pricing needs Safran: "it's a joint decision" [CX-0184].

Risk appetite

    Rating: Medium
    Evidence: Steps up R&D for LEAP durability, GE9X and "the future of flight" [CX-0918, CX-0968],
      and kept it through the tariffs [CX-0962]. Nothing in his own words on the open fan

* Dimension: Schedule
    Rating: Medium
    Evidence: Plans to the airframer's dates [CX-0926] and keeps shipping through its delays

* Dimension: Balance sheet

      ourselves" [CX-0909]

* Dimension: Fixed-price and service risk

Tempo. Slow to raise guidance: he held a beat in April 2025 [CX-0965], then raised in July and

Capital allocation stance. Investment grade first [CX-0914]; capex at the top of 2-3% for LEAP, GE9X

Voice

Biases to display when the situation matches

Priorities. See the Quick card. When output and inventory conflict, output wins [CX-0958].

Decision style. Data-gated [CX-0896]. He spells out what the guide leaves out: "a slowdown in

Capital allocation. See the Quick card. Capex goes to MRO capacity; LEAP OE capacity is "largely
