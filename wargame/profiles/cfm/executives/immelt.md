# Jeff Immelt: GE Chairman & CEO, to 2017

## Header

- **Role in the sources.** Chairman & CEO at every event, from the 2015-12-16 outlook to the 2017-07-21 call. In May 2017 he spoke of the stock's "mismatch" having happened "3 times in 16 years" [CX-1081], which suggests about 16 years in office **(inference)**. He said the board had targeted a handover for summer 2017 since about 2013 [CX-1101]. Flannery was named his successor on 2017-06-12 [CX-0789]. The transcripts print his title as "Former Chairman of the Board".
- **CFM.** GE holds half of CFM International; Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227], and Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Immelt never mentions Safran or CFM governance. CFM appears through LEAP, the joint engine.
- **What he set up.** He chose GEnx, LEAP and GE9X at the 2009 trough [CX-1018].
- **On his watch:** LEAP entered service in 2016 with 77 delivered against about 100 planned, "in coordination with the airframers" [CX-0073]; orders and commitments reached 12,200 by April 2017 [CX-1071]; GE product margins fell from "5-ish" [CX-1003] to about 1% [CX-1082]; capex ran at 1.4-1.5x reinvestment [CX-1149]; a LEAP-1B turbine disc retrofit hit 2017 shipments [CX-0121]. Within months of his exit, his successor cut guidance [CX-0794] and halved the dividend [CX-0818].
- **Evidence base.** 110 items from 16 events (2015-12-16 to 2017-07-21), all in his own words. About 58 concern aviation, CFM or LEAP.
- **Confidence.** Medium. The record is rich on aviation economics and guidance style but covers only his last 20 months. There is no LEAP durability or GE9X detail.

## Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in his terms):
1. The installed base and services. "Having control of your installed base, that's the game," with returns "in excess of 30%" [CX-1094]. "The most valuable asset to a GE investor is always the installed base" [CX-1093].
2. Launch execution and product cost. In aviation it is "the business model as it pertains to services ... And then execution on the LEAP" [CX-1067]. Restoring equipment margins from 1% to 5% is "job one" [CX-1082].
3. The financial "bridge": $2 EPS in 2018 [CX-1048], tied to management pay [CX-1045].
4. The dividend. The day it was cut "was the worst day of my tenure as CEO" [CX-1103].
5. Share, but only at good margins: "there's no reason to do that at lousy margins" [CX-1099].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- Accept dilutive OE to seed the service annuity [CX-1106], but do not buy share at "lousy margins" [CX-1099].
- Invest through the cycle: "We invested in technology. We invested in customers. We played through" [CX-1019, CX-1046].
- On the Advanced Turboprop he had "our first launch customer" and "half the funding from European governments" [CX-1016]. **(inference:** he would seek both for a new engine in an adjacent segment.**)**
- Where a supplier takes "none" of the risk but earns 15% against GE's 5%, it "is going to get corrected" [CX-1097]. GE now owns the designs once handed to partners [CX-1061].
- Underwrite deals on cost synergies only [CX-1054], on "the world we see today" [CX-1028], and do one big integration at a time [CX-1055].
- New capability gets one central attempt: "Is this a hobby or is this real?" [CX-1053].

**Tempo.**
- He moves first in downturns: "position for growth when others pull back" [CX-1046].
- On bad news he resets gradually and recasts targets rather than withdrawing them. Growth was "trending close to the bottom end" in July 2016 and cut three months later [CX-1036]. The $2 target became "the high end of the range" [CX-1085]. The cash-conversion goal went from 95% to 80-90% without being called a cut [CX-1084].
- He does state misses plainly: a weak Q1 2017 cash start [CX-1069], the missed 2016 cash target [CX-1086], product margins down to about 1% [CX-1082], and a GEnx launch "we did not execute ... all that well" [CX-1021].
- There is no evidence of him reacting to a rival's launch.

**Capital allocation stance.**
- The "bedrock" comes first: safety, the dividend, new products and the pension take about half the cash [CX-1076].
- The rest goes "to the best returns" [CX-0999].
- In May 2017 he chose buybacks over M&A [CX-1077].
- Capex falls back "closer to 1:1" after "a historic set of launches, mainly in Aviation" [CX-1074]. LEAP and the H turbine were "cash pigs" [CX-1075].

**Product stance.**
- Hard launches build moats: "We like things like the LEAP engine because it creates a competitive advantage" [CX-1059].
- He chose to launch GEnx, LEAP and GE9X at the 2009 trough [CX-1018]. That the megaproject era was waning after them is the reader's note on his answer, not his quoted words.
- He favours Joint Ventures and buying suppliers. "Aviation has created a series of joint ventures" and Avio "gave us depth in gears" [CX-1017].
- He expects additive manufacturing to "sweep in" more of the engine [CX-1058].

**How he reads airframers and rival engine makers.**
- Airframers are tables to sweep: "100% at Boeing; 100%, COMAC; 55% at Airbus" [CX-1098].
- He scores the contest as a multiple of the "nearest competitor" (10x at Paris 2017) [CX-1107], and claims "an unprecedented advantage" [CX-1108].
- He never names Pratt & Whitney or Rolls-Royce.

## Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.** December 2015: "bolt on" M&A [CX-1002], a better LEAP dashboard than GEnx's [CX-1008], product margins "5-ish" against "8-ish" historically [CX-1003], megaprojects waning (reader note) [CX-1018]. 2016: LEAP enters service, Baker Hughes [CX-1002], growth guidance cut [CX-1036]. December 2016: the "$2 bridge" [CX-1048], inventory [CX-1056], a 95% cash goal [CX-1051]. May 2017: equipment margin at 1% [CX-1082], $2 recast [CX-1085], LEAP "swept the table" [CX-1096]. July 2017: handover, dividend fenced off [CX-1102].

**Priorities.** The installed base is "the ballast for the company" [CX-1026]; Power and Aviation make 60% of earnings [CX-1038]. A 1% fuel gain is worth "$2 billion to $3 billion" a year to customers, captured through service contracts [CX-1014]. 85% of engines and turbines sell abroad [CX-1057].

**Decision style.** He reviews engine-launch dashboards himself [CX-1008] and decides make-versus-buy on should-cost and returns [CX-1009]. "More big portfolio moves ... than at any time" [CX-1088]; "if we're not good at a business, we're going to sell it" [CX-1089]. He licensed his successor's "fresh look" [CX-1100].

**Risk.** He credits the 1999-2003 downturn investment with a 3.5x rise in aviation earnings [CX-1019]. He rules out a "big IT acquisition" [CX-1020] and keeps a cost-out pipeline of "almost twice what we need" [CX-1062].

**Capital allocation.** Restructuring is "an 18-month payback type of thing" [CX-1000]; Alstom should return "mid-teens" [CX-1047]. Big-programme spend levels off [CX-1063], with net plant and equipment over $1B below expectations [CX-1064]. Avio is the template for supply-chain buys [CX-1044].

**Product and strategy.** The advanced turboprop is "designed fully with additive in mind" [CX-1041], with 500 additive parts targeted by 2020 [CX-1049]. GE makes rather than buys where it has "very specific expertise" [CX-1042].

**Operations.** LEAP's learning curve "gets steeper and down faster" than GEnx's [CX-1011]. A product cost council tracks launches [CX-1031]; he accepted a LEAP inventory build [CX-1037] and planned negative LEAP mix [CX-1070]. $125M expanded the Asheville LEAP plant [CX-1073]. Once share was won, the focus moved to cost [CX-1090, CX-1091]. In-service GEnx upgrades hurt service-contract economics [CX-1092]. He backs engine-by-engine analytics [CX-1010, CX-1033] and combing the backlog for margin [CX-1012].

**Rivals.** Aviation pricing was positive, with $25B+ of Farnborough 2016 commitments [CX-1039]. "We're gaining share with LEAP" [CX-1068]. Service contracts are "the absolute wall" against threats to the installed base [CX-1095].

**Communication.** Self-deprecation ("maybe all these years, I'll be right") [CX-1001]; "GE is not perfect" [CX-1032]; "it's up to us to execute" [CX-1078]. He answered an accounting question with strategy [CX-1040] and thanked retiree critics without changing course [CX-1072].

**Crisis response.** When the Appliances sale was blocked: "we kind of march on" [CX-1007]. He holds outlooks and offsets weak units with strong ones [CX-1029].

**Team.**
- Pay steers delivery: internal plans "roll up to more than we talk about externally" [CX-1027], and gross margins are in every business's incentive metrics [CX-1022].
- He pushes authority down [CX-1035] and put additive under Joyce, the "biggest practitioner" [CX-1043].

## In the game: if Immelt is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with the partner (2024) [CX-0184]. In his era the parents studied supply-chain "pinchpoints" together [CX-0127], and each kept its own technology lines [CX-0129]. Immelt left no evidence of how he handled Safran. Every CFM stance below assumes partner agreement **(inference)**.

### 1. As the market cell's CFM/GE today

These situations post-date him. Each line applies a cited rule of his and is **(inference)**.
- **fps or NGSA launch and engine tender.**
  - Exclusivity: go for sole source on both airframes [CX-1098].
  - Price: accept dilutive OE for the annuity [CX-1106], but not "lousy margins" [CX-1099]. Push service-contract attachment (about two-thirds on LEAP in his era) [CX-0775].
  - Ramp: build capacity ahead, as at Asheville [CX-1073]; his era ran the LEAP supply chain 6-8 months ahead of demand [CX-0125].
- **Rolls-Royce narrowbody UltraFan, or a next-generation Pratt & Whitney GTF.**
  - Claim the better product [CX-1099] and hold price.
  - Invest counter-cyclically [CX-1046] and defend the installed base with service contracts [CX-1095].
- **Concession request.** Trade on value through service contracts: fuel and data gains shared with airlines [CX-1014]. Discount OE only where the installed base follows [CX-1106].
- **Durability or supply-chain inject.**
  - Fix the supplier, buy it or bring the work in-house [CX-1097, CX-1044].
  - Inventory: he cut it in steady state ("All roads lead to inventory") [CX-1056], but in a launch ramp he accepted LEAP inventory builds [CX-1037]. Default for this inject: protect the ramp and carry the inventory **(inference)**.
  - Expect upgrade cost to land in service contracts [CX-1092]. Expect him to state the hit, then frame it as "one-timers" [CX-1069].
- **787 or A350 Re-engine widebody tender** (GE alone).
  - Offer `ge_genx_next` as a derivative, applying the GEnx lessons [CX-1021] and learning curve [CX-1031]. A new widebody megaproject is out **(inference**, from the reader's note on [CX-1018]**)**.

- He frames the question "micro, not macro", business by business [CX-1024].
- He asks for returns (mid-teens [CX-1047]; 30%+ on service contracts [CX-1094]), the payback, and a cost-out pipeline at twice the need [CX-1062].
