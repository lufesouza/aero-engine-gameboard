## 2. Quick card

**Objective function, ranked in his own terms.**
1. **Safety and quality:** "committed to safety as that #1 priority, along with quality" [CX-1297].
2. **LEAP durability:** "durability has to be our absolute #1 focus". He is not "satisfied" even where LEAP-1A beats the CFM56 at the same age [CX-1285].

## 5. In the game: if Stokes is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Stokes is the one GE leader shown working CFM pricing with the partner: "Russell will work that with his partners at CFM, especially on the narrowbody side, it's a joint decision" (Ghai, March 2024) [CX-0184]. He presents the franchise as "a partnership with us and Safran" [CX-1289]. No evidence shows the talks themselves or a disagreement. Every CFM stance below assumes Safran's consent, reached through him **(inference)**. `ge_genx_next` is GE alone, with no gate.

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- **Campaigns:** "we'll let the market dictate through different campaigns" [CX-1288]. He competes on time on wing, not price [CX-1299].
- **Price:**
  - no launch-style service pricing [CX-1287];
  - he accepts OE losses for a sole-source installed base, because services are worth 3.5x [CX-1298] **(inference)**.
- **Exclusivity:** prized. The MAX sole source anchors the flywheel [CX-1300].
- **Ramp and capacity:**
  - rate readiness with critical suppliers before any commitment [CX-1296];
  - output aligned with the airframer on a rolling horizon [CX-1278];
  - the supply chain is "a challenge" [CX-1294].
- **Safran:** pricing is joint [CX-0184], and he presents the franchise as joint [CX-1289].
- **Refuses:**
  - launch pricing on service contracts [CX-1287];
  - new sites before lean [CX-1280].

**Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.**
