# John Flannery: GE Chairman & CEO, 2017-2018

## Header

- **Roles in the sources.** He was President & CEO of GE Healthcare at the 2016-11-03 and 2017-06-01 events. GE named him CEO-elect on 2017-06-12 [CX-0789], and his first earnings call as CEO was on 2017-10-20 [CX-0792]. The last item is dated 2018-07-20. His successor spoke of being "30 days in" on 2018-10-30 [CX-0235], which puts the handover at the start of October 2018 **(inference)**. Every transcript page prints his title as "Former Chairman & CEO".
- **CFM.** GE holds half of CFM International; Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227], and Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Flannery never mentions Safran or CFM governance; he speaks for GE's half only through GE Aviation and LEAP.
- **What he inherited.** Expected 2017 operating cash flow had fallen from $12-14B to about $7B [CX-0806]. LEAP was ramping from 77 engines in 2016 [CX-0073] to 459 in 2017 [CX-0842] and 1,100-1,200 planned for 2018 [CX-1172]. Capex ran at about 140% of replacement because of the LEAP and H-turbine launches [CX-0817].
- **On his watch:** the dividend was halved [CX-0818]; $20B of disposals were announced [CX-0804, CX-0880]; a $6.2B insurance charge was taken [CX-0834]; a break-up plan kept Aviation in the core [CX-0878]. LEAP deliveries ran about 6 weeks late in March 2018 [CX-1118] and 4 weeks late in July [CX-1180]. A CFM56-7B fan blade failed in April 2018; the sources hold no statement from him on it [CX-0854].
- **Evidence base.** 112 items from 13 events (2016-11-03 to 2018-07-20): 111 in his own words, one shareholder question; 48 on aviation, LEAP, CFM or GECAS. Two events are from his Healthcare years.
- **Confidence.** Medium on capital, guidance and portfolio. Low to medium on CFM-specific conduct, because he left Aviation detail to David Joyce [CX-0815].

## Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in his terms):
1. Cash and the balance sheet. He named "an obsession with cash generation and capital allocation" [CX-0843] and set targets: net debt/EBITDA below 2.5x by 2020 [CX-0867], A1/P1 ratings and $15B+ of cash [CX-0838].
2. Execution and a high "say-do ratio" [CX-0821].
3. Aviation, "a premier asset" to keep investing in [CX-0868], "GE at its best" [CX-0811]. R&D and launches sit before the free-cash-flow test [CX-0859].
4. LEAP economics as the launch matures: engine cost, working capital and capex [CX-0812].
5. Portfolio focus: "focus means focus" [CX-0810].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- No dividend above free cash flow, and about a 50% payout [CX-0818, CX-0819].
- Capex returns to a 1.0 reinvestment rate as launches mature [CX-0802]. R&D continues [CX-0800].
- Stage spending (as Healthcare CEO, June 2017): the risk is "spending $300 million on a program and then find out whether it's going to work" [CX-0787]. Test, then "double down" or "pull back" [CX-0783].
- Small, adjacent and supply-chain deals rather than big integrations [CX-0809].
- Assume price pressure continues "indefinitely" (as Healthcare CEO, June 2017) [CX-0788], and that troughs persist [CX-0887].
- Do not build inventory into a forecast [CX-0822]. Do not win launch orders with aggressive delivery dates (his lesson from the H turbine) [CX-0830].
- Short paybacks: cost-out returning in "6 months to 2 years" (as Healthcare CEO, November 2016) [CX-0779].

The Healthcare-era rules above were said about GE Healthcare. Applying them to CFM engines, as § 5 does, is an inference from a Healthcare rule.
- Do not be rushed: "being deliberate ... is just my style" [CX-0861].

## Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.** November 2016, as Healthcare CEO: short-payback cost-out [CX-0779] and "heavy investors in our own shares" [CX-0780]. June 2017, CEO-elect: digital "a defining characteristic" [CX-0790]. October-November 2017, the reset: "unacceptable" results [CX-0792], dividend halved [CX-0818], $20B of disposals [CX-0804]. January 2018: even Aviation inside the structural review [CX-0835]. June 2018: Aviation, Power and Renewables the core [CX-0878], "the end of the beginning" [CX-0871].

**Priorities.** Cash first; Aviation is the offset to Power [CX-0885] and leads his message with orders [CX-0884]. He reads demand from traffic data (7.7% RPK growth) [CX-0844].

**Decision style.** "Equal parts investor, operator" [CX-0791]. He keeps a business if it grows faster inside the group than outside [CX-0782], and favours "critical assets that can't fail" [CX-0808], which fits jet engines **(inference)**. He triages first, then redesigns deliberately [CX-0847], and closes a review phase firmly [CX-0871].

**Risk.** Large unstaged programmes frighten him (said as Healthcare CEO) [CX-0787]. Problems raised early stay small; hidden ones "metastasize" [CX-0814]. He accepts a share band ("45%, 50%" in gas turbines) with discipline on "the risk and return" rather than chasing volume [CX-0849].

**Capital allocation.** R&D and launch spending sit outside the cash squeeze [CX-0859]. He expects capex to fall as LEAP matures [CX-0817], and he counts on "The LEAP engine maturing" to ease margin pressure [CX-0801]. He kept GECAS as "a premium asset" [CX-0870], but put smaller Aviation platforms into the disposal pool [CX-0846].

**Product and strategy.** Technology moats such as LEAP come from "decades of heavy investment" [CX-0813]; franchises are held through bad years ("90% of the people wanted us to get out of that in 2002") [CX-0825]. Time on wing is the airline's economic lever [CX-0828]. He backs additive parts for engine structures [CX-0845] and military growth [CX-0874].

**Operations.** Cost-out applied to Aviation too ($200M in 2017 to date) [CX-0797]. Services and cost-out offset LEAP drag: Aviation margins rose 100 bp in 2017 [CX-0842] and 340 bp in Q1 2018 on 186 LEAP shipments against 77 [CX-0848]. As Healthcare CEO he set a target to cut single-source supply from 70% to 50% [CX-0785].

**Rivals.** See the Quick card. The evidence on this dimension is thin: four items.

**Communication.** He owns misses bluntly: "this performance is simply not acceptable" [CX-0795]. He says the guidance range carries contingency, so the team is "not mailing all of them right back immediately to investors" [CX-0840]. He declared the strategy settled and "an execution story going forward" [CX-0879].

**Crisis response.** He names causes: "too optimistic for too long" in Power [CX-0822], "slow to recognize" [CX-0853], "we underappreciated the risk" in insurance [CX-0834]. He kept the insurance problem inside GE Capital, with GECAS as a backstop [CX-0836].

**Team.** "A small central government" [CX-0815]; the Aviation chief "can't get too comfortable" [CX-0816]. He put Global Research under Joyce [CX-0877], moved Aviation-trained operators into troubled units [CX-0850], promotes "spreadsheet, roll up your sleeves" leaders [CX-0831], and said senior leaders took no 2017 bonuses [CX-0858].

## In the game: if Flannery is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with the CFM partner (2024) [CX-0184]. Under his predecessor (February 2017), GE's Bill Fitzgerald described joint supply-chain reviews [CX-0127] and near-daily contact with his Safran counterpart [CX-0128]; nothing in Flannery's own era shows the partnership. No evidence shows how he handled the partner; every CFM stance below assumes Safran's agreement, sought through the Aviation chief **(inference)**.
