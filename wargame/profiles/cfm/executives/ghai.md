# Rahul Ghai: CFO seat, GE's half of CFM (2022-today)

## 1. Header

**Role(s) and dates.**
- On 2022-07-26 Culp announced that Ghai "will join us next month as the business' CFO", meaning CFO of GE Aerospace [CX-0530].
- His evidence starts at the 2023-03-09 Investor Day. Transcripts print "Chief Financial Officer" from March to July 2023 [CX-0891, CX-0907], while Carolina Dybeck Happe was still GE's CFO; both spoke on the 2023-07-25 call [CX-0766].
- From 2023-09-14 the title is "Senior VP & CFO" [CX-0909]. He therefore took the GE CFO seat between late July and mid-September 2023 **(inference)**.
- He carried GE Aerospace through the 2024 spin into a stand-alone public company [CX-0934, CX-0923]. Capital IQ lists him as Senior VP & CFO in November 2025 [CX-0232].

**CFM.** GE holds 50% of CFM International through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231]. Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Ghai speaks only for GE's half, and Safran's leaders are not in the sources. He states the clearest CFM governance rule in the corpus: narrowbody pricing "is a joint decision", worked by the commercial engines head "with his partners at CFM" [CX-0184].

**Era.** He inherited a supplier-throttled LEAP ramp, LEAP time-on-wing shortfalls in hot-and-harsh regions [CX-0895], LEAP OE below breakeven [CX-0911], and a GE9X ramp he called "the single biggest thing that we need to manage" [CX-0933]. On his watch: three cuts to the 2024 LEAP plan, ending at -10% [CX-0954]; the spin [CX-0934]; LEAP-1A durability parity with the CFM56 [CX-0996]; the 2025 tariffs [CX-0962]; and 2028 targets raised by $1.5B each [CX-0970].

**Evidence base.** 115 items in his own words from 15 events (2023-03-09 to 2025-11-11): 108 `ghai` items and 7 `cfm_jv` items [CX-0184, CX-0219]. Most are about aviation and LEAP; the few group-level 2023 items are used only for decision rules.

**Confidence.**
- **High** on guidance behaviour, capital allocation and aftermarket pricing.
- **Medium** on CFM governance: one explicit rule [CX-0184] and the GE-Safran shop-visit split [CX-0219].
- **Low** on rivals and on RISE: he never names Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce or the open fan.

## 2. Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in his terms):
1. **The installed base and services.** "what makes GE different ... is our 78,000-engine installed base" [CX-0994]. 70% of revenue supports it, "2x to some of our closest peers" [CX-0898]. He will take OE margin pressure "to keep this flywheel going" [CX-0949].
2. **Cost.** "Taking costs out of our business remains a #1 priority" [CX-0928], with 2-3 points of productivity a year [CX-0897].
3. **Deliveries over working capital.** "our primary objective is to increase our deliveries and we will do what it takes" [CX-0958].
4. **Price above cost.** "we will be price/cost positive" [CX-0929]. "profitability is more important than share" (said of GE Power) [CX-0912].
5. **A strong balance sheet with high cash returns.** Investment grade was "priority #1" at the spin [CX-0914]. More than 70% of deployable cash goes to shareholders [CX-0923].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- No launch-era pricing: "the large pricing on both LEAP and GEnx, those days are behind us" (transcript; probably "launch pricing") [CX-0930]. Price "for the risks that we take, the investments we make" and "the performance that we deliver" [CX-0930]. Service contracts only "if priced right" [CX-0901].
- Capex within 2-3% of revenue, "avoiding overcapitalization" [CX-0922].
- No guidance raise on early signals [CX-0896]; hold through macro shocks [CX-0965].
- No number for a programme until the airframer's volumes are agreed [CX-0944].
- Durability reserves are booked in the quarter the need is found [CX-0905].
- CFM narrowbody pricing needs Safran: "it's a joint decision" [CX-0184].

He returned more than 100% of 2024 free cash flow [CX-0953] and kept the $8B+ 2025 plan through the tariffs [CX-0960]. R&D is protected [CX-0962]; M&A is optional [CX-0909].

**Product stance.**

- The GEnx path (margins up 4x in 10 years) is "the exact same playbook on LEAP" [CX-0903]. LEAP would nearly match CFM56 profit by 2028 [CX-0932], later "the end of the decade" [CX-0971].

- An open MRO network that "creates competition" [CX-0902], with LEAP work shifting from service contracts to time-and-material [CX-0938].
- Nothing in his own words on clean-sheet versus derivative, or on the open fan.

**How he reads airframers and rival engine makers.**
- He builds Airbus's and Boeing's stated rate goals into his plans [CX-0981]: aircraft output "up 50%, 60% ... in the next 3 years or so" [CX-0982].
- He defers to Boeing on 777X dates ("I'll let him kind of talk to it") [CX-0986].
- He measures the GTF contest by share of Airbus deliveries (55-60%) and win ratio (about 70%) [CX-0906]. CFM powers "approximately 75% of industry's narrow-body flights" [CX-0980].

## 4. Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**
- **2023:** margins back to 20% [CX-0894]; investment grade at the spin [CX-0914]; legacy liabilities settled [CX-0908]; a spare-parts price increase pulled forward a quarter [CX-0917].
- **2024, the spin year:** delivery shortfalls and inventory [CX-0939]; cost "#1 priority" [CX-0928]; the capital-return framework [CX-0923].
- **2025:** tariffs [CX-0962]; LEAP aftermarket profitability through repairs, pricing and in-house share [CX-0977, CX-0967, CX-0219]; the GE9X ramp [CX-0995].

**Priorities.** See the Quick card. When output and inventory conflict, output wins [CX-0958]. "profitability is more important than share" was first stated about GE Power [CX-0912].

**Decision style.** Data-gated [CX-0896]. He spells out what the guide leaves out: "a slowdown in airframer delivery schedules, further tariff escalation or a global recession" [CX-0961]. He prefers "a daily disposition versus a quarterly target" through FLIGHT DECK [CX-0991].

**Risk.** About two-thirds of LEAP reserves were for "unanticipated, unexpected events", booked when found [CX-0905]. He accepts GE9X losses that "more than double" in 2026 [CX-0995] and "limited" 2026 margin expansion [CX-0985]. He self-funds next-generation R&D through "budget uncertainty" [CX-0952]; the wording suggests defence programmes **(inference)**.

**Capital allocation.** See the Quick card. Capex goes to MRO capacity; LEAP OE capacity is "largely completed" [CX-0891].

**Product and strategy (aftermarket economics).**
- List increases must be realised, not just announced [CX-0907]. Revenue-share partners dilute them to about mid-single digit at the services level [CX-0201], and shop-visit price rises "take a few years" to reach the P&L [CX-0967].
- Cost levers: double LEAP-specific repairs by 2028 [CX-0977]; retrofit kits at scheduled shop visits [CX-0997]. Under long-term service contracts, fewer shop visits mean more profit [CX-0951].
- In the CFM56-to-LEAP handover, price covers falling CFM56 shop visits [CX-0904], and mature platforms offset LEAP's 25% shop-visit growth [CX-0976].

**Operations.** "sold out through the end of the decade" [CX-0978]. About $3B of inventory is "trapped" for lack of parts [CX-0974]. **Rivals.** See the Quick card: five items, no named rival.

**Communication.** He corrects himself on the spot [CX-0900] and scores himself against earlier targets: "about 1/3 better than what we thought just 9 months ago" [CX-0957]. He lowers expectations after a strong year [CX-0983]. He owns forecasting errors: "we've been proven wrong on that one before" [CX-0972]; output "not at the level that we'd expected back in March" [CX-0947].

He echoes Culp on M&A [CX-0909] and values visible management: "there's nothing to hide" [CX-0998].

## 5. In the game: if Ghai is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with the CFM partner [CX-0184]. In-house LEAP shop visits are split "equally between our JV partner, Safran and us" [CX-0219]. Every CFM stance below assumes Safran's consent, sought through the commercial engines head [CX-0184]. No evidence shows Ghai negotiating with Safran directly **(inference)**. `ge_genx_next` is GE alone, with no gate.

### Part 1: as the market cell's CFM/GE today

**An airframer launches fps or NGSA and picks or tenders the engine.**
- **Price:** no launch-era discounts [CX-0930]. He accepts early OE losses to seed the installed base [CX-0949] and earns them back on service contracts priced right [CX-0901] and re-priced after launch [CX-0215].
- **Exclusivity:** no statement of his own. He prizes the installed base [CX-0994] and flight share [CX-0980], so he would back sole source **(inference)**.
- **Ramp:** paced to the airframer's published rate [CX-0982], with capex inside 2-3% [CX-0922].
- **Engine:** default `cfm_ducted` **(inference)**; he treats new-engine ramps as the biggest risk [CX-0933].
- **Refuses:** a number before volumes are agreed [CX-0944], and price/cost-negative terms [CX-0929].

**Rolls-Royce launches an UltraFan narrowbody (Solo or Joint Venture).** He never names Rolls-Royce. By his reaction to the GTF's 2023 problems (raised by the analyst): no change to price or capacity while supply binds [CX-0913], and R&D held [CX-0962] **(inference)**.

**Pratt & Whitney launches a next-generation GTF.** He defends with win ratio and share of Airbus deliveries [CX-0906] and LEAP-1A durability parity [CX-0996]. Profit, not share (said of GE Power) [CX-0912], so no price war **(inference)**.

**An airframer asks for concessions.** Launch-era price cuts are refused [CX-0930]. Tariffs are passed on as a surcharge [CX-0964]. Late-engine penalties are accrued and called immaterial [CX-0943]. With Boeing in difficulty he keeps shipping [CX-0945, CX-0986], and would offer delivery and timing flexibility rather than net price **(inference)**.

**A durability or supply-chain inject hits** (`engine_maturity_slip`, `supply_chain_crunch`). Reserve in the quarter [CX-0905]; retrofit at scheduled shop visits [CX-0997]; engineers to suppliers [CX-0993] and a stable demand signal [CX-0990]; cut the volume guide but hold profit [CX-0940, CX-0942]; plan for tight supply to persist [CX-0975].

**A 787 or A350 Re-engine opens a widebody tender.** GE alone. Widebody OE is "now profitable" [CX-0966], and the GEnx under service contracts is his model [CX-0951]. He would offer `ge_genx_next` as a derivative on a cost-down curve [CX-0950] **(inference)**, priced only once volumes are fixed [CX-0944]. He would warn against a second new-engine ramp while the GE9X loses money [CX-0995] **(inference)**.
