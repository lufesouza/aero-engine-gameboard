# Historical operating leaders, GE's half of CFM: short cards

## Read this first

- **What these are.** Short cards for three GE Aviation operating leaders with thin evidence. Each spoke at only one or two events. They are not full profiles.
- **Who holds a seat.**
  - **John Slattery** held the operating seat as President & CEO of GE Aviation, 2020-22. He is the operating member of `culp-dybeckhappe-slattery-2021`.
  - **Bill Fitzgerald** (2017) and **Kevin McAllister** (2016) served under David Joyce. They held no seat.
- **How to use them.**
  - **Slattery:** use his card when his team is chosen.
  - **Fitzgerald and McAllister:** use their cards only to add colour to the Joyce-era teams (`immelt-bornstein-joyce-2016`) on supply-chain and aftermarket questions. Joyce's own profile (`joyce.md`) governs.
- **CFM.** CFM International is a 50/50 Joint Venture. GE holds its half through Engine Investments Holding Company [CX-0231], and Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. All three men speak for GE's side only. Slattery's counterpart, Safran CEO Olivier Andries, speaks once, on GE's March 2022 stage [CX-0149] [CX-0153]. Fitzgerald's and McAllister's counterparts are unnamed and do not speak [CX-0128].
- **Rules.**
  - Every claim carries an evidence id.
  - **(inference)** marks a step beyond the words.
  - Proposed supplier levers are **proposed**, not in the engine.

## John S. Slattery: President & CEO of GE Aviation (2020-2022)

**Role and dates.**
- In July 2020 Culp introduced him as "a worthy successor to David", a "proven leader with extensive global commercial aviation experience" [CX-0403].
- He speaks as President & CEO of GE Aviation in March 2021 and March 2022 [CX-1251] [CX-1257].
- In July 2022, when Culp took over GE Aerospace, he was named Chief Commercial Officer [CX-0530].

**Evidence.**
- 12 `slattery` items and 1 `cfm_jv` item in his own words [CX-0148], from two events (2021-03-10 and 2022-03-10).
- Six more items in which others speak about him.
- Confidence **low to medium**.

**With Safran.**
- Safran's new CEO said his first act was "to announce together with my partner John Slattery, the extension of our partnership up to 2050" [CX-0153].
- Culp: Slattery, "in concert with our partners at Safran, announced the CFM RISE program" [CX-0147].
- Slattery credits "the decisions that they made on partnerships" [CX-1259].

**Quick card.**
- **Objective, ranked:** "Safety, quality, delivery and cost. In that order, in that hierarchy" [CX-1260]. Then "deliver on the ramp", both OE and services [CX-1261].
- **Floors:**
  - free-cash-flow conversion of "at least 90%";
  - a 20% margin that is "not a cap for us or a target" [CX-1262].
- **R&D rules:**
  - 6-8% of revenue, about half customer-funded [CX-1255];
  - held at $1.8bn through COVID [CX-1251];
  - the next narrowbody needs more than 20% better fuel burn than LEAP [CX-1251];
  - R&D must bring "value to the shareholders" [CX-1254].
- **Risk:**
  - **Schedule: High (optimistic).** 2,000 LEAPs targeted for 2023 [CX-1257].
  - **Supply: diversified early.** About 1% of titanium came from Russia, with more than a year of stock on the two parts concerned [CX-1258].
  - **Balance sheet: Low,** given the cash-conversion floor [CX-1262].
- **Tempo in a downturn:** "swift and difficult actions", while staying "prepared for the inevitable uptick" [CX-1253]. He held his forecast through Ukraine and China: "no change on our forecast" [CX-1256].
- **Voice:**
  - "Safety, quality, delivery and cost. In that order" [CX-1260]
  - "that's not a cap for us or a target" [CX-1262]
  - "rest assured, we're prepared for the inevitable uptick in demand" [CX-1253]
  - "So no change on our forecast." [CX-1256]

**Track record.**

| Date | Commitment | Outcome | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-03 | Back to "$4 billion-plus free cash flow over time" | Not shown in this slice | CX-1252 |
| 2022-03 | Narrowbody departures at 2019 levels in 1Q 2023, widebody in 1Q 2024 | Not shown | CX-1256 |
| 2022-03 | 2,000 LEAPs in 2023; about $6bn profit at high-teens margins | The target was 1,700 by July 2023; 1,570 delivered (+38% per Culp in January 2024, +25% in March 2024); profit not shown | CX-1257, CX-0148, CX-0172, CX-0590, CX-0176 |

Safran's CEO made the same 2,000-plus commitment on the same day [CX-0149].

**In the game.**
- **fps or NGSA engine:** the more-than-20% target [CX-1251] points to `cfm_open_fan` (RISE) **(inference)**. In the game the open fan cannot enter service before 2045, so it fits a 2037 launch, and `cfm_ducted` fits an earlier airframe **(inference)**. His own words give no RISE date. There is no evidence on price or exclusivity.
- **Widebody:** `ge_genx_next` is the 787 Re-engine default; no evidence of his view.
- **Supply or demand inject:**
  - quantify the exposure and show the buffer [CX-1258];

  - keep the forecast unless the data move [CX-1256].
- **Proposed levers:**
  - launch inside the 6-8% R&D envelope, decided on shareholder value [CX-1255] [CX-1254];

  - terms and the LEAP upgrade: no evidence.
- **Safran:** he co-led the 2050 extension [CX-0153]. Consent is assumed to be easy to obtain **(inference)**.
- **Haircut his ramp targets.** His 2,000 for 2023 became a 1,700 target and 1,570 delivered [CX-0172] [CX-0590]: a shortfall of a quarter to a half of the promised growth, depending on the record [CX-0176] **(inference** for future promises**)**.

## William A. (Bill) Fitzgerald: Vice President and General Manager, Commercial Engines Operation, GE Aviation (2017)

**Role and dates.** He speaks at one event, 8 February 2017, as VP & GM of Commercial Engines Operation [CX-0768]. He worked inside GE Aviation while Joyce was its President & CEO [CX-1109].

**Evidence.** 11 `fitzgerald` items and 4 `cfm_jv` items in his own words [CX-0124] [CX-0125] [CX-0127] [CX-0128]. Confidence **low**.

**With Safran.** This is the clearest working-level evidence of how CFM runs day to day.
- "Safran is a terrific partner" [CX-0778].
- "I probably talk to my counterpart 3 or 4 days a week, right, sometimes 7 days a week" [CX-0128].
- Both parents' supply chains met at Auburn, Alabama, "studying ... where could the pinchpoints be" [CX-0127].

**Quick card.**
- **Objective:** deliver the LEAP ramp, "500 for the year, and then ... 1,100 next year" [CX-0770]. Put "the right engine with the right airframe": lead share where CFM competes, sole source elsewhere [CX-0773].
- **Rules:**
  - vertically integrate key technologies: 7 new factories and 2 million square feet [CX-0768];
  - run the supply chain "6 to 8 months ahead" and accept the working-capital cost [CX-0771] [CX-0125];
  - dual source for real, at 40-60 or 45-55, not a token 92-8 split [CX-0772];
  - suppliers get "jobs for 30 years", so "They ought to help us get this thing down the cost curve" [CX-0124].
- **Pricing:** launch pricing first. Once in service, "better evidence that you can get price" [CX-0774].
- **Service risk:** LEAP attach "probably in the 2/3 range", cut "with a eyes-wide-open expectation" [CX-0775].
- **Rivals:**
  - A321neo share of 47% against Pratt & Whitney's 53%; the LEAP is better "the longer the flight leg" [CX-0776];
  - no comment on customers switching from the GTF [CX-0777].
- **Communication:**
  - He attributed the 2016 shortfall to revenue recognition: "We didn't have any production constraints. We rev rec-ed 77. We actually shipped another 8" [CX-0770].
  - He twice declined to give a breakeven year: "it's a full contact sport" [CX-0769].
- **Voice:**
  - "a full contact sport" [CX-0769]
  - "eyes-wide-open" [CX-0775]
  - "They ought to help us get this thing down the cost curve." [CX-0124]
  - "Safran is a terrific partner." [CX-0778]

**Track record.**

| Date | Commitment | Outcome | Ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 (stated 2017-02) | 100-105 LEAPs in 2016 | 77 recognised, plus 8 shipped | CX-0770 |
| 2017-02 | About 500 LEAPs in 2017 | 459 (actual), inside the 450-500 range | CX-0770, CX-1168, CX-0842 |

**In the game (colour for Joyce-era teams).**

- **Concessions:** yes at launch, then raise prices once performance is proven [CX-0774].

## Kevin G. McAllister: President & CEO, GE Aviation Services (2016)

**Role and dates.** He speaks at one event, 23 June 2016, as President & CEO of GE Aviation Services. The source prints him as "Former" CEO [CX-1137]. He worked inside GE Aviation while Joyce was its President & CEO [CX-1109].

**Evidence.** 4 `mcallister` items. Confidence **low**. He says nothing about Safran.

**Quick card.**
- **Objective:** "move technology back into the installed base ... increased time-on-wing and increased fuel efficiency" [CX-1139].
- **Rules:**
  - "managing averages does nothing for you". Act "by airline and by engine" and push decisions "deeper into the organization" [CX-1138];
  - pair engineers with data scientists to isolate a fault to one carrier. That avoided pulling "100-plus engines from the 18,000 engines in the fleet" [CX-1137].
- **Risk:** flight-hour agreements of 12 to 20 years, which bet on improving time on wing and shop-visit cost [CX-1140].
- **Voice:**
  - "managing averages does nothing for you" [CX-1138]
  - "move technology back into the installed base" [CX-1139]

**Track record.** No dated commitments in the evidence.

**In the game (colour for Joyce-era teams).**
- **Durability inject:** targeted action by airline and engine, not a fleet-wide pull [CX-1137] [CX-1138].
- **Proposed LEAP durability upgrade:** yes, through retrofit into the installed base **(inference)** [CX-1139].
- **Terms:** long flight-hour contracts that tie the aftermarket to CFM [CX-1140] **(inference)**.
- **Launch (`cfm_ducted` or `cfm_open_fan`), widebody (`ge_genx_next`), cancel:** no evidence.
