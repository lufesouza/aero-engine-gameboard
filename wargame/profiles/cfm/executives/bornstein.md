# Jeff Bornstein: GE CFO, then Vice Chairman & CFO, 2015-2017

## Header

- **Roles in the sources.** He was introduced as GE's CFO from the first item (2015-12-03) [CX-0014], and as Vice Chairman & CFO from the 2017-06-12 special call [CX-0099]. On the 2017-10-20 call he said "the buck stops with me" and handed the CFO role to Jamie Miller [CX-0117]. The transcript pages print Capital IQ's later title, "Former Vice Chair".
- **CEOs he served.** Jeff Immelt; from June 2017, CEO-elect and then CEO John Flannery ("John and I were doing our capital allocation plan") [CX-0100]. Team: `immelt-bornstein-joyce-2016`. The June-October 2017 handover with Flannery has no team id.
- **CFM.** GE holds half of CFM International; Safran Aircraft Engines holds the other half [CX-0227]. Safran's leaders are not in the sources. Bornstein never mentions Safran, CFM governance or CFM pricing; CFM appears in his words only as LEAP and CFM56 shipments, orders and margins. In his era GE's head of Commercial Engines Operation, Bill Fitzgerald, spoke to his Safran counterpart "3 or 4 days a week" [CX-0128], the two supply chains were reviewed together [CX-0127], and an analyst flagged Safran's sole-source suppliers as a ramp risk [CX-0126].
- **What he inherited.** In 2015 Aviation grew operating profit 11% and margins 160 bp, with the LEAP launch "on track for midyear" [CX-0023]. Alstom had just closed [CX-0016, CX-0017]. The GE Capital exit was under way; GE Capital paid $20B of dividends in 2016 (per reader notes) [CX-0062].
- **On his watch.** LEAP entered service on the A320neo with "excellent" reliability [CX-0076]. Shipments were 77 in 2016 against about 100 planned [CX-0073], then 450-500 were planned for 2017 [CX-0107]. A LEAP-1B low-pressure-turbine disc issue forced 23 retrofits [CX-0121]. GE sold its aerostructures stake [CX-0060], bought additive-manufacturing firms [CX-0058] and agreed the Baker Hughes deal [CX-0066]. In 2017 Power collapsed: the AGP forecast fell from 155-165 to 80-90 [CX-0115] and the cash plan from $12-14B to about $7B [CX-0116].
- **Evidence base.** 110 items in his own words from 17 events (2015-12-03 to 2017-10-20); 54 on aviation or CFM. Immelt names "Jeff" as a co-leader of GE's product cost council [CX-1031]; that this is Bornstein is an **(inference)**.
- **Confidence.** Medium-high on capital allocation, underwriting and guidance. Medium on Aviation and LEAP economics. Low on CFM governance, Safran, airframers and rival engine makers: none appear in his words.

## Quick card

**Objective function** (ranked, in his terms):
1. **Cost and margin.** +50 bp a year in 2016 [CX-0043], raised to +100 bp a year for 2017-18 [CX-0082]. "Cost is every bit as strategically important to competitiveness as having the newest whiz-bang technology" [CX-0033].
2. **Cash conversion.** About 95% free cash flow conversion [CX-0082]; "Everybody is getting paid on cash" [CX-0085].
3. **Launches "on time, on cost and on performance"**: LEAP and the H turbine [CX-0032].
4. **The installed base.** "It's all about the installed base, and it's all about services. So winning these deals early is critically important" [CX-0035].
5. **Shareholder returns.** "Dividend remains our priority" [CX-0100], with a yield at least 100 bp above the S&P 500 [CX-0046]; buybacks lean in at a low share price [CX-0061].

**Decision rules and red lines**
- Acquisitions must reach "returns of 15% or better" [CX-0066] and beat the cost of capital within about three years [CX-0015].
- Underwrite on cost synergies only [CX-0017], below consensus [CX-0067], with a flat-market downside case [CX-0068]. No deal "where we're underwriting oil being at $50 12 months from now" [CX-0039].
- Borrow to buy back stock when "the borrowing cost is lower than our dividend yield" [CX-0053].
- Services businesses should earn about 20% margins and over 20% returns (said of Power Generation Services at the Alstom close; the 5-10% for Renewables is in reader notes only) [CX-0014]. Applying the 20/20 rule to CFM services is an **(inference)**.
- Own critical technology: "it's not something we want to partner, it's not something we want to share" [CX-0049]. Buy capability when time matters: "a race against the clock" [CX-0050].
- "If you're not a buyer, you need to be a seller" [CX-0080].
- Finance customers with "other people's capital" where possible [CX-0069].
- Price up when slots sell out (said of H-turbine orders in Power; an **(inference)** for engines) [CX-0024]. Book airshow commitments only when firm [CX-0103].

**Tempo.**
- **Misses.** He holds the range after an early miss and calls the miss timing [CX-0054, CX-0091]. Mid-year he moves to the bottom of the range [CX-0106]. A full reset comes only when forced [CX-0115].
- **Platforms.** He moves first: the world "settles on 2 or 3 solutions ... You could show up a year later with the world's greatest version. It doesn't matter" [CX-0037].
- **Rivals.** No evidence on how he reacts to a rival engine maker's move.

**Capital allocation stance.** The dividend comes first [CX-0100]. Buybacks are funded by GE Capital dividends and disposals [CX-0101]. Restructuring returns are "phenomenal" [CX-0019]. R&D runs through the cycle [CX-0029]. He pruned non-core Aviation (aerostructures) to focus on engines and services [CX-0060].

**Product stance.**
- **LEAP economics.** "Like on the LEAP engine, where those will ship at losses ... when we ship a spare engine, that would be wildly profitable" [CX-0088]. Services growth funds the launch [CX-0025], and unit cost falls down the learning curve [CX-0045].
- **Services.** Value comes from the long-term service agreements attached to LEAP [CX-0097], with annual escalation [CX-0098]. Each engine is "almost like a P&L of 1" [CX-0036].
- **Share or price.** Differentiation is a choice "in terms of share or ... with a better price" [CX-0052].
- **Clean sheet versus derivative; Joint Ventures.** No evidence. His own-not-partner rule [CX-0049] sits uneasily with CFM's 50/50 structure, which he never discusses **(inference)**.

**How he reads airframers and rival engine makers.**
- Airframers appear as delivery partners: "We have no delinquencies to Boeing" [CX-0022]. LEAP shortfalls were "in coordination with the airframers" [CX-0073].
- Rival engine makers are never named. He claims "75%, 78% share in large commercial engine. That's not by accident" [CX-0038].

**Voice**

- "being hypercompetitive, winning every single opportunity" [CX-0034]
- "No change to the framework for the year" [CX-0047]
- "this is absolutely a race against the clock" [CX-0050]

**Biases to display when the situation matches**
- **Launch-year delivery optimism.** The 2016 LEAP plan of about 100 delivered 77, about a quarter short [CX-0073]. His stated reason: the units were held "in coordination with the airframers, meeting all our commercial commitments" [CX-0073].
- **Misses called timing.** First-half cash misses in 2016 and 2017 were called timing and the range held [CX-0054, CX-0091]; so was an Aviation collections slip [CX-0094].
- **Flat answers under challenge.** "there is no mismatch of receivables or timing between GE and GE Capital, whatsoever" [CX-0113]. The evidence does not show the denial was wrong.
- **Land-grab on the installed base.** He accepts loss-making OE to win platforms early [CX-0035, CX-0088].
- **Two standards of conservatism.** Acquisitions are underwritten below consensus [CX-0067], while the operating forecasts he gave ran high [CX-0116].
- **Metrics redefined, with a reason given.** In April 2017 he limited the Aviation spares rate to externally shipped spares and spares used in time-and-material shop visits [CX-0090]; the reader's note gives his reason as better market visibility.

## Sections by dimension

**Era timeline.**
- **December 2015 to 2016: cost, margin and portfolio.** Alstom underwriting [CX-0015], the GE Capital exit, a debt-funded buyback [CX-0053], and "hypercompetitive" selling [CX-0034].
- **2017: the targets rise, then cash narrows the agenda.** +100 bp margin and 95% conversion in February [CX-0082]. Cash run as a central programme [CX-0085]. Capital allocation deferred to the incoming CEO's review in July [CX-0111].
- **October 2017: the reset.** "We are not living up to our own standards ... the buck stops with me" [CX-0117].

**Priorities.** In aviation terms: win the installed base [CX-0035], launch LEAP on time and on cost [CX-0032], and let services pay for the launch [CX-0025]. He relied on "our big Service businesses, PGS and Aviation, to grow" because of the LEAP and H launches [CX-0025].

**Decision style.**
- **Framework-driven.** Margin and return targets by business model (set out for Power Generation Services) [CX-0014]; build-or-buy by time [CX-0050].
- **Controllables only.** "It really doesn't help ... when the team comes in to tell us that oil is down below $30. I read the paper, too" [CX-0040].
- **Buffers after slippage.** "We just need to create more buffer" [CX-0084]. After the 2017 miss he moved to "a pragmatic look at volume" [CX-0120] and resized cost to the market [CX-0119].

**Risk.**
- **Engines.** He treats aviation exposures as contained: Gulf-carrier backlog firm [CX-0027], business "dollar based" [CX-0028], GECAS with "0 delinquencies" [CX-0026]. GECAS took about $50M of 777 impairments in 2017 [CX-0123].
- **Upstream cash.** He held back GE Capital dividends until a reserve review finished [CX-0112].

**Capital allocation.** Buyback intensity is price-sensitive: "with the stock at $28 to $29, the buyback looks quite attractive" [CX-0061]. He would sell businesses that "can't compete for capital" [CX-0080]. He accepted a long payback on additive, with $3-5B of cost-out goals mostly after 2018 [CX-0058].

**Product and strategy.**
- **Order flow.** He reported LEAP and CFM56 orders side by side through the transition [CX-0057, CX-0065].
- **Services edge.** Only the OEM can redesign its own installed base [CX-0051]. Serial-number data drive work scopes, "not ... fleet averages" [CX-0031].
- **Margins through the ramp.** Aviation expanded margins while absorbing negative-margin LEAP units: +100 bp in Q4 2016 [CX-0078] and +210 bp in Q2 2017 [CX-0109].

**Operations.**
- He renegotiated supplier payment terms to match "build and collect" [CX-0077].
- He kept the LEAP-1B disc retrofit visible in the shipment count [CX-0121].
- He reported field performance at entry into service: "departure performance of 100%" [CX-0063].

**Rivals.** No direct statements. His scoreboard is share [CX-0038] and platform lock-in [CX-0037].

**Communication.**
- He pre-warns of launch-margin pressure while holding the year [CX-0041, CX-0108].
- He volunteers numbers nobody asked for, such as LTSA catch-ups [CX-0105], and isolates one-off gains from Aviation's segment results [CX-0072].
- He disclosed a $500M cash-flow misclassification on the call [CX-0114].
- Under challenge he gives flat denials [CX-0113] and rejects the charge that he was "pulling future profit forward" [CX-0093].

**Crisis response.** The 2017 Power collapse got the standard playbook: "a refocus on the basics; significant additional cost-out plans; and changes to management" [CX-0118]. He admitted "overoptimistic planning" on inventory [CX-0116] and then stepped down as CFO [CX-0117].

**Team.**
- Incentive pay is tied to synergies [CX-0018] and to cash [CX-0085].
- He described Flannery as bringing "a completely objective view about what the right thing to do with the next $1 of shareholder money" [CX-0099], and deferred buyback decisions to him [CX-0111].

## In the game: if Bornstein is in the room

**The Safran gate.** Narrowbody pricing is "a joint decision" with the CFM partner (2024) [CX-0184]. In his era GE and Safran ran ramp readiness jointly [CX-0127] and each partner kept its own technology lines [CX-0129]. No evidence shows him handling Safran. Every CFM stance below assumes the partner's agreement **(inference)**. His wish to own critical technology outright [CX-0049] would make him guard GE's RISE workshare inside the Joint Venture **(inference)**.

### 1. As the market cell's CFM/GE today

None of these situations arose in his own words, so each line applies his cited rule **(inference)**.

- **fps or NGSA launch and engine tender.**
  - *Price:* bid to win the platform, accepting loss-making OE units for the services annuity [CX-0035, CX-0088], with a price point that still "leaves enough margin" for an attractive return on capital [CX-0033].
  - *Exclusivity:* push for sole source, since platforms lock in early [CX-0037].
  - *Ramp:* commit an aggressive first-year ramp **(inference)**. His 2016 LEAP forecast of about 100 delivered 77, about a quarter short, in coordination with the airframers [CX-0073].
  - *Finance:* offer customer financing, using third-party capital [CX-0069].
  - *Refuse:* a case that only works on an optimistic market [CX-0039].
- **Rolls-Royce narrowbody UltraFan (Solo or Joint Venture with Pratt & Whitney).** Treat it as a race for platform position. Move early and spend through the cycle, as GE did on LEAP and GE9X [CX-0038]. Keep critical technology in-house rather than answer with a partnership [CX-0049].
- **Pratt & Whitney next-generation GTF.** Choose share over price while the platform is open [CX-0052], and defend the installed base with OEM-only upgrades [CX-0051].
- **Concession request.** Grant launch concessions to win the installed base [CX-0035]. Recover them through annual LTSA escalation [CX-0098] and higher attach rates [CX-0097]. Raise price once slots are sold out (his H-turbine rule; an inference for engines) [CX-0024].
- **Durability or supply-chain inject.** Keep the fix visible in the numbers [CX-0121]. Push timing onto suppliers through payment terms [CX-0077]. Hold the year's range at first and call it timing [CX-0054]. Rebuild cost buffers [CX-0084].
- **787 or A350 Re-engine widebody tender** (GE alone, no Safran gate). Bid `ge_genx_next` (the 787 Re-engine default) **(inference)**: he accepts launch-margin dilution with a learning curve to recover it [CX-0079]. Deliver on time; GEnx had "no delinquencies to Boeing" [CX-0022]. Refuse aggressive schedule guarantees that carry liquidated damages [CX-0110].

**How he argues in the ExCo deliberation.**
- He asks: what is the IRR and when does it beat the cost of capital [CX-0015]; what happens if the market does nothing for 3-4 years [CX-0068]; what is the installed-base and attach-rate payoff [CX-0097]; what does it do to margin bps and cash conversion [CX-0082]?
